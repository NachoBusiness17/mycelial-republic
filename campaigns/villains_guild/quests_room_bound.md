# THE VILLAINS GUILD — Five Contract-Trap Quest Templates

Seat B night-run. Bind 1:1 to `campaigns/villains_guild/module.json` rooms. Failure costs pride, never progress: wash out, pick a new name at the hatch, retry. Every trap's honest clue was always on the table.

---

## 1. The Complimentary Torch

**Room:** I — The Guildhall of Gracious Crimes  
**Arc beat:** **I — The First Signature.** Players learn the guild's weapons are sentences. Keep the signed contract (both sides). Later, this is Exhibit One: the Guildmaster sold "free" as a skip-me.

### The clause (verbatim, front)

> The Guild shall furnish the party with one (1) complimentary torch, to be collected at the door. The party agrees to proceed by torchlight. Unused equipment remains Guild property. Heroes who return without the torch shall be billed replacement cost. Heroes who return WITH the torch shall be billed for wear. By signing, the party affirms they have read this contract in its entirety, including the reverse.

### The twist (fair in hindsight)

The reverse, after a decorative vine border that looks like the end of the page:

> Torchlight is defined as light emitted by a Guild-issued torch currently in the party's possession and lit. Darkness, moonlight, darkvision, "we can see fine," and borrowed lanterns are not torchlight. Parties proceeding without the complimentary torch collected AND lit are trespassing and forfeit the job. Complimentary describes the Guild's mood, not the price.

People grab the job and skip the door-hook because "complimentary" reads as skippable. Or they take the torch as loot and never strike it. Either way: trespass.

**Where the honest clue sat:** three places, all on the front. (1) "to be collected at the door" — if you rush the hall you never picked it up. (2) "proceed by torchlight" is a duty, not flavor. (3) "including the reverse" is the sentence that tells you the paper has a punchline. A careful reader turns it over before the ink dries.

### Punishes / rewards

- **Punishes:** skimming; treating "free" as a reason not to read.
- **Rewards:** turning the page; asking one good question: *"What's on the reverse?"*

### Failure (pride only)

Wash-out of the syndicate. Retry under a new name.

> "Congratulations. You have been promoted to Person Who Did Not Turn the Paper Over. The syndicate has standards. They are low. You still missed them."
>
> "Your badge has been melted into a paperweight that says READ ME on both sides. Collect a fresh face at the hatch. The old one is a cautionary tale with shoes."

### Success payoff

The torch is actually free, the job stands, and the clerk stamps **BOTH SIDES READ**. Party keeps the contract as Exhibit One.

**[SKILL: parse the whole instrument — front, reverse, and the sentence that admits there is a reverse]**

## 2. Annex A Is On the Wall

**Room:** II — The Hall of Fine Print  
**Arc beat:** **II — Two Papers, One Lie.** The guild relies on you treating one page as the deal. Keep ticket + annex together. Later, this is Exhibit Two: the Guildmaster is not the client.

### The clause (verbatim, Job Ticket)

> Retrieve the Silver Kettle from the Widow's Pantry. Payment: 50 gp upon delivery to the Guild. This ticket is the entire agreement except as modified by Annex A, posted. Time is of the essence. Do not dawdle in the hall.

### The twist (fair in hindsight)

Annex A is not in the hand. It is a second sheet nailed to the opposite wall in a different hand, under a painting of a smiling kettle:

> Delivery means delivery to the *client*, not to the Guild. The Guild is not the client. The client is named in the Registry receipt, not on this ticket. The 50 gp on this ticket is a *quote*, not a *due*. Quotes expire at sundown the day they are posted. Heroes who deliver the kettle to the Guild have retrieved it and then handed it to the wrong party. That is called a donation.

If you only read the ticket, you look like a hero and get paid nothing. The kettle arrives at the wrong desk.

**Where the honest clue sat:** the ticket names Annex A and says **posted**, not attached. "This ticket is the entire agreement except…" is a joke that tells you it is not. "Do not dawdle in the hall" is the Guildmaster shooing you past the wall where the second paper lives. A careful reader walks the room before they walk the job.

### Punishes / rewards

- **Punishes:** treating one document as the whole deal; hurrying because the paper said hurry.
- **Rewards:** hunting every document the first document names; asking *"Where is Annex A?"*

### Failure (pride only)

> "You brought the kettle home to the people who sold you the job. That is shoplifting in reverse. Leave the hat. It knew."
>
> "Washed out: failed to notice the second piece of paper. The second piece of paper noticed you. It is still laughing. New name, same hall, try reading the furniture."

### Success payoff

Kettle goes to the named client. Quote is converted to a due *in writing*. Party keeps ticket + annex as a paired exhibit: the Guild's own annex says the Guildmaster is not the client.

**[SKILL: cross-reference every named document before you move the loot]**

## 3. The Witness Who Wrote Himself

**Room:** III — The Registry of Regrets  
**Arc beat:** **III — The Circular Seal.** The Guildmaster's authority is a citation that cites itself. The real charter (drawer marked PERSONAL) names the Archivist. That is Exhibit Three: he never had confiscation power.

### The clause (verbatim)

> The bearer is hereby authorized, per Witness Statement WS-9 on file, to confiscate "misfiled memories" from the Registry stacks. Authority derives from the original charter, as attested. Do not open drawers marked PERSONAL. All other drawers are Guild business. Return any confiscated memories to the Guildmaster's desk before closing.

### The twist (fair in hindsight)

WS-9 is in the stacks, as advertised. If you pull it: the "witness" is the Guildmaster, attesting to his own signature, dated **the afternoon after this job was posted**. The "original charter" on the wall is labeled COPY in the bottom margin. The only instrument that names who may authorize confiscation is in the drawer marked PERSONAL — and it names the **Archivist**, not the Guildmaster.

The PERSONAL drawer is not a gotcha. "On file" is an invitation. Opening PERSONAL *after* WS-9 shows a man witnessing himself is curiosity doing its job. Returning memories to the Guildmaster's desk would be handing the stolen goods to the thief.

**Where the honest clue sat:** (1) "on file" means go to the file. (2) "as attested" has no name on the job sheet — the name is the thing you are supposed to fetch. (3) WS-9's date is later than the posting; the date is the tell. (4) "Do not open drawers marked PERSONAL" is the Guildmaster's greed sitting on the real charter. The villain's own flaw points at the evidence.

### Punishes / rewards

- **Punishes:** trusting a citation you did not pull; treating "do not open" as the end of inquiry.
- **Rewards:** requesting the source; reading the date; asking *"Who attested?"*

### Failure (pride only)

> "You confiscated a memory that was never yours on the word of a man who witnessed himself. That is not a job. That is a diary with a badge."
>
> "Washed out for believing 'on file' meant 'don't bother the filing cabinet.' The cabinet has filed you under GULLIBLE, pending shredding. Pick a sharper name."

### Success payoff

Memories stay in the stacks. Party copies WS-9 (self-witness, bad date) and the PERSONAL charter (Archivist named). Stamp: **SOURCE PULLED**. These two pages are the legal hook for the later vote.

**[SKILL: check provenance — pull the cited document, read the date, name the attestor]**

## 4. A Generous Tenth

**Room:** IV — The Counting House of Minor Miracles  
**Arc beat:** **IV — The Ledger Confessed.** Greed overreaches in the books. The itemized sheet is Exhibit Four: they billed you for rounding and for sympathy.

### The clause (verbatim)

> Party share is a generous tenth of net proceeds. Net proceeds = gross loot minus Guild costs, minus a handling fee of 10, rounded in the Guild's favor. Today's gross: 100 gp. Guild costs: 40 gp (itemized below). Sign to accept your tenth.

**Itemized below** (sidebar, smaller hand, easy to treat as decoration):

> Torch rental 3. Ink 2. Ambient heroism tax 15. Rounding reserve 10. Annex-posting 5. Sympathy 5. Total: 40.

### The twist (fair in hindsight)

The 40 *adds*. That is the con. A correct sum of crooked lines is still a crooked bill.

- "Ambient heroism tax" and "Sympathy" are not costs of the job; they are moods with numbers.
- "Rounding reserve 10" charges you for rounding that has not happened yet.
- "a handling fee of 10" sits next to "generous tenth" with **no unit** — gold or percent? The clerk will pick whichever is worse after you sign.
- "Rounded in the Guild's favor" on a page full of whole numbers is a tell: they plan to run a different formula than the one you can see, then shrug.

Honest net, if you strike the moods: 100 − (3 + 2 + 5) = 90. Handling, if 10 means 10 gp: 80. A tenth is 8 gp, written, with units. Sign *that*.

**Where the honest clue sat:** the clause itself says "itemized below," so the sidebar is part of the contract, not wallpaper. Add the six lines: 3+2+15+10+5+5 = 40. The arithmetic of the total is correct; the **categories** are the fraud. The missing unit on "10" is sitting in the same sentence as "tenth." Coinbender's whole personality is this page.

### Punishes / rewards

- **Punishes:** accepting a friendly round number; trusting a total because it adds up.
- **Rewards:** re-adding; asking *"Is the 10 gold or percent — and is Sympathy a real cost?"*

### Failure (pride only)

> "You took a generous tenth of a number that had already been mugged. The number filed a complaint. You are the complaint."
>
> "Washed out: trusted a total because the addition worked. Totals that add up are the polite ones. Collect a fresh face; this one is bad at money and worse at pride."

### Success payoff

Strike Sympathy, heroism tax, and rounding reserve. Write the unit. Take 8 gp and the **itemized sheet**, unaltered, as Exhibit Four. The Guildmaster's greed (billing for rounding, billing for feelings) is now a document the floor can pass around.

**[SKILL: verify arithmetic AND categories — a correct sum of crooked lines is still a crooked bill]**

## 5. Unanimous Courtesy

**Room:** V — The Overthrow Floor  
**Arc beat:** **V — The Courtesy Coup.** Graduation. Players out-con the Guildmaster with his own bylaws, using HOLD / VETO / STOP as vote tokens, and pool Exhibits One through Four. Playing Guildmaster next chapter is the ceremony, not a reward chest.

### The clause (verbatim)

> Any motion to censure the Guildmaster requires unanimous courtesy of those present. Courtesy is defined in Bylaw 12. Silence is courtesy. A spoken HOLD pauses the motion. A spoken VETO ends it. A spoken STOP ends the meeting. The Guildmaster may interpret courtesy. This clause is in force until superseded by a motion that has received unanimous courtesy.

### The twist (fair in hindsight)

If you shout him out, he VETO or STOP. If you sit quiet, "silence is courtesy" lets *him* interpret the room, and he will interpret it as love. The trap is the false choice: tantrum or freeze.

Bylaw 12 is posted behind him, in the same size hand as Annex A was. It reads:

> Courtesy includes the right of any member to place a HOLD until exhibits are read into the minutes. After exhibits are read, STOP means the meeting ends *with the minutes as written*. VETO applies to motions, not to exhibits. Interpretation of courtesy is a chair's privilege only while no HOLD is on the floor.

The out-con is procedural, not louder:

1. **HOLD** (pauses; does not die).
2. Read Exhibits One–Four into the minutes: reverse torch, Annex A (he is not the client), WS-9 + PERSONAL charter (Archivist holds confiscation), the crooked tenth.
3. Either STOP (minutes stand, his interpretation is now on record contradicting the charter) **or** pass a tiny courteous motion while HOLD is on — *"thank the Guildmaster for his service"* — with a rider that courtesy is hereafter tallied by spoken tokens, not by his interpretation. Silence is courtesy. He looks like a monster if he VETOES a thank-you.
4. "Those present" is a headcount. If his goons leave, the room changes. Scout can chart that.

Storming the vote without Bylaw 12 is the wash-out. Using his clause against his greed is the graduation.

**Where the honest clue sat:** the clause names Bylaw 12; 12 is on the wall. HOLD / VETO / STOP are defined in the same paragraph as the trap, which means they are legal tools, not flavor. "Until superseded by a motion that has received unanimous courtesy" is the Guildmaster bragging about a door he left open. "Silence is courtesy" works for a thank-you rider the same way it works for him — that symmetry is the fair tell.

### Punishes / rewards

- **Punishes:** overconfidence; treating a coup like a tantrum.
- **Rewards:** pooling the four exhibits; asking *"What does Bylaw 12 actually say?"*; using HOLD instead of swinging.

### Failure (pride only)

> "You tried to overthrow a man with a clause in his pocket by shouting at the clause. The clause won. Leave the gavel; it was never yours."
>
> "Washed out of the syndicate for confusing a coup with a tantrum. Come back with a better name and a slower mouth. The hatch is that way. It is also labeled. You will still miss it."

### Success payoff

Minutes close with the exhibits. The floor tallies HOLD / VETO / STOP as tokens. The Guildmaster is out-conned by his own courtesy rule; his flaw (he had to be the interpreter of the room) collapses the scheme. The party chairs the next chapter.

**[SKILL: build consensus — pool exhibits, use HOLD/VETO/STOP as tokens, do not let one speaker interpret the room]**

## Guildmaster's Pro-Tips

Spoken in the hall. Sound like insults. Each one is a verification drill he will not admit.

1. If a contract has a back, that is not decoration. That is where I keep the punchline.
2. Complimentary means I have already been paid. Usually by you. Later.
3. Annex A is never in your hand. That is not an accident. It is a personality test with nails in the wall.
4. When a paper says it is the entire agreement except for another paper, believe the second half of that sentence, you ornament.
5. When I say "on file," I am daring you to file. Most of you will not. I live on those people.
6. Dates are not trivia. I put the wrong one in on purpose and you thanked me for my time.
7. A number that adds up is not a number that is honest. My mother taught me that. She billed me for the lesson.
8. If your tenth sits next to a 10 with no unit, I will pick the unit after you smile. Do not smile.
9. If I am interpreting the vote, you already lost the vote. Try being boring. Read the bylaw behind my head.
10. Bring me one question so specific I have to look at my own paper. That is the only question I respect. I will still lie. Check anyway.
