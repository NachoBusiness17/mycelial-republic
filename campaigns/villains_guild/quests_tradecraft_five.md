# THE VILLAINS GUILD — Five Tradecraft Quest Templates (B2)

Contracts dated from the future arrive through deniable channels. The paper still looks like a job. The job is whether you can tell a source from a costume. Failure costs pride, never progress: wash out, pick a new name at the hatch, retry. Every trap's honest clue was always on the table.

---

## 1. The Tuesday That Hasn't Happened Yet

**Room:** I — The Guildhall of Gracious Crimes  
**Theme:** time-shifted anonymous tip (provenance-check before trust)  
**Arc beat:** **I — The Future Postmark.** A letter from later this week is still a *source*, not a command. Keep the pigeon-band and the two inks. Later this is Exhibit One: the Guildmaster's own loft mailed him a future.

### The clause (verbatim)

> The enclosed tip is dated the 3rd of Harvest — that is next Tuesday — and shall be accepted as current. Do not inquire after the sender. Unsigned intelligence is a Guild courtesy. Act on the tip before the date written on the letter, or the opportunity expires. The pigeon is not a witness. By signing, the party affirms they have treated the postmark as proof.

### The twist (fair in hindsight)

The tip inside says, in a heroic hand:

> Tomorrow the Widow's Kettle is unguarded at dawn. Take it for the Guild. Do not ask who told you. Asking spoils deniability.

The kettle is guarded. The "tomorrow" on a letter dated next Tuesday is a calendar trick: treat the *letter's date* as today and you rob the pantry on the wrong morning, caught by a baker who has not even pre-heated yet.

The letter is not from the future. It is from the Guild loft this morning, wearing next week's clothes.

### Where the honest clue hides

Four places, all handleable before anyone runs:

1. **Two inks.** The date "3rd of Harvest" is a greener ink than the body. Hold it to the window: the date sits *on top* of the paper, not in it.
2. **The pigeon-band.** The bird on the sill wears a copper ring stamped **GUILD LOFT 4**. Loft 4 is on today's register under "birds out this morning," not "birds from later." The clause says the pigeon is not a witness because the pigeon would testify against the Guildmaster.
3. **"Accepted as current"** is an instruction to *stop checking*, not a finding that you checked.
4. **The wall calendar** still says today is the 28th of Reaping. Next Tuesday is blank. Blank is not a source.

A careful reader asks one question — *"Who banded this bird?"* — and the clerk has to point at Loft 4.

### Punishes / rewards

- **Punishes:** treating urgency plus anonymity as a reason to skip provenance; obeying "do not inquire."
- **Rewards:** matching date to calendar; matching band to loft register; asking *"Who sent a bird that isn't allowed to talk?"*

### Failure (pride only)

> "You followed a letter from next Tuesday. Next Tuesday has filed a restraining order. Collect a name that exists in this week."
>
> "Washed out for trusting a bird with no return address. The bird is union. It will not testify. You will, under a dumber name."

### Success payoff

The kettle stays put. Party keeps the letter, the two-ink date, and a rubbing of GUILD LOFT 4. Clerk is forced to stamp **SOURCE ASKED**.

**[SKILL: provenance-check time-shifted unsigned tips — date, ink, loft, sender — before treating them as jobs]**

## 2. The Kindest Copy Survived

**Room:** II — The Hall of Fine Print  
**Theme:** chain of forwarded forwards; only one early copy is honest (survivorship-bias trap)  
**Arc beat:** **II — The Honest Ancestor.** The guild needs you to trust the copy still on the desk. Keep Copy A + Copy F paired. Later this is Exhibit Two: he is a forwarding service, not a witness.

### The clause (verbatim)

> You are issued Copy F of Memo 14, forwarded with endorsements. Each endorsement attests that the previous copy was received "as received." Act on Copy F. Earlier copies have been withdrawn as obsolete. Do not clutter the hall with outdated paper. The current copy is the true copy because it is current. Payment and target are as stated in the copy in your hand.

### Copy F (the one in hand)

> Pay the baker thirty (30) gp from the kettle-job and thank the Guild for noticing. Endorsed: A→B→C→D→E→F. All prior copies withdrawn. Current.

### The twist (fair in hindsight)

Copy F survived because every desk improved it. Improved is a verb that means *ruined, with love.*

Copy A is not destroyed. It is "withdrawn," which in this hall means **nailed behind the painting of a smiling kettle**. Copy A reads:

> Pay the baker three (3) gp. That is what we owe. Do not thank anyone. This is a bill, not a ballad.

Each hop added either a zero or a compliment:

| Copy | Mutation |
|------|----------|
| A | 3 gp. A bill. |
| B | 3 gp. "with thanks." |
| C | 13 gp. thanks kept. |
| D | 30 gp. thanks kept. |
| E | 30 gp. "thank the Guild for noticing." |
| F | 30 gp. thank the Guild. "Current." |

The baker is owed three. Copy F is a popularity contest that learned to add zeroes.

### Where the honest clue hides

On Copy F itself, if you do not treat the header as the whole paper:

1. **"as received"** is in quotation marks. Quotes around a receipt are how a liar says they photocopied a vibe.
2. **The smudge in the left margin** is a wax-blot print of Copy A's seal. Hold F to A and the dents kiss.
3. **"Withdrawn as obsolete"** is a *status*, not a truth-value. Obsolete means "stopped being useful to the forwarder."
4. **"Do not clutter the hall with outdated paper"** is the dawdle-shoo. The outdated paper is in the hall. It is furniture.

A careful reader asks *"Where do withdrawn copies go?"* The painting is not shy.

### Punishes / rewards

- **Punishes:** survivorship bias — trusting the copy that lasted because it flattered each desk; treating "current" as "honest."
- **Rewards:** hunting the earliest copy you can still hold; lining up hops; asking *"Who withdrew A, and did they throw it away or just hide it?"*

### Failure (pride only)

> "You implemented the version of the memo that got popular. Popular is not a source. Leave the hat; it endorsed you 'as received.'"
>
> "Washed out: believed 'current' meant 'honest.' Current means 'still here.' Liars are very current. New name, old hall, find Copy A before Copy G finds you."

### Success payoff

Baker gets 3 gp. Party keeps A-through-F as a stapled mutation-chain. Stamp: **EARLIEST COPY HELD**.

**[SKILL: survivorship-bias hygiene — the last forward is the least honest; pull the earliest copy you can still hold]**

## 3. The Oracle Who Meant the Other Door

**Room:** III — The Registry of Regrets  
**Theme:** a prophecy whose accuracy is real; the interpretation is the trap  
**Arc beat:** **III — The True Sentence, Wrong Door.** His certification of *accuracy* was never a certification of the *caption*. Keep sealed slip + posted gloss. Later this is Exhibit Three: he sold meaning as a service.

### The clause (verbatim)

> The sealed prophecy in Box 12 is accurate. The Guild certifies accuracy, not meaning. Follow the interpretation posted beside the box. Do not unseal the original; originals confuse the public. Heroes who fulfill the posted interpretation shall be paid. Heroes who fulfill some other reading shall be billed for originality. The box is not a witness. The poster is a courtesy.

### The posted interpretation (beside the box, large hand, wreath doodle)

> WHEN THE CLOCK SHOWS TWELVE, TAKE THE LEFT DOOR.  
> Meaning: at noon, take the LEFT door of this hall (the one with the harvest wreath). The other door is the Archivist's, and you are not to bother her.

### The twist (fair in hindsight)

Unseal Box 12 and the original slip, in a smaller, older hand, says exactly:

> When the clock shows twelve, the left door saves you.

That sentence is true. The poster is a costume sewn onto it.

- The Registry clock is a **tide-clock**. Its XII is high tide, mid-afternoon today, not noon. A plaque on the base, kid-height, says *XII = TIDE, NOT TOWN NOON*.
- "Left" on the slip is **left of the clock**, the door the hand points toward at XII: the Archivist's door. The wreath door is left *as you enter*, which is a different left.
- Noon + wreath-door dumps you into a closet of last year's chicken hats. Pride. Instant retry. The prophecy did not fail. The caption did its job.

### Where the honest clue hides

1. **"Certifies accuracy, not meaning"** is the Guildmaster telling on his own shop. Meaning is the uncertified half.
2. **"Originals confuse the public"** is a dare dressed as policy. Confuse means "they might read."
3. **"The poster is a courtesy"** — courtesy, in this guild, has never meant true.
4. **The tide-plaque** is on the clock the prophecy named. You do not need a sage. You need to look at the furniture.
5. The Archivist's door has a small sign: *LEFT OF XII*. It looks like a room number. It was always there.

Unsealing is curiosity, not vandalism. The clause forbids it the way "PERSONAL" forbade the real charter.

### Punishes / rewards

- **Punishes:** treating a certified-true sentence as a certified-true gloss; obeying the caption because it is larger.
- **Rewards:** unsealing; asking *"Twelve on which clock? Left from whose facing?"*; reading the plaque the prophecy pointed at.

### Failure (pride only)

> "The prophecy was right. You were loyal to the poster next to it. That is a different hobby. Collect a name that can tell a sentence from a caption."
>
> "Washed out for confusing 'true' with 'explained.' The oracle did its job. The caption did mine. The hatch is that way, captioned."

### Success payoff

At tide-XII they take the Archivist's door and find the real registry key, plus her note: *He certifies flour. He sells cake.* Stamp: **SENTENCE HELD, GLOSS DROPPED**.

**[SKILL: separate the true sentence from the posted gloss — accuracy is not interpretation]**

## 4. Three Seals, No Recitals

**Room:** IV — The Counting House of Minor Miracles  
**Theme:** compartmentalized heist; each player holds partial truth and must exchange WITHOUT revealing their whole hand  
**Arc beat:** **IV — Need-to-Know Is a Kindness.** Greed assumed they would dump-to-help. They open his till without him ever hearing the digits. The courtesy-bell log is Exhibit Four.

### The clause (verbatim)

> The Miracle Till opens when three apprentices each present one (1) true fragment of the combination, in any order. Fragments are issued under seal. Reciting another apprentice's fragment, reading a fragment aloud, writing a fragment on the table, or otherwise making a fragment common knowledge voids the opening and sounds the courtesy-bell. Sharing whether your fragment is odd or even, short or long, or "the kind that looks like a date" is permitted. Pointing at furniture is permitted. The Guild is not responsible for teammates who cannot shut up. Sign to accept your seal.

### The three seals (issued facedown; DM reads each privately)

| Seat | Fragment (payload — never spoken) | Legal metadata they may share |
|------|-----------------------------------|-------------------------------|
| Quill | "The first digit is the number of chairs in this room." | "Mine is something you can count in this room without opening a drawer." |
| Scout | "The second digit is one less than the first." | "Mine is a relation to someone else's, not a thing on a wall." |
| Coinbender | "The third digit is the Guild's public floor number, posted on the stairs we already climbed." | "Mine is already public. It is behind us, not in my envelope." |

The room has **four chairs**. The stairs they climbed are labeled **2**. Combination: **4–3–2**.

If anyone reads their scrap onto the table, the courtesy-bell rings, confetti of IOUs, chicken-hat, new name, same seals, retry.

### The twist (fair in hindsight)

This is not a vault puzzle that needs a genius. It is a **talking puzzle that needs a shut-up**. The winning move is in the same paragraph as the trap: you may share *kind*, not *content*. The Guildmaster's flaw is the last line. He cannot imagine teammates who point at chairs instead of performing their homework.

Presenting a fragment means each player, in any order, does a *silent* thing the till can verify — Quill taps four chairs, Scout holds up three fingers without saying "three," Coinbender flashes the stair-numeral they already walked under. The till clicks. Nobody's envelope has been made common knowledge.

### Where the honest clue hides

1. **The permitted list is the walkthrough.** Odd/even, short/long, "kind that looks like a date," pointing at furniture — that is the legal exchange protocol, printed because he thought you would skip it as jokes.
2. **"In any order"** means you do not need a leader to recite the whole combo. Reciting the whole combo is how you lose.
3. **"Common knowledge"** is defined by examples: aloud, on the table, recited. Metadata is carved out on purpose.
4. **"The Guild is not responsible for teammates who cannot shut up"** is him betting on helpfulness-as-broadcast. The villain's own mouth is the tell.

A careful table asks: *"What kind of thing is yours — not what is it?"*

### Punishes / rewards

- **Punishes:** dumping your whole hand to be helpful; treating "share" as "newsletter."
- **Rewards:** trading type-not-content; pointing; asking *"Is yours countable, relational, or already public?"*

### Failure (pride only)

> "You told the table everything you knew so nobody would feel left out. The bell felt included. You are now a cautionary pamphlet titled OVERSHARE. Pick a quieter name."
>
> "Washed out for confusing teamwork with a press conference. The Till heard you. The Till is a gossip. New face, same seals, try pointing at furniture instead of reading your mail."

### Success payoff

Till opens. Inside: the real tenth (8 gp, units written) and the **courtesy-bell log**, which shows he tested this trap on himself last week and rang it. Stamp: **PAYLOAD UNSPOKEN**.

**[SKILL: compartmentalized exchange — trade metadata, not payload; do not make your fragment common knowledge]**

## 5. Yesterday, Edited

**Room:** V — The Overthrow Floor  
**Theme:** worldline audit — diff two versions of yesterday's contract to find which clause changed  
**Arc beat:** **V — Yesterday Was Edited.** Graduation. The coup is a diff. They read both yesterdays into the minutes under HOLD; STOP with the minutes as written. Playing Guildmaster next chapter is the ceremony, not a chest.

### The clause (verbatim)

> Motion papers shall be the contract as it stood yesterday. Yesterday's contract is on the lectern. A courtesy copy is in the members' pigeonholes. They are the same document. Do not waste the floor's time comparing identical papers. Any discrepancy is a filing error and shall be resolved in favor of the lectern. Sign to confirm you have yesterday.

### The two yesterdays (same date, same title block, not the same worldline)

**Pigeonhole copy (Yesterday-A)** — Archivist's stamp in the footer, dry ink, slightly faded:

> Clause 4. The Guildmaster may interpret courtesy only while no HOLD is on the floor.  
> Clause 7. Dates are binding as written and as stamped.

**Lectern copy (Yesterday-B)** — no Archivist stamp, Clause 4 one line shorter, ink still faintly tacky if you thumb the margin:

> Clause 4. The Guildmaster may interpret courtesy.  
> Clause 7. Dates are binding as written.

The date line on both: **28th of Reaping, yesterday.** Same day. Different sentence. The worldline split is Clause 4's missing tail, and Clause 7 quietly dropping "and as stamped."

### The twist (fair in hindsight)

Sign "yesterday" off the lectern and you grant him interpreter-power with no HOLD-limit, and you waive the stamp that would have caught him. The motion to censure then requires his interpretation of courtesy. He will interpret it as a standing ovation.

The out-con is not a speech. It is a **diff**:

1. **HOLD** (pauses; does not die).
2. Line the two papers up, clause by clause. Read the missing tail of Clause 4. Thumb the tacky ink. Show the Archivist stamp that exists on A and not on B.
3. Exhibits One–Four go in with it: future-dated loft-band; Copy A vs Copy F; sealed prophecy vs caption; the bell-log from the till.
4. **STOP** — meeting ends *with the minutes as written*. His "they are the same document" is now a minute the floor can falsify. He looks like a forger if he VETOES a comparison he already called identical.

Storming the vote without the diff is the wash-out. Using his identical-papers clause against the one clause he moved is the graduation.

### Where the honest clue hides

1. **"They are the same document"** is a *claim*, not a hash. Claims want checking.
2. **"Do not waste the floor's time comparing identical papers"** is the dawdle-shoo. Identical is the word a rewriter needs you to skip.
3. **"Resolved in favor of the lectern"** admits a discrepancy procedure exists. You cannot resolve what you refuse to see.
4. **Pigeonhole vs lectern** are two locations named in the same sentence. Two locations is an invitation to walk.
5. Clause 7 on A still says **"as stamped."** That phrase is the Archivist pointing at her own footer from inside the contract. The lectern dropped it because the stamp is the thing it cannot fake yet. The ink on B is tacky because yesterday ended last night and this sentence did not.

A careful reader asks *"Who stamped which, and why is one still wet?"*

### Punishes / rewards

- **Punishes:** treating two papers with the same date as the same paper; obeying "don't compare"; letting the lectern win a fight it named.
- **Rewards:** clause-by-clause diff; thumbing ink; asking *"If they are identical, why are you scared of the comparison?"*

### Failure (pride only)

> "You signed 'yesterday' and got this morning's rewrite. Time is not a witness. It is a filing cabinet with a hobby. Collect a name that can tell Tuesday from Tuesday-prime."
>
> "Washed out for believing identical dates meant identical sentences. I moved one clause while you were being polite. Polite is how worldlines die. Hatch is still in this timeline. Use it."

### Success payoff

Minutes close with the diff. Clause 4's missing tail is on the record. HOLD / VETO / STOP tally as tokens; his interpretation-privilege is off because a HOLD is on and Yesterday-A says so. He is out-conned by his own "same document." The party chairs the next chapter.

**[SKILL: worldline audit — diff two same-dated instruments; the clause that moved is the lie; same day is not same text]**

## Guildmaster's Pro-Tips (B2 — Provenance Hygiene)

Spoken in the hall. Sound like insults. Each one is a source-drill he will not admit.

1. If it arrived from later this week, that is not a miracle. That is a postmark I bought wholesale.
2. Unsigned is not mysterious. Unsigned is me, in a hat, hoping you like mystery more than names.
3. The pigeon is never a witness. I clipped that line from a better manual. Check the band anyway.
4. A forward that survived six desks survived because each desk improved it. Improved is a verb that means ruined, with love.
5. Current copy means the copy still willing to live here. Honest copies get withdrawn for being difficult.
6. I certify accuracy the way a baker certifies flour. I do not certify the recipe you scrawled on the bag.
7. If I posted the meaning next to the prophecy, I am selling you the poster. The sentence in the box is unemployed.
8. Telling the table your whole secret is not teamwork. It is a newsletter. I subscribe.
9. Ask what kind of thing your friend holds. Do not ask them to read it. I am always listening. That is not a metaphor. That is acoustics.
10. Two papers dated yesterday are a dare. If I tell you not to compare them, compare them until your eyes hurt. Then compare the stamps.
