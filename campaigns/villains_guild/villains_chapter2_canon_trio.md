## 1. Joss “Later” Caliber

**Forward-Deployed Clerk of the Unsigned Stamp; Cowboy of Box Five-One-Hundred; Temporary-Permanent Witness of Next Thursday**

### Schtick

He does not predict. He *postmarks*. Every job sheet arrives already dated 14 March 2038, signed with a grid coordinate, and written like a warning from a nicer later. He is very friendly about how little you are allowed to know. When you ask for a source, he quotes a divergence number and taps the humming box on his hip. The box reprints old notices with new dates if you only read the postmark.

### Six voice lines

1. “I posted this yesterday from next Thursday. Don’t check the ink. The ink is shy. Check the date. The date is wearing a hat.”
2. “Coordinates aren’t a secret. They’re a map. Maps are for people who walk. Most of you will treat them like a riddle and then trip over the filing cabinet they name.”
3. “If I were making this up, would the box hum? That’s science. That’s also a humidifier. Science is a humidifier with confidence.”
4. “Need-to-know means I know. You need. Those are different verbs. Don’t conjugate them in public.”
5. “A civil war of headlines is still a war if you pick a side before you read both boards. Congratulations. You enlisted in a poster.”
6. “I never hide the punchline. I timestamp it after the joke lands so it looks like prophecy. You’re welcome. You’re also late, which is funny, because I’m ‘from later.’”

### Flaw-and-collapse

**Flaw:** vanity of the postmark. He cannot stand a document that admits when it was *written*. He will date anything after the event so the event looks obedient.

**Collapse (the clue was always readable):** every Caliber contract has three dates, all on the same sheet.

| Mark | Looks like | Is |
|---|---|---|
| **POSTMARK** (huge) | 14 March 2038 | the costume |
| **MILL MARK** (tiny, deckle edge) | `paper lot 2026 / still sticky` | the writing-date |
| **COORDINATE** (middle) | `STACK 5 / DRAWER 100 / COWBOY` | a map to the undated morning draft |

Believe the postmark: wash out. The hatch stamps you **ARRIVED BEFORE YOUR BRAIN**. Retry under a new name.

Demand the provenance date — *when was this written, not when does it claim to be from?* — and the mill mark plus sticky ink are the answer. Walk the coordinate as a map. STACK 5 / DRAWER 100 holds a draft dated this morning, titled *Copy For Later, Do Not Show Them The Morning*. His humming box then prints a notice he did not authorize: **CLERK FORGOT WHICH DIRECTION LATER IS.**

**Skill taught:** *demand provenance dates.* A date on a paper is a claim. Ask when the ink dried. Ask who stamped it. A future date is not a witness.

### Redemption beat

Mid-arc, a kids’-club notice from when he was twelve: *I, Joss, will be famous later.* He dated it *later* the same afternoon so the other kids would think he’d already won. The other kids dated theirs later too. Nobody won. If the party reads him his own mill mark out loud, he teaches the three-date test — not from kindness. From professional rage that his twelve-year-old self used his move first, sloppier.

### Three future-dated clauses

1. **As-Of.** *This instrument is executed as of 14 March 2038, which is the present for the Forward-Deployed Clerk. Signatories agree that “present” means the date printed largest.*  
   **Fair-in-hindsight twist:** largest is not oldest. The mill mark is smaller on purpose. Defining “present” as “printed largest” is the joke telling you size is the con. Challenge the as-of. The sticky ink is Exhibit Date.

2. **Clearance.** *The writing-date is classified Need-To-Know. Signatories who have not yet signed are not cleared to know when this was written. Signing confers clearance to the postmark only.*  
   **Fair-in-hindsight twist:** circular lock. Clearance that only reveals the costume is not clearance. The coordinate on the same page names the drawer where the writing-date sits in plain letters. Walking is clearance.

3. **Divergence.** *A worldline shift of 1.048596% voids any duty to update this notice. The Clerk’s later self has already approved this wording.*  
   **Fair-in-hindsight twist:** a number with extra decimals is not a later self. The humming box’s serial plate reads **1048576**. He added 20 “for cowboy luck” and dropped a decimal. Recompute: the void-clause is a serial number in a hat. It does not void anything.

## 2. Auntie Splits Needlow

**Licensed Fixer of Partial Truths; Chair of the Subcommittee on You Didn’t Need That Paragraph; Official Friend of I’ll-Handle-It; Notary of the Friendly Envelope**

### Schtick

She is the nicest person who will ever hide a page from you. Every contract comes in envelopes labeled FOR YOU, NOT FOR YOU, and FOR A FRIEND OF A FRIEND. She will hold your hand while she compartments the deal until nobody in the room — including her — holds a full sentence. She posts “helpful summaries” of old jobs on the Loud Board. The archive is quiet. She prefers you loud. When you ask why the quiet room exists, she dates the shrug 2039 and calls it a favor.

### Six voice lines

1. “I’ll handle it. That’s not a promise. That’s a filing system. You go in the folder marked Don’t Worry.”
2. “You don’t need the other page. That’s why it’s called the other page. Needing is so needy.”
3. “I told each of you the truth. I told none of you the same truth. That’s called being a people person.”
4. “The summary is the archive in a hurry. Archives wear shoes. We don’t wait for shoes. Shoes ask questions.”
5. “If it were a secret, would I be smiling? Checkmate. That’s friendship. Friendship is a stamp that says TRUST ME in lavender.”
6. “Coordinates? Those are for people who don’t have an Auntie. You have an Auntie. Put the map down before it learns your name.”

### Flaw-and-collapse

**Flaw:** she cannot bear a room where everyone knows the same thing. Shared knowledge feels like a leak. She will split a sentence until it cannot testify.

**Collapse (the clue was always readable):** the job is posted as three friendly envelopes. Each envelope is true. Together they are a different job.

- **Envelope A (FOR YOU):** *Retrieve the kettle.*
- **Envelope B (NOT FOR YOU):** she “accidentally” lets you peek if you ask. *The kettle is evidence. Do not return it to the person who hired you.*
- **Envelope C (FOR A FRIEND OF A FRIEND):** on the quiet archive wall, dated 2038, actually a copy of a 2024 notice. *Auntie Splits is not the client. The client is the Archivist. Summaries on the Loud Board are not the archive.*

Trust the Loud-Board summary, or only your envelope: wash out. Stamp: **NEED-TO-KNOW, KNEW-TOO-LITTLE.** Hat in lavender: **I HAD A GUY.**

Ask to see every envelope the first envelope names. Walk to the archive instead of quoting her repost. Pool the three pages. The kettle goes to the Archivist. Her own TRUST ME stamp then prints on her sleeve: **AUNTIE COMPARTMENTALIZED HERSELF OUT OF THE ALIBI.**

**Skill taught:** *read archives yourself instead of trusting reposts.* A summary is a person. The archive is a place. Go to the place. (Secondary tell: “a friend of a friend” is not a citation. Check sources.)

### Redemption beat

Mid-arc, the party finds her childhood group-project folder. Four kids. She split the assignment so well that nobody turned anything in, including her. She still has the teacher’s note: *Splits, a secret is not a plan.* If they read her all three of *her* envelopes (she kept them), she shows the archive key — because the teacher’s note is in the archive, and she is tired of being the only one who remembers it wrong.

### Three future-dated clauses

1. **Compartments.** *Each signatory receives only the annex cleared for their envelope color. Combining annexes without Auntie’s smile constitutes over-clearance and voids payment. This clause is dated 2 January 2039 so that combining them is “later’s problem.”*  
   **Fair-in-hindsight twist:** the clause that forbids combining is the sentence that admits the annexes *combine*. “Later’s problem” is a calendar costume over a present trick. Over-clearance is the winning move named as a sin.

2. **Repost.** *Notices on the Loud Board are the official memory. The downstairs archive is for people who do not trust family. Family is defined as Auntie. This notice copied forward to 11 October 2038 for convenience.*  
   **Fair-in-hindsight twist:** defining family as herself is the tell. The Loud Board’s footer, same ink: *Copied from downstairs. Original dated. Copy dated later.* The footer is the archive invitation wearing a brag.

3. **Need-To-Know.** *You agree you did not need to know the client’s name, the kettle’s job, or the writing-date, unless you ask for the page titled ASK. Asking is scheduled for 2038. Today is early.*  
   **Fair-in-hindsight twist:** there is a page titled ASK. It is in the archive under A. “Scheduled for 2038” is a lock painted on a drawer you can open. Curiosity is written in as a verb. Reckless skipping is not.

## 3. Professor Wick of the Other Percent

**Chancellor of 1.048596%; High Priest of Hits; Archivist of Successful Worldlines Only; Cowboy-Emeritus of the Highlight Reel**

### Schtick

He is a scientist the way a carnival scale is a judge. He claims a 1.048596% shift in the world, which is why *your* copy of the ledger looks different from his. He does not hide misses. He files them under OTHER WORLDLINE, which sounds like physics and is a crate. He is delighted to show you the Hits Board. He is less delighted if you count the crates. He will broker you a “need-to-know” glimpse of one hit. He will post, from later, that he already knew you’d ask.

### Six voice lines

1. “It’s 1.048596 percent. I counted. Please don’t count. Counting is how worldlines get shy, and I just got them to come out.”
2. “That prediction didn’t fail. It succeeded in a world we are too rude to be standing in. Sit down. Rude is a unit.”
3. “The Hits Board is the truth in a nice shirt. The crate is the truth in work clothes. We don’t do laundry during visiting hours.”
4. “Six decimal places is how you can tell I’m not guessing. Guessing has fewer jewelry.”
5. “You want the misses? That’s need-to-know. You need to know I said no. That’s knowing. You’re welcome.”
6. “I posted the correction from 2038. If the correction is late, that is because you are early. Please stop being early at my crate.”

### Flaw-and-collapse

**Flaw:** he cannot show a full set. A full set would include the jokes that didn’t land. He would rather be from another worldline than wrong in this one.

**Collapse (the clue was always readable):** the Hits Board shows three prophecies, all dated 2038, all “fulfilled.”

- “A kettle will move.”
- “Someone will sign something.”
- “The Guild will be talked about.”

The crate marked OTHER WORLDLINE (label facing the wall; the end-stamp still says CRATE, not PHYSICS) holds nine dated misses with mill marks from this year: *dragon over the clock tower by Tuesday; complimentary torch is actually complimentary; Auntie is the client; 2+2=5 in 1.048596% of rooms.*

The humming box serial is **1048576**. His favorite number is **1.048596**. Same digits, extra luck, decimal dropped in like glitter.

Trust the Hits Board: wash out. Recite the three hits until you hear they predicted weather in the shape of “something will happen.” Stamp: **SURVIVED THE REEL, FAILED THE COUNT.** Hat: **OTHER WORLDLINE (THIS ONE).**

Recompute the number against the serial plate. Count hits *and* crate. Read the misses with their mill marks. The vault of “shifted gold” opens to a snack drawer labeled **HIGHLIGHTS ARE NOT PROOF.** Wick’s chalk then writes, without him: **PROFESSOR FORGOT THE DENOMINATOR.**

**Skill taught:** *recompute claimed numbers.* Extra decimals are jewelry. Check the plate. Check the denominator.

**Taught in the same collapse, not as a sermon:** *spot survivorship bias.* A wall of hits is a wall. Ask where the misses live. If they live in a crate called physics, it is still a crate.

### Redemption beat

Mid-arc, a spelling-test from when he was ten. Twenty words. He displayed the four he got right on the fridge and dated the paper NEXT YEAR so it looked like prophecy of genius. His mother kept the other page. If the party puts both pages on the Hits Board together, he does not apologize. He writes the denominator on the crate in letters you can screenshot: **HITS OVER TRIES, NOT HITS OVER FEELINGS.** He leaves it there until the bell. That is a crack.

### Three future-dated clauses

1. **Constant.** *The worldline constant 1.048596% is binding on all ledgers, clocks, and feelings. Disputes must recompute the constant from first principles or accept the Hits Board. Constant adopted 7 July 2036 by the Chancellor’s later self.*  
   **Fair-in-hindsight twist:** “recompute from first principles” is the winning move, written down. First principles are on the box: serial 1048576. 1.048596 is 1048596 with a decimal and +20 luck. Feelings are not a unit. A later self who adopted the number is still a stamp.

2. **Other Worldline.** *Failed forecasts are true on a worldline 1.048596% away and therefore not discoverable in this room. Discovery constitutes trespass into later. Filed 19 December 2038.*  
   **Fair-in-hindsight twist:** the crate is in this room. The label OTHER WORLDLINE is a costume over a box. Trespass is the skill. “Later” is the lock he painted on a lid you can lift.

3. **As-Posted.** *This correction posted 8 August 2038. Any contradiction found before that date is early and void. Signatures today are fashionably early and still bind.*  
   **Fair-in-hindsight twist:** a correction dated after the argument is not a correction. It is a postmark. The mill mark on the same sheet is today’s. Early is the honest time. Void-for-being-early is the con confessing the clock.
