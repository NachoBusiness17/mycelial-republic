# THE RAMP: grow your own

This whole thing - the story, the hidden rules, the blind machine readers,
the grading, the public kill conditions - costs almost nothing and runs on
any computer. Here is the ladder from stranger to someone running their own.
Each rung needs only the rung below it.

## RUNG 1 - READER (10 minutes, free)

Read The Book. Answer the seven questions. Send them back. You are now data
in the experiment - the anonymous kind, which is the kind that proves
things.

## RUNG 2 - PLAYER (an afternoon, free)

You already own chatbots. Every big AI service has a free tier. Do this:

1. Pick three chatbots. Write your own ten rules - anything you believe
   about how a group should treat its records, its strangers, or its
   mistakes.
2. Write a story that teaches your ten rules WITHOUT stating them.
3. Write a second story of the same length with none of your rules in it.
   This is the placebo. Do not skip it.
4. Have each chatbot read your real story and ask: "what rules do these
   characters live by?" Write down what each one finds.
5. Grade against your key. Now the interesting part: give them the placebo
   and ask the same question. Anything they "find" in the placebo was never
   in your story - that is the sound of your own grading tool lying to you.
   Fix your questions until the placebo scores zero.

Congratulations: you are now measuring understanding instead of vibes.

## RUNG 3 - BUILDER (a weekend, free)

Run the full loop:

- **Bake**: encode what you believe into an artifact (a story, a manual, a
  constitution - anything with structure).
- **Control**: build the version without the structure.
- **Distribute**: same text, many independent readers - chatbots, friends,
  strangers. Never let them see each other's answers. Independence is the
  whole experiment.
- **Extract**: ask them to give the structure back.
- **Score**: against your key, never against the majority. If three readers
  agree on something you did not bake, you may have found something that
  exists outside any one of them. That is the real prize.
- **Falsify first**: write down, before you start, what result would prove
  you wrong. If you cannot name it, you are not running an experiment - you
  are decorating an opinion.

## RUNG 4 - OPERATOR (ongoing, nearly free)

Turn the loop into a place:

1. **One writer owns the record.** If several people (or machines) keep a
   shared log, only ONE of them may ever write to it. Everyone else writes
   messages to that one writer. This one rule prevents almost every fight.
2. **Deletion is theft.** The record only grows. Mistakes get corrected
   beside the mistake, dated, with a line from the wound to the cure.
3. **Presence is not authority.** Being somewhere proves being there. Claims
   need evidence; harm gets recorded as harm, not as a story about a culprit.
4. **Keep a kill list.** A public page of things that would prove your
   beliefs wrong, each with a date. Update it when the world answers.
5. **Pulse forever.** One reading, one exploration, every day, recorded. The
   machine that measures something every day for a year knows things a
   one-time experiment never can.

## THE HOUSE RULES

We will not print our answer key next to the story - that ruins the game for
every reader after you. Write your own ten first; then, if you want to
compare with ours, send us your readers' answers and we will send the
overlap map: which laws your house and ours share, and where the floors
disagree. The disagreement is the useful part - both houses learn the floor.

If you run the ramp and your readers find laws we do not have, tell us
immediately. That is not failure. That is the discovery the whole game
exists to catch.
