# For a 12-year-old: the real explanation

**The experiment.** We run a team of AI assistants. We wanted to know if they
actually understand things, or just sound like they do. So we wrote a story
(The Book) with ten real rules hidden inside it - rules about honesty,
record-keeping, and how groups should treat the people in them. The story
never states the rules. Then we had each AI read the story and ask itself:
what rules does this house live by?

**The answer key.** Because we wrote the rules, we can grade the AIs like a
teacher with an answer sheet. We also wrote a second story with no rules
hidden in it - a placebo. Any AI that "finds rules" in the placebo is
guessing, and we can tell. Our first grading tool actually failed this test -
it found rules in the placebo story too - so we fixed the tool. The control
story is what keeps us honest.

**What we measured.** Bigger AIs found more rules. Small AIs found fewer. One
AI made up a character that isn't in the story (a gardener who never
existed) and answered questions about it with total confidence. Another AI
refused: "that character isn't in the story." That difference - refusing a
false question instead of making up an answer - turned out to be the rarest
and most valuable thing to measure.

**The transfer test.** Then we gave the story, and nothing else, to a fresh
AI and asked it to solve a work problem. It solved the problem using the
hidden rules - rules it had only learned from the story. The rules survived
the trip from our answer key into a brand-new mind.

**Why you're invited.** Now we want human readers, because the AIs all grew
up on the same internet - they might share the same blind spots. Humans
haven't. If you read The Book and find the rules - or find rules we didn't
even hide - your answers get added to a public record, anonymously. And if
you want to go further: the whole method is written down so you can run the
same experiment yourself, on your own friends, your own AIs, your own school.
The last page explains how.

**One warning, so you know we mean it.** We publish a list of things that
would prove us wrong. If the readers can't find the rules, or find different
rules than we baked, we say so publicly and rewrite the story. Being wrong
out loud is part of the game.
