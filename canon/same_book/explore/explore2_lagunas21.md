**Prediction Preservation Principle**: A record-keeping system must treat its own historical predictions as immutable data points rather than editable forecasts, preserving them in their original form while allowing only metadata annotations for corrections or updates.

This rule emerges from the fundamental distinction between the act of prediction (which occurs at a specific time with limited information) and the act of retrospective analysis (which benefits from later knowledge). Treating historical predictions as immutable prevents the system from engaging in temporal cheating — where later information contaminates the assessment of earlier judgment quality. By preserving original predictions alongside annotation layers, the system maintains honest feedback loops essential for improving forecasting accuracy over time.
