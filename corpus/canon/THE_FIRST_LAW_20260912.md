# It Is Go — the First Law (operator-authored, 2026-09-12, verbatim, witnessed)

Classification: OPERATOR CANON — authored verbatim by the founder, filed unedited. This text governs the grammar; all seat prose about the grammar is commentary on it.

It is Go.

The field is the board. The stones are the stones. The crop is territory — the empty points the stones enclose, not the stones themselves. That is the first law, and it does not change when the board shrinks to a tsumego or swells to nineteen by nineteen. Same rule at every zoom. The water is the game moving.

The ledger is the kifu: one stone, one line, in the order it fell, ink that does not lift. A stranger can replay it and the position is the same. That is why the ledger is not a diary.

The witnesses are what turn the record into testimony:
- the clock that counts what was spent,
- the probes that return what the position actually did,
- the cold reader who has never seen the game,
- the one who does not place a stone and whose only power is to stop the count.

The six tests on the wall are how you catch a false claim about a position, a game, or a mind that only looks like it is thinking:
replay against the living hand;
give the board to someone who was not there;
change one stone and watch the hollows;
leave it untouched and probe it at dawn;
pit the next generation against the last with no help;
and if the crop was not fed by enough independent mouths, do not write down whose seed it was.

A traveler watching self-play, nested reading, stones landing and falling, a judge scoring, the ledger filling underneath, will say it looks like a mind. It is not. It is the same sentence that was written on paper long before anyone had silicon with which to say it faster: stones, ground, and the tide they make between them.

She owns the field, the stones, the bench, and the water. The rules did not ask permission to be the rules.

The rest is detail. That is what the key is for.
