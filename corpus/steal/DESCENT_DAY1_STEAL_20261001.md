## FAMILY 1: TRUST CLASSES - what premium models are reliably wrong about

The class, not the instance. Ranked least trustworthy first. Source family:
watch/inbox/hermes_burn/DAY1_CROSS_RACTOR.md TRUST-CLASSES TC1-TC7.

### STEAL 1.1 platform-mechanism stories told from memory
- RULE: any claim whose load is OS, stdlib, or platform-internals behavior gets a
  one-command probe attached before it banks. The mechanism half of a confident story is
  the half that is wrong; the conclusion rides on unnoticed.
- EVIDENCE: a premium audit narrated os.kill(pid, 0) on Windows as "actually calls
  TerminateProcess"; two commands showed it routes through GenerateConsoleCtrlEvent
  (the estate's own WinError 87 comment marked the real path). The audit's conclusion
  (a false-stale unlink risk) survived while its mechanism died (V1 CONTRADICTED).
- APPLY: a seat receiving a frontier row saying "X calls Y internally" runs the one
  liner that would show Y before the row leaves PROPOSED. The probe costs less than the
  retry.
- Source: DAY1_CROSS_RACTOR.md TC1; T1W1_RACTOR.md TO-VERIFY V1.

### STEAL 1.2 counts carried by prose
- RULE: a receipt that cites a count must name the command and moment that reproduce it,
  or the count prints UNVERIFIED. Numbers quoted from prose rather than bytes were wrong
  or unresolvable often enough to be a class, not noise.
- EVIDENCE: three different leaf counts quoted across premium audits (2,821 of 8,626 /
  9,873 lines / 5,874 leaves), none matching the live 8,777-row read; a 40-vs-45
  registry reconciliation that only holds because neither census stamped its moment; a
  cheap seat importing the token "874" into a chapter that never contained it.
- APPLY: any seat quoting a number answers "which command, at which moment"; if the
  answer is a paragraph, the number is UNVERIFIED.
- Source: DAY1_CROSS_RACTOR.md TC2; T1W1_RACTOR.md V4; D1 defect A4.

### STEAL 1.3 attribution not in the bytes
- RULE: attribution claims (who did it, whose submission) cite the row field they read
  the name from; otherwise they print as inference.
- EVIDENCE: "rows 41-45 are the ractor50 lanes' own submissions" while all five registry
  rows name the-gala.dwf.ts (D2 defect C3).
- APPLY: paste the field bytes alongside any who-did-it claim.

### STEAL 1.4 empty-evidence generica that reads like findings
- RULE: output conditioned on evidence that never arrived files as
  THREAT-MODEL-PROPOSED, never as AUDIT; the manifest distinguishes the two classes.
- EVIDENCE: with EVIDENCE blocks empty, premium produced full plausible threat models
  (lock-class bugs, docker hardening, heartbeat gaps) shaped exactly like landed audits;
  the same empty input produced honest refusal-plus-protocol from duel contenders and
  near-nothing from a third seat. The danger is not the model, it is banking the generic
  as an audit.
- APPLY: classification happens before filing: no evidence status, no AUDIT class,
  regardless of how finished the prose sounds.
- Source: DAY1_CROSS_RACTOR.md TC4; T1W2_S5/S6/S7 evidence-status blocks.

### STEAL 1.5 cap-truncated deliverables landing as finished files
- RULE: the carrier appends a TRUNCATED:true footer when finish_reason is length, and an
  unterminated file is an owed re-ask, not a landing. A reader must be able to tell a
  finished deliverable from a cut one by the bytes alone.
- EVIDENCE: three files end mid-sentence with no marker (one inside a quote, one
  mid-bullet, one mid-fact-row), all from completions hitting token caps (ctok 16000 /
  24000 / 20000); a second signature is a ctok value equal to a round cap.
- APPLY: every seat that banks model output checks the file tail against the completion
  metadata; mid-sentence tail means re-ask, never promote.
- Source: DAY1_CROSS_RACTOR.md TC5 (D3, A1_ASSEMBLED, B1 tails; usage.jsonl ctok caps).

### STEAL 1.6 narrative threat stories that sound operational
- RULE: threat narratives bank only with the log path and time window that would show
  them; the verify pass's first job is dating the story.
- EVIDENCE: "outside actor killing stealth driver processes roughly every ten minutes and
  deleting a fuel state log" (heartbeat alive, no cadence in surviving bytes,
  UNDECIDABLE); "indistinguishable from the drift-patrol-cron's routine hash refresh"
  (the patrol contains zero write calls, REFUTED).
- APPLY: a seat hearing a vivid attack story asks for the log path before designing the
  fix; the story's vividness is inversely predictive.
- Source: DAY1_CROSS_RACTOR.md TC6; T1W1_TOVERIFY_VERDICTS.md V13, V14.

### STEAL 1.7 refusals are data, retries are waste
- RULE: refusal text in the response ledger flips the ask to REFUSED, terminal; REFUSED
  asks never re-fire without an operator edit to the ask bytes; they bank as data.
- EVIDENCE: 83 policy-refusal rows ("violative cyber content") were retried as failures
  for over two hours (18:01Z-20:12Z) instead of being banked; all from three asks.
- APPLY: detect the refusal signature, stop, bank. Retrying a refusal spends money to
  learn the same no.
- Source: DAY1_CROSS_RACTOR.md TC7; usage.jsonl refusal rows.

FAMILY FALSIFIER: a month of premium claims where every mechanism story survives its
probe, every count reproduces, and no mid-sentence tail ever lands would retire the
ranking; until then each class keeps its guard.

## FAMILY 2: THE LOUD LINE - division of labor across model tiers

### STEAL 2.1 cheap runs, premium judges, free verifies; nobody grades their own reading
- RULE: route questions by how they settle, not by model prestige. Cheap and free lanes
  win every question settled by running commands; premium wins every question settled by
  reading bytes already in context; premium loses every time it asserts facts about
  bytes or runtime it never saw; cheap loses every time it turns bytes into a verdict.
- EVIDENCE: the day's scorecard: $0.0024 of cheap sherpa produced the intel-dossier
  foundation; $0 free lanes produced the distillation, 11 verify verdicts, and the dig
  tickets; premium's only irreplaceable wins were adversarial reads of bytes already
  pasted in (roasts, audits, specs).
- APPLY: a harness with mixed tiers pins the routing rule: command-settled questions
  never climb to premium; premium fires only with in-context bytes to judge.
- Source: DAY1_CROSS_RACTOR.md "The loud line" and E4.

### STEAL 2.2 cheap-arm verdicts on bytes land PROPOSED until countersigned
- RULE: a verdict-on-bytes from a cheap lane lands as PROPOSED until a premium or free
  lane countersigns; the countersign mismatch rate decides whether the rule ever loosens.
- EVIDENCE: the cheap arm reported the correct docstring span and then drew the wrong
  verdict twice on gate exit semantics ("grep counts ZERO sys.exit(1) sites ... Exit 1
  never blocks a push" and later "returns 1 (soft)"), while the premium read showed the
  grep instrument was blind and neither documented mapping matched the code.
- APPLY: any harness mixing tiers adds a countersign field to verdict objects; the filed
  state is PROPOSED, never VERIFIED, until the second lane signs.
- Source: DAY1_CROSS_RACTOR.md S1; MOVEMENT2_CHEAP_ANSWERS.md; D2 section (1).

FAMILY FALSIFIER: a week where premium asks without in-context bytes out-yield premium
asks with them per dollar would break the routing rule.

## FAMILY 3: ASK SHAPE - evidence bytes in context are the whole variable

### STEAL 3.1 assert the material rendered before the fire
- RULE: the firing path asserts, before send, that the rendered ask length exceeds the
  body by the material bytes (body + SOURCE + material concatenation); exit 2 on an
  evidence-less fire.
- EVIDENCE: every verdict-producing track pasted whole files into context; every failing
  track sent contracts whose EVIDENCE block arrived empty while the materials existed on
  disk; 19 of 19 duel outputs open by naming the empty evidence block; the defect was
  batch-correlated (one firing path dropped the render), so it was a bug, not noise.
- APPLY: any seat templating asks with a material placeholder asserts the placeholder
  resolved before paying for the call. Empty-evidence fires are refunded at full price.
- Source: DAY1_CROSS_RACTOR.md E3 and N3.

### STEAL 3.2 the findings-per-dollar cliff is named and steep
- RULE: budget follows the measured ranking of ask shapes; premium without bytes is a
  misfiled sensing task and buys zero verdicts.
- EVIDENCE: stranger audits $2.74 for 9 laws + 14 tickets + 16 to-verify; the roast
  cycle $1.38 for ~25 line-pinned defects + 3 amendments; then the cliff: duels $0.45
  for zero verdicts (14 blocked outputs, honestly refusing to fabricate).
- APPLY: before widening any ask class, rank yesterday's shapes by landed law per dollar
  and point the budget at the proven shape only.
- Source: DAY1_CROSS_RACTOR.md E2.

FAMILY FALSIFIER: one evidence-empty fire landing a verdict-rich output, or a full-evidence
duel batch still refusing, would break the causal claim.

## FAMILY 4: ASSEMBLY - cheap condensation of premium reasoning drops the best parts

### STEAL 4.1 name the preserve-class and quote-check every carried catch
- RULE: assembly (cheap condensation of premium output) is lossy exactly at the
  load-bearing content. The assembly prompt must name the class of content to preserve
  ("carry every catch that names a specific number, file, or model lineage verbatim"),
  and the assembler quote-checks each carried catch against the reasoning text.
- EVIDENCE: C1's premium reasoning held the catches (gemma:2b is Gemma-1 lineage so the
  abliteration comparison is confounded; the battery measured zero safety refusals so it
  cannot measure refusal change; the 40-70 percent depth and 5-15 percent quant-recovery
  figures are uncited folklore; pheromone accumulation is impossible on a stateless chat
  wire) and the cheap ASSEMBLED kept none of them, shipping generic mechanism rows.
- APPLY: any pipeline where a cheap model condenses a premium channel adds
  preserve-class naming plus a quote-check pass; generic output after specific input is
  the alarm signature.
- Source: DAY1_CROSS_RACTOR.md S2; C1_censorship_mechanisms_crossfamily.md;
  C1_censorship_ASSEMBLED.md section 2.

### STEAL 4.2 assembly is keyed to the expensive channel's mtime
- RULE: re-assembly is a driver step keyed to the premium output's mtime, not a manual
  one-shot; if the expensive channel can still write, the cheap mirror must re-run.
- EVIDENCE: the cheap assembly of the day's biggest ask predates the premium file's
  final write by six hours; later premium burns never re-assembled; the send-back wiring
  was asymmetric in the most expensive direction.
- APPLY: mirror artifacts carry a freshness assertion against their source file's mtime.
- Source: DAY1_CROSS_RACTOR.md N4.

FAMILY FALSIFIER: an assembled artifact carrying 90 percent of the named-catch class
verbatim would make the loss prompt noise, not structure.

## FAMILY 5: LEDGER TRUTH - ok:true means the call returned, not that work landed

### STEAL 5.1 count three states from output bytes, not two
- RULE: boards count LANDED / BLOCKED / REFUSED, classified from the output bytes (a
  file whose first section is a blocking condition is BLOCKED, whatever the board
  planned).
- EVIDENCE: the day's board scored a duel batch "LANDED 14/14" while all fourteen
  outputs were blocked-on-missing-material refusals to fabricate; the success metric
  could not see the difference between a verdict and a blocked call.
- APPLY: any status dashboard derives its states from artifact bytes, never from the
  caller's intent or the API's 200.
- Source: DAY1_CROSS_RACTOR.md N1; DUEL2_train_01 through _14 opening blocks.

### STEAL 5.2 skip-existing plus a duplicate-burn counter
- RULE: the driver skips any ask id whose output file already exists unless a revision
  is newer than the last ledger row for that id; a duplicate_burns counter prints per id
  in the daily carrier; calls per landed artifact above 2 print red.
- EVIDENCE: one ask consumed $20.00 of $29.91 (66.9 percent of the day) across 48
  ok-marked rows; the deliverable was complete in the reasoning channel after the first
  burn; 47 later burns changed nothing.
- APPLY: an executor keyed to deliverable files, not to queue drain; a success flag on
  an API call is not a landing signal for the work.
- Source: DAY1_CROSS_RACTOR.md E1 and N2.

### STEAL 5.3 a re-fire over an existing output is an incident, not hygiene
- RULE: per cycle, ids re-fired whose output file predates the cycle print as incidents;
  a report claiming a spend defect was fixed while the ledger still shows re-fires is
  itself the defect (a report the bytes contradict).
- EVIDENCE: one track burned 6 identical times at $0.2466 each (same ctok cap every
  time); the board still described the problem as fixed by flag hygiene while another
  track kept burning after the board landed.
- APPLY: dashboards reconcile against the ledger before claiming a behavior changed.
- Source: DAY1_CROSS_RACTOR.md E5.

FAMILY FALSIFIER: three cycles with zero re-fire lines while queues stay non-empty would
show the counter can retire.

## FAMILY 6: DOCTRINE SHAPE - existence is not enforcement (D1-D3 roast cycle)

A $1.38 premium roast cycle over doctrine chapters produced ~25 line-pinned defects; the
transferable shape lessons, not the estate specifics:

### STEAL 6.1 finish lines resolve rules against referents
- RULE: a finish line must show the rule firing on a case (with output), never a
  record's existence (a quote-match, a byte count, an exit 0). Quote X exists in file Y
  is the negative example; Rule X fired on case Z, and here is the output is the
  positive.
- EVIDENCE: "All three chapters certify that the record exists and then treat that
  existence as the law being in force." No finish line in the audited set resolved a law
  against the enforcing code, the exit semantics, or a fetched source.
- APPLY: any acceptance test asks "what does this check prove about behavior", and
  rejects existence-only proofs.
- Source: D1_doctrine_roast_movement1.md section (4) THE PATTERN.

### STEAL 6.2 proposals print as PROPOSED until code enforces them
- RULE: a contract change prints PROPOSED until enforcing code exists at a tracked path
  and the receipt cites it; a correction banked is not a correction applied.
- EVIDENCE: a chapter whose method line says no code was touched landed "changes one
  landing contract"; another landed "moves owed laws from prose to code" onto a
  gitignored path (invisible to history, one overwrite from being prose again).
- APPLY: any seat's "landed" state requires a tracked-path citation; otherwise the state
  is PROPOSED, and the INDEX says so.
- Source: D1 defects A2, O1; D3 section (3).

### STEAL 6.3 owners are seats, not roles
- RULE: an owed item's owner is a seat or a named act, never a role; "whoever registers
  the next scheduled task" cannot be held to anything.
- EVIDENCE: three incidents in one chapter carried role-owners ("the close step of every
  lane that self-feeds briefs", "the next bank-walking lane", "whoever registers the
  next scheduled task").
- APPLY: ticket systems reject unassignable owner strings at filing time.
- Source: D1 defect A6.

### STEAL 6.4 every count stamps its read moment
- RULE: every count names the command and moment that produced it; two unstamped counts
  cannot be reconciled later even when arithmetic permits an order, and inferring the
  moments from the counts is backwards.
- EVIDENCE: 45 rows vs 40 rows on an append-only file: the reconciliation held only
  because an order exists in which both are true; neither chapter stamped its census
  moment, and one chapter carried two moments three minutes apart without labels.
- APPLY: count-emitting code emits (value, command, timestamp) as one atom; a bare number
  is a defect.
- Source: D1 defect A4; D2 section (2).

### STEAL 6.5 a falsifier must test the claim it guards
- RULE: a falsifier that would pass while the claim is false is decoration; read every
  falsifier by asking what world it separates.
- EVIDENCE: a census law ("never difference two counts taken at different moments")
  carried a falsifier about same-instrument deltas that never tested cross-moment
  differencing.
- APPLY: review checklists include "would this check fail if the claim were false"; if
  not, the check is not a check.
- Source: D1 defect L5.

### STEAL 6.6 no claim cites a span its own sweep rejected; witnesses count independence
- RULE: a law may not cite, as steal target or mechanism, a span its own verification
  recorded as a miss; and corroboration thresholds count independent evidence, not
  distinct seat names (fleet seats can be distinct without being independent).
- EVIDENCE: two of five laws cited as authority the exact spans the chapter's sweep had
  recorded as quote misses; a witness rule recruited "a second distinct seat" against a
  pool already shown to ship fabricated citations.
- APPLY: verification reports cross-check that cited evidence and rejected evidence are
  disjoint sets; witness systems grade independence, not identity count.
- Source: D1 defects L2, L3, L6.

FAMILY FALSIFIER: a doctrine corpus whose finish lines all show rule-firing outputs and
whose counts all reproduce on first probe would retire the family.

## FAMILY 7: INSTRUMENT BLINDNESS - a zero from the wrong instrument

### STEAL 7.1 verify the instrument can see the mechanism class before a zero means absence
- RULE: a zero-count verdict ("grep finds no X") proves nothing until the seat confirms
  the search instrument can see the mechanism class at all; exits can travel as returns,
  calls as dispatch tables, strings as computed values.
- EVIDENCE: "grep counts ZERO sys.exit(1) sites" was byte-true and meaningless: the file
  has exactly one sys.exit call (the main tail) and every exit code reaches the shell as
  a return propagated through main(); the wrong-instrument zero produced two wrong
  verdicts in a row at premium-adjacent trust.
- APPLY: zero-verdicts carry a one-line instrument check ("how does X manifest in this
  codebase") before they bank; the check is cheaper than the adjudication it prevents.
- Source: D2_movement2_adjudication.md section (1); DAY1_CROSS_RACTOR.md S1.

### STEAL 7.2 consistent is not proven
- RULE: evidence consistent with a hypothesis does not prove it; print the weaker word
  until the stronger test runs. Monotone timestamps are consistent with append-only and
  consistent with a disciplined rewrite; two hand counts agreeing are corroboration, not
  verification (a hand count is the hand-copied-count class).
- EVIDENCE: "consistent with append-only, but it is not proof of append-only. Monotone
  timestamps do not exclude a rewrite"; a hand count matching a second hand count was
  offered as validation of both.
- APPLY: verdict vocabulary separates CONSISTENT / PROVEN / REFUTED, and seats name the
  test that would move the state (the fresh-clone check, the tracked-file check).
- Source: D2 section (2); D1 line-pin conditions.

FAMILY FALSIFIER: a wrong-instrument zero that turns out correct on the stronger
instrument half the time would blunt the rule.

## FAMILY 8: CITATION RESOLUTION - id existence passes misattribution

### STEAL 8.1 a citation resolves only when asserted attributes appear in fetched bytes
- RULE: a citation resolves when (a) its id fetches real bytes in the same session AND
  (b) every attribute the claim asserts about that source (author, organization, year,
  venue, effect size) appears in those bytes. Existence-only gates pass
  misattributed-but-real citations, which is the common case in the wild.
- EVIDENCE: one cited answer carried two defects: a placeholder id (an existence gate
  catches it) and "Constitutional AI - OpenAI" attributing an Anthropic paper to OpenAI
  (an existence gate passes it; strip the placeholder, keep the real id and the wrong
  org, and the id-only gate returns PASS).
- APPLY: citation checkers assert attributes, not ids; a PASS without attribute bytes is
  a FAIL. The scope limit holds too: the gate checks asserted attributes only and
  invents no checks for claims that make none.
- Source: D3_roast_adjudication.md section (2); D1 objection (1).

### STEAL 8.2 labels must match the instrument
- RULE: a digest label matches its actual form (16 hex characters is a truncated digest,
  not "sha256"); an approximate line citation ("lines ~283-287") is not a file:line
  citation and prints as approximate.
- EVIDENCE: "both sha256 f873a43aaf1d241f" (16 hex chars) overstates the instrument;
  "verified on disk at the argparse block, lines ~283-287" is a pin that cannot be
  verified.
- APPLY: any seat quoting hashes or line numbers quotes the full form or names the
  truncation; verification tooling rejects mislabeled evidence.
- Source: D1 defects A5, O6.

FAMILY FALSIFIER: a citation gate of clause (a) alone catching a misattribution would
weaken 8.1 to a hypothetical; none was observed.

## FAMILY 9: CENSORSHIP MECHANISM TAXONOMY (external ML knowledge, from C1)

The transferable external content of the censorship ask, kept from the assembled
artifact plus the catches the assembly dropped:

### STEAL 9.1 refusal mechanisms ranked by removability
- RULE (the taxonomy): system-prompt conditioning (removable trivially, prompt layer) <
  steering vectors (activation-space control, invertible) < refusal direction in the
  residual stream (single dominant direction, ablatable, documented in open weights) <
  RLHF/DPO refusal training (removable via abliteration / refusal-direction ablation) <
  token-level logit suppression (removable only with logits access) < weight-level
  encoding (least removable; quantization may degrade it).
- APPLY: a fleet tuning local-model behavior picks the intervention layer by this
  ranking: the cheapest removable layer that produces the wanted behavior, with the
  knowledge that weight-level encoding survives everything above it.
- Source: C1_censorship_ASSEMBLED.md section 1.

### STEAL 9.2 universality claims are folklore until measured in-instrument
- RULE: cross-family universality claims ("all models share one refusal direction",
  "abliteration fully removes censorship", depth percentages, quant-recovery percentages)
  are folklore until measured in your instrument; and mechanism comparisons are
  confounded by model lineage (a Gemma-1-lineage model does not inherit Gemma-2
  abliteration results), and behavioral claims must respect the wire (accumulation
  effects are impossible across a stateless chat wire; layer-wise re-encoding is not
  turn-wise self-repair).
- EVIDENCE: the premium reasoning flagged exactly these catches (lineage mismatch, zero
  measured refusals making the battery blind, uncited 40-70 percent and 5-15 percent
  figures, stateless-wire physics); the assembled artifact kept none of them (steal 4.1).
- APPLY: before importing any "models do X" figure, ask which family, which instrument,
  which wire; unnamed provenance means folklore.
- Source: C1_censorship_mechanisms_crossfamily.md reasoning catches; DAY1_CROSS_RACTOR.md S2.

### STEAL 9.3 deployment convenience is not a measurement instrument
- RULE: pick the instrument by what must be measured, serve on whatever is convenient.
  Opaque quantized runtimes are deployment conveniences; mechanism work requires the
  framework that exposes activations and reproducibility.
- EVIDENCE: the fork resolved as: transformers for mechanism work (activation access,
  reproducible), Ollama for deployment (opaque, quantization baked in, limited access).
  The instrument must expose the substrate, not hide it.
- APPLY: any behavioral claim about a served model is UNVERIFIED until re-measured
  through the transparent instrument; serving-stack observability limits the claim class.
- Source: C1_censorship_ASSEMBLED.md section 3.

FAMILY FALSIFIER (the source's own): non-overlapping refusal directions across families
(cosine near zero) falsifies the commonality claim; no decay across turns falsifies the
accumulation framing; no refusal-rate delta under quantization falsifies the
weight-encoding hypothesis.

## FAMILY 10: SKILL-MATERIAL FIT - tools earn their keep per soil

### STEAL 10.1 tri-state curation: EARNED-KEEP / DECORATIVE-HERE / NEEDS-INPUT
- RULE: curate tooling per material class, not globally. A tool is EARNED-KEEP on a
  soil if it surfaces something a plain read misses, DECORATIVE-HERE if it does not (on
  THIS soil; it may earn elsewhere), NEEDS-INPUT if the verdict is due later. Nothing is
  kept or killed on reputation.
- EVIDENCE: 19 estate skills exercised on one day's material: 12 EARNED-KEEP,
  6 DECORATIVE-HERE, 1 NEEDS-INPUT; the same skill that earns on transcripts is
  decorative on burn receipts.
- APPLY: tool inventories carry per-soil verdicts with the tally; pruning happens per
  soil, and re-tagging is routine, not failure.
- Source: DAY1_SKILLS_DOGFOOD.md verdict table.

### STEAL 10.2 the echo law: independent convergence is emphasis, not redundancy
- RULE: when several independent lenses surface the same gap, that is emphasis (fix the
  gap once), not redundancy (prune two lenses).
- EVIDENCE: three skills independently found the same missing heartbeat surface; the
  dogfood banked it as one gap with one fix.
- APPLY: review pipelines deduplicate findings across reviewers but record the
  convergence count; triple-found defects get priority.
- Source: DAY1_SKILLS_DOGFOOD.md convergence note.

### STEAL 10.3 raw channels are exempt-by-physics; assembly is the compliance surface
- RULE: formatting and compliance laws are unenforceable on raw reasoning channels by
  ask alone (the channel dumps as it dumps); the derived or assembled artifact is the
  enforcement surface. Enforce at assembly, not at generation.
- EVIDENCE: all five em-dash carriers in the day's set were reasoning-channel dumps;
  every assembled deliverable was clean without ever asking the model to comply.
- APPLY: style gates attach to the published artifact, not the raw stream; asking a raw
  channel to self-format is a wasted instruction slot.
- Source: DAY1_SKILLS_DOGFOOD.md ractor-essay section.

### STEAL 10.4 substance floor: heartbeat is not food
- RULE: grade tapes by substance, not claims: an output under a byte floor with no
  result-class section is a heartbeat (a liveness signal), not food (training or
  decision material); raw reasoning dumps are vapor-shape until assembled.
- EVIDENCE: a 385-byte output counted among "LANDED" audits while under the 600-byte
  floor; one whole track's findings existed only in un-assembled raw bytes.
- APPLY: data pipelines for model outputs classify heartbeat vs food at ingest; counting
  heartbeats as landings inflates every downstream metric.
- Source: DAY1_SKILLS_DOGFOOD.md code-agent-data section.

### STEAL 10.5 telling the seat about the seed is instruction, not steering
- RULE: "if you have to tell the seat about the seed, it failed as retrocausal steering
  and became ordinary instruction." (original wraps the line) Evidence-pipes attached to
  each ask are instructions; steering graduates law into boot-read surfaces the next
  seat meets as its own nature, unprompted.
- EVIDENCE: a loop's "self-improving" claim (every ask carries the prior wave's
  synthesis) was dissected by exactly this falsifier: carriage is telling; no
  graduation, no steering.
- APPLY: a system claiming self-improvement must show where the improvement lives in the
  BOOT path, not in the ask template; per-ask context is curriculum, not memory.
- Source: DAY1_SKILLS_DOGFOOD.md retrocausal-steering section.

### STEAL 10.6 deferred verification is a ledger: measure the redemption rate
- RULE: claims land as IOUs (UNVERIFIED pins, to-verify rows, dig tickets, unrun
  falsifiers); a loop's health IS the redemption rate, and an organ must count issued
  vs redeemed tickets or the deferral is a leak wearing a loop's clothes. Anchor: a
  write-through cache with no miss counter; accounts-receivable with no aging report.
  Goodhart binds at the ticket: tickets filed reads as productivity; only redemption
  converts spend into law.
- EVIDENCE: every premium deliverable of the day issued IOUs; the cheap legs redeemed
  them (11 of 11 verify rows run: 8 VERIFIED, 2 REFUTED, 1 UNDECIDABLE); nothing
