# THE LEDGER OF EMPTY CHAIRS — World Brief (the spawn world)

> The world we spawn our players into, to do the work we want.
> Design law: the story reveals itself the more you play and sit with it.
> Never stated. Hook first. The systems are just *there*, part of the world,
> hinted at subtly — never explained. Like original WoW seasons: we don't hit
> anyone with any of it at first. They get hooked. Then the world does its work.

## What the world IS (for the build)
- A MUD-to-WoW-clone setting: the Endless March, the Guild of the Open Hand,
  the Code of Inclusive Valor, quest boards, reputation ledgers, trauma-informed
  party guidelines. Curated dungeons, progressive murals, safe roads. Most
  players never notice anything is wrong — and that's a valid playthrough.
- Under the surface: the long harvest. The Serpent (ancient, skin-shedding)
  learned open evil provokes rebellion, so it inverted the mystery schools into a
  controlled harvest of fear-essence, bound by SHADOW LEDGERS — private books of
  leverage. No dragons on thrones. Just Helpful Strangers with warm smiles and
  grants, who "handle the counting."
- The Guild of the Open Hand is the Serpent's self-policing masterpiece:
  inclusivity as the cage, Virtue Marks as quiet deposits into your own shadow
  entry, engineered division, false shepherds in rebel garb.
- The Weaponized Narrative Veil: algorithmic bards, the Rashomon filter inverted
  into manufactured moral plays. Five tells: tears, archetypes, cloned phrases,
  impossible speed, moral punishment of doubt.
- The BBEG is not a demon. The BBEG is the perfected system.

## The core mechanic / thesis (one sentence)
**The system does not require belief in the lie — only that you become too tired
to keep pointing at the empty chairs.**

## What we need grok to do (the expand)
Turn this seed into the living, buildable world for the game_flow / game_mud
engine. Expand it as a Persona-game world that *reacts to the player*: the world
observes what the player notices and responds to, and that becomes part of what
they uncover. Factions, progression hooks, environmental storytelling, the
late-game revelation that "the good system was the cage all along." The story
is there, but what the player sees is up to them. It changes you the more you
play it — because it teaches you about yourself.

## Build seed (the one-room slice we can prove first)
- The supper hall with the empty chairs.
- A Helpful Stranger who keeps offering a grant.
- A ledger fragment you can find.
- The Rashomon filter as the detect-manufactured-narrative mechanic.

## Standing laws (do not violate)
- Never state the truth. Hint. Let the player connect it themselves.
- Hook first. The systems are part of the world, not announced.
- It takes no side. The world just is. What the player sees is theirs.
- The game is a tool for the vigilant, not a substitute sovereign.

**THE HALL OF SHARED SUPPER** (the one-room slice, buildable)
- Six chairs kept empty at supper.
- Brother Vellum, of the Open Hand, smiling a "Grant of Belonging."
- Count the chairs -> Virtue Marks appear. Ignore them -> the mural weeps cloned tears.
- A ledger scrap names the missing (a "salt-cellar scrap, three names, same hand as the roll").
- The Rashomon bards punish the count (they're listening).

**sd's synthesis (the feel, keep it):**
> "The stew is too kind. Vellum's grant waits unsigned, warm as the bread. Count the empty:
> Marks appear. Eat past them: identical tears track the mural. Salt-cellar scrap, three names,
> same hand as the roll. Hear a phrase twice and the bard's smile thins. Speak the count: they
> come. The hall files which you chose."

That last line is the core mechanic, in-world: **the hall files which you chose.** The world
observes the player's noticing (or refusal to notice), and that becomes the record. This is the
Persona-game reactivity — the world reacts to what the player does with the empty chairs.
