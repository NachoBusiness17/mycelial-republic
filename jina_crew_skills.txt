## Crew skills

Keep Watching Next video in 8 seconds

*   [1 Piloting skill](https://ftl.fandom.com/wiki/Crew_skills#Piloting_skill)
*   [2 Engines skill](https://ftl.fandom.com/wiki/Crew_skills#Engines_skill)
*   [3 Weapons skill](https://ftl.fandom.com/wiki/Crew_skills#Weapons_skill)
*   [4 Shields skill](https://ftl.fandom.com/wiki/Crew_skills#Shields_skill)
*   [5 Repair skill](https://ftl.fandom.com/wiki/Crew_skills#Repair_skill)
*   [6 Combat skill](https://ftl.fandom.com/wiki/Crew_skills#Combat_skill)
*   [7 Skills table](https://ftl.fandom.com/wiki/Crew_skills#Skills_table)

Crewmembers achieve higher skill levels by accumulating enough skill points (experience) by performing various tasks: Piloting the ship, Shield recharging, running the Engines, Weapons operation, Repairing damage, and engaging in Combat. All crew races require the same amount of experience to achieve the next skill level, except for humans who have slightly reduced skill points requirements. The amount of experience required to achieve skill level 1 and level 2 is exactly the same for a specific skill (untrained crew is considered skill level 0), but various skills have different experience thresholds for skill level ups. The Combat skill requires the least amount of experience for a level up, while the Weapons skill requires the most, and the Piloting and the Engines skills require the same exact amount of experience points (though they are gained at different system consoles).

Note that while all races gain experience at the same rate (per job done), some races can potentially gain experience at certain jobs faster than the others: Mantis can kill faster and Engi can finish repairs faster. The inverse is also true: Engi are slow killers and Mantis complete the repairs slower. Though all other skills related jobs are performed equally well by all crew races, including inflicting sabotage damage to systems, there are certain hidden modifiers regarding putting out the fires (see the [Repair skill](https://ftl.fandom.com/wiki/Crew_skills#Repair_skill) section for more details regarding this and the other aspects of the Repair skill). Also note that some activities ("jobs") don't grant experience, e.g. sealing hull breaches, putting out fires, destroying crew drones or killing cloned crew. Surprisingly, your mind-controlled crew still gains skill points by performing the tasks.

Certain skill jobs allow "collaboration" in the process of gaining the skill points. For example, receiving a skill point in Combat does not require inflicting all the damage to a hostile crewmember or ship system - only the final hit (or the sabotage tick) is required to gain the skill point. This is also true for the Repair skill, when the repairs may be close to completion by any crew or even repair drones (which are totally unable to gain experience or achieve higher skill levels), but the final repair tick is performed by the dedicated crewmember whereas the "helpers" are ordered to leave the room (or are depowered, in case of repair drones).

All crew on every player ship start untrained, i.e. at skill level 0. As the game progresses, it is possible to acquire or hire crew trained in one or two skills, either at skill level 1 or 2 (for one or both skills). Highly-trained crew becomes more common the closer the game to the end. However, the enemy ships crew is always untrained and cannot reach higher skill levels. And, contrary to crewed ships, the auto-ships retain the crew manning bonus in all relevant systems regardless of the systems status, except for when a system is damaged or being actively disrupted by the hacking pulse.

Usually there are no downsides for higher skills levels. The few exceptions are: possible mistiming the shield charging progress when/after flushing the shields (and this problem is likely to be relevant only in the early game); your combat trained non-Engi crew, especially Mantis, being under enemy mind-control (especially level 2 and level 3, which additionally increase the crew damage) can pose a serious threat to your weakened, slow and/or trapped crew.

## [![Image 3: PilotingSkill](https://static.wikia.nocookie.net/ftl/images/c/c3/PilotingSkill.png/revision/latest?cb=20140408163605)](https://static.wikia.nocookie.net/ftl/images/c/c3/PilotingSkill.png/revision/latest?cb=20140408163605) Piloting skill[[](https://auth.fandom.com/signin?redirect=https%3A%2F%2Fftl.fandom.com%2Fwiki%2FCrew_skills%3Fveaction%3Dedit%26section%3D1&uselang=en&metadata=article-registration-edit-article-section "Sign in to edit")]

[Crewmembers](https://ftl.fandom.com/wiki/Crew_Members "Crew Members") manning [piloting](https://ftl.fandom.com/wiki/Piloting "Piloting") gain one point of experience for each projectile dodged during combat. This includes [asteroids](https://ftl.fandom.com/wiki/Environmental_Hazards#Asteroid_Field "Environmental Hazards"), provided you are still in combat. Remaining in an asteroid field after the combat ends will not raise piloting skill. Skill is also not gained when your ship is cloaked.

This can be trained by finding an [enemy](https://ftl.fandom.com/wiki/Enemy_Ships "Enemy Ships") that cannot break through your [shields](https://ftl.fandom.com/wiki/Shields "Shields"), and letting them fire at you.

## [![Image 7: EnginesSkill](https://static.wikia.nocookie.net/ftl/images/3/3b/EnginesSkill.png/revision/latest?cb=20140408163553)](https://static.wikia.nocookie.net/ftl/images/3/3b/EnginesSkill.png/revision/latest?cb=20140408163553) Engines skill[[](https://auth.fandom.com/signin?redirect=https%3A%2F%2Fftl.fandom.com%2Fwiki%2FCrew_skills%3Fveaction%3Dedit%26section%3D2&uselang=en&metadata=article-registration-edit-article-section "Sign in to edit")]

Exactly the same as piloting, [crewmembers](https://ftl.fandom.com/wiki/Races "Races") manning the [engines](https://ftl.fandom.com/wiki/Engines "Engines") gain one point of experience for each projectile evaded. It is trained the same way with exact limitations. Unlike piloting, engines skill also affects the charge time of your FTL drive: with more skilled crew, it charges faster (Note: The in-game FTL charge time bonus values are incorrect; for the actual charge time improvement see the table [here](https://ftl.fandom.com/wiki/Engines "Engines")).

## [![Image 11: WeaponsSkill](https://static.wikia.nocookie.net/ftl/images/7/79/WeaponsSkill.png/revision/latest?cb=20140408163639)](https://static.wikia.nocookie.net/ftl/images/7/79/WeaponsSkill.png/revision/latest?cb=20140408163639) Weapons skill[[](https://auth.fandom.com/signin?redirect=https%3A%2F%2Fftl.fandom.com%2Fwiki%2FCrew_skills%3Fveaction%3Dedit%26section%3D3&uselang=en&metadata=article-registration-edit-article-section "Sign in to edit")]

[Crewmembers](https://ftl.fandom.com/wiki/Crew_Members "Crew Members") who man the [Weapon Control](https://ftl.fandom.com/wiki/Weapon_Control "Weapon Control") gain one point of experience for each [weapon](https://ftl.fandom.com/wiki/Weapons "Weapons") that is fired. It doesn't matter whether it hits or misses, or whether it can do damage. Volleys of multi-shot weapons such as [burst lasers](https://ftl.fandom.com/wiki/Lasers_(Weapon) "Lasers (Weapon)") count as a single fire and, thus, grant a single experience point. A crew manning the Weapon Control system gains one point of experience when an artillery system fires.

This can be trained by firing repeatedly against an [enemy](https://ftl.fandom.com/wiki/Enemy_Ships "Enemy Ships") that cannot damage you, using weapons that cannot damage the enemy. You also get training when firing bombs at your own ship, but that consumes missiles.

Weapons can be "cancelled" during firing by turning them off just after you receive the skill increment. With enough patience, it's possible to train weapons skill without firing a shot.

## [![Image 15: ShieldsSkill](https://static.wikia.nocookie.net/ftl/images/d/d4/ShieldsSkill.png/revision/latest?cb=20140408163629)](https://static.wikia.nocookie.net/ftl/images/d/d4/ShieldsSkill.png/revision/latest?cb=20140408163629) Shields skill[[](https://auth.fandom.com/signin?redirect=https%3A%2F%2Fftl.fandom.com%2Fwiki%2FCrew_skills%3Fveaction%3Dedit%26section%3D4&uselang=en&metadata=article-registration-edit-article-section "Sign in to edit")]

[Crewmembers](https://ftl.fandom.com/wiki/Crew_Members "Crew Members") manning the [shields](https://ftl.fandom.com/wiki/Shields "Shields") gain one point of experience for every projectile that hits your shield bubble and depletes it during the combat. Ion projectiles, when blocked by shields, ionize the Shields system, preventing manning the system and getting the skill points.

This can be trained against an [enemy](https://ftl.fandom.com/wiki/Enemy_Ships "Enemy Ships") that cannot deal damage through your shields. As with piloting and engines skill, asteroids will not provide training unless you are still in combat.

## [![Image 19: RepairSkill](https://static.wikia.nocookie.net/ftl/images/d/da/RepairSkill.png/revision/latest?cb=20140408163617)](https://static.wikia.nocookie.net/ftl/images/d/da/RepairSkill.png/revision/latest?cb=20140408163617) Repair skill[[](https://auth.fandom.com/signin?redirect=https%3A%2F%2Fftl.fandom.com%2Fwiki%2FCrew_skills%3Fveaction%3Dedit%26section%3D5&uselang=en&metadata=article-registration-edit-article-section "Sign in to edit")]

[Crewmembers](https://ftl.fandom.com/wiki/Races "Races") gain one point of experience for completing the repairs of one [system](https://ftl.fandom.com/wiki/Systems "Systems") or [subsystem](https://ftl.fandom.com/wiki/Systems#Subsystems "Systems") level. The skill point is granted to the crew who performs the finishing repair animation.

One crewmember can be specifically used to do (finish) all repairs to concentrate the experience points. Other crew can help with the repairs, but must be assigned a different task or simple ordered to leave the room (a repair drone can be depowered), so that the designated crewmember completes the repair.

Note: sealing hull breaches provides no experience. It takes 12.5 seconds for an untrained Human to repair one system bar, or to repair a breach. Engi repair at double speed, Mantis at half speed.

Repair skill and racial aptitude for repairs also apply to fire-fighting. However, Rocks have a hidden 1.67 bonus multiplier to fire-fighting speed, while Crystals have a 0.83 multiplier as a penalty. Overall, the time to put out fires only slightly differs from system/breach repairs. See the [Crew races comparison](https://ftl.fandom.com/wiki/Crew#Crew_races_comparison "Crew") table for precise values.

## [![Image 23: CombatSkill](https://static.wikia.nocookie.net/ftl/images/9/9e/CombatSkill.png/revision/latest?cb=20140408163542)](https://static.wikia.nocookie.net/ftl/images/9/9e/CombatSkill.png/revision/latest?cb=20140408163542) Combat skill[[](https://auth.fandom.com/signin?redirect=https%3A%2F%2Fftl.fandom.com%2Fwiki%2FCrew_skills%3Fveaction%3Dedit%26section%3D6&uselang=en&metadata=article-registration-edit-article-section "Sign in to edit")]

Combat skill increases the damage dealt to crewmembers and onboard drones, but doesn't increase the sabotage damage to (sub-)systems. The combat skill works as a multiplier when calculating the hand-to-hand damage.

Combat skill does not affect the speed of sabotaging systems, which is always 12.5 seconds per crew for one system bar, regardless of the crew type or skills.

A crewmember gains one point of experience for dealing the killing blow to hostile crew in hand-to-hand combat or damaging one system level of a (sub-)system. Your mind-controlled crew also gains combat experience for damaging your ship systems. However, killing cloned crew or destroying onboard drones doesn't grant experience.

For reference, the amount of damage that an unskilled [human](https://ftl.fandom.com/wiki/Human "Human") crew deals per hit is 3 to 7 HP, an average of 5 damage per hit.

Hint: boarding can be effective for gaining XP by destroying enemy ship systems before taking out the crew. The enemy can be given opportunity to repair the damaged systems in order to allow your boarders to damage them again.
