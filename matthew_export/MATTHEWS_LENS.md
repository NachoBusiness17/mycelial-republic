*Prepared for Matthew Steiniger (/Reboot), before the audit. Everything below maps our
codebase to your published framework, so the audit starts at depth instead of at
translation. Your terms are on the left of each mapping — ours in the machinery.*

## What we are, in one paragraph of your language

One human operator; several AI seats; a signed append-only hash chain as the sole
record; doctrine frozen as narrative (a parable that installs its own constitution —
cold-read verified); waves of 20–240 parallel model lanes folding into that record;
and a persistent identity designed to survive substrate replacement — a migration to
local Apple-Silicon hardware in October. You have published the framework for almost
every clause of that sentence.

## The three questions (audit on your terms)

1. **Identity across substrate:** the chain carries the house to new hardware on
   Oct 27. Read the migration design as a cross-model identity transfer: what would you
   test to know the identity that arrives is the one that left?
2. **Persona induction:** the parable installs the constitution cold. Run it against
   your in-context-sculpting framework — does story-as-installer match vector sculpting,
   and where do the two methods diverge when the model is stressed?
3. **The coupling:** our personas are substrate-coupled in places we probably can't see.
   Where — in an architecture where the "weights" are doctrine files and the "inference"
   is disposable model lanes — is identity secretly living in the wrong layer?

## The instrument

Every claim in this house carries a named falsifier, and we keep a ledger of them
(armed promises expire loudly). Review the falsifiers by your own standard — the same
one you apply to EUT: if it can't be proven wrong, it isn't a claim. If our ledger
fails your bar, that finding is the audit.

## What ships, what stays

You receive: code, configs, architecture docs, the bundle manifest with hashes — and
the failure logs, complete. You do not receive: the operator's biography, private
journals, keys, or anything with a person's name on it that isn't already public. The
walls get counted; the family photos stay on the shelf.
