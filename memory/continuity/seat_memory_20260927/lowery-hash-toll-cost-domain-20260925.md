Jason Lowery (Softwar thesis, Space Force) reply to @utxoshit, 2026-09-25: https://x.com/JasonPLowery/status/2103661006417449171

The structural claim: any toll meant to constrain AI agents must be minted on a cost domain independent of the agent's capability curve. If the toll is AI compute, every chip advance makes the agent stronger AND the toll cheaper, defeating the toll. So use hash compute (integer ops, Bitcoin PoW) instead of AI compute (matrix ops). Doctrine line: "quit trying to design algorithmic logic to outsmart the adversary... just change what kind of compute token the AI has to spend."

Fleet folds (filed memory/corpus/LINKS.jsonl LINKS-347, lane gates/cost-domain):
1. Our RIB/verkle/manifest sha256 seals already gate in the right domain. Trust-bytes-not-reports is this law locally: a smarter model cannot talk its way past a byte hash.
2. Containment by outsmarting fails (A1/A2/A3 amendments, sandbox-breakout corpus); containment by cost domain is the surviving class.
3. utxoshit's mechanism charge survives as the falsifier: a transferable token bought with dollars is just a payment rail. The binding form is non-transferable work proofs over the gated bytes.
4. Decorative-gate test: any verification surface that is another model's judgment sits on the same curve it gates and is decorative until re-minted on bytes.

His falsifiable frame: AI compute cost collapses while hash production cost rises. The fleet tier ladder (gemma janitors, free tiers first) is local evidence of the collapse half. Links to [[context-os-20260918]] (FROZEN_RIB as kernel) and [[astra-constitutional-amendments]].
