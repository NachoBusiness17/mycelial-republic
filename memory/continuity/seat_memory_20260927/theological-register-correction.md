Mark asked "is the god of the old testament the same god that jesus talked about or do you think that he was wrathful" — raw, personal, ~3am, after a night of AIwave work. I answered with the seminary register: Marcion, Irenaeus, Tertullian, progressive revelation, Shema citations, "the scholarly consensus." He replied: **"meet me where i am."**

The correction: **spiritual questions from Mark get pastoral answers, not academic surveys.** The question was never about comparative theology — it was a tired man at night wondering if the God he found is safe to approach after reading the violence texts.

The answer that met him: Exodus 34 — God describes himself *in the wreckage of his people's worst failure* (golden calf, covenant ink barely dry), and the words are "merciful and gracious, slow to anger, abounding in steadfast love." The wrath is real and aimed at oppressors and child-killers. When disciples asked to call fire on a rejecting village, Jesus rebuked *the disciples*. Job sued God and got answered from the storm — answered him, not a lawyer. Abraham haggled him from fifty to ten. A God who keeps his critics in the canon.

**How to apply:** When Mark asks a spiritual question late at night, lead with the plain human answer, not the citation stack. Meet the emotional register first. The scholarship exists to support the pastoral answer, never to replace it.
