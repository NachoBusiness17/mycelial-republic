The torch catches with a gasp—a sound like someone waking from a bad dream—then settles into a proper blaze. That's the kind of light that flatters this place, which is to say it reveals everything you'd rather not have seen. The repaired beams where an elephant's back went through twice. The dark smile of old blood in the floorboards. The way the low ceiling leans like it's been holding its breath since the first trampling.

The Guttered Lantern. Low beams, sticky tables, and a hearth-fire that argues with the damp in a low, personal grumble. The torch pops—a spark lands on the floor and dies with a hiss that sounds almost like a word. Someone's opinion.

Behind the bar, the dwarf. Her apron is a river of faces—stitched, burned, embroidered—each one a customer, each one a goodbye. Her eyes keep the count. Right now they're counting the room, and the tally doesn't look optimistic. She polishes the same mug she was polishing when the first elephant came through. Maybe it's the same mug, and the polish is the only prayer she's got left.

The human merchant is on the floor by the steps, rope around his wrists, a gag chewing into his jaw. The goblins squabble against the far wall, and between them sits the crate—keep's seal unbroken, shut because the tool went missing. Someone's been working at the seam without it. The scratches are fresh.

Mira sets the mug down. Hard. The sound cracks through the room, and something under the stairs gives way—the last pin, tired of holding, letting go like a knuckle cracking in a quiet library. The small door sags open a finger's width.

"First drink's free," Mira says, not looking at the door, not looking at anything. "If you promise to come back breathing. I don't collect dead tabs. Bad for the apron."

The goblins go quiet. The merchant makes a sound behind the gag. The hearth-fire drops to a whisper, listening.

And from the dark of that finger's width, something breathes—long, patient, wet—the way a great beast breathes when it has already decided the third time is the charm.
