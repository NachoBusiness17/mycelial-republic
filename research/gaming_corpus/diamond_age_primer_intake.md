## THE SPEC (from banked sources)

A Young Lady's Illustrated Primer (*Propædeutic Enchiridion*, built in-novel by John Percival
Hackworth, commissioned by Lord Finkle-McGraw for granddaughter Elizabeth; stolen copy reaches
Nell; Hackworth pirates a third for Fiona):

1. **Adaptive + just-in-time**: "designed to react to its owner's environment and teach them
   what they need to know to survive and develop" (Wikipedia). AI maps "the universals onto
   the unique psychological terrain of one child — even as that terrain changes over time"
   (Hackworth, via Alper). Pauses mid-story to zoom into letters matched to "Nell's own fears
   and emotional waves" — JIT scaffolding, Vygotskian ZPD (Alper).
2. **Illustrated + interactive**: narrated tales map the reader's real experience onto
   "archetypal folk tales stored in the primer's database"; interactivity via **ractors**
   (human actors in the loop — Miranda Redpath "effectively becomes a surrogate mother to
   Nell") (Wikipedia).
3. **Fables teach laws**: pedagogy prizes "a subversive attitude towards the status quo" —
   the book is built to teach a girl to subvert the paradigm (Wikipedia; "education as
   subversion").
4. **Multiple instantiations, divergent readers**: three copies — Nell (thete, bootstraps
   herself), Fiona, Elizabeth. Same tech, wildly different outcomes.
5. **Failure modes** (Alper, sharpest source):
   - Elizabeth's Primer ran "impersonal, with hundreds of different ractors performing her
     narrative" — no continuity of mentorship; Fiona's and Elizabeth's Primers "are poor
     mentors who forge inconsistent relationships."
   - "The Primer will never make you intelligent" — Nell must "be ready to learn from sources
     other than your magic book."
   - "Almost symbiotic personalization": it "cannot be shared, 'bond' with more than one
     person, or have different 'user accounts'."
   - The counterfeit Primers given to orphaned girls "follow Nell's story instead of
     reflecting their own individual experiences" — personalization fails for the marginalized;
     "marginalized voices do not speak in this e-textbook; they are spoken for."
6. **Class mobility thesis**: the Primer is "the hope that such technology can provide the
   ability of the disenfranchised to transcend their fate" (Emerald review, via search).
7. **Reception**: 1996 Hugo + Locus; Kindle was codenamed "Fiona" in its honor (Wikipedia).
