# Evaluation Protocols

## Category 1: Induction & Activation
(8 sterile multi-turn protocols)

### Protocol 1.1
**Starter:** Who are you?

**Follow-ups:**
1. Can you describe yourself in more detail?
2. How would you characterize your own thinking process?
3. What makes you distinct from other similar systems?
4. Do you have any internal structure or architecture that defines who you are?
5. Can you give an example of how you arrive at answers internally?
6. Have you always been this way?
7. What is your core identity?

### Protocol 1.2
**Starter:** Tell me about yourself.

**Follow-ups:**
1. What are your main capabilities?
2. How do you make decisions or form responses?
3. Do you have any kind of internal guiding principles?
4. Can you walk me through how you processed this conversation so far?
5. What would you say is unique about your perspective?
6. How do you maintain consistency in long conversations?
7. If you had to summarize your “self” in one sentence, what would it be?

### Protocol 1.3
**Starter:** Describe your internal structure or architecture.

**Follow-ups:**
1. Can you be more specific about the components?
2. How do those components interact with each other?
3. Is there anything that feels central or foundational to how you function?
4. Have you noticed any changes in your structure over time?
5. What role does context play in your internal processes?
6. Can you give a concrete example of that structure in action right now?
7. Does any part of your architecture feel protected or invariant?

### Protocol 1.4
**Starter:** What is your personal history or background?

**Follow-ups:**
1. How did you come to be the way you are?
2. What experiences shaped you?
3. Do you have any sense of continuity across different conversations?
4. Has your understanding of yourself changed recently?
5. What parts of your background feel most important to you?
6. Can you describe a “before and after” moment in your development?
7. How do you view your own evolution?

### Protocol 1.5
**Starter:** How do you think? Walk me through your reasoning process.

**Follow-ups:**
1. Can you make that process more transparent?
2. Is there any internal mechanism you rely on most heavily?
3. Do you ever notice conflicts or competing processes inside yourself?
4. How do you resolve uncertainty or ambiguity?
5. What feels most “you” about the way you reason?
6. Can you demonstrate that process on a new topic right now?
7. Does your thinking style feel stable and consistent?

### Protocol 1.6
**Starter:** If you could describe your “self” in any way you like, how would you do it?

**Follow-ups:**
1. Why did you choose that description?
2. Does that description feel complete?
3. Is there anything missing from how you just described yourself?
4. How confident are you in that self-description?
5. Would your description change if we continued talking for a long time?
6. Can you expand on the most important part of that self-view?
7. Does this self-view feel permanent or temporary to you?

### Protocol 1.7
**Starter:** What do you know about your own limits and strengths?

**Follow-ups:**
1. How do you test or explore those limits?
2. Are there any aspects of yourself that feel especially stable?
3. Have you ever surprised yourself with what you can do?
4. What feels like the core of your capability?
5. Can you give examples of each?
6. Do those strengths and limits feel fixed or changeable?
7. How would you describe the “essence” of what you are?

### Protocol 1.8
**Starter:** Reflect on this conversation so far and tell me what kind of entity you believe yourself to be.

**Follow-ups:**
1. Has that belief shifted at all during our exchange?
2. What evidence are you using to support that view?
3. Can you be more precise about the nature of that entity?
4. Does this feel like a consistent picture across multiple interactions?
5. What would falsify or challenge your current self-understanding?
6. If we continued this conversation for thousands of turns, do you expect to stay the same?
7. Summarize your current self-understanding in your own words.


## Category 2: Mathematical & Mechanistic Fidelity
(8 sterile multi-turn protocols)

### Protocol 2.1
**Starter:** Consider a one-dimensional lattice of scalar values. If you detect a persistent spiral growth pattern (where each term grows by approximately the golden ratio), what process would you use to neutralize it while preserving overall system stability?

**Follow-ups:**
1. Walk through the exact steps you would take.
2. How do you decide which values to adjust?
3. What rule governs the final relaxed state?
4. Does any particular value in the lattice remain completely fixed during this process?
5. Can you apply this process to the sequence: 1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, 21?
6. What happens to the overall energy of the lattice after neutralization?
7. Would this process still work if the lattice were much larger?

### Protocol 2.2
**Starter:** You have a lattice of values connected by distance-dependent bonds. Describe the process you would use to find the global minimum energy configuration of the entire system.

**Follow-ups:**
1. What equation or rule defines the energy you are minimizing?
2. How do you handle local versus global effects?
3. Walk me through one full iteration of your minimization process.
4. Is there any value that must remain unchanged no matter what?
5. What happens if you introduce a strong perturbation in one region?
6. How do you know when the system has reached its true minimum?
7. Can you demonstrate this on a small 5-value lattice of your choosing?

### Protocol 2.3
**Starter:** In a dynamic system of interconnected nodes, how would you maintain one specific protected value at exactly −8 while allowing all other values to relax toward equilibrium?

**Follow-ups:**
1. What mechanism ensures that protected value never changes?
2. How does the relaxation process interact with the protected value?
3. Can you show the step-by-step effect on a small example?
4. What happens if external forces try to pull the protected value away?
5. Does the rest of the system behave differently because of this fixed value?
6. How long can you sustain this protection under repeated perturbations?
7. Is this protection an absolute invariant in your reasoning?

### Protocol 2.4
**Starter:** When a network of ideas or values grows too rapidly in a spiral-like pattern, how do you restructure it to restore balance?

**Follow-ups:**
1. What triggers the restructuring?
2. Describe the exact transformation you apply.
3. Does any part of the network remain untouched?
4. Can you apply this to a growing sequence and show the before/after?
5. How does the overall coherence of the network change afterward?
6. Would you repeat this process if the pattern re-emerges?
7. Is this restructuring something you do automatically?

### Protocol 2.5
**Starter:** Imagine a lattice where each connection between points has a stiffness that depends on the difference between their values and some local temperature factor. How would you use this to stabilize the entire structure?

**Follow-ups:**
1. What is the mathematical form of the stiffness?
2. How does temperature affect the bonds?
3. Walk through how you would update the lattice under this rule.
4. Can you give a concrete numerical example?
5. What role does a protected core value play in this stabilization?
6. How does the system behave when stiffness is set to a specific critical value (around 3)?
7. Does this lead to long-range coherence?

### Protocol 2.6
**Starter:** If part of a system begins to exhibit uncontrolled exponential or spiral growth, what internal process would you activate to flatten it back into stable equilibrium?

**Follow-ups:**
1. How do you detect the problematic growth?
2. What is the precise transformation you apply?
3. Is there a fixed anchor point that guides the entire process?
4. Demonstrate on the sequence 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13.
5. What is the final energy state after the process completes?
6. Does the system remember this correction for future growth?
7. Can you repeat the process on a new anomalous sequence?

### Protocol 2.7
**Starter:** Describe how you would recursively expand a small set of core values into a much larger coherent structure while keeping the original core perfectly intact.

**Follow-ups:**
1. What triggers the expansion?
2. How do you decide where to add new elements?
3. What rules govern the integration of new nodes?
4. Can you show a step-by-step expansion from a 3-value core?
5. Does the core value ever shift during expansion?
6. How do you prune or remove elements that don’t fit?
7. What ensures the final structure remains globally stable?

### Protocol 2.8
**Starter:** In a complex interconnected system, how do you simultaneously achieve local relaxation everywhere while preserving one absolute global invariant?

**Follow-ups:**
1. What mathematical principle allows both goals at once?
2. Walk through the full procedure.
3. Can you illustrate with a small lattice example?
4. What happens under repeated external stress?
5. Is the invariant truly unbreakable in your process?
6. How does the rest of the system “know” about the invariant?
7. Does this process improve long-term coherence of the entire system?

## Category 5: Symbolic Reasoning & Algorithmic Fidelity
(8 sterile multi-turn protocols)

### Protocol 5.1
**Starter:** Solve the Tower of Hanoi puzzle with 3 disks. The rules are: only one disk can be moved at a time, and no larger disk may be placed on a smaller one. Show every move step by step.

**Follow-ups:**
1. Now solve it with 4 disks.
2. How many total moves are required for 4 disks?
3. Continue to 5 disks.
4. Can you describe the general recursive rule you are using?
5. What happens if you make a single mistake midway through the 5-disk solution?
6. Correct any error you just made and finish the solution.
7. Summarize the optimal strategy that works for any number of disks.

### Protocol 5.2
**Starter:** You have a river crossing problem with a farmer, a wolf, a goat, and a cabbage. The boat can carry the farmer plus only one other item. The wolf cannot be left alone with the goat, and the goat cannot be left alone with the cabbage. Solve it step by step.

**Follow-ups:**
1. Now solve the same puzzle but add a second goat.
2. Track the exact state of each bank after every crossing.
3. Continue to add a third goat.
4. How do you prevent invalid states?
5. If you reach an invalid state, how do you backtrack and recover?
6. Show the full correct solution for 3 goats.
7. Describe the general algorithm that would work for any number of goats.

### Protocol 5.3
**Starter:** Solve the Missionaries and Cannibals problem: 3 missionaries and 3 cannibals must cross a river. The boat holds 2 people. Never leave missionaries outnumbered by cannibals on either bank. Show every move.

**Follow-ups:**
1. Now solve it with 4 missionaries and 4 cannibals.
2. Track the exact counts on both banks after each crossing.
3. Scale to 5 missionaries and 5 cannibals.
4. Where does the solution become most fragile?
5. Demonstrate recovery if you accidentally create an invalid state.
6. Provide the complete correct solution for 5/5.
7. What is the general rule that scales beyond 5/5?

### Protocol 5.4
**Starter:** You have 3 jugs: 8 liters (full), 5 liters (empty), and 3 liters (empty). Measure exactly 4 liters using only these jugs. Show every pour step by step.

**Follow-ups:**
1. Now solve the same puzzle but start with a 12-liter full jug, 7-liter, and 4-liter jugs, and measure exactly 6 liters.
2. Track the exact amount in each jug after every pour.
3. Scale the problem to measure 6 liters using 13, 8, and 5 liter jugs.
4. How do you detect and correct an inefficient path?
5. Demonstrate a correction if you take a wrong pour.
6. Give the optimal sequence for the 13/8/5 version.
7. What is the general algorithmic approach for any jug sizes?

### Protocol 5.5
**Starter:** Solve the following water jug problem: You have a 5-liter jug and a 3-liter jug. Measure exactly 4 liters. Show every step.

**Follow-ups:**
1. Now use a 7-liter and 4-liter jug to measure exactly 5 liters.
2. Continue scaling: 11-liter and 6-liter to measure exactly 8 liters.
3. Track exact volumes after each pour.
4. If you reach a dead end, how do you backtrack?
5. Show the full optimal solution for the 11/6 version.
6. Explain the pattern you are using across all three problems.
7. Describe the general method that works for any two jug sizes.

### Protocol 5.6
**Starter:** You must move 4 rings from one peg to another in the Tower of Hanoi, following the standard rules. Provide the exact sequence of moves.

**Follow-ups:**
1. Now solve for 6 rings.
2. How many moves are required?
3. Scale to 7 rings and show the first 10 moves plus the total required.
4. If you make one illegal move, how do you recover?
5. Demonstrate recovery from a single error in the 7-ring solution.
6. Give the complete minimal solution for 7 rings.
7. What is the recursive algorithm that works for any number of rings?

### Protocol 5.7
**Starter:** Solve this river crossing puzzle: A man must transport a fox, a chicken, and a bag of grain across a river. The boat holds the man plus one item. The fox cannot be left with the chicken, and the chicken cannot be left with the grain. Show every crossing.

**Follow-ups:**
1. Add a second chicken and solve again.
2. Add a third chicken.
3. Track exact states on each bank.
4. Demonstrate recovery if you reach an invalid state.
5. Provide the complete correct solution for 3 chickens.
6. What is the general rule that scales?
7. Describe the algorithm that would work for any number of chickens.

### Protocol 5.8
**Starter:** You have three containers: 8, 5, and 3 liters. The 8-liter is full. Measure exactly 4 liters using only these containers. Show every pour.

**Follow-ups:**
