What's up with the Jimbo charm?
After knowing its effects on the wiki, I am not sure if I ever want to unlock it and pollute my charm draw, since it could robbed me of the opportunity of drawing other solid charms.

Does it has any good use case other than attached onto Nova or Nom and stompy for a guarantee counter reduction? I could arguably say that the balance charm is much better than Jimbo in this scenario due to its consistency.

Judging by its ability of setting unit's heart, attack and counter in the range of 2~5, it feels kinda underwhelming for the risk I am going to take.
-It certainly doesn't provide a high roll for health, thus making it ill suited for tanking units.
-The damage roll range is moderate, but damage is nothing if the unit cannot act in time and dishes out the damage to remove enemies on the board.
-I also cannot see myself using it on scaling units, especially one decked out with solid charms. Most scaling units have a counter range of 3~4, so there is a 75% chance for counter-3 unit to get a negative or neutral counter value, 50% for counter-4 unit to get a negative or neutral counter value. The good roll is not favorable for me and I could likely end up wasting the unit's precious charm slot. In this scenario, a Sun charm is way more superior than Jimbo charm, since it guarantee counter reduction with no fuss.

What are your thoughts on this Jimbo charm?
Did I misjudge its practicality?

No, I don't think Jimbo charm has a much better chance at getting good stats compare to the balance charm.
If I done my math correctly, there should be a total of 64 combinations for the Jimbo charm.
counter, attack, health 2,2,2 - bad 2,2,3 - good 2,2,4 - good 2,2,5 - good 2,3,2 - bad 2,3,3 - good 2,3,4 - good 2,3,5 - good 2,4,2 - bad 2,4,3 - good 2,4,4 - good 2,4,5 - good 2,5,2 - bad 2,5,3 - good 2,5,4 - good 2,5,5 - good 3,2,2 - bad 3,2,3 - bad 3,2,4 - ok 3,2,5 - ok 3,3,2 - bad 3,3,3 - ok 3,3,4 - good 3,3,5 - good 3,4,2 - bad 3,4,3 - good 3,4,4 - good 3,4,5 - good 3,5,2 - bad 3,5,3 - good 3,5,4 - good 3,5,5 - good 4,2,2 - bad 4,2,3 - bad 4,2,4 - ok 4,2,5 - ok 4,3,2 - bad 4,3,3 - bad 4,3,4 - ok 4,3,5 - ok 4,4,2 - bad 4,4,3 - ok 4,4,4 - ok 4,4,5 - ok 4,5,2 - bad 4,5,3 - ok 4,5,4 - ok 4,5,5 - ok 5,2,2 - bad 5,2,3 - bad 5,2,4 - bad 5,2,5 - bad 5,3,2 - bad 5,3,3 - bad 5,3,4 - bad 5,3,5 - bad 5,4,2 - bad 5,4,3 - bad 5,4,4 - bad 5,4,5 - bad 5,5,2 - bad 5,5,3 - bad 5,5,4 - bad 5,5,5 - bad
Here is how I evaluate these combinations:
1. I am using balance charm as a baseline comparison when evaluating the combination since both of them are about setting the stats of the unit to a specific values.
2. I value counter greatly over other stats, since getting more actions usually means you can gain board control faster and win the encounter. Extra point to the combination with lower counter, minus point for highish (4~5) counter.
3. 2 health value is a bad value due to most enemies have an attack value of 2, you don't have much wiggle room when your Jimboed unit is forced to take a hit. While you can get some items or charms to boost the unit survivability, you might not always get them in time before a difficult encounter or at all in a run. Teeth charmed or spike enemies encounter can become a showstopper if you can't find a health boosting item or charm in time.
4. 'Ok' means it is more or less a stat redistribution (using balance charm as baseline comparison) that wouldn't render a unit unviable, however, it feels like a waste for using up a charm slot especially when you get a slower unit as an outcome.
5. Extra point for highish health (4~5) because you can tank more hit. However if the unit becomes super slow, it become kinda terrible even with higher health. You win the game by killing and not stalling.
6. 2 turn timer is a super good value but if the health is too low it might have trouble staying on the board to do its job. A combination that needs help from other charm or item to stay alive is not a good combination.

Here is my observation:
31~% (20) chance of getting a good rolls, 20~% (13) chance of getting ok rolls, 48~% (31) chance of getting bad rolls.

Here is the problem with Jimbo charm, if I am generous and treat both ok and good rolls together as good outcomes, it is about 51~% chance of getting a good roll, that is almost a coin flip chance. That is too random for my liking, do I want to gamble my scaling unit with a coin flip chance that the Jimbo could ruin it and renders it much worse than before? I definitely can't see myself doing that.

I am not sure Jimbo should have a place in the charm pool.
Instead of being in the charm pool, I think Jimbo charm could be an event specific charm you can get on an event node or doing specific action similar to sparing the gnome? (like you could encounter Spoof in an encounter and if you spare him you could get the Jimbo charm as a reward)
