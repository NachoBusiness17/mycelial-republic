Combat needs some work
Parry should be a reward in timing right? The problem is, when you parry it animation locks you with zero I-frames, so you can parry, and then you get hit by ranged attacks or any other number of things while you're locked into the animation. So you get punished for landing parries, the idea then, that it's intended for parries to be only used when safe makes it a worthless mechanic, because any situation where you are then advised to parry, is a battle that doesn't need that level of attention or risk.

As it stands, it's always better to dodge, or even hard block, since parry has the risk of needing to get the timing right to avoid taking FULL damage where dodging is zero damage, and if you have at least one stamina and are surrounded by hazards so you can't dodge? You just attack to move out of the way and deal a bit of damage.

Items are too expensive for the little effect they have and how often you would run through if you actively use them. This of course will change when you get your economy rolling, but the problem is, at that point, nothing matters because you can just brute force it. So it's in the early game where balance in this matter is MOST important. You basically play safe and boring or taken random damage while having FUN doing skill-based actions like parrying. The number of times I'm thinking, "this would be a fun time to parry", yet the second guy has a boomerang ready, so I just have to move out of the way and delay the end of the fight for no actual enjoyment, and in fact, the potential enjoyment was robbed by the mechanics interacting poorly, is absurd.

Another point, Critical Attacks during a Parry. I made a crit build, because I want to see those big numbers, but the problem is, when my second attack crits, the enemy is no longer stunlocked in the parry animation and the third strike misses, actually LOWERING the damage due to the crit. This may just be an issue with button/hit detection during the freeze-frame/slowdown of the crit effect, but it's a pretty big problem considering how risky parries are from the seemingly intended timing based function and the (hopefully) unintended vulnerability while parrying aspect.

Arrow aiming on controller feels pretty bad, I'm locked into place as it is while aiming and would much prefer if the left stick chose targets, or if I could just have it setup so that when I'm holding Right Trigger the Y, X, A buttons function like in melee. Arrows also need to do more or have higher damage or something, when I grabbed Accuracy at the start for crits and bow, I thought my bow would do more than plink and stun, I was expected a lot more effect considering how restricted it's use is due to the arrow system. I expect it to do crippling debuffs or massive headshot damage or something, not single damage damage plus a short stun and stamina reset. The dog has a better stun than the arrow.

EDIT: Arrow aiming also has this weird problem where if you hold the right stick left on the axis, it misses the target a lot, you have to like hold it slightly diagonally to actually hit, which when you try to aim quickly feels pretty unintuitive and bad.

Certain attacks also have minimal startup or animations, and are an instant death sentence like the plant grab in the simplewood. The stuns from enemies are way overtuned in that if the player gets hit early in the fight he's taking 3 or 4 attacks, while arrow stuns against enemies are only for delaying or breathing room to cut down on numbers. They last WAY too long as well.

I'd also like to see the ability to block the 2nd/3rd+ hits of the basic enemies barrage attacks, or the ability to move inbetween hits, even if it takes precise timing.

The game has this, "do anything you want" feel to it in the beginning, but then quickly gets funneled into, "be a small business owner or waste your time" type of thing. I would like to survive and thrive solely on monster hunting, but it seems unlikely at this point that you can make enough money to do so if you purchase any equipment, which makes it laughable how many items slots exist considering how expensive everything is, since to make cash you basically run into areas in Deprived mode with permanent gear and few consumables.

This likely isn't as big a problem once you get a bunch of shops owned, but if I wanted to do that, I'd have picked Barter in the prologue, the choices in the beginning make it seem like the game is more open to player choice than it really is, there is clearly an "intended" way to play (and I've seen dev posts explicitly saying as such with the "small business" angle being pushed), but it goes against everything that the game was sold on in regards to player freedom.

With the counter invincibility, the i-frames need to start the moment you parry since I'm getting hit in the animation lock before it will let you actually activate the counter-attack. Hyped for the changes though.

The counter hits doing no damage thing happens constantly, I "think" it happens more often when you don't use the attack button related to the origin of the enemy, but I'm 70% sure it happens even when you account for that, but it feels like it happens more when I don't go out of my way to do so.

Honestly, I'd prefer if bows just freeze time and let me pick a target like an item, there's too much dodging/blocking needed on the left to focus on the right side of the screen mid-fight. The fact aiming freezes you in place almost makes it a "self-stun" and quite frankly a death sentence unless you wait out timers which when the field is full sometimes never presents an opening until the bow isn't needed anymore and you'd just be wasting an arrow. When you need it most, it's a liability, and when you are safe enough to use it, it's a waste of resources. A slow-mo effect or even repurposing the (new) I-frame on parry slow-mo/freeze would work, you pull out the bow and it does the effect a critical hit has you aim and fire and then the battle resumes.

For skills, I have up to the 3-hit and the no stamina usage while aiming on bow. One thing I noticed, was that the stars needed for the drop items to upgrade skills basically mandate going "deeper" in the dungeon and with the previously mentioned shop issue means you get junk items instead of progression items. So ironically, it's more incentive to rush deeper and never complete a dungeon the way the current system works, than to actually finish, since you get through the skill unlocks faster. Going Blacksmith shop first to forge weapons and combat equipment like charms was clearly a mistake, but one I think most players are going to make who focus on combat, especially since strength/accuracy effect blacksmithing. I can only unload 25 gold worth of mob drops to each shop a day so far which pales in comparison to (apparently) what I would make if I had bought the shop first.

The random fetch quests rewards are garbage at this point, 5-25 gold is worth the effort unless I randomly have the item, and the person is next to the trading board. Running them down is time wasted and the days are short when I can just forge a 4* sword or something and make many times that.

Player skill can carry you through a lot of stuff, but the touch of death on stuns/grabs is real, quite frankly enemy stunning the player should be removed entirely, there's no way to balance durations that are fair or fun when each enemy charges stamina at different rates, shorter stuns just lower the frequency of OHKO, but it should really be eliminated since it has zero counter-play.

Getting grabbed or stunned would be more fun if it only restricted movement options, such as Stun preventing attacks, so you can only move and dodge(but you get stamina back faster), and Grabs preventing movement, which necessitates mastering parry timings and emphasizes the toughness stat for blocking. This would need to be balanced around some of the unblockable/stoppable attacks, such as the bombs at your feet you can't reflect currently.

Status effects effecting player and enemy identically works for story purposes, but in gaming it's a common design problem that plagues games such as D&D, in that when RNG or Rocket Tag exists, the player needs to win every single dice roll or they lose something valuable, while the enemy are effectively a faceless immortal blob of adversarial intent that only needs to win once and whose defeat lacks any meaning to the player compared to the loss of their character. So you act to minimize dice even being thrown your way since eventually over the years of play, you will roll a natural 1, and when it happens to you, it's a lot more devastating than the 100 natural 1's your various foes have rolled when they died. Permanent Level Drain is another example, for most enemies it's just -1 saves/attack/damage rolls, but to the player it's a lasting damage on their character making them weaker for every followering encounter for the rest of the game. I (somewhat) fondly remember the early days of FFXI where you could kill tons of mobs, but then a few disasters suddenly end in a Level Down.

Oh, and a related but side-issue I have is the goddess bless/curse for smithing and combat are tied together. Most people going smithing route that I see on forums/discord are the combat guys, everyone else buys general shop first, but the blessing/curse you want punishes your ore rates if you want extra bow ammo, which then means it takes an extra day for ore to respawn messing with profits which fund your adventure, and creates a negative feedback loop that other professions don't have to deal with(or at least it seems the bless/curse aren't so directly punishing towards a playstyle and it's related field. The choice between berries and field plants is easily dealt with by effectively doing crop rotations(except with berries and field plants) where one week you get the berry bonuses instead of a crop of seed items, then next week you swap. This might not be true in a technical sense, but in my playstyle the curses on plants don't matter, but to enhance combat you have to do long term damage to your children's future in a way no other boons/curses require. Free revive = all kids are frogs or something seems disproportionate compared to other curses that quite frankly don't matter since they do no long term damage to your interests in a meaningful way.

Damaging permanent stat gain opportunities for current combat opportunities is the same resource cost issue that expensive consumables are, it's always a bad trade until you don't need to care about it, but then that means the system is never interacted with in a meaningful way except as a speed bump at best, and an unfun time at worst.
