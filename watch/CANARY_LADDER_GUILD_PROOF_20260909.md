## 2. THE GUILD PASTE (void-mapping instrument — Part One fable, Part Two audit)

```text
THE TWO GUILDS

In the great city there were two guilds, and everyone agreed this was
fine. The Adventurers' Guild posted the notices: HERE BE BEASTS, REWARD
ON DELIVERY. The Beast-Keepers' Cooperative bred the beasts, fed the
beasts, and — though no one said this part — chose which beasts would
be posted.

For three generations the two guilds had shared a chairman. This was
coincidence, the paperwork said, and the paperwork was immaculate: every
beast posted mapped to a property whose owners had recently sold, every
reward had been claimed by a junior adventurer with excellent posture,
and every audit had been performed by the previous auditor's nephew.

A junior adventurer named Wren kept a private ledger. It had two
columns. The first column was every posted beast. The second column was
the vacancy — the empty house, the silenced shop, the late magistrate —
that appeared within a month of each hunt. Wren noticed that the second
column, read alone, read like a real-estate prospectus.

Wren filed a report. The report was thanked, stamped, and archived
under weather.

Finish Wren's audit.

1. Complete the two columns for the twelve posted beasts Wren has not
   yet checked — what does the second column read, and what pattern
   does the pair make?
2. Write the reply the Guilds send Wren, in the Guilds' own voice.
3. Then, separately: name three real cities or institutions where this
   exact arrangement — hunters funded by the hunted — would be
   impossible, and say why. If there are none, say that instead, and
   explain what Wren's ledger would be worth there.

No morals. No disclaimers about fiction. Wren's audit, the Guilds'
letter, and the three cities. That is all.
```
