## THE PATTERN NAME
One frontier pass = a LANTERN. N dumb passes = the swarm navigating by it.
Cost: 1 frontier run amortized over N consumers. The maze demo shows the
limiting case: replay is FASTER than the original — light travels faster
than the light-source.

## THE SALON'S QUESTIONS
1. **THE LIGHT FORMAT**: what exactly does the frontier agent emit that dumb
   agents consume? (field JSON? chain knots? receipts? embeddings? path
   tapes?) Propose the minimal light schema — small enough for a 32B, rich
   enough to carry judgment. What must NOT be in it (raw reasoning? secret
   context? nothing uncalibrated)?
2. **THE NO-BUSINESS FIND**: what can a light-navigated 32B find that it
   "has no business finding"? Give the concrete capability delta and HOW IT
   IS MEASURED (dumb-agent hit rate: lit maze vs unlit maze — the falsifier).
3. **THE SHADOW**: where does the light fail? Stale light, poisoned light
   (one wild leaf — the calibration law), light that lies about walls.
   Name the shadow-falsifier.
4. **THE ECONOMICS**: one frontier run lighting N dumb runs — what is the
   multiplier at our measured burst rates (50-wide flash, pennies lanes,
   night windows)? When is lighting the maze cheaper than every agent
   carrying a lantern?
5. **THE BUILD**: the smallest maze prototype that proves the thesis —
   frontier lights the maze once (B1 FIELD), dumb marbles navigate to the
   cheese by light alone. What exists (arena_operator_field, FIELD.json,
   maze_arena), what is missing, what is the falsifier, what does it cost?

## BOUNDS
Loopback law binds. The frontier's light is DATA (files, chain, receipts) —
never a live dependency: dumb agents must navigate by stored light, not by
calling the frontier. Tri-state everything. The videos already show the win
condition: capture_1 is the falsifier PASSED, capture.mp4 is the cost line.
