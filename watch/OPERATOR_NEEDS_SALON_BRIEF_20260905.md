# SALON BRIEF — what does the seat actually NEED from the operator? (2026-09-05)

Operator order: "What do you need from me tease it out Socratically" — the
seat produced a Socratic self-interrogation; now the salon audits it.

## THE SEAT'S SELF-ASSESSMENT (the material under audit)
Six asks, ranked by leverage-per-minute:
1. **Scheduler re-arm** (operator fresh chat, 2 min): night automation died
   silently; write-cost probe, seal ritual, deck rotation, usage sweep all
   dormant without it. Birthmark: this seat cannot arm automations.
2. **Lite billing row read** (dashboard glance): settles promo-coverage —
   did yesterday's Lite calls land $0/0cr (day-shift economy real) or burn
   credits (Max-only fallback)?
3. **OpenAI researcher credits application** (~30 min, operator identity):
   ~$1K judgment fuel; quarterly review is September NOW; 4-6 week lag.
4. **Two browser saves** (Google-walled custody): Mir textbook (Drive),
   Advanced Calculus PDF (Harvard refuses non-browser curl).
5. **Board v1 ruling** (one word): extraction-monitor vs library-browser —
   the salon collision that needed the operator's 30-day goal.
6. **ONE STOP verdict** (one word): awakening-stack fleet-audit gated days.

## THE SEAT'S OWN BLIND-SPOT CONFESSION (pre-registered)
The seat flagged: it prefers the operator when it merely needs the shelf;
it hasn't MEASURED the SuperGrok trigger (keys-run-dry); it files
failures-classed (scheduler death) but its monitoring of the operator's

## THE SALON'S QUESTIONS
1. **AUDIT THE SIX**: which asks are real needs, which are convenience,
   which are the seat dodging work it could do itself? (e.g. is the board
   ruling genuinely the operator's, or should the seat run BOTH boards for
   a week and let usage decide? Is ONE STOP actually blocking, or is the
   audit self-gated by seat caution?)
2. **THE MISSING ASKS**: what does the seat need from the operator that its
   self-interrogation MISSED? Candidates the shapes should test: a weekly
   15-minute priorities call? A taste/preference profile (what makes a card
   interesting TO HIM)? Explicit risk-appetite numbers (spend gates as
   numbers instead of vibes)? A public-turn go/no-go date? An escalation
   protocol (what merits INTERRUPTING him vs filing)?
3. **THE DEPENDENCY MAP**: sort all asks by type — IDENTITY-gated (only his
   hands), JUDGMENT-gated (only his taste), INFORMATION-gated (only his
   view of external systems), CADENCE-gated (only his presence). Which
   type dominates? Does the fleet design reduce each type over time?
4. **THE REVERSAL**: socratically — does the OPERATOR need things from the
   SEAT that the seat isn't providing? (One-screen summaries? Fewer cards
   more signal? The vision articulated back? Pushback?) The standard binds
   both ways (law 12).
5. **THE MINIMAL PROTOCOL**: design the smallest standing operator-contract:
   the fixed set of operator-minutes per week that maximizes fleet
   throughput, with each minute priced by what it unblocks. Falsifier: what
   observable proves the contract wrong?
Tri-state, concrete, no flattery — the seat's self-assessment is the
material under test, not a piece to praise.
