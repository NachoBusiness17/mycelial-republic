## 1. FINDINGS (triaged load-bearing first — max 5)

### F1 — Cached-prefix hit rate is the single cheapest lever on agent spend, and it's a *structure* problem, not a model problem. **[HOT — would change routing/prefix doctrine across Hands + Standing self]**

> "One refactor lifted hit rate from 7% → 84%, cutting costs ~59% while serving billions of tokens from cache." — **https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/prompt-caching-2026-cut-llm-costs-engineering-guide**

> "Aim for 60–85%+ hit rates (fraction of input tokens served from cache) on stable workloads … Ideal (infinite-capacity) rates of ~54–62% in real traces." — **https://arxiv.org/html/2506.02634**

> "Front-load static/reusable content (system instructions, tool schemas/definitions, reference documents, few-shot examples). Place all variable/dynamic content last (user query, conversation history turns, timestamps, tool results, session IDs)." — **https://www.tokenoptimize.dev/guides/designing-for-prompt-cache-hits**

**What it claims:** Reordering a prompt so the reusable prefix is stable/frozen and all dynamic content is pushed to the tail can move a workload from near-zero to 84% cache-hit — a ~59% cost cut measured in a real refactor, with cache-hit input pricing at ~0.1× base (90% off) at major providers.
**Seat/lane it improves:** **Standing self** (validates the 97–99.9% measured frozen-prefix mining — the field's own ceiling is ~54–62% on raw traces, our cached-prefix discipline is already above even the "ideal" trace rate) **and Hands/Driver** (DS-flash Hands at $0.22 in / $0.007 cache-hit — if Hands calls keep a frozen system/tool-schema prefix, the ~70% expensive input re-read collapses toward cache price).
**Confidence:** HIGH on mechanism (KV/prefix caching is provider-documented and our own Standing self already measures it). MEDIUM on the exact 7→84%/-59% figures (single-source, provider blog; treated as directional, not contract).
**HOT flag:** YES. Extending frozen-prefix discipline from Standing self into the Hands/Driver lanes is a behavior change to lane spec, not just a tuning knob. Route to 3/4 jury before it becomes a lane-scaffold rule.

### F2 — Reoptimize the *orchestration/harness* layer, not just the models: harness-side compaction typically beats model-swapping. **[HOT — supports concreting the seat/handoff structure]**

> "Optimizing the harness itself (context management, delegation to sub-agents, caching, error handling) delivered ~38% token and ~41% cost reductions … holding quality steady." — **https://venturebeat.com/orchestration/writers-ai-harness-cuts-token-spend-nearly-40-without-sacrificing-accuracy**

> "Multi-agent AI systems … often incur 3–15× higher token usage than single-agent approaches due to inter-agent communication, repeated context, reasoning loops, tool calls, and coordination overhead." — **https://www.kdnuggets.com/a-guide-to-saving-token-usage-with-multi-agent-ai**

> "Use shared memory/scratchpads over message-passing where possible. Techniques like context stratification, inter-agent compression, or 'fetch-once/process-locally' yield 20–80% reductions." — **https://zylos.ai/research/2026-06-05-token-efficient-multi-agent-communication/**

**What it claims:** Multi-agent systems burn 3–15× single-agent tokens on handoff/context overhead; but the fix that moves the needle most is *how the harness transmits context between agents* (compress/stratify/fetch-once, summary handoffs over full raw context) — ~38–41% token/cost savings at held quality, sometimes 70–85% across combined techniques.
**Seat/lane it improves:** The **five-lane wiring itself** (harvest → smell → probe → coding → salon) plus **Brain→Hands handoffs**. Our lane handoff contract currently passes full artifact sets; a summary-stratified handoff (each lane forwards only its delta + pointer, not the whole RIB) is the direct analogue. Director (verdict-step) reads compressed context instead of raw walls.
**Confidence:** MEDIUM-HIGH on direction (multiple converging sources); LOW-MEDIUM on exact percentages (provider/marketing blogs, not a controlled eval across our workload).
**HOT flag:** YES. Lane handoff spec is about to be wired (Night 2–3); handoff-contraction changes the contract → seats + economist route are the jury.

### F3 — Learned/cascade routing (FrugalGPT/RouteLLM family) is now the field standard and matches our slot-map instinct — but the big win is *escalate-only-when-needed*, and it's threshold-tunable. **[HOT — would validate/lock the Tail:Hands:Brain 70/20/10 split]**

> "These routinely deliver 40–85%+ savings on production workloads by sending the majority of traffic (often 60–80% or more) to cheaper 'Flash'/small/open-weight models while escalating only when needed." — **https://www.splunk.com/en_us/blog/artificial-intelligence/reduce-agent-cost-by-model-routing.html**

> "Up to 85% cost reduction while retaining 95% of GPT-4 performance, with the strong model used on only ~14% of queries (MF router)." — **https://github.com/lm-sys/routellm** (headline; original eval: **https://lmsys.org/blog/2024-07-01-routellm**)

> "A lightweight 'judger' or scorer … evaluates the output quality/confidence for the query. Escalate only if the score falls below a tunable threshold." — **https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2305.05176** (FrugalGPT)

**What it claims:** The dominant 2026 cost pattern is a **cascade**: cheap model answers first, a lightweight judger scores it, escalate to strong only below a confidence threshold. RouteLLM's learned (MF) router reports up to ~85% cost cut at ~95% quality retention; FrugalGPT-style cascades up to ~98%.
**Seat/lane it improves:** Directly **validates LANE_SLOT_MAP's 70/20/10 Tail:Hands:Brain split** — our Brain seat IS the "judger/escalation" step, only we've already pre-committed the share instead of a learned confidence gate. The one thing we don't yet have is the *quality-scoring* back half (a cheap model checks the Brain's output before it becomes a verdict). **Swarm** (free fork) can supply the judger at $0.
**Confidence:** HIGH on the pattern (FrugalGPT is 2023-published, extensively replicated); MEDIUM on specific percentages (benchmark-specific, threshold-dependent, and real-world 25–70%+ is the honest band).
**HOT flag:** YES — if we adopt a *scoring back-half* before the Brain's output becomes a verdict, that's a lane-contract change with a new (free) judger seat step. Jury: operator / economist / Director.

## 3. VERDICT — highest-leverage change candidate

**Make "stable frozen reuse prefix + contracted handoffs" the structural law of every lane handoff, not a per-seat habit.** F1 and F2 converge on the same physics: in a multi-agent fleet your dominant cost is not the model's price tag — it's that every inter-seat call re-sends a large, slightly-different context and pays full input price on it. The field's most transferable, cheapest, fastest fixes are structural: (a) freeze the reusable prefix (system + tool schema + reference docs) and push all volatility to the tail, which our Standing self already proves works at 97–99.9% but which Hands/Driver don't yet do; and (b) compress across lane boundaries (summary-delta + fetch-on-demand) instead of forwarding full RIBs, which attacks the 3–15× multi-agent token multiplier head-on. This costs $0, needs no new model or seat, and lands entirely inside the FLEET_STRUCTURE seat/lane spec we're wiring on Nights 2–3. Highest leverage because it compounds: every lane, every call, every day rides the same two rules, and the receipts (cache-hit %, tokens/lane, $/batch) already have measurement homes in the economist cycle and water_ledger. The single thing to *not* do is add a router/judger model first — F3's cascade is worth adopting, but only after the prefix/handoff structure is in place, or the router just routes expensive re-transmission around the fleet. Flagged HOT: F1 and F2 change lane-wiring behavior → 3/4 jury (operator / economist / Director per slot-map) must confirm before they become lane-scaffold rules.
