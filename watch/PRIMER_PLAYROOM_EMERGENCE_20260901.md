## The scene, compressed
Nell is sent to the common playroom after an incident at home. Other children snatch the stolen Primer, play keep-away, torment her. She gets it back, finds a quiet spot, reads on. The Primer continues Princess Nell's tale — and the dinosaur companion tells HIS story: he survived the extinction event by studying martial arts under a wise mouse named Dojo for millions of years (tic-tac-toe with kings of species, humility training, last surviving T. rex). Nell's toy dinosaur maps to him.

## The emergence mechanics (why storyline appears without being plotted)
1. **The world wounds first; the story answers.** The keep-away beating is real. The Primer does not shield her — it transmutes: the next beat processes what just happened (trolls in the Dark Castle). Story = state-transition function over lived experience. The engine reads the save file and writes the chapter FROM the wound.
2. **Isomorphic re-encoding.** Toy dinosaur → last T. rex. Real torment → trolls. The Primer never redacts Nell's life; it re-encodes it as fairy-tale logic she can act on, then the solution transfers back. (This is the riddle law: isomorphic re-encoding, not redaction — and map-precedes-territory.)
3. **Story-within-story recursion.** The dinosaur tells his OWN tale — the narrative tree branches sideways. Companion depth generates narrative without moving the main plot. Emergence lives in the edges, not the trunk.
4. **Characters are memory carriers.** Dinosaur = millions of years of survival memory (skill + humility). Each companion (duck, rabbit/Peter, Purple) = a different domain + coping strategy. The stuffed animals are personality-indexed external memory.
5. **Engine owns truth; child owns agency.** The book only advances when Nell acts/reads; consequences persist; the story adapts as she grows. (game_campaign.py L4 law, stated 25 years early.)
6. **Heterogeneous voice routing.** When the tale needs human warmth, a ractor (Miranda) fills the voice. One continuous intelligence, many engines.
