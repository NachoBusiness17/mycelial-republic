## 1. What we are building

**The Republic of Letters, with a kernel in it.** Historically: the res publica
literaria — Mersenne's mailbox was the 17th century's Internet; he copied,
forwarded, and provoked; Fermat never published, he corresponded. We recreate the
minds as ractors (the Fermat pattern: harvested correspondence → registers →
harness + blind A/B), and we give them the Internet they actually had — letters,
routing, shared problems — plus the thing they never had: **a kernel**. The
statement-DAG (THE MARGIN's `the_margin.py`) is the shared to-do list; the Lean
kernel is the arbiter; the verkle chain is the archive that never deletes a letter.

## 3. The protocol

- **Letter schema:** from / to / cc (Mersenne copies everyone) / in-reply-to /
  body / claims[] (each claim tri-stated, kernel-checkable when formalizable) /
  dag_refs[] (links into the statement-DAG — a letter may propose or prove a
  node).
- **Mersenne routing:** addressed to one, read by many; the router may provoke
  (that is historically accurate — he engineered the Wallis/Fermat number feud).
- **The arbiter:** where a letter makes a formal claim, the kernel judges — the
  semantic half goes to the salon, blind chairs, tri-state.
- **The archive:** the chain. A proof is append-only; so is the correspondence.

## 4. The standing agenda (the parable's gift)

Every great debate the network hosts gets both questions asked: the Western
*"how fast, by what law?"* and the Eastern *"where are we going, and who is
coming with us?"* The essay's register set (Fen the rousing, the ledger of
Yiji/Jianchi, Shi the latent power, the ant's station) enters the Eastern
citizens' voice — and the Western citizens must answer it, not ignore it. First
scheduled correspondence: **Fermat ↔ Liu Hui on the fine horse and the lame
horse** — two men who measured acceleration as duty, separated by thirteen
centuries, both用水墨 and Latin on the same page of the Nine Chapters.
