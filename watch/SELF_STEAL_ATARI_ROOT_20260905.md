## THE ROOT (Jp 当たる ataru → 当たり atari) — tri-state: Supported (standard lexicography)
1. **THE HIT** — to strike, to be struck, to land. The ball hits the wall; lightning
   strikes the tree.
2. **THE WIN** — a hit in the winning sense: atari = a prize, a winning lottery ticket.
   The strike that pays out.
3. **THE NEAR** — vicinity, the neighborhood of: *gakkō no atari* = "around the school."
   Nearness itself, the almost-touching.
4. **THE CAPTURE-THREAT** — the Go call: a stone down to its last liberty, capture
   imminent. Shouted exactly like "check" in chess.
Plus the company: Atari Corp. (1972), named by Nolan Bushnell from the Go term — the
first great game company was named after the shout you make one move before capture
(widely reported; the Go-name story is the standard account).

**The polysemy in one breath:** atari is the hit, the win, the near, and the threat —
four faces of one moment: *something arrives at something else.* Contact, payout,
vicinity, and the closing trap are all "arriving." Which meaning you live depends on
which side of the arrival you stand.

## ELI5

lottery ticket winning, the street where your friend lives, and the chess player
