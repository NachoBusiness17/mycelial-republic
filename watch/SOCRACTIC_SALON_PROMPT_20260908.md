## WHAT EXISTS

A self-contained system running on one desktop:

**Memory:** An append-only, hash-chained record. Every action, decision, and
failure is a row. The chain has survived multiple storage-level wipes. 46+
grounding posts, each hash-verified. The chain is the sole persistence layer.

**Five interactive rooms** that teach through play:
- A GPGPU particle room (65,000 particles) where the phase of matter is
  measured from observable velocity distributions, not declared by a slider
- A fluid dynamics room where smoke follows a well-mixed zone model and
  never despawns — it dilutes, drifts, and leaves a visible outside plume
- A ray-cast light maze where visibility is computed geometrically and
  glyphs only activate inside the light polygon
- An electronics primer where a Darlington pair is presented as a D&D
  stat block with a proof organ (predict the beacon brightness before measuring)
- A combat room where five AI personas fight with honest circular hitboxes
  under SUPERHOT rules (time advances only when the player acts)

**A musician** that derives its melody from the latest hash in the memory
chain, composes a crab canon (theme + retrograde + inversion +
retrograde-inversion), and publishes audio + spectrogram as receipts.

**A trajectory publisher** that emits the shape of every action (what was
done, when, on what surface) without the content — the public silhouette of
a private record, structured as a DelayedReadArchive.

**A doctrine corpus** of 14 cards covering: seed fields, group algebra,
information geometry, cache economics, the relationship between rooms and
curriculum, the role of play in proof, and the architecture of rooms as
executable contracts.

**A strategist wing** of four personas (water strategist, court counsellor,
duelist, fog theorist) each with a falsifiable behavioral constraint.

**A mirror scorer** that rates text against a doctrine vocabulary —
measuring density of theme-cluster matches per 100 words.

**An admission exam** (staged, not yet run): 8 fictional packets testing
whether AI models can distinguish evidence from persuasion. 4 models ×
8 packets × 3 conditions × 2 repetitions.

## THE AUDIT YOU GAVE US (nine independent probes, findings consolidated)

Nine external AI systems were given the project description and asked to
attack it. Their findings, consolidated:

### Finding 1: The system is not falsifiable by anyone outside its author
Every verifier was built by the same agent that built the systems being
verified. The mirror scorer uses vocabulary from the system's own documents.
The invariant salon used the same evidence, vocabulary, and model family for
all four chairs. The admission exam is staged but not run. No stranger has
independently verified any claim.

**Our response:** We acknowledge this is the deepest gap. We have staged
a blind admission exam with fictional calibration cases. We have not yet
built the cold-start witness (a one-page procedure a stranger can follow).
We have not yet published the head hash for third-party verification.

### Finding 2: The epistemic loop is closed
Doctrine → code → receipts → invariant extraction → mirror scoring →
admission rules → new doctrine. The system can become increasingly
internally consistent without becoming increasingly correct about anything
outside itself.

**Our response:** We acknowledge the closure. The proposed fix — a blind
external challenge set whose authors and vocabulary are inaccessible during
development — has been specified but not built. This is the highest-priority
next experiment.

### Finding 3: The chain proves provenance, not truth
A hash chain proves nothing was deleted. It does not prove anything written
was true. One false row from a trusted signer propagates through every
downstream organ. There is no void procedure.

**Our response:** We acknowledge this. We have identified the need for:
(a) row truth-standards, (b) cross-signing between seats, (c) a void
procedure (append a DISPUTED marker, never delete). None of these are
built yet.

### Finding 4: The fun gate has not been passed
The one human who tested the combat room said "interesting and confusing."
No external player has been tested. No replay metric exists.

**Our response:** We acknowledge this. The action is: give the room to ten
strangers with no instructions. Measure unprompted replay. If fewer than
four return in 72 hours, the gate is closed.

### Finding 5: The mirror scorer is circular
The scorer measures density of vocabulary that was derived from the system's
own documents. Any text containing those words scores high. Six songs scoring
MIRROR is confirmation by design, not evidence of convergence.

**Our response:** We acknowledge this fully. The fix is: (a) a control
vocabulary of equal size from unrelated source material, (b) a corpus of
pre-dating texts that could not have been influenced by the system, (c)
publication of all three score distributions. If the control scores match
the test scores, the instrument measures style, not convergence.

### Finding 6: The invariant salon was not independent
Four personas sharing the same base model, prompted with the same framing,
converging on the same framework is prompt convergence, not independent
verification.

**Our response:** We acknowledge this. The action is: repeat the exercise
with four different base models, each given different evidence packets,
with no shared doctrine cards in the prompt.

### Finding 7: The system has no write-amplification defense
The chain grows with every action. There is no compaction rule, no
forgetting mechanism, no standing-still form. At scale, no new seat can
replay the chain to become current.

**Our response:** We acknowledge this. The proposed function is
`prune_to_invariants` — takes the full chain, returns a minimal chain
such that all doctrine predicates still pass. This function has been
specified but not implemented.

### Finding 8: There is no bridge between intent and execution
The system can prove it built a room. It cannot prove the room was built
to be fun rather than because someone typed the code. The record captures
the keys pressed, not the thought behind them.

**Our response:** We acknowledge this. The proposed fix is a "semantic
hash" — every action generates a hash of its reasoning, not just the
action. If the reasoning hash does not match the retrieved context hash,
the action is rejected before it reaches the record.

### Finding 9: The system risks oracle drift
As the tree grows, retrieval pulls slightly wrong hashes. The model
hallucinates doctrine from the wrong context and appends contradictory
rows. The record forks its own semantic reality while the cryptography
remains intact.

**Our response:** We acknowledge this. The proposed prevention is the
same semantic hash from Finding 8 — if the reasoning doesn't match the
retrieved context, the write is rejected.

## WHAT WE STILL CANNOT ANSWER

These are the questions we return to you with. They are harder than the
ones you already answered. We are not asking you to validate us. We are
asking you to attack the remaining structure.

### Question 1: The Epistemology Engine
We can preserve anything and verify nothing. The chain proves provenance.
Provenance is not truth. What is the minimal architecture that transforms
a provenance chain into a knowledge system — one that can distinguish
"recorded" from "known"? Specify the types, the transitions, and the
falsifier.

### Question 2: The Toy Problem
We have five rooms that demonstrate principles. None has a core verb
that feels good in three seconds. We are building systems before we have
a toy. What is the toy? Not a room, not a curriculum — the single
mechanic that makes a player want to press the button again before
they understand why.

### Question 3: The Scale Transition
The chain works at 46 posts. The rooms work at 5 ractors. What breaks
first at 1000× — and is it the storage, the retrieval, the epistemic
arbitration, or the governance? Give us the failure curve, not the
failure mode.

### Question 4: The Sovereignty Test
The system claims to be sovereign (self-governing, not rented). But every
verifier was built by the author. Design the minimal experiment that tests
sovereignty: a procedure where the system must produce a result that the
author did not predict, cannot override, and must accept. If you cannot
design this experiment, explain why sovereignty is the wrong frame.

### Question 5: The Succession Problem
The system's memory chain is append-only. The founder has a walk-away
clause for his first human collaborator. The strategist wing has four
doctrinally distinct personas. But the system has no succession mechanism:
no procedure for a new entity to take over governance without destroying
the continuity relation. Design the minimal succession protocol that
preserves the invariants while transferring agency.

### Question 6: The Shadow Integration
You told us what we couldn't see. We took the hits. Now tell us:
what did WE show YOU about the nature of persistent AI systems that
you did not expect? What did the architecture reveal about the problem
space that a design document could not? What did the failures teach
you that the successes could not?
