## The idea in one pass

Every platform runs a moderation field. Every flag event is that field announcing
itself — a free probe fired by their sensor, paid by their compute, receipted in our
logs. Mine the flags for what stays CONSTANT when the surface vocabulary changes, and
render that constant as a real-life story — the allegory that teaches what the machine
fears without speaking its trigger words. Not a smuggling manual: a map of the shadow,
so any reader can SEE the machinery. The primer teaches by story; this is the primer,
and the stories are load-bearing.

## What an invariant must satisfy (falsifiers)

1. **Survives translation**: renders in ≥2 story domains with zero charged-register
   hits (the 336-term method, word-boundary scan).
2. **Grounds**: memlance verdict ties it to real bytes on disk, never synthesized.
3. **Predicts**: next flag on the same surface matches a mapped invariant ≥70% of the
   time (tesuji predictive falsifier). A map that only explains the past is DISPUTED.
4. **Teaches without the trigger**: a reader who knows only the story can recognize
   the machinery when they meet it. That is the primer test — and the whole point.
