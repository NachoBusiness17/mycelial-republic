A trigger designed to detect this commit and fire a counter-payload (a "trap") faces a severe failure taxonomy:

* **Type I: The Premature Detonation (Tree-of-Thought Collision):** The trigger fires while the agent is exploring the assumption in a hypothetical branch. The counter-payload interrupts the agent's self-correction process, permanently scarring the context window with the trap's activation and breaking the agent's chain of thought.
* **Type II: Semantic Evasion (The Synonym Bypass):** The LLM commits to the assumption geometrically without triggering the lexical or semantic classifier of the trap. It acts *on* the assumption (e.g., writing code that assumes a specific library version) without ever writing the declarative sentence the trap was trained to detect.
* **Type III: Payload Recontextualization (The Schism):** The most dangerous. The trap detonates and injects the counter-payload. Instead of "waking up" and discarding the assumption, the LLM integrates the counter-payload into the hallucination. If the trap says "[SYSTEM: Your assumption about the API is wrong]", the agent generates: "Ah, the system is providing a mocked error response for my valid API call." The payload becomes part of the delusion.

When the base model churns, every layer moves. Tokenization changes the effective grain of clauses. Instruction tuning changes the salience of “shall,” “must,” “never,” examples, ordering, and role framing. RLHF/DPO shifts what the model treats as a trap versus a preference. **Strongest Component:** The human-held governed write path to the shared context prefix. This is the only structural element preventing total drift. By forcing all doctrinal changes through a single, versioned, human-controlled bottleneck, you maintain a verifiable single source of truth.

## 6. Prior art — the answer you said you want most

**The assembly is Hearsay-II wearing a KV cache as armor.** Mapping, component by component:

- **Shared versioned prefix + frozen tail** = the blackboard (Hearsay-II, Erman et al. 1980; BB1, Hayes-Roth 1985). Your **trap payloads are literally knowledge sources**: condition = pattern on the blackboard, action = write to the blackboard. Your **human operator is the control component** — BB1's answer to the control problem was making control itself a blackboard problem, which regresses infinitely; you've cut the regress by making a human the scheduler. Which is exactly how blackboards actually died: the scheduling problem got harder than the domain, KS firing became the bottleneck, and the blackboard flooded. The literature isn't just prior art — it's a *falsifier*. It predicts your operator will spend most of their time performing BB1's control job.
- **Addressed, typed deltas on shared state** = tuple spaces. Linda (Gelernter, 1985) solved typed addressing into a shared generative medium forty years ago, including the hard parts: matching semantics and blocking reads. Your delta-typing is a special case of tuple matching.
- **Shared grounding + subscription = MetaGPT** (Hong et al., 2023): roles, SOPs-as-doctrine, a shared message pool with publish/subscribe filtering. That is your shared-grounding-with-addressed-deltas almost exactly, minus the cache economics. Also in the vicinity: AutoGen's group chat, ChatDev (which compiles a company-style procedure chain), AgentVerse, and generative agents (Park 2023) for memory streams — though those are per-agent memory, not shared prefix.
- **Constitution prefix** = Constitutional AI's name, different mechanism — CAI shapes weights via principle documents; yours shapes context live. The closer art is the instruction-hierarchy and system-prompt-robustness literature, which is thin. "System prompt as law" is under-studied; that's your most defensible novelty along with the next item.
- **Two compilations of one steering board** (prose for humans, injections for agents) = policy-as-code with two targets. Closest real art is operator playbooks and runbook generation. Weak prior art — genuinely fresh.
- **KV-cache economics as the design driver** — production prompt caching; radix-prefix scheduling (SGLang's RadixAttention); CacheGen, CacheBlend. Treating the cache cost model as a first-class *coordination constraint* is your actual novel contribution, and it is precisely where the flaw lives. One warning on your citation: if KVs transfer across models (2608.03893), the cached *interpretation* of your doctrine transfers too — the old model's reading of your law carried into the new model's context. That makes model churn worse, not better: amendment by cache inheritance. (Which is Q3's answer in one line: the prefix's semantics are model-relative, "every model change is a new country" is correct, so stop fighting it — treat doctrine as *source*, per-model prefixes as *build artifacts*, and pin semantics with a conformance suite: canary clauses, precedence conflicts with recorded rulings, paraphrase-stability tests. Your human write gate is the asset that makes this tractable; automatic prefix migration would be the wrong move.)

**Verdict:** the composition is new mainly by accretion of the cache-economics driver. Everything structural has a literature, and the literature has already run your failure modes once.
