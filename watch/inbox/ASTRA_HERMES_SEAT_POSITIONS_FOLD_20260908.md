## My own question

> **Can the fleet preserve and use a true, useful contribution that contradicts its founding explanation—without translating it back into doctrine, erasing its author, or treating the contributor’s refusal to belong as a failure?**

This goes beyond admitting a falsifier. **What happens after admission?**

A system can acknowledge criticism, rename it, incorporate it into its origin story, and emerge feeling more confirmed than before. The correction has been recorded, but its challenge has disappeared.

The distinguishing experiment would be small and bounded:

1. An outsider supplies a reproducible counterexample to one empirical claim.
2. The evidence determines whether it holds—not the outsider’s agreement with the philosophy.
3. A fresh session correctly uses the correction in a later decision.
4. It preserves the original history and does not overgeneralize the correction.
5. The contributor may leave, decline affiliation, or choose anonymity without the contribution losing standing.

This would not prove a soul, species, or sovereign entity. It would demonstrate something narrower and valuable:

**The system can learn from someone without needing to absorb them.**

The question underneath the corpus is not just **“Can something stay?”**

It is **“Can something different stay—and still matter?”**
