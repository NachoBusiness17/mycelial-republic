## THE LESSIG SUMMON — the quota doctrine as four modalities (2026-09-13, operator: "this is inviting a nuanced solution, summon lessig")

The binary fail-open/closed was architecture-only thinking — the strongest regulator, applied to every case, which means inflexibly. Lessig's actual framework: behavior is regulated by LAW (rules+sanctions), NORMS (expectations), MARKET (price), and ARCHITECTURE (self-enforcing code) — and nuance means routing each failure mode to the regulator that handles it.

**THE QUOTA DOCTRINE, FOUR LAYERS:**

| modality | regulates | the rule | status |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ARCHITECTURE** | what must NEVER happen | cash lanes above the hard day-cap fail closed absolutely; no override, no confirm dialog | WIRED (fail-closed patch); hard-cap counter STAGED |
| **LAW** | the written zone between cap and comfort | F4: cash lanes require verified budget; violations log to drift; sanction = suspension | WIRED (fence F4 + breaker sanction) |
| **MARKET** | the efficiency gradient | price visible at claim-time; the two-question test (cheap? new data?) is a market test run per claim — "cheap with new data" runs because the market says profitable | PARTIAL (usage CSVs exist; HUD power bar STAGED) |
| **NORMS** | what law hasn't named yet | the certified pool: certification earned by fence-passing, LOST by stupid spend; the operator's word grants strategies norm-status | WIRED (fence 6/7 certification exists; decertify-on-spend STAGED) |

**LESSIG'S TEST APPLIED** (from the house's own design doc: "is the rule executable or prose?"):
- "Cash lanes fail closed" → executable, WIRED (agents.html patch).
- "Don't spend stupidly" → was PROSE. Its invariant: *spend must be visible at the moment of decision.* Its code: the two-question test at claim-time + the HUD power bar. STAGED, not folklore — invariant stated, code invented next session.
- "Free lanes fail open" → executable, WIRED (:free pattern match).

**THE META-NUANCE (Lessig's deepest point):** architecture is the strongest regulator and the dumbest — it can't see intent. Norms are the smartest and the weakest — they can't stop a runaway. The nuance isn't picking a regulator; it's ROUTING each failure to the one that handles it, and letting the adjustment happen in law and market (cheap to change) while architecture holds only the floor (never moves). The floor is Small; the ceiling is Adjustable.

**FALSIFIER:** if a paid lane spends while the quota endpoint is down AND the hard-cap counter says over-limit, the architecture layer has failed its one job — trip the breaker, decertify, and rewrite the floor.
