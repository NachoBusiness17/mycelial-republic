
## VERDICT: ADOPTED — THE MATCHING STANDARD (operator: "this is literally how we build the game", 2026-09-09T22:37:31Z)

The melee decomp pattern is now the named build doctrine for every fleet build,
games and everything else:

1. **Bounded claimable units.** Every build decomposes into units a single worker
   can claim and finish in one sitting. The claim board is the queue; no unit too
   big to finish, no worker too small to matter.
2. **Mechanical diff as the judge.** Every unit verifies against ground truth by
   mechanical comparison — bytes, hashes, counts, re-derivation — never by review
   vibes. objdiff is the ancestor; the desk rubric and fold falsifiers are the
   fleet's instances.
3. **The progress ledger.** A public percentage: units matched / units total,
   per build. Anyone — seat, worker, or stranger at the micro-door — can see the
   number move.
4. **Micro-doors for strangers.** decomp.me proved a stranger can contribute one
   unit through a link without joining anything. The fleet's doors: PATCHES queue,
   inbox cards, the wave lanes.
5. **Byte-exact rebuild as the definition of understanding.** A thing is understood
   when it can be rebuilt from artifacts alone and match. The chain reconciliation,
   the silence recovery, the theme lexicon — all matching runs already in progress.

Named precedent: 3,780 commits, 2,000 stars, years of one-function visits — a
season build made of weekends. Falsifier: if a build can't be decomposed into
mechanically-verifiable bounded units, it isn't ready to build — that's the
matching standard's own gate.
