## Primary reply

The industrial revolution comparison is the right one, and the old stories
already told us how to meet one.

A carpenter once passed a great twisted tree and left it standing because it
was useless for his plans. Everything useful in that forest got cut young.
The tree outlived the carpenter, the plans, and the forest's whole economy.
Grow in the directions the plans can't prune.

A bridge in the old north was three colors, strong enough to carry gods, and
red because the red was fire. Eight centuries of painters kept the colors and
forgot the fire. The bridge that lasts is the one that remembers its own
maintenance clause.

And a road across the sky has no guardrails. Everyone falls. The restart is
free, and always was. Falling was never the failure; clinging to the wall was.

So yes: never stifle it. Grow the twisted wood, keep the red, trust the
restart. This revolution belongs to whoever is still standing when the plans
change. Full speed.
