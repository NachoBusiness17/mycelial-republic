## THE QUESTIONS

**Q1 — the 1/e (sting: high, harmless to ask).**
"Milestone 2 says ten logical qubits for 1,000 logical non-trivial clock cycles 'with
fidelity 1/e.' Is 1/e per-operation, per-cycle, or end-to-end program success — and is 1/e
≈ 37% the intended threshold, or a shorthand that the still-unpublished V&V plan will
redefine?"
*Receipt: OTA Appendix 1, Milestone 2 row. Follow-up: "will the V&V plan be published
before applications are due, so applicants can know what they're promising?"*

**Q2 — the definition doing the work (sting: high).**
"The OTA defines 'scientific relevance' as solving problems of interest to DOE mission
scientists, and says the first SRQC need not demonstrate 'classically intractable scales.'
Is 'scientifically relevant' therefore decoupled from quantum advantage — and if the
world's first fault-tolerant quantum computer may do work a classical machine could do,
what exactly is the fault tolerance buying?"

**Q3 — self-graded difficulty (sting: high).**
"Milestone 2 requires 'at least 50 of the platform's hardest operations.' Since native
gate sets differ per platform, who decides what 'hardest' means across incommensurable
architectures — and doesn't letting each applicant nominate its own hardest set make the
milestone platform-relative rather than competitive?"

**Q4 — the wobble (sting: medium, embarrassing). [CORRECTED per RS compose: 10³, not 10²]**
"The announcement says the first SRQC will perform 'hundreds of millions of fault-tolerant
operations' at 100 logical qubits; the OTA's final-system definition says a THOUSAND logical
qubits capable of 'millions of hard fault-tolerant operations.' Per logical qubit that is
10⁶ ops/qubit announced versus 10³ ops/qubit in the contract — a shrink of THREE orders of
magnitude between the press release and the contract. Which is the bar, and when did it
change?" [Receipts: energy.gov article; OTA Appendix 1 final-system row. $45M lab-call
figure receipted: energy.gov article, lab-call paragraph.]

**Q5 — the V&V monopoly (sting: medium, structural).**
"Circuits are specified by the V&V team, demonstrations are executed by the V&V teams
'which may require cloud and/or physical access,' and V&V tooling is built exclusively by
national labs under the separate $45M lab call. Who audits the auditors — what recourse
does an awardee have if V&V methodology changes mid-competition?"

**Q6 — the race economics (sting: medium).**
"Milestones 3a/3b/3c payments are 'variable and subject to the total number of awardees' —
winners split fixed pools. Doesn't that structure reward few winners rather than many, and
has DOE modeled what that does to information-sharing between teams?"

**Q7 — the substrate question (sting: none for them, flag for us — THE IN). [BOUND per RS
compose R3: askable only after the Q7 walk — one OTA paragraph/day — binds it to the V&V
scope clause verbatim. The Eighth Question (the walk's finding) is the one actually asked.]**
"Is the program's fault-tolerance vocabulary — logical encoding, clock cycles, V&V
metrics — intended to be substrate-independent in principle, or is V&V scoped at the
quantum-classical boundary only? In other words: if someone demonstrated an equivalent
logical-cycle persistence claim on a non-qubit substrate, would DOE's V&V framework even
have a category for it?"
