# Orphans and candidate connections

Version 1.1 · 2026-09-16 · Generative supplement to the mapped research brief

The operator describes this corpus as **"the best ideas we have had at the time."** Read it as a dated research notebook: evolving mechanisms, metaphors, doubts, experiments and aspirations. A hypothesis need not be proven to deserve preservation. An earlier mistake may still contain a useful question. An audit should prevent unsupported promotion, not erase the search that generated the idea.

This supplement adds the generative half of the map: what deserves another look, what lacks a useful connection, and what could be tested by combining separate strands. **Supported / Disputed / Unproven describe evidence; they do not rank an idea's creative worth.** Research lifecycle (seed, open lead, test candidate, dormant, superseded) is a separate field. A proposal can be Unproven and worth testing; a measured result can be Supported but irrelevant to the next decision.

"Orphan" here means an underconnected motif, an unbuilt bridge, an unresolved measurement or a parked branch. It does not mean the operator forgot it, that nobody developed it, or that it is absent from the live system. All 37 source files already have a substantive claim link, even after excluding the inventory claim. That graph result does not establish complete idea coverage. The twelve candidates below arose from a second targeted reading; the list is not exhaustive.

"Novel connection" means newly surfaced or made more concrete in this dossier. Several are recovered from the corpus itself. Each entry credits its precursors and names what this review adds. **No first-in-literature claim is made.** The external map supplies research context already inspected; it does not establish novelty or validate the combinations. No new literature-wide novelty search was conducted for this supplement.

## Candidate connections (N01–N10, all Unproven, each with a discriminating test)

- **N01** — Make every cheaper representation carry a recovery contract (recipe/version/loss/equivalence check).
- **N02** — Separate an idea's storage state, truth status and permission to act (residency ≠ evidential status ≠ authorization).
- **N03** — Use game variants to generate bounded tests of agent authority and recovery.
- **N04** — Choose additional agents by measured rescue value and information access.
- **N05** — Let the next decision guide both information retrieval and human escalation.
- **N06** — A retraction should invalidate dependent products, not merely edit a sentence.
- **N07** — Historical game variants can test whether dissent survives compaction.
- **N08** — The model passport can become an interface for measured stochastic services.
- **N09** — Compare a source-first reading with a map-first reading before the next fold.
- **N10** — Ask whether assistance leaves the operator better able to act independently.

## How another model should extend this notebook (adopted as fold law)

1. Recover the strongest useful version of an idea before judging it. Preserve the original passage and distinguish human intent, model elaboration, metaphor and reported observation.
2. State what the proposed connection changes: a data structure, decision, prediction, experiment or available choice. Shared vocabulary alone is not a mechanism; an analogy may still be worth keeping as a seed.
3. Credit earlier versions. Mark a connection as recovered, elaborated or newly proposed here. Leave global novelty Unproven until the relevant literature is checked.
4. Preserve alternatives and reasons for parking them. Lack of current budget or a missing prerequisite is different from falsification.
5. Offer a small discriminating comparison. Record its scope and expected failure, then keep outcome status separate from the idea's lifecycle.
6. Return any relevant observation this schema cannot express in plain text with its source. The schema is itself revisable. Do not silently discard a useful lead because it lacks a place in the current map.
