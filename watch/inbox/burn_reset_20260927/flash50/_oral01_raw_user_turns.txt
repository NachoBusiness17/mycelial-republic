**Date:** 2026-08-14

## Self-steal

The necessary organs already existed:

- `mag/seat_steer.py` — live transport path, but party aliases and driver response shapes diverged.

- `mag/frontier_salon.py` — multi-seat owner, but it sent isolated copies of one question.

- `mag/game_dm.py` — party surface, but same-turn players did not see earlier moves and it read the

- `mag/agent_corpus.py` — content-addressed durable per-agent ledgers.

- `mag/quest_roshomon.py` — disagreement is evidence; consensus is not automatically truth.

- `mag/rib_bus.py` — shared instruction layer; it remains separate from candidate novelty.

The build is therefore a composition, not a new coordination throne: normalize transports, pass an

attributed append-only transcript in deterministic round-robin order, and file every contribution.

## External validation

1. The official A2A protocol separates agent identity/capability discovery, messages, stateful

tasks, and durable artifacts. It also treats remote agents as opaque: collaboration does not

require exposing internal memory or tools. We adopt that boundary locally with participant

metadata, per-turn provenance, a roundtable id, and a durable artifact; we do **not** claim A2A

wire compatibility.

- https://a2a-protocol.org/latest/specification/

- https://a2a-protocol.org/latest/topics/key-concepts/

2. Microsoft AutoGen's official `SelectorGroupChat` documentation describes participants taking

turns while messages are broadcast to all members. It normally permits an LLM speaker selector.

We keep the shared-context benefit but use deterministic round-robin selection, avoiding a new

model-based chair.

- https://microsoft.github.io/autogen/stable/user-guide/agentchat-user-guide/selector-group-chat.html

3. Du et al. report that multi-agent debate over multiple rounds can improve factuality and

reasoning on evaluated tasks. This supports a multi-round option, but not unbounded conversation

or automatic truth promotion. The local protocol therefore caps rounds at three and extracts

falsifiable tests instead of declaring a winner.

- https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14325

## Build decision

One bounded protocol:

1. A logical participant resolves through one canonical transport registry.

2. Before every turn, the seat receives the frontier question and attributed prior transcript.

3. Each response must expose `POSITION`, `CHALLENGE`, `NOVEL DELTA`, and `TEST`.

The strongest papers for Mag are not primarily “agent” papers—they’re about convergent histories, selective communication, verification, and economical model routing.

Read these five first

Merkle-CRDTs: Merkle-DAGs meet CRDTs (2004.00107) (https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.00107)
The closest match to your frozen invariant. It combines content-addressed history with data that converges despite weak or out-of-order communication.
Steal: evolve party_subchain into a multi-parent Merkle-DAG: each seat maintains a tip, references causal parents, and merges deterministically without a central narrator.

The Blocklace: A Byzantine-repelling and Universal CRDT (2402.08068) (https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.08068)
A signed, partially ordered generalization of a blockchain that can expose equivocation while remaining decentralized.
Steal: cryptographic seat identity, fork visibility, explicit dissent, and containment of malfunctioning or dishonest seats—without installing a throne.

Improving Multi-Agent Debate with Sparse Communication Topology (2406.11776) (https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11776)
Shows that agents need not all communicate with everyone; sparse topologies can retain or improve results at substantially lower cost.
Steal: your “cold-edge router”: choose a few high-value, deliberately unlike communication edges per question instead of broadcasting the full transcript.

RouteLLM: Learning to Route LLMs with Preference Data (2406.18665) (https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.18665)
Learns when to send a request to a strong versus weak model and can transfer as the underlying models change.
Steal: train Mag’s tier router from the skill/outcome ledger. The router learns task requirements; model names remain replaceable implementation details.

## The Ordinary World

You are in the Land of Forgetfulness, which is exactly as advertised: you forget things here. Not your name — not yet — but the specific weight of your own hands, the reason you came in from the rain, the name of the fish nailed above the bar. (It is Gar, and Gar is older than everyone in the room except the stains.)

The tavern belongs to Mira. It has been rebuilt twice since the elephant trampled it — once on a Tuesday, once on a Thursday — and the second time the elephant came back to apologize and did it again, so Mira stopped counting repairs on the walls and started counting them on her apron. The apron is a river of faces. Every regular, every drifter, every fool who promised to come back breathing — their features swim in the fabric, and Mira's eyes keep the count of all of them. Her gaze is a ledger. You avoid it.

The torch on the wall catches with a gasp — that is Calcifer, the Hearth, who argues with the damp every night and only shows his face when the argument gets personal. He pops now, a proper blaze, and the flattery of it is that he reveals everything you'd rather not see: the water stain where the elephant came through the roof (twice), the chalk marks where the Cartographer sat last week and let her coastline skin unfurl across the table, mapping a route to somewhere she refused to name. Her palms still itch whenever you look at them. You look at them. She looks back. You look away.

You are beneath the stairs. The small door. The lock.

The last pin gives way with a sound like a knuckle cracking in a quiet library — you feel it in your teeth — and the door sags open a finger's width. Inside: a crate. You shouldn't. This is the illegal part, the part where the Truth-Teller watches from her plain corner, unadorned, and you cannot make her look away. She never blinks. She never asks. Mira calls from the bar, "First drink's free if you promise to come back breathing."

The crate is warm. It should not be warm. It smells of iron and something older, something that has been waiting for you to remember what you came to forget.

You open it anyway.

Inside, there is a map — but not the Cartographer's kind. This one is skinned from someone who once knew the way out. Folded tight, still breathing.
