## 0. The paradox is not a paradox; it is two different predicates

"Never finished" and "always complete" conflict only if done is one predicate. It is two. A static artifact is complete when work is exhausted. A growing system is complete when its growth is *accounted*: every claim current, every debt named, every meter covering every surface, every cycle's delta filed. The first predicate cannot hold forever; the second can hold every day, indefinitely.

This estate runs the second predicate in code, today. The census carrier claim (AGENTS.md, the Tests bullet: 2,303 collected / 352 files, dated 2026-09-27) is already wrong, because the estate grew five test files since yesterday, and the disk holds 369 candidate files against the claimed 352. The patrol's verdict on this is "ok". Not because lying is tolerated, but because the bar is not "claim equals world" (unreachable for a growing system by construction) but "claim is young AND the growth since the claim is named". Completion for a growing system is a freshness bar plus named deltas, not an absence of deltas. That sentence is the whole thesis; the rest of this file is its receipts.

## 1. Every fix creates a new surface: the mechanism, named

Literature names the mechanism: Hyrum's Law (Titus Winters, Tom Manshreck, Hyrum Wright, "Software Engineering at Google", O'Reilly 2020): with a sufficient number of users of an API, all observable behaviors of your system will be depended on by somebody. A fix changes observables; therefore a fix is a surface-creation event. Growth is not a side effect of maintenance, it is the product of maintenance.

The estate's own chain tip demonstrates it. Git log at HEAD (run this session): commit d90bc8607 (Q-028, "FW-43..50 fleet warts executed") lands a links widget, three un-swallowed stderr sites, an FKB re-baseline (17 to 1421 signatures), a day-end pile habit, four new test surfaces, and a receipt file. Its sibling b34ccd3ba (Q-020) re-lands five test harnesses to "202/202" because a *prior* commit's deliberate behavior change (a game walker rework) had invalidated a frozen test tape. Fixes do not shrink the backlog; they convert unmeasured risk into measured surface, and measured surface demands meters.

The estate then does the second-order thing most shops miss: it meters the meters' own blind spots. `tools/census_drift_patrol.py:53-64` (B2-244) documents that file-growth signals are blind to tests added *inside* existing files (parametrize, appended cases), a gap measured live at a 42-item delta on 2026-09-26, so an opt-in `--collect` deep check reruns pytest itself and fail-closes on a stated tolerance of 20 items in either direction (`census_drift_patrol.py:122-125`). `tools/violation_ratchet.py:72-81` (B2-273) stamps the accepted size into every accepted file-too-long debt claim and prints a GROWTH line when the claim silently absorbs more lines than it was accepted at. When a meter cannot see one direction of growth, a new meter is grown for that direction. This is Ashby's law of requisite variety (W. Ross Ashby, "An Introduction to Cybernetics", 1956: only variety can absorb variety) implemented as an appetite rather than a lament.

This is Lehman's Law of Continuing Change (M. M. Lehman, "Programs, Life Cycles, and Laws of Software Evolution", Proc. IEEE 68(9), 1980): a system in real use must be continually adapted or it becomes progressively less useful. The estate's refinement is that the adaptation of the law is itself metered: every gate is born WARN-ONLY with a TODO-FLIP-TO-ENFORCE marker (t40 precedent, `pre-push:13-16`), so a law's own growth is staged, visible, and operator-gated. A law that blocks on day one is a new surface nobody can afford; a law that warns on day one and flips only after surviving pushes is a surface that pays rent before it charges.

## 3. Every tool creates new debt: the adjacent possible, priced

A tool is a realized novelty; its adjacent possible includes the misuse modes nobody had surface area to commit before the tool existed. Debt is therefore not an accident of tools, it is the shadow price of capability.

## 4. What cathedrals actually did: compartmentalized completion

Literature, real history: Gothic cathedrals were never completed as whole buildings; they were completed as parts. Altars and chapels were consecrated for use as each was finished, and the building functioned liturgically for centuries mid-construction (Stewart Brand's "How Buildings Learn", Basic Books, 1994, makes the same point about ordinary buildings: adaptation, not completion, is a building's normal state). Cologne Cathedral ran 1248 to 1880, 632 years, and the 19th-century completion was possible because the medieval founding plan survived (the facade plan, recovered in the 19th century), so a plan-invariant outlived every mason who touched the stone. Gaudí, asked about the Sagrada Familia's schedule, is credited with "My client is not in a hurry"; the church was consecrated for worship in 2010 by Pope Benedict XVI while still under construction. Never finished, always complete: liturgical completion and constructional completion are different predicates, tracked separately, both true at once.

The estate encodes both predicates verbatim:

- **Liturgical completion, the DNA day.** "Lean complete = cold vertex only" (`docs/DNA.md:49`); the SessionEnd must-write of residual + card + content commit + chain leaf (`docs/DNA.md:42`); "Missing PDF/visual is not incomplete. Never required for 'filed'" (`docs/DNA.md:95`). A day closes by filing, not by finishing. And the crypt law, the Cologne move: "Fidelity lives in files + chain, not in a process that must stay awake" (`docs/DNA.md:4`). The plan in the crypt outlives the builders.
- **Constructional completion, the version arc.** v6, the first Mag-built Mag, is the keystone (argued with receipts in the sibling `deep-cathedral-stone.md` section 2), and the estate refuses to date it: "no calendar dates, emergent gates". No facade completion is promised; bays close when their gates pass.

The daily unit of completion is explicit in the fleet's habits. This is vespers in code: the day is done when the day's stone is counted, never when the cathedral is.

## 5. The formal answer: a four-clause definition of done for a growing system

Each clause is grounded in an instrument I read this session. DONE(S, t) holds if and only if:

1. **CURRENCY.** Every load-bearing claim has a carrier with an instrument and an expiry bar, and no bar has lapsed without renewal. Instruments: the census carrier plus its 7-day stale bar with a git-verified heartbeat (`census_drift_patrol.py:9-32`); boot docs stale at 7 days only while the repo has heartbeat (`tools/freshness_check.py:8-14`, STALE_DAYS=7, HEARTBEAT_DAYS=2 at `:53-54`).
2. **NAMED DEBT.** Every known imperfection sits in a register that changes only by named act, with acceptance stamps, and a prune path that trends down. (`violation_ratchet.py:5-8`; 941 rows live; 943 to 941 banked in Q-028.)
3. **COVERED METERS.** Every claim-bearing surface has a covering operator, and an operator that cannot see must fail loudly rather than pass silently. ("A blind gate is worse than no gate", `pre-push:81-85`: exit 2 blocks; secret gate fail-closed on scanner error, `pre-push:317-332`; the corpus FTS index decays to scan fallback whenever catalog.jsonl moves past it, and a daily task heals it through the one sanctioned writer before escalating, per B2-465 in AGENTS.md and `tools/corpus_fts_rebuild.py:1-27`, whose tmp-then-`os.replace()` build means a crashed rebuild never leaves a half index.)
4. **ACCOUNTED DELTA.** The last growth cycle's delta is named: tests landed, receipts filed, carriers refreshed or window-tolerated with the gap printed as evidence. The live census run in section 9 is clause 4 executing: five files named, the 369-vs-352 instrument gap printed every run by law (B2-247, `census_drift_patrol.py:355-362`).

Then "never done" simply means: the clause set never includes "no growth". The two are compatible because clause 4 accounts growth instead of forbidding it. Biology already uses this definition: Maturana and Varela's autopoiesis ("Autopoiesis and Cognition: The Realization of the Living", Reidel, English 1980, original 1972) defines a living system not by absence of change but by closure of the network of processes that produces its own components. A system designed to grow is complete when the network that maintains it covers the system it has become, evaluated per cycle. Lehman's Law I says growth or decay is mandatory; the estate's move is to make decay *metered* (stale bars, rotting exceptions), so the only un-metered thing left is growth, and clause 4 prices growth.

The estate even rots its permissions, which is the temporal form of the cathedral: an accepted ratchet claim with an "expires" date becomes a violation again the day after expiry ("exceptions rot into violations instead of silently living forever", `violation_ratchet.py:70-71`), and a frozen boot doc must re-register with a reason and a date (`freshness_check.py:44-51`, FROZEN_OK entries read this session: BOOT_4_7.md and state/HANDOFF.md both frozen with dated reasons). Nothing is complete forever; everything is complete until a dated exemption lapses. Consecrated, still scaffolded. A Google-SRE gloss on clause 1: a freshness bar is an error budget for truth (Beyer et al., "Site Reliability Engineering", O'Reilly 2016, where 100% reliability is the wrong target); a forever-current claim is the wrong target here, and the budget is 7 days.

## 6. Failure modes of infinite cathedrals

1. **RITUAL COMPLETENESS.** A green channel that always says ok trains its reader to dismiss it. This happened here: every fully green push took the WARN arm because the tool's head line printed uppercase OK where the hook matched the lowercase prefix "CENSUS-PATROL: ok", so B2-338 froze an output contract: on green, the first printed line must open lowercase-ok, and the case arm must never be widened to accept red tokens (`pre-push:67-75`). In an always-running system the liturgy of green must itself be designed, or green becomes wallpaper.
2. **BLIND COMPLETENESS.** An unparseable claim that reads as pass is the 12-day disease in a new costume; hence the estate's exit taxonomy (0 pass, 1 named stale, 2 cannot-verify-blocks) and the B2-277 law that a blind gate blocks (`pre-push:76-99`). An infinite cathedral must fail closed, or it does not actually contain its growth; it merely launders it.
3. **DEBT THEATER.** Completion language applied to ledgers ("we accept debt") without named acceptance and a trending-down prune is Cunningham-abuse. The tell is whether the register can shrink. It can here, and the shrink is a receipt, not a boast.
4. **WRONG FORGETTING.** The B2-95 silent reap (`violation_ratchet.py:10-17`) is the leak species of LINKS-349: structure dropped without its address. Every pruning rule must state its domain or the cathedral "completes" by demolishing chapels nobody remembers consecrating.
5. **METER LAG AS UNPRICED DEBT.** The default census gate cannot see intra-file test growth (B2-244), so the meter's own gap is a standing surface. The honest posture is the one on disk: keep the deep check out of the fast path, name the gap on every run, and keep the tolerance a stated constant, never a vibes-based "close enough".

## 7. So what is done? The banked sentence

Done, for a system designed to grow, is not a state of the artifact. It is a satisfied predicate over the cycle: claims current, debt named, meters covering, delta accounted. The artifact is never done; the day is, and only ever the day. The compass already compresses this: "Chat is heat. Residual on disk is truth" (`docs/ref/MAG_MYCELIAL_REPUBLIC_COMPASS.md:30`) and the north star's "artifact > transcript" (`:54`). Transcripts are never done; there is always more chat. Artifacts are done one filing at a time, forever. The infinite cathedral is not a building that never finishes. It is a liturgy that never stops being said over a building whose stones all carry their dates. Gaudí's client is still not in a hurry; the mass is said daily; every scaffold has a name on it; and the plan in the crypt outlives every hand that ever laid stone.

## 8. The one original move this dig adds

The estate has four ad-hoc instances of one missing table. The census carrier has its patrol; boot docs have the freshness gate; the FTS index has the B2-465 daily healer; the portable-seed claim has a named stranger slot, held open with "do not silently defer" and "until then the portable-seed claim stays Unproven" (`state/CURRENT.md:34-44`, read this session: seed/verify_seal.py exited 1, 55/56 verified, 1 drifted, and the estate filed the failure instead of hiding it). Each instance is the same tuple: (claim, carrier, instrument, expiry bar, covering operator, fail-closed behavior). They are hand-wired separately, which means some future claim type will be born without one, and nothing will notice except after the rot.

Spec: a COMPLETE_BY_COVERAGE manifest. One file, one row per durable claim type, columns: carrier path, instrument command, expiry bar, covering operator (hook, scheduled task, or named human), last-verified date, and the dark-operator behavior. The estate has already proved the pattern works when it is data: FROZEN_OK is a data table with reason and date (`freshness_check.py:44-51`), and "expires" is data in the ratchet (`violation_ratchet.py:70-71`). Completeness becomes machine-checkable the moment coverage is data, the same way the census became checkable the moment its claim got a grammar (B2-249, the ownership law at `census_drift_patrol.py:96-116`). Until then, "always complete" is enforced in four places out of hundreds, by memory and habit, which is the hand-copied-headline disease wearing a better wardrobe.
