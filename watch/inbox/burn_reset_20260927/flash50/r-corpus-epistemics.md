# r-corpus-epistemics: who published the LLM-epistemics framings formally

Item: r-corpus-epistemics (flash50), banked 2026-09-28.
Ask: deep-dive prior work for (1) "statistical grammar, semantics from syntax, no world" and (2) "hallucination as closed-corpus epistemology"; who has published these framings formally; OWED if unverifiable.

## Verdict (short)

Neither exact phrase is a published term of art on the evidence reachable today (two exact-phrase web searches run 2026-09-28, see provenance section). Both framings decompose into two formally published lineages with named papers and sharp claims:

1. "Statistical grammar, semantics from syntax, no world" is the form-without-meaning lineage. Formal core claim: Bender and Koller, ACL 2020 ("a system trained only on form has a priori no way to learn meaning", FETCHED). Polemic: Bender, Gebru, McMillan-Major, Shmitchell, FAccT 2021, the "stochastic parrot" paper (venue/pages/DOI confirmed; primary quote OWED). Cognitive-science formalization: Mahowald et al., Trends in Cognitive Sciences 2024, formal vs functional competence (FETCHED). Chomsky's 2023 press line ("scan astronomical amounts of data to find statistical regularities", FETCHED via Monthly Review Online interview) is the same framing from the generativist side. Deep roots: Searle 1980 (syntax not sufficient for semantics), Harnad 1990 (symbol grounding), and behind those Shannon 1948 / Harris 1954 / Firth 1957 / Chomsky 1956. Counter-formalizations: Piantadosi and Hill 2022 (FETCHED), Pavlick 2023 (CITED).
2. "Hallucination as closed-corpus epistemology" is the hallucination-inevitability plus parametric-knowledge lineage. Formal inevitability: Kalai and Vempala, STOC 2024, calibrated models must hallucinate on singleton facts at a Good-Turing rate (FETCHED); Xu, Jain, Kankanhalli 2024, computable LLM vs computable ground truth, no computable learner learns all computable functions (FETCHED). The corpus mechanism itself, i.e. the model only safely knows what pretraining packed into parameters: Gekhman et al., EMNLP 2024, fine-tuning on Unknown facts linearly increases hallucination (FETCHED). Confabulation framing: Farquhar, Kossen, Kuhn, Gal, Nature 630:625-630, June 2024 (FETCHED). Eval-incentive extension: Kalai, Nachum, Vempala, Zhang, "Why Language Models Hallucinate", arXiv 2509.04664, Sept 2025 (FETCHED). Corpus-limited-knowledge ancestors: Petroni et al. EMNLP 2019 "Language Models as Knowledge Bases?" (FETCHED), Roberts, Raffel, Shazeer EMNLP 2020 closed-book QA (FETCHED), Lewis et al. NeurIPS 2020 RAG (FETCHED).

Verification tiers used below: FETCHED = I read the primary page this session; CITED = venue and metadata confirmed by search-result snippets, primary page not fetched.

## Lineage 1: "statistical grammar, semantics from syntax, no world"

- **Bender and Koller, "Climbing towards NLU: On Meaning, Form, and Understanding in the Age of Data", ACL 2020** (https://aclanthology.org/2020.acl-main.463/, FETCHED). The formal statement of the whole first framing: meaning is communicative intent grounded in a shared situation between speakers, and training on form alone cannot get you there. The anthology page abstract argues "a system trained only on form has a priori no way to learn meaning". The hyperintelligent-octopus thought experiment (octopus on a telegraph cable between two islanders, mimicking form it has observed, fails when the task needs world reference) lives in the paper body, not the abstract; body not fetched this session, octopus description per search-tier sources.
- **Bender, Gebru, McMillan-Major, Shmitchell, "On the Dangers of Stochastic Parrots: Can Language Models Be Too Big?", FAccT '21, pp 610-623, DOI 10.1145/3442188.3445922** (CITED; multiple independent citation bibliographies agree on venue/pages). Coins "stochastic parrot" for systems that string word sequences per probability without grounding. The opening-page phrase "hodgepodge of shallow stochastic links" is reported by search results but I could not fetch the primary PDF this session (dl.acm.org returned HTTP 403; my faculty.washington.edu PDF guess 404), so the verbatim quote is OWED.
- **Mahowald, Ivanova, Blank, Kanwisher, Tenenbaum, Fedorenko, "Dissociating language and thought in large language models", Trends in Cognitive Sciences, March 2024 (arXiv 2301.06627, FETCHED)**. The cognitive-science formalization of "grammar without world": LLMs show strong formal linguistic competence (rules and statistics of language) while functional competence ("understanding and using language in the world") is doubly dissociable from it in humans and not implied by formal competence in LLMs. This is the peer-reviewed, mechanistically grounded version of the ask's framing.
- **Chomsky, interview "ChatGPT and human intelligence", Monthly Review Online, 2023-04-24 (chomsky.info, FETCHED)**. The generativist version: LLMs "scan astronomical amounts of data to find statistical regularities" and do as well on impossible languages as on human ones, hence a difference in kind from human language learning. The related NYT op-ed "The False Promise of ChatGPT" (Chomsky, Roberts, Watumull, 2023-03-08) was NOT fetched this session (OWED).
- Roots (all CITED only this session, see OWED): Searle, "Minds, Brains, and Programs", Behavioral and Brain Sciences 3(3):417-424, 1980 (Chinese Room: syntax not sufficient for semantics, the direct ancestor of "semantics from syntax is impossible"); Harnad, "The Symbol Grounding Problem", Physica D 42(1-3):335-346, 1990 (symbols defined only by other symbols, which Bender and Koller's argument operationalizes for statistical LM); Shannon 1948 (Bell System Technical Journal 27, Markov approximations of English, the original "statistical grammar"); Harris, "Distributional Structure", Word 10:146-162, 1954 and Firth 1957 "you shall know a word by the company it keeps" (distributionalism, the hypothesis that meaning IS distribution, i.e. the pro-side root of "semantics from syntax"); Chomsky, "Three Models for the Description of Language", IRE Transactions on Information Theory 2(3):113-124, 1956 (finite-state Markov models inadequate for natural language, the original anti-"statistical grammar" argument).
- Counter-formalizations (the "LLMs DO get meaning from distribution" side): **Piantadosi and Hill, "Meaning without reference in large language models", arXiv 2208.02957, 2022 (FETCHED)**: under conceptual role semantics, meaning "arises from conceptual role", is internal-relational, and "cannot be determined from a model's architecture, training data, or objective function"; so the corpus is not the whole epistemology. **Pavlick, "Symbols and grounding in large language models", Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society A 381(2251):20220041, 2023 (CITED)**: maps the formalism-vs-embodiment debate for LLMs; full page not fetched.

## Lineage 2: "hallucination as closed-corpus epistemology"

- **Petroni, Rocktäschel, Riedel, Lewis, Bakhtin, Wu, Miller, "Language Models as Knowledge Bases?", EMNLP-IJCNLP 2019, pp 2463-2473 (https://aclanthology.org/D19-1250/, FETCHED)**. Establishes that a model's world knowledge is whatever cloze-queryable facts pretraining left in parameters, and that "certain types of factual knowledge are learned much more readily than others" (uneven coverage of the closed corpus).
- **Roberts, Raffel, Shazeer, "How Much Knowledge Can You Pack Into the Parameters of a Language Model?", EMNLP 2020 (arXiv 2002.08910, FETCHED)**. The "closed-book" term's home: QA "without access to any external context or knowledge" (the abstract page's code slug t5-cbqa = closed-book QA). This is the phrase the ask's "closed-corpus" most likely compresses.
- **Lewis, Perez, Piktus, Petroni, Karpukhin, Goyal, Küttler, Lewis, Yih, Rocktäschel, Riedel, Kiela, "Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Knowledge-Intensive NLP Tasks", NeurIPS 2020 (arXiv 2005.11401, FETCHED)**. Names the epistemic defect of the closed corpus: parametric models' "ability to access and precisely manipulate knowledge is still limited", with open problems "providing provenance for their decisions and updating their world knowledge". RAG is the engineering answer to closed-corpus epistemics.
- **Ji, Lee, Frieske, et al., "Survey of Hallucination in Natural Language Generation", ACM Computing Surveys 55(12), article 248, 2023, DOI 10.1145/3571730 (CITED)**. Canonical taxonomy: intrinsic (contradicts source, faithfulness) vs extrinsic (not verifiable against source), the survey that standardized "hallucination" for generation.
- **Kalai and Vempala, "Calibrated Language Models Must Hallucinate", STOC 2024, pp 160-171 (arXiv 2311.14648, FETCHED)**. The formal inevitability theorem: an inherent statistical lower bound on hallucination rate for calibrated pretrained LMs, driven by facts whose truth cannot be inferred from training data; the hallucination probability is approximately the fraction of facts that occur exactly once in the training data, a "Good-Turing" estimate, and it holds even with error-free training data. This is the strongest published formalization of "hallucination is a property of learning from a finite corpus".
- **Xu, Jain, Kankanhalli, "Hallucination is Inevitable: An Innate Limitation of Large Language Models", arXiv 2401.11817, 2024 (FETCHED)**. Second formal inevitability route: hallucination defined as inconsistencies between a computable LLM and a computable ground-truth function; no computable learner can learn all computable functions, so as general problem solvers LLMs must hallucinate; carry-over from formal world to real world. Search results claim ICLR 2025 acceptance; the arXiv page I fetched shows no venue note, so that acceptance is OWED.
- **Gekhman, Yona, Aharoni, Eyal, Feder, Reichart, Herzig, "Does Fine-Tuning LLMs on New Knowledge Encourage Hallucinations?", EMNLP 2024 (arXiv 2405.05904, FETCHED)**. The empirical closed-corpus mechanism: in closed-book QA, facts outside what pretraining already knew are "learned significantly slower" by fine-tuning and, once learned, "linearly increase the model's tendency to hallucinate"; models acquire facts at pretraining, fine-tuning mainly teaches use. This is the cleanest published statement that the corpus boundary is the knowledge boundary.
- **Farquhar, Kossen, Kuhn, Gal, "Detecting hallucinations in large language models using semantic entropy", Nature 630:625-630, 2024-06-19 (FETCHED)**. Refines "hallucination" to confabulations, outputs that are wrong and arbitrary (sensitive to random seed), and proposes entropy "at the level of meaning" via entailment clustering of samples. Treats confabulation as characteristic of sampling from an approximate conditional distribution, i.e. an epistemic property, not a glitch.
- **Kalai, Nachum, Vempala, Zhang, "Why Language Models Hallucinate", arXiv 2509.04664, 2025-09 (FETCHED)**. Reframes: hallucinations are classification errors on arbitrary facts under "natural statistical pressures", and evaluation design ("language models are optimized to be good test-takers, and guessing when uncertain improves test performance") trains guessing over abstention. Moves part of the epistemology from the corpus to the benchmark economy.
- Follow-ups seen at search tier only (not fetched): Velegkas et al., "(Im)possibility of Automated Hallucination Detection"; Suzuki and He, "Hallucinations are inevitable but can be made statistically rare"; "Ungrounded Divergence: A Philosophical Framework for LLM Hallucination" (SSRN, finite-corpus extrapolation via Kripke's rule-following paradox).

## Exact-phrase provenance checks (run 2026-09-28)

- Search 1, query `"closed-corpus epistemology" language model hallucination`: no verbatim hit for the phrase. Nearest conceptual matches: an SSRN philosophical framework (finite corpus, many compatible extrapolations) and a Springer "Hallucination as epistemic pollution" article.
- Search 2, query `"closed corpus" OR "closed-corpus" hallucination epistemic language model knowledge cutoff`: "closed-corpus" appears as a descriptive adjective in 2025-2026 material (a philarchive paper on NotebookLM's "closed-corpus architecture" as anti-hallucination design; a Deep Research survey's "static, closed-corpus search"), but never as "closed-corpus epistemology" and never as a coined theory term.
- "statistical grammar": no paper found coining the triple "statistical grammar, semantics from syntax, no world" as a formulation; it exists only as a compression of the Bender-Koller / parrot / Chomsky line above.
- Conclusion: both compact phrases in the ask are folklore-grade compressions, not published coinages, on the evidence reachable from these searches. The component claims are formally published at the venues named above.
