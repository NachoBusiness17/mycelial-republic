## 1. Three different things get called "water computing"

The field splits into three lineages that are usually blurred together. Wikipedia's Fluidics article (fetched 2026-09-28) defines the term: fluidics, or fluidic logic, is "the use of a fluid to perform analog or digital operations similar to those performed with electronics", and the term is normally reserved for devices with no moving parts, so hydraulic cylinders and spool valves do not count. That definition separates:

1. Hydraulic analog computation: continuous water levels and flows as the state variables of a differential equation (Lukyanov's water integrator, the MONIAC). The computer IS the plumbing.
2. Fluidic logic: discrete gating done by jets deflecting jets, with no moving parts (Horton vortex amplifiers, bubble logic). The plumbing switches itself.
3. Microfluidic and droplet computing: bits as discrete droplets or bubbles in chip channels, the modern research front (Prakash and Gershenfeld onward).

The same article gives the physics ceiling up front: a fluidic amplifier's bandwidth is in the low kilohertz range, so systems built from them are "quite slow compared to electronic devices". Every era of this field lives downstream of that fact.

## 2. Era 1: hydraulic analog computers, what was actually built

**The Water Integrator (USSR, 1936).** Wikipedia's Water Integrator article (oldid 1336078662, fetched 2026-09-28): built in the Soviet Union in 1936 by Vladimir Sergeevich Lukyanov; it functioned "by careful manipulation of water through a room full of interconnected pipes and pumps"; water level in chambers, precise to fractions of a millimeter, represented stored numbers, and flow rate between them represented mathematical operations; it solved inhomogeneous differential equations. Timeline from the same article: in the 1930s it was the only computer in the USSR for solving partial differential equations; a modular redesign came in 1941; two-dimensional and three-dimensional versions followed; standardized units were developed at the NIISCHETMASH institute in 1949 to 1955; the Ryazan plant of calculating and analytical machines began serial production in 1955 under the factory brand IGL (Lukyanov Hydraulic Integrator). Units were delivered to Czechoslovakia, Poland, Bulgaria and China. Documented engineering uses: design of the Karakum Canal in the 1940s and construction of the Baikal-Amur Mainline in the 1970s. Water analog computers ran in Soviet geology, mine construction, metallurgy and rocket production into the 1980s; two machines are kept in the Polytechnic Museum in Moscow. The article's external links also preserve a 1953 collection of US Army Corps of Engineers technical reports, written by MIT's civil and sanitary engineering department under contract to build a hydraulic computer for diffusion-type PDEs, i.e. the West independently built the same class of machine.

**The MONIAC / Phillips Machine (LSE, 1949).** Wikipedia's Phillips Machine article (fetched 2026-09-28): "an analogue computer which uses fluidic logic to model the workings of an economy", also called the Monetary National Income Analogue Computer and the Financephalograph, the name MONIAC riffing on money plus ENIAC. Created in 1949 by the New Zealand economist Bill Phillips while a student at LSE, prototyped with Walter Newlyn in a garage in Croydon at a cost of GBP 400 (about GBP 12,000 in 2025 terms), built partly from scrounged war surplus parts including bits of old Lancaster bombers, with Leeds University advancing GBP 100 toward the prototype. Roughly 2 m tall, 1.2 m wide, 1 m deep: transparent plastic tanks and pipes on a wooden board, a treasury tank at the top, colored water as money, taps for spending on health and education, pumping back to the treasury for taxation with pumping speed as the tax rate, a surplus-balances tank draining on savings and filling on investment income, imports and exports as water leaving or entering the model, all controlled automatically through floats, counterweights, electrodes and cords. Phillips and Newlyn found it could be calibrated to 2 percent accuracy. At least twelve machines were built and donated or sold to organizations worldwide; the Fluidics article says twelve to fourteen; as of 2023 several are in working order.

**FLODAC (1964).** The Fluidics article's Uses section: "The FLODAC Computer was built in 1964 as a proof of concept fluid-based digital computer." That is the entire sentence the source gives, so the honest statement is: a fluid-based digital computer was demonstrated in 1964, and this session did not retrieve its design details (one line, cited as such).

## 3. Era 2: fluidic logic, the jet-deflection decade

History per the Fluidics article: in 1920 Nikola Tesla patented the valvular conduit (Tesla valve), a leaky fluidic diode whose diodicity is frequency dependent, usable in a fluid full-wave rectifier. The field proper starts in 1957, when Billy M. Horton of the Harry Diamond Laboratories (later part of the US Army Research Laboratory) realized he could redirect flue gases with a small bellows and proposed stream interaction theory: amplification by deflecting a stream of fluid with a different stream. In 1959 Horton, R. E. Bowles and Ray Warren built a family of working vortex amplifiers out of soap, linoleum and wood; the published results triggered a surge of interest in "fluid amplification" that lasted through the 1960s, and in 1961 the three were among the first 27 recipients of the Army Research and Development Achievement Award.

The gate inventory from the same article: logic gates can be built with water powering the gating function, orientation dependent; an OR gate is two merged pipes; a NOT gate is stream A deflecting a supply stream to produce NOT-A; AND and XOR are drawn in the article's diagram, and XOR doubles as an inverter since A XOR 1 = NOT-A. The amplifier itself: supply fluid (air, water or hydraulic fluid) enters at the bottom, control ports C1 or C2 deflect the stream to exit via O1 or O2, and the control stream can be much weaker than the deflected one, which is gain; fluidic oscillators play the flip-flop role; the article also lists a fluidic triode, fluid diodes, and hydraulic circuits including one with no electronic counterpart.

Where it survived, per the Uses section: fluidic components in some hydraulic and pneumatic systems including some automotive automatic transmissions; no-moving-part valves in some anaesthetic machines; fluidic oscillators in pressure-triggered 3D printable emergency ventilators during COVID-19; fluidic amplifiers generating ultrasound for nondestructive testing; a fluidic sound amplification system demonstrated in a synagogue where electronic amplification is religiously barred; logic-enabled textiles researched for wearables; pulsating consumer products from toy spray guns to shower heads and hot tub jets. The military-aerospace arc: fluidic injection researched for aircraft control via circulation control (wing trailing edge slots replacing ailerons and flaps) and thrust vectoring (nozzle injection replacing swiveling parts), with tests showing jet exhaust deflection up to 15 degrees and claimed advantages of lower mass, up to 50 percent lower cost, up to 15 percent less drag, lower inertia, mechanical simplicity, and reduced radar cross section for stealth. The original niche also still holds: the article notes fluidics is used mostly where electronic digital logic is unreliable, i.e. high electromagnetic interference or ionizing radiation environments.

## 4. Era 3: droplets as bits, the microfluidic research front

**The founding modern result.** Prakash M, Gershenfeld N, "Microfluidic bubble logic", Science 2007 Feb 9;315(5813):832-835, doi 10.1126/science.1136907, PMID 17289994 (abstract fetched verbatim from PubMed efetch, receipt in _sw14_fetch2.py output): "We demonstrate universal computation in an all-fluidic two-phase microfluidic system. Nonlinearity is introduced into an otherwise linear, reversible, low-Reynolds number flow via bubble-to-bubble hydrodynamic interactions. A bubble traveling in a channel represents a bit..." Demonstrated: AND/OR/NOT gates, a toggle flip-flop, a ripple counter, timing restoration, a ring oscillator, and an electro-bubble modulator, showing "the nonlinearity, gain, bistability, synchronization, cascadability, feedback, and programmability required for scalable universal computation", pitched as on-chip process control integrating chemistry and computation. The Fluidics article adds the deep reason bubble logic works: bubbles are neither produced nor destroyed in a gate operation, so gates conserve bit count, the direct analog of billiard-ball computer gates. A companion comment ran in the same issue (Science 315:776, doi 10.1126/science.1138325).

**The review layer.** Azizbeigi, Zamani Pedram, Sanati-Nezhad, "Microfluidic-based processors and circuits design", Scientific Reports 11:10985, 2021, doi 10.1038/s41598-021-90485-z (PubMed abstract fetched): droplets as bits of data, with flow through channel networks steered by stimulation of movements, trajectories and interaction timing to form logical microfluidic circuits that mimic electrical logic.

**Around it, the decade's build-out** (Crossref records fetched via _sw14_fetch.py, metadata grade): Lab on a Chip 2009, "Microfluidic pneumatic logic circuits and digital pneumatic microprocessors for integrated microfluidic systems", doi 10.1039/b904354c; Advanced Science 2020, "Pressure-Driven Two-Input 3D Microfluidic Logic Gates", doi 10.1002/advs.202070007; Soft Matter 2025, "Microfluidic Memristive Oscillators as Universal Logic Gates for Neuromorphic Computing", doi 10.1039/d5sm00601e (peer-review records visible; the memristive framing is the field's current move to couple microfluidics with neuromorphic vocabulary).

**2023 to 2026 state of the art, abstracts fetched:** Science Robotics 2023, "Desktop fabrication of monolithic soft robotic devices with embedded fluidic control circuits", doi 10.1126/scirobotics.adg3792: most soft robots are pneumatically actuated and need added electronic pumps and microcontrollers even for simple functions; desktop FFF 3D printing now embeds the fluidic control circuitry monolithically in the robot body, which is fluidic logic dissolving into the actuator. Advanced Materials 2026 (38(26):e18042, doi 10.1002/adma.202518042, PMID 41952608, Peking University and NUS): oxide-mediated continuous electrowetting of gallium-based liquid metal droplets gives programmable flow field control, demonstrated "bubble-free pumping, reconfigurable fluidic logic, and sustained performance exceeding 19 h", aimed at intelligent milli-microfluidic systems for adaptive thermal management. Also on record: Zhang et al., Phys Rev E 78:066305 (2008), PMID 19256943, electrorheological carrier fluids digitally steering microdroplet generation with millisecond response, explicitly for "microfluidic logic computation".

## 5. Water as a reservoir computer

Fernando C, Sojakka S, "Pattern Recognition in a Bucket", ECAL 2003, Lecture Notes in Artificial Intelligence 2801, pp 588-597, doi 10.1007/978-3-540-39432-7_63, 279 citations per the Springer page (abstract fetched verbatim, receipt in _sw14_fetch3.py output): waves on the surface of water used as the medium of a "Liquid State Machine" that preprocesses inputs so a simple perceptron can solve XOR and do speech recognition; wave interference provides nonlinear parallel computation on simultaneous sensory inputs; temporal stimulation patterns are converted into spatial patterns of water waves on which a linear discrimination is made. The authors' argument is that where Maass' Liquid State Machine needs fine tuning of spiking network parameters, water gives strong local interactions, time-dependent spread to distant areas, inherent input stability and high complexity "for free".

## 6. Synthesis: state of the field

What has been built, summed: (a) analog hydraulic equation solvers in production for half a century (IGL serial production 1955, Soviet use into the 1980s, 2 percent accurate economic simulator adopted by a dozen institutions); (b) a moving-parts-free digital logic family that reached a proof-of-concept computer (FLODAC 1964) and lives on in radiation/EMI-hard control, aviation flow control, and medical devices; (c) a microfluidic digital-logic literature that since 2007 has demonstrated the complete universal-computation checklist (gain, bistability, cascadability, feedback, counter, flip-flop, oscillator) at chip scale, now converging with soft robotics (embedded control circuits, Science Robotics 2023) and neuromorphic framing (microfluidic memristive oscillators, Soft Matter 2025) and liquid metal reconfigurability (2026); (d) physical reservoir computing in plain water (2003 onward).

Why it lost and why it persists: the loss is speed, one line in the source record, fluidic amplifier bandwidths in the low kilohertz range versus electronics; the persistence is everywhere electronics is the wrong material, ionizing radiation and EMI fields, no-moving-part medical valves, soft bodies where silicon pumps are dead weight, chemistry where the computation medium and the processed medium should be the same fluid, and education where the computation is visible. The through-line from Lukyanov's 1936 chamber levels to the 2026 liquid-metal logic gate is unchanged: put the state variable in the fluid and the physics does the algebra for free.
