## 1. The finding that organizes everything: a market with a pricing desk and zero trades

The estate built a genuine price system for operator attention and then never traded on it. The ranker accepts five signals (`ALLOWED_SIGNALS = {"useful", "wallpaper", "pin", "mute", "acted"}`, `attention_ranker.py:17`), prices them (+4 per useful, -8 per wallpaper at `:241`; pin forces P1 and floor 80 at `:234-236`; mute forces P5 and zero at `:237-239`), persists them as `attention_feedback.v1` events (`:69-79`), and the dashboard exposes the POST endpoint (`rest.py:5242`). Yet the ledger file does not exist. Meanwhile the one surface that actually spends operator attention on this machine, `memory/attention.md`, is by direct census 100% wallpaper: 125 notifications of the same shape, written by the biographer (`biography.py:511`), read at every seat boot and by at least four token-paying consumers (the 600-, 3,000-, and 15,000-byte reservations above, plus AGENTS.md's "When a specialist seat is used" bullet listing `memory/attention.md` as a first-read file). The priced market is idle; the unpriced market runs at full throughput. That inversion, not the score formula, is the central fact any attention market here must fix.

Information-theoretically the feed is even worse than noisy: the 125 headers are identical strings, so each marginal notification carries zero conditional bits. N duplicates of one message do not carry N messages. Every seat that reads attention.md pays context bytes for a signal whose entropy is zero past the first occurrence.

## 2. The currency

Three distinct things get called "attention" and the design must split them:

- **Glimpses (impressions):** one render of an item into operator view. The base flow, currently unrecorded: `build_ranked_attention()` (`:228-254`) returns up to 12 ranked items per call and writes nothing. There is no impression ledger anywhere in the module. Without issuance records you cannot compute conversion, so the estate's own census-denominator law (B2-216: a count is a snapshot of one tree at one moment, never a constant) is violated in advance for the market's most important count.
- **Trades (signals):** the five verbs. These are the only recorded expressions of preference, and there are zero of them.
- **Settlements (acted):** the signal that proves attention converted into work. Notably, `acted` has **no price effect**: the branch structure at `:233-242` handles pin, mute, and the useful/wallpaper count delta, and `acted` only rides along in the stored `signals[-5:]` display (`:242`). The market records proof of value delivered and pays nothing for it.

Proposed currency: **regret-denominated attention units.** The numeraire is not minutes (operator minutes are heterogeneous: a P0 handoff blocker and a roadmap glance are not the same commodity) but avoided regret, i.e., the cost of the worst outcome the item could have prevented. This matches what the code already believes: the formula at `:89-93` (`raw = 3*actionability + 2.5*impact + 2*urgency + 1.5*centrality + 1.2*novelty + uncertainty - 1.5*redundancy - wallpaper_cost`, normalized by 11.2, the all-ones ceiling) is a linear regret proxy, and the hard bands are pure regret floors (a P0 `repo:handoff` at `:133` exists precisely because an unrestorable handoff state is unbounded regret). Concretely: log every impression to `memory/attention_impressions.jsonl` (item_id, band, score, rank position, generated_at). Position-weighted conversion (acted at rank 1 is worth more than acted at rank 9) becomes measurable, and the feedback ledger becomes a market with both sides of the book.

## 3. What determines price

The current price system is a **planner's administered schedule with an unused spot market bolted on**. The weights at `:90-91` are fixed coefficients chosen by the designer, not discovered from outcomes. Hayek (1945, "The Use of Knowledge in Society") is the exact frame: prices are valuable because they condense distributed knowledge no planner holds; a fixed weight vector is central planning, and the only discovery channel the system has (operator feedback) was never opened. The design should keep the planner layer where it is load-bearing and open discovery where it is not:

- **The constitution layer (non-tradeable).** Hard bands (`:95-96`, policy string at `:250`: "feedback may reorder ordinary items but cannot hide hard rules") are rights of way, the estate's code-as-law pattern applied to attention. Keep them. Today's live clear returned six items, all hard-rule: pin and mute were structurally powerless on this feed because their guards (`not item["hard_rule"]` at `:234,237`) exclude every item shown. On today's estate, the soft market has nothing soft to price. The fix is not more feedback UI; it is generating a soft tail worth pricing (see §4).
- **The spot layer.** The +4/-8 asymmetry at `:241` is already the best-designed price in the file: wallpaper is taxed at double the reward for useful. That is a Pigouvian tax on a negative externality (noise imposed on every future feed render) and also loss aversion (Kahneman and Tversky 1979) encoded as policy, whether or not it was deliberate. Keep and extend: make the tax **organ-persistent**. A wallpaper signal currently prices only the individual item; the organ that emitted it (repository, worker, guidance, roadmap, and the unpriced biography surface) pays nothing. Aggregate signals per organ kind into a rolling reputation, and add it to each organ's future `wallpaper_cost` terms. This is the estate's own correction-ledger culture (operator-corrections, the failure ratchet) turned on its own organs: debt registered by name, never silently forgiven.
- **The memory window.** `_feedback()` reads only the last 500 rows (`:49`). Count-based forgetting means a burst of feedback erases the market's memory of older trades; time-based decay (a half-life, e.g., 30 days) is the honest forgetting curve. The window should forget by age, not by volume.
- **The settlement price.** Pay `acted`. When an item the operator acted on was rendered, the emitting organ should realize credit (a score boost for future items of that organ, decaying), closing the loop from attention spent to value delivered. `acted` is currently a free option: the one signal that cannot lie (it required work) is the one the market ignores.
- **The cost of computing the market.** Measured this session: 4.903s per clear (vs 0.049s import), dominated by the four `_collect()` snapshots (`:115-225`), consistent with the s2-11 boot-census finding that `orchestrator.list_tasks` glob-reads thousands of task JSONs (~1.6s alone). A market that costs ~5s per render must not be recomputed per dashboard hit: snapshot `ranked_attention.v1` to `memory/` on an interval and let surfaces read the snapshot. Machine time is the estate's second scarce resource; the driving ladder exists because of it.

## 4. Allocation across competing organs

The ranker already allocates by construction, and the construction is a quota system wearing a scoring costume: at most 3 failed-worker items (`failed[:3]`, `:166`), at most 4 active-task monitors (`active[:4]`, `:177`), the repository organ holds a permanent P0 right of way (`:133`), guidance inbox P1 (`:203`), roadmap P2 (`:219`). Per-organ caps are correct and should be named for what they are: antitrust. No organ may saturate the operator's feed; the biographer's 125-identical-headers file is exactly what happens to an organ with no cap.

Three allocation defects fall out of the census:

1. **Success is priced into invisibility.** `repo:ready` is the only soft item the collector can emit, and it is constructed to lose: actionability .05, redundancy .5, wallpaper_cost .4 (`:142-143`). Reasonable for a nag, but it means the soft market's total supply is one permanently suppressed item. The estate under-generates priced candidates: valuable-but-not-blocking organs (improve scout results, brief deltas, residual dig edges) never enter the book. Allocation across organs needs bidders before it needs prices.
2. **The biggest spender is outside the market.** `memory/attention.md` consumes operator context on every seat boot (AGENTS.md seat load list; `token_economy.py:50` reserves it 15,000 bytes; `lanes.py:293` reads 3,000; `context_pack.py:620` clips 600) and it is 100% biographer session-record wallpaper by census. Worse, two organs treat its *existence* as signal: `ghost.py:998` checks `is_file()` (tautologically true while any writer lives) and `chronicle.py:42` cites it as "proof". The market design must annex this surface: session-record notifications belong to the biographer organ, enter the book as P4/P5 candidates at most, and the operator-facing file should be a rendered projection of `build_ranked_attention()`, not an append-only log. Reclaiming its 15,000-byte budget line is the single largest immediate dividend.
3. **Maintenance is excluded by keyword, an arbitrary ration.** Failed tasks tagged soak/self-test/heal/smoke/pytest/[improve] are filtered from the failure channel (`:158-164`). Correct instinct (expected failures are not regrets), wrong mechanism: a keyword list is a price ceiling of zero that any future organ can dodge by renaming. The principled rule is the estate's own empty-claim law (RIB_GAME_CLAIMS_DESK): a failure enters the book only if it carries evidence of unmet expectation, not because of what its tag says.

The allocation rule that falls out: operator attention is a **daily non-storable budget** (Goldhaber 1997: attention cannot be saved; today's unspent operator attention is destroyed, it does not carry to tomorrow). P0/P1 are served unconditionally (rights of way, already true in code). P2 is rationed to the day's budget: the estate's real scarce input, per its own burn doctrine, is the one human; everything the fleet can verify itself (RIB verification gates, trust-bytes law) must never be billed to that human. The `[priority]`/`[L2]` lane tags (`configs/lanes.yaml`, via AGENTS.md) are already an escalation market with published tolls; the pile habit (contribute a state summary before leaving) is already a toll paid in the other direction. The design completes the symmetry: organs bid attention upward only by paying evidence downward.

## 5. Simon's ledger, closed

Simon (1971, "Designing Organizations for an Information-Rich World") stated the conservation law: a wealth of information creates a poverty of attention. This estate is the reductio. It burns machine surplus by the terabyte-hour (burn_reset, flash50, 50-wide fan-outs at 109.4M tok/h per the fan-out width law) while its human attention is inventoried in three files a specialist seat reads first, one of which is 40,441 bytes of zero-entropy duplicates. Pirolli and Card (1999) would say the feed's information scent (`rank_reason`, `:106-109`) is good where it exists; Coase (1937) would say the estate is a firm whose organs are allocated by planner, and the firm's transfer prices (the score formula) are sound but its internal market (feedback) never opened. The thermodynamic statement is simply: attention is the only non-substitutable input the estate has, it is currently billed chiefly for zero-entropy wallpaper, and the machinery to reprice it exists, is wired to the dashboard, and has recorded zero trades. The design's first deliverable is therefore not a new mechanism but a first trade: annex attention.md, log impressions, pay `acted`, and let the ledger exist. A market you can prove has never traded is at least falsifiable; that is more than the current file can say.
