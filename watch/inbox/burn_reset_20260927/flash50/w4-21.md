## 2. Findings

**F1. The estate is 99.86% landfill by mass, and that is its design.** All enforced order (commits, seals, ratchet pins, gate law) fits in 8.7 MB. The other 6.39 GB is escrow: receipts, tapes, instruments, censuses nobody re-read. Landauer's asymmetry is the reason: appends are logically reversible and physically near-free; erasure is the only fundamentally priced act. The estate maximizes cheap ops (every ledger is jsonl, every claim carries a sha256) and rations expensive ones (prune, VOID, freeze are named, operator-gated acts; `githooks/pre-push:4-7`). The 736:1 ratio is not waste; it is the estate refusing to pay Landauer's price for forgetting, and paying disk instead. Disk is ~free; attention is not; the design is correct about its own economics.

**F2. A hand-written claim now has a twelve-receipt defense budget.** The census sentence in AGENTS.md ("2,303 tests collected / 352 files, 09-27 census") is defended by two dedicated tools (`census_drift_patrol.py`, split-born `census_collect.py` under the 400-line cap, B2-248/B2-365), a frozen-grammar regex that owns the claim shape (`:117-119`), an ownership law forbidding fixture auto-sync (`:97-116`, B2-274), a self-test module, a pytest pin file, a pre-push arm with a fail-closed exit-class split (`githooks/pre-push:76-102`, B2-277), a daily 08:30 cron, and at least twelve named receipts (B2-69 through B2-365). One sentence, twelve court cases. The estate internalized the externality.

## 3. Answers

**Total affordable order.** The compute inequality is slack: the full gate chain costs ~100 s of local silicon per push and ~1% of token income at 50-wide boot tax; nothing is compute-bound. The binding constraints are (a) delivery: 1,800 + 2,800 + 1,200 chars of law/rib/doctrine per seat means at most ~48 of 307 compiled laws ride at any moment; (b) triage: standing reds (4 tonight) consume the one input with no elastic supply; (c) the 7-day re-verification week, which caps how much decaying claim-mass can be carried before staleness compounds. Formally: O_max is the claim-graph whose (delivery bytes + triage looks + weekly re-derivation walls) fit inside (pack caps + one funded quota + one operator week). Tonight the estate holds ~941 named debts + 2,410 pins + 5,678 index events + 43 ribs against that bound, produces ~445 escrow files/h and ~187 index events per 43 minutes during a wave, and retires debt at 0/day (no claim has ever carried an expiry date; the rot mechanism at `violation_ratchet.py:70-71` has never fired because `expires` is set on zero of 941 claims). Production is winning. The estate is not approaching its compute limit; it is past its *servicing* limit for anything that is not byte-re-derivable or change-scoped, which is why those two disciplines are already the house style (`--changed` gates, sha256 everything, name-by-acceptance).

**Where the entropy is exported.** Four exhausts, all measured tonight: (1) local heat: ~127 s of gate CPU during this one measurement window, 11.3 s per deep collect, the janitors' cycles; (2) rented datacenter thermodynamics: the 109.4M tok/h flux and its 429/1302 throttles, funded quota as fuel gauge; (3) sediment: 8.7 MB of immortal order plus 6.39 GB of escrow growing at ~9 MB/h tonight, plus frozen docs and tombstones (the estate's only true erasures); (4) the operator: every WARN-only arm and every standing red is disorder re-routed into human attention, the one exhaust with no capacity meter. The WARN-only convention (`githooks/pre-push` uses it on 5 of 10 blocks) is best read thermodynamically: it converts blocking failures into attention debt, trading a hard stop for an unc serviced liability. Four standing reds says the liability is coming due.

**Is the estate a dissipative structure?** Yes, with three refinements beyond the earlier verdict. First, it is hibernation-tolerant: sediment persists without flux, so it does not die when the gradient stops, it *rots*, and its patrols are the immune response to its own dormancy (the 12-day census ride is the canonical infection). Second, its autocatalysis is not emergent but mandated: G2 passoff and the FAIL-row citation law make reproduction a legal requirement, so its order chain has genotype as well as metabolism. Third, its Rayleigh number is set by a calendar: the 7-day constant means the structure's convection cells are sized to one operator week. The final twist: it keeps books on itself. This document and its 16:20 sibling are the estate computing its own thermodynamics, a dissipative structure whose fluctuation-dissipation theorem includes self-auditing. The prediction that follows: the next organ it grows will not be another gate (variety there is saturated; two enforcing gates sit red with nobody harmed) but an expiry engine: scheduled decay for accepted debt and standing reds, so that order which cannot afford its upkeep is retired on a clock instead of accumulating as invisible liability. The `expires` field already exists. It has been waiting, unused, since the ratchet landed.
