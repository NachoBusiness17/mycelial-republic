## 1. What the feed actually is: a flooded ring with an inverted eviction policy

`memory/attention.md` is nominally the organ named for attention. Measured, it is a ring buffer of about 124 records at roughly 323 bytes each, and 100 percent of its current contents are the lowest-value template the estate emits: a positive notification that a session narrative exists. Meanwhile the two highest-value alerts fired this week, the COLLECT-DRIFT and the fleet task doctor hit logged on 09-26 and 09-28, are gone. The mechanism is exact and worth stating precisely, because it is a design defect and not bad luck.

Two ordering conventions coexist on one file. The biography writer and the daemon attention writer prepend newest-first and behead the tail (`mag/biography.py:526`: `att.write_text(note + prev[:40000])`; `mag/act.py:100-103` prepends and slices `body[:50000]`). The improve probe and the 08:30 patrol append at the bottom (`mag/improve.py:535` opens mode "a"; the patrol uses Add-Content). On a prepend ring with a byte cap, the bottom is the eviction zone. So the writers that emit the most (hundreds of session pings per day against a 2,070-per-day narrative rate) pin their output at the head with guaranteed retention, while the writers that emit the least and matter most (patrol incidents, a few per week) place their output first in line for the guillotine. Severity anti-correlates with retention probability. The feed pays interest to noise and defaults on signal. At the measured arrival rate the ring turns over in hours, so a patrol alert appended at 17:20 survived roughly until the next handful of session summaries.

Three aggravating defects ride on the same mechanism. First, the behead slices at a character offset, not a block boundary: the file's last line today ends mid-identifier (`mag-agent-orc-t0`, verified by tail), and every consumer that splits on `\n---+\n` (`mag/seats.py:87`, `mag/chronicle.py:20`) routinely ingests a headless half-record. Second, the session-record template carries no timestamp (`mag/biography.py:517-523`), so the operator cannot tell how old a ping is or whether the feed is even alive. Third, the feed is polluted with synthetic traffic: at least one record in the production feed is a pytest artifact (`mag-agent-_pytest_agent_wd`, attention.md lines 303-309; 6 pytest matches in the file), meaning the test suite writes into the operator's attention surface.

## 2. The estate already has an attention budget, and it regulates a dead channel

The ask asks to design an attention budget. One exists. `mag/daemon.py:91-99` checks `_attention_over_cap` before every attention action; `mag/daemon.py:166-176` reads `watch/attention_count_day.txt` and compares against `max_attention_per_day`; `configs/mag.yaml:5` sets 48, raised from 8 after the operator found the idle cap-reached state felt broken. Three things are wrong with it, and they are the three classic failure modes of any budget.

First, it taxes the wrong transactions. The counter increments only inside the daemon's attention action path (`mag/act.py:110-117`). The channels that actually fire daily, the biography session pings and the patrol appends, bypass the counter entirely. A budget that levies only on a channel emitting zero traffic is not a budget; it is a monument. The counter's own bytes prove the point: `2026-08-07 48`, fifty-two days stale, while `logs/mag.jsonl` shows the daemon loop alive as of Sep 28 20:14.

Second, it was inflated under pressure. 8 felt broken, so it became 48, which is the whole history of monetary debasement in one config line. A cap on the wrong axis (raw count, no severity classes) does not survive contact with a flood; it gets raised until it is meaningless. The config comment is the operator's own testimony that count-rationing without classing fails.

Third, the enforcement is misdirected even where it fires. `mag/act.py:110-118` writes the alert to the file first and only suppresses the push when over cap. That is actually the correct primitive (records are cheap, interruptions are the scarce spend), but the push it throttles is `_toast_optional`, which does not toast anything: it overwrites the single-slot flag file `watch/attention_flag.txt` (last-writer-wins, `mag/act.py:244-245`), and the repo-wide sweep shows nothing reads that file. The interruption channel is a queue with buffer size 1 and no consumer. Budget enforcement, such as it is, spends against a void.

Absent from every effective surface: `memory/attention.md` itself, the file named for the resource it fails to ration. The estate's escalation packets, the messages that by definition accompany scarce operator attention, each carry 3,000 characters of session-record spam as a prefix tax.

## 4. The cost model of one alert

An alert's cost is not its bytes on disk. Itemized, using only measured numbers:

- Write cost: negligible, about 323 bytes for a session record.
- Eviction externality: in a class-blind ring, the displacer pays nothing and the displaced loses everything. The session-record flood evicted both of this week's patrol alerts. Every cheap alert imposes an unpriced externality on every expensive one.
- Read tax: recurring and levied on every consumer on every read (section 3). At the observed spam ratio this tax purchases approximately zero bits: the head of the ring is a constant, and a constant has entropy near zero. The estate would never accept a retrieval index that returns the same document for every query; the attention feed is that index for the operator.
- Credibility depreciation: each spam read lowers the operator's prior that the next alert matters. After 125 of 125 template reads, the rational update is to stop reading the feed, which is what the reverse funnel shows has already happened. This is the expensive cost because it does not decay: it must be paid back by a streak of true positives, and the eviction mechanism guarantees the feed cannot produce them.
- Dead-channel opportunity cost: the one channel designed to cost the operator nothing unless urgent (`watch/attention_flag.txt`) is written to no reader. For contrast, the s2-11 boot census measured this estate taxing its own agents 1.9 to 3.0 seconds per Write/Edit call in hooks. The machine rations its agents' attention per tool call; the operator's attention, the scarcest input in the system, is unrationed and unfenced.

## 5. Attention bankruptcy, defined and staged

Attention bankruptcy is the steady state in which every alert's expected value to the reader is negative (read cost exceeds information content) and the reader's trust prior has collapsed far enough that genuine positives are discounted along with the spam. It is the alert-system analog of a currency losing both redeemability and the public's willingness to hold it.

The estate is at stage two of three. Stage one, flood: 125/125 template alerts at the head. Stage two, signal eviction: both patrol alerts this week delivered into the guillotine zone and destroyed, unrecoverable because the file is gitignored. Stage three, silence as health, has not arrived but is one step away, and the early sign is already visible: the patrol's runbook routes the operator around the organ to raw logs. Routing around an alert organ is rational for the operator and fatal for the organ; from then on, any alert that genuinely matters will be read only by accident. The fleet task doctor (patrol script lines 121-157) exists precisely because an instrument that never runs is invisible; an alert organ that is never read is the same disease wearing the operator's face.

## 6. The budget design

Seven mechanisms, each anchored to something that already exists in the tree, none requiring new doctrine from scratch.

1. Split record from interruption, and class the spend. The primitive already lives in `mag/act.py:110-118`: write always, gate only the push. Formalize three classes. Sev1 (patrol cannot-verify exit 2, secret exposure, chain break): push and unconditional retention. Sev2 (drift, gap, task-doctor hits): coalesced rows, one open row per class per day, updated in place, a bounded number open at once. Sev3 (session record ready, probe clean): never enters the operator viewport; it belongs in a dated append-only ledger, and the pattern already exists in `watch/inbox/DATED_METERS.jsonl`, where the meter patrol records RECEIPT and NOBS rows without pushing anything.
2. One ring, one convention, class quotas instead of a byte guillotine. Every writer prepends newest-first, and the cap keeps whole blocks, never slicing mid-record as `prev[:40000]` does today. Retention is by class: sev1 and sev2 are never evicted by sev3 volume; only sev3 ring-buffers.
3. Make the flood pay rent. The 125 identical session records compress to one rolling line: N session records since T, latest S. That single line replaces 40,441 bytes of constant text with the roughly five bits of information the template actually carries (a session id). This one change converts the feed from 100 percent noise to mostly capacity for signal.
4. Price alerts in operator seconds, not bytes, and meter them. The estate meters tokens (`logs/usage.jsonl`) and even metered its own hook tax per tool call (s2-11), but nothing meters operator attention. A budget cannot be enforced on an unmetered resource. Minimum viable meter: every alert write records class, target surface, and an estimated read cost; a daily rollup gives operator-seconds spent per class, the denominator the fleet currently guesses at.
5. Numerics of the budget. Income is the operator's daily reading capacity; the morning stack alone fields nine automated surfaces (02:30 through 09:00 plus a 5-minute watchdog). The patrol's own docs concede the drift class is expected on active days (script lines 16-22; measured +31 same-day in B2-246, +107 on 09-28). So expected-on-active-days signals must coalesce, not re-fire: budget roughly three open sev2 rows per class-day, sev1 unbounded because incidents are rare by definition, sev3 zero push. The 48-per-day cap failed because it rationed count without class; classing is what makes a cap hold.
6. Bankruptcy tripwires, all generalizations of instruments that already run at 08:30. A feed-composition heartbeat: one daily meta-row stating counts by class, so a spam flood is itself detectable (the watchdog applied to the watchdog, the same move as the fleet task doctor watching Mag* tasks). A retention canary: append a marked row today, assert it still exists tomorrow; this mechanical check would have caught the eviction defect on 09-27 without anyone noticing missing alerts by memory. A precision ratchet, modeled on `configs/violation_baseline.json`: alerts the operator dismisses as noise register by name, repeat classes demote automatically, and demotion is never done by silence.
7. Repair or delete the push path. Repo-wide, `watch/attention_flag.txt` has no reader (writer `mag/act.py:244`, a test of that writer, archaeology copies; nothing else). Either wire it to a live surface (the lab UI or the docker watchdog are natural pollers) or remove the channel. A budget that throttles deliveries to a dead mailbox is not thrift; it is loss with extra bookkeeping.

The model to copy already exists and is proven: the Clippy block. It is hand-rationed, every claim carries a receipt path, gates parse its grammar and fail closed, and a stale claim trips an alarm. That is an attention budget in miniature: admission is expensive, retention is policed, and the reader's trust is defended by machinery rather than by hope. The file feed never received that treatment. The design move is not to invent a new organ; it is to promote the clippy pattern into the feed and demote the feed's current contents into ledgers.

## 7. The deep principle

Herbert Simon's 1971 formulation remains the founding statement: a wealth of information creates a poverty of attention. The estate is a machine for manufacturing information wealth, receipts, briefs, residuals, chains, and it did so while leaving its one scarcest input unpriced. The measured irony is exact: the feed caps the severity of what it keeps by the frequency of who writes, so the more the fleet produces, the less the operator can know. An attention budget is not a device for limiting information; it is an eviction policy for memory, and eviction policy is where the estate's real values live. The verkle chain keeps what matters by making retention verifiable; the attention feed needs the same property: what reaches the operator's viewport must be provably the maximum-severity pending item, not the maximum-frequency writer's latest output.

The question the fleet is not asking: what is the exchange rate between operator seconds and receipts, and which lanes are unprofitable at that rate? Until attention has a meter the way tokens have one, every claim that "the operator should see this" is unfunded spending, and the estate will keep minting alerts the way it mints everything else, at industrial scale, into a mailbox no one holds the key to.
