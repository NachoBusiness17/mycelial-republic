## The Parable of the Twelve Windows

A grower dreamed of twelve windows.

In the dream they stood in a row on the bench of a workshop that did not exist yet, and inside each window a living floor had learned the weather. Not a picture of weather. Weather: damp arriving, warmth leaving, a spreading thread deciding where the food was. The windows were small and honest and each one held a different species of patience, and the grower understood, in the way of dreams, that twelve small honest things were stronger than one large impressive one.

The grower woke and did what careful people do. Drew one window. Drew it perfectly: the panes, the wire, the moisture that would be asked for and the moisture that would be refused, the question the window would be made to answer, and the rule that if the window could not answer better than a tossed coin, the window would be called what it was, which is a box of dirt, and the calling would be written down.

The drawing was good. Visitors came to see it. They praised the thickness of the panes and the fairness of the rule. One visitor copied the drawing. One visitor framed it. One visitor wrote a smaller drawing about the drawing. Nobody asked when the glass would hold water, because the drawing was so complete that the question felt already answered.

Seasons went around. The drawing grew a soft gray shape of handling. In the village ledger it sat filed under PROMISED, and the ledger, which does not flatter, kept the word. Twelve windows had been dreamed. One window had been drawn. Zero windows had been built. And the field, which owes nothing and answers nothing, went on not answering, exactly as a field does when nothing has been planted in it.

In time a debt collector came to the workshop. Collectors are not welcome and this one was correct. It did not shout. It set three lines on the bench beside the drawing.

First: a drawing is a promise the ground has not heard. Credit accrues to the thing that exists, and only to the part of the thing that answers when asked.

Second: the dream is owed exactly one window. Not twelve. Twelve is the harvest. One is the debt. A grower who plants none and drawings all is not a farmer with a large farm; it is a farmer with a large drawing.

Third: the rule the grower wrote against themselves is the strongest thing on the bench, and it stays armed even before the glass exists. The moment one window stands, it answers better than chance or it is called a box of dirt, in ink, by the grower's own hand. Until then the honest word in the ledger is DRAWING, and the debt is re-read aloud at every counting, because silence would let the drawing pass for the window.

The grower laughed, because it was that kind of correct. Then the grower did the only thing the parable respects. Gathered the panes. Asked the moisture. Planted the one.

The field was not impressed. The field never is. It answered the question or it did not, and either answer was worth more than the drawing, because it was an answer.
