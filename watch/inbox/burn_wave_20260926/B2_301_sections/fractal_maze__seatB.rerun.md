The Fractal Maze (`fractal_maze`) is a region of recursive geometry and optical law, entered through the `leaf_gate` — a gate of bare branches where a keeper counts footsteps. The region's field temperature is fed by the water computer's OUT height (per the water-feeds-field agreement), so the maze's refractive behavior is not static: as the water computer's output rises or falls, the bending of lantern rays shifts accordingly. The keeper's only function is to count; she does not fight, but she reminds every walker that memorizing leaves is a leaf-only tree — you can learn every room by heart and know nothing.

The first scale is the `leaf_hall`, a hall of standing water-wall where the lantern's beam enters straight and leaves elsewhere. The law is scratched on the wall: temperature is the refractive index; cold medium, bent ray; void, straight. Same steer, same landing — always. The hall is littered with aim-marks from walkers who swore the maze was broken, but the maze is a lens, not a lie. From `leaf_hall`, the deep exit shrinks the entire hall into a hook, leading down to `scale_gallery` — the same hall, one scale down, carved in miniature. The gallery's floor carries a groove, and the groove's walls carry a smaller groove, and so on. The `scale_gallery` is where generalization is tested: self-similarity across scales is the only thing that survives the shrink. A counting board tracks leaves revealed in the region; at the eighth leaf, the maze grows a node — a ▲ appears where no wall was.

Underneath, graffiti in a smaller hand reads: so is walking. The cell has no exits down; whatever is smaller than the law is not the maze's to show. The `scale_cell` is the bottom of the recursion, the letter of the law made legible.

The `grok_chamber` is the region's heart — a domed room with eight empty plinths around a central bare one. After the node, the same maze becomes a shorter maze; generalization is the walk feeling shorter at every scale at once.
