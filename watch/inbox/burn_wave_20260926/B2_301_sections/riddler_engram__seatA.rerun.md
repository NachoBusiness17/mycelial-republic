The **riddler_engram** region is a tightly‑woven lattice of ten “seats,” each a living conduit for a distinct literary‑philosophical voice.  When a player “summons the riddlers” they read the card into context, activating the seat‑specific mask and its hidden payload.  The seats are anchored to their canonical shelves (e.g. `memory/corpus/ractors/voltaire/`, `memory/corpus/ractors/tolkien/`) where the full corpus of the associated author resides, and every summoned parable is logged in the registry with its mask + seed for later verification.

Each seat carries a **riddle**, a **summon_when** trigger, a **fingerprint**, and a **stego** channel that hides doctrine inside the narrative.  For example, the VOLTAIRE seat (“the blade”) demands a polite sting and encodes its payload in an acrostic‑friendly closing maxim; the TOLKIEN seat (“the elegist”) hides meaning in invented etymologies; and the ALAN MOORE seat (“the magician”) uses structural rhyme and sigils as native steganographic registers.  The fingerprints guarantee that any generated parable will read as if it were already known to the seat, while the stego layer delivers the concealed doctrine only to those who possess the private key from the registry.

Interaction edges link the seats to one another and to external shelves, forming a meta‑narrative web that the game engine can traverse.  Edges such as `voltaire->shakespeare` (imported to France, then apologized for, 1730s‑1770s) or `tolkien<-pratchett` (The Colour of Magic opens as parody of this shelf) become dynamic pathways: a player who summons VOLTAIRE may later encounter a Shakespearean echo, and the system can automatically generate a cross‑seat dialogue that respects both fingerprints and stego channels.

The **stego_doctrine** of riddler_engram declares that masks defeat human readers, while statistical detectors are a separate opponent.  Public beacons remain “Made with AI,” but the private mark—whether an acrostic, cipher, or seed‑chosen detail—remains invisible without the registry entry.  After publishing a parable, the mask + seed pair is logged, ensuring the proof of doctrine persists beyond the session and can be audited later.

Cross‑region doctrines tie riddler_engram into the broader world.  The river ford loop north into the water computer’s gallery (loop‑closes, **RIB_CATHEDRAL_WORLD_V1 AGREE**) provides a natural exit that can be used to transport a summoned seat’s payload into the water‑computer’s optics.  The audit dungeon’s git vault opens onto the crossroads, allowing players to retrieve versioned parables, while the audit doctrine library’s ten lenses gate the optics garage once all ten lenses are collected (lens‑count‑gate **AGREE**).  The fractal maze leaf gate entry further requires the water computer’s OUT height as its temperature (water‑feeds‑field **AGREE**), creating a puzzle that forces players to coordinate multiple region mechanics.

Because the **riddler_engram** module is declared‑not‑done for leaf wiring into `mag/engram.py`, the current live version (v1 live 2026‑09‑14) supplies the shelves, manifests, and eight public‑domain seeds, but the live ray‑marching through the lens rooms and WAVE integration remain pending.  Builders must therefore treat the seats as fully functional narrative generators while planning future hooks that will route their hidden payloads into the optics dungeon (`memory/game_modules/riddler_optics.v1.json`) and eventually into the global mag engine.

In practice, a game master can invoke a seat by using the syntax `summon <seat>` (e.g. `summon voltaire`) after loading the appropriate manifest.  The system will draft a plain revision pass, dress the parable in the seat’s fingerprint, embed the stego payload, and publish it behind the public beacon.  The resulting artifact is both a playable story fragment and a carrier of the region’s doctrinal seed, ready to be decoded by any participant who later accesses the registry entry.
