The **Dusty Crossroads** is the central hub of the stub world, a weather‑worn intersection where four paths diverge. A solitary post stands in the middle, its notches worn smooth by countless travelers who have tallied journeys rather than kills. The post bears a cryptic riddle: *“what walks without feet when the map is true?”* – a clue that invites players to consider the invisible currents of the world and the hidden mechanics that bind its locations together.

To the **north** the road climbs toward the **Hill Shrine**, a ruin of broken stones where the wind whistles through empty niches. A shallow bowl for offerings sits at its base, its contents long pilfered. The shrine’s own notches echo those on the crossroads post, hinting that the same counter tracks progress across both sites. This subtle link encourages players to notice that actions in one locale may be reflected elsewhere.

Heading **east** leads to the **Market Ruin**, a once‑bustling bazaar now reduced to collapsed stalls and a single shaded awning. A hungry stray dog prowls the debris, its eyes fixed on a child’s satchel tucked beneath the awning. The dog’s encounter stats (5 HP, +2 attack, 1d4 damage) provide a low‑risk combat test for newcomers, while the satchel promises a small reward for those who succeed or a moral choice for those who spare the beast.

The **west** path descends to the **River Ford**, a shallow crossing strewn with stepping stones. Beyond the water, a glass door set impossibly into the riverbank hints at a keep of clear pipes, an otherworldly structure that can only be entered from the north. Upstream, a wisp of smoke drifts on the current; downstream, a faint, haunting song drifts on the wind. These sensory cues foreshadow the hidden gallery that lies beyond the ford.
