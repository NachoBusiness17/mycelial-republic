The 2d6 Adventure Stub is a compact, non-5e test region built on a light 2d6 system: any total of 7 or more succeeds at ordinary tasks, while combat uses opposed 2d6 plus modifiers. The player begins as the Wanderer, a curious traveler with 10 HP, AC 12, a +1 attack bonus, 1d6 damage, and a walking stick and waterskin. The region is a dusty crossroads of four ways, each leading to a distinct micro-location, and it is deliberately sparse to test how a minimal module can hook into a larger persistent world.

The central hub is the Dusty Crossroads, marked by a post carved with notches—a counter of journeys, not kills. A riddle is scratched into the post: "what walks without feet when the map is true?" The crossroads has four exits: north to the Hill Shrine, east to the Market Ruin, west to the River Ford, and a lantern-lit path to the Tavern Lantern (a hook that may connect to another module). The post's notches are echoed in the Hill Shrine, suggesting a single unseen hand has been tracking movement across the region.

North of the crossroads, the Hill Shrine is a broken stone circle with a wind-scoured bowl for coins long stolen. Its only exit is south back to the crossroads. The shrine's hooks note that the notches on its stones match those on the crossroads post—the same counter has been here. This is a place of quiet observation, perhaps a waypoint for a mysterious surveyor, but no encounter is defined here; it is a pure atmosphere and clue room.

East lies the Market Ruin, a collapsed bazaar with one stall still shaded. Here the Wanderer meets a Hungry Dog (5 HP, AC 12, +2 attack, 1d4 damage) that guards a child's satchel. The dog is not hostile by default; it is hungry and protective. The satchel may contain a clue, a key, or a simple reward—the module leaves it open for the GM to improvise. The ruin's only exit is west back to the crossroads, making it a dead-end encounter node.

West of the crossroads, the River Ford is a shallow crossing with stones placed for small feet. The north bank holds a glass door, impossible and out of place, leading into a keep of clear pipes—this is the explicit loop-closing connection to the water computer's gallery (per the agreed doctrine: river_ford loops north to water_computer.gallery). The ford also has hooks: upstream there is smoke, downstream there is singing. The invariant confirms this loop closes, so the region is not isolated but part of a larger network.

The event table for safe rests is minimal: on a rest, the Wanderer may roll on a table with two outcomes—"Stars wheel. You mark another notch on a stick." (weight 3) or "You dream of a post that writes itself." (weight 1). These reinforce the theme of counting and self-writing maps, tying the region's mystery to the broader world's meta-narrative.

The region's cross-region doctrine also states that the audit dungeon's git vault opens onto the crossroads. This means the Dusty Crossroads is a nexus not only for the four local exits but also for a hidden or conditional passage to a version-controlled dungeon. The exact trigger is not defined here; it is left as a hook for the combined world's GM to integrate, perhaps through the riddle or the notches.

Overall, the 2d6 Adventure Stub is a low-complexity, high-flavor region that tests the generalization of the 2d6 system beyond 5e. Its rooms are small, its encounters are few, and its hooks are deliberately open-ended. The region's primary function is to serve as a connective tissue between other modules—the water computer to the north, the git vault at the crossroads—while offering a self-contained mini-adventure for a lone traveler. The notches and the riddle suggest a deeper pattern, but the module does not resolve it; it invites the larger world to answer.
