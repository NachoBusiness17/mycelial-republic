The region defined by `adventure_2d6_stub` is a sparse, wind-scoured pocket of the persistent world, governed by the `2d6-adventure-lite` system. All task resolution here uses the `2d6` roll with a `pass_on` threshold of `7`; a total of 7 or higher succeeds at ordinary tasks, while combat is resolved as opposed `2d6+mod` rolls. The player begins as the `Wanderer`, a traveler with 10 `hp` (max 10), `ac` 12, `attack_bonus` 1, `damage` `1d6`, the `curious` trait, and a `walking stick` and `waterskin` in inventory.

The central hub is `crossroads`, a Dusty Crossroads marked by a post carved with notches—a tally of journeys, not kills. Its exits lead north to `hill_shrine`, east to `market_ruin`, west to `river_ford`, and via a `lantern` exit to `tavern_lantern` (a hook into another module). A riddle is scratched on the post: *what walks without feet when the map is true?* This riddle is the region's primary puzzle seed, and the notches on the post are the key to understanding the region's history.

North of `crossroads` lies `hill_shrine`, a broken stone ruin with a wind-scoured bowl for coins long stolen. Its only exit is south back to `crossroads`. The hooks here confirm that the notches on the shrine's stones match the notches on the crossroads post—the same counter has been at work, marking journeys across both locations. This suggests a single, unseen traveler or surveyor has been mapping this land, and the riddle may be answered by understanding their method.

East of `crossroads` is `market_ruin`, a collapsed market with one stall still shaded. This room contains a fixed encounter: the `Hungry Dog` (5 `hp`, `ac` 12, `attack_bonus` 2, `damage` `1d4`). The dog guards a child's satchel, and the hook implies the satchel is the object of the encounter—whether the `Wanderer` fights the dog, feeds it, or distracts it, the satchel is the reward. The dog is not hostile by default; it is hungry, and the `2d6` resolution can be used for a non-combat approach (e.g., a `2d6` roll to calm it, pass on 7+).

West of `crossroads` is `river_ford`, a shallow crossing with stones placed for small feet. The north bank holds a glass door, impossible, leading into a keep of clear pipes—this is the explicit loop-closing hook to the water computer's gallery. The invariant is recorded as `loop-closes (RIB_CATHEDRAL_WORLD_V1: adventure_2d6.river_ford loops to water_computer.gallery - AGREE)`. The `river_ford` has an exit north to `gallery`, and its hooks note *upstream: smoke, downstream: singing*, providing ambient direction for exploration.

The region's rest mechanic is defined by the `rest_safe` event table. When the `Wanderer` rests in a safe location, roll on this table: the `stars` event (weight 3) describes stars wheeling and the traveler marking another notch on a stick, reinforcing the journey-counting theme; the `dream` event (weight 1) describes a dream of a post that writes itself, directly foreshadowing the riddle at `crossroads`. These events are the only rest outcomes in this module, and they tie the region's identity to the act of marking journeys.

Cross-region doctrine is explicit: the `river_ford` loops north into the water computer's `gallery` (the `loop-closes` invariant), and the audit dungeon's git vault opens onto `crossroads`. This means `crossroads` is a true nexus—not just a local hub but a junction between the 2d6 adventure region, the water computer's glass-and-pipe keep, and the audit dungeon's versioned vault. The `lantern` exit from `crossroads` to `tavern_lantern` further extends the world, but the git vault's opening onto `crossroads` is the primary external connection for this module.

In practice, the `2d6` resolution governs all player actions in this region. The `Wanderer` starts with 10 `hp` and a `1d6` damage die; the `Hungry Dog` has 5 `hp` and deals `1d4`. Combat is opposed `2d6+mod`—the `Wanderer` adds `attack_bonus` 1, the dog adds 2. For non-combat tasks (riddle-solving, calming the dog, crossing the ford), a flat `2d6` roll of 7 or higher succeeds. The region is deliberately small, but its hooks—the riddle, the notches, the satchel, the glass door—are designed to chain into the larger persistent world, making `crossroads` a memorable threshold for any traveler.
