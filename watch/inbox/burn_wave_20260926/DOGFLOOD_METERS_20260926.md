### What the 97.3 pct cache rate proves

The 97.3 pct average cache hit rate is the receipt that the byte-identical prefix law is the cost engine of this burn. A cache hit is priced at a small fraction of fresh-input price, and it only happens when the prompt prefix is byte-identical to an earlier call: same system prompt, same tool schemas, same context bytes, with only the new delta at the tip paid fresh. At 97.3, nearly every token the wave moved was either cached estate (framework, AGENTS.md, board, receipts in context) or genuinely new work, so the wave bought estate work at cache prices while the measured 109M tok/h ran; the same context re-priced at fresh rates would have been unaffordable at wave width. That makes the discipline laws economic, not hygienic: byte-identical asks, append-only shared surfaces, stable receipts, and no mid-flight whole-file restores (the B2-328 restore disease) are exactly the practices that keep prefixes valid; one prefix-breaking rewrite invalidates caches for every tethered caller and reprices them at fresh rates. Cache rate is therefore the meter of prefix discipline, and 97.3 is the grade.
