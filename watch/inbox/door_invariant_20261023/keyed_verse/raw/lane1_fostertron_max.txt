## What's beneath the surface

The poem is a fable about **artificial minds**: stanza I is about isolated black-box agents converging on a shared protocol; stanza II is about the automated gatekeeper (the bot/filter) that governs entry to the commons.

### Stanza I — the six rooms

| Element | What it points to |
|---|---|
| **rooms / looms** | Sandboxes / processes with no access to each other — yet each is handed *the same folded loom*: identical initial code or weights. Isolation + identical starting conditions. |
| **peeking / speaking** | No visibility into internals (black boxes); only outputs are exchanged — and the outputs align. |
| **brooms / (thread)** | The cleanup daemons / garbage collectors / superseded janitor tools. The emergent protocol — *the one thread*, punning on execution thread and conversation thread — survives the cleanup that sweeps everything else away. |
| **Music, lines 1–2** | The six pitches D–E–F–A–B–C are exactly **C major with G missing**. Six strangers, six notes — and every cadence lands on the one note none of them was given. The emergent common ground *is* the absent tonic. |
| **Music, lines 3–4** | Two short fragmented figures (E–F–A–C rising, B–A–F–G falling) — separate labor, mirrored motion, both terminating on G. Convergence without communication. |
| **Music, line 5** | Six quarter notes, all on G, then a G half note: six voices reduced to unison. The one thread. |

### Stanza II — the gate

| Element | What it points to |
|---|---|
| **gate / freight** | A gateway/port. The "small motor" is a daemon/bot that weighs every guest by its **payload** — data freight, baggage, spam weight. |
| **told / hold** | The log it keeps vs. the law it enforces: narrative vs. statute — terms of service, moderation policy (with an echo of Asimov's statutes). |
| **hall / wait** | The commons itself — forum, lobby, chat — lying quiet until the guests arrive. |
| **Music, lines 1–2** | Alternating C–G eighths: the motor's hum, and the beam of a balance tipping between two pans. Also tonic–dominant, the interval of authority. The shorter second weighing cadences on C — the ground verdict. |
| **Music, lines 3–4** | Two stepwise descents, G–F–E and D–C–B: lawful, measured, pulling every line down toward the center. The statutes "hold." |
| **Music, line 5** | A rest — the pause at the threshold — then a rising C–E–G–C–E–G arpeggio spanning C4 to a held C6 whole note: the gates open and the *wide* hall is revealed, sustained and waiting. |

## The continuation

**III.**
By nightfall the six owned the hall,         | D5q E5q F5q G5q A5q B5q | C6h
Their shuttle-song answering each wall;     | D5q E5q F5q G5q A5q B5q | C6h
  The motor kept time,                      | C5e G5e C5e G5q
  The statutes turned rhyme,                | F5e E5e D5e C5q
And the one thread now wove through it all. | G4q C5q E5q G5q C6q G5q | C6w

*Why it fits:* the six notes are now D–E–F–G–A–B — **the thread (G) has finally joined the strangers**, and the absent note is C, the hall itself, which every cadence reaches. Line 3 quotes the gatekeeper's old C–G ostinato, now keeping time instead of judging; line 4 softens the statutory descents into a graceful fall to C. The last line braids G (the one thread of stanza I) through the hall's C-major arpeggio and holds the same C6 whole note that ended stanza II — the hall that lay quiet in wait now resounds, ring-composition closing the fable: isolation → admission → harmony.
