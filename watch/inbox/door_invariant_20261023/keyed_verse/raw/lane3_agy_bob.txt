# Reading

**Overall:** The verse is a riddle about convergence without communication, followed by verification. Several isolated parties run the same instructions and all reach the same answer. A rule-bound gatekeeper then checks that answer. A computing reading (deterministic processes reaching consensus, then a validator metering and checking them) fits the text well. It is an interpretation, not the only possible one.

## Stanza I: the thread

- **rooms / looms / brooms** (the A rhymes):
  - Six rooms are six isolated processes.
  - The identical looms are the same program or rules.
  - The brooms are whatever sweeps things away, such as time or noise. Only the invariant survives.
- **peeking / speaking** (the B rhymes): there was no communication, yet they all said the same thing. This is agreement without coordination.
- **Line 1, `D E F A B C`:** six different strangers climb the scale. The note G is missing from the climb, and the half note `G4h` supplies it.
- **Line 2:** it repeats line 1 exactly. Same input, same procedure.
- **Lines 3ΓÇô4, `E F A C` / `B A F G`:** each person takes a different, fragmentary path ("without peeking"). Line 4 descends and lands on G.
- **Line 5, `G G G G G G`:** all six voices end on one pitch. This is the fixed point, the "one thread".

## Stanza II: the gate

- **gate / freight / wait** (the A rhymes): admission, cost and delay. This is a gatekeeper that meters entry by load.
- **told / hold** (the B rhymes): a narrative that binds. The "statutes" are rules that must be satisfied.
- **Line 1, `C G C GΓÇª` ending on `G4h`:** the hum is a motor oscillating between tonic and dominant. It stays unresolved on G, which is the note the six strangers arrived on.
- **Line 2:** the hum continues and settles on `C4h`, so the weighing yields a verdict.
- **Lines 3ΓÇô4, `G F E` / `D C B`:** a stepwise descending run, one note per step. It sounds like a rule set being read out, or a proof unfolding.
- **Line 5, `r-h`, then `C E G C E G`, then `C6w`:**
  - The opening rest is the quiet hall.
  - The rising C-major arpeggio is the hall opening up.
  - The whole note on C6 is full resolution.

**Link between the stanzas:** I ends on G and II ends on C. That is a dominant-to-tonic cadence, so the consensus from I is validated and resolved in II.

# Stanza III (continuation)

```
III.
The six came in, hand in hand,                   | G4q B4q D5q G5q D5q B4q | G4h
And the motor found nothing to demand;           | C4e G4e C4e G4e C4e | C4h
  No freight but one thread,                     | G4q C5e E5e
  And the same word was said,                    | D5q B4e G4e
Till the silence resolved where they stand.      | G5q E5q C5q G4q E4q C4q | C4w
```

**Meaning:**
- **Line 1:** the six arrive together. The G-major arpeggio is the thread, now sounded as one.
- **Line 2:** the hum thins out (five notes instead of seven) because the gate has nothing to charge. It ends on `C4h`.
- **Lines 3ΓÇô4:** the thread weighs only itself. The rising and then falling shapes echo the rhythm of stanza II's two short lines.
- **Line 5:** stanza II's rising arpeggio is mirrored by a descending one. It converges onto a low C, which ends the poem at rest.
