## Beneath the surface

This is an allegory of **deterministic agreement among isolated processes** — six workers, one program, no communication, one identical result — followed by a **gatekeeper that validates every input** before the system falls silent, quiescent, holding its answer.

### Stanza I — the six weavers

**Rhyme pairs**
- *looms / brooms* — creation against erasure. The loom is the given program; the broom is the sweep that resets the room (clock ticks, garbage collection, crashed processes). The punchline sits on this pair: the single thread **outlasts the sweep**. Whatever the six computed is durable; the clearing cannot uncompute it.
- *peeking / speaking* — isolation versus report. They never consult each other ("without peeking"), yet all emit the same "thread." Agreement without communication — that is the definition of a shared deterministic function.
- The envelope is AABBA: the fifth line returns to the first pair, closing the loop the way the weave closes on itself.

**Musical line**
- `D4 E4 F4 A4 B4 C5 | G4h` — six quarter notes, one per stranger, a hexachord (D–E–F–A–B–C) walking upward, all arriving on a single held G. Six rooms, one tone.
- `E4 F4 A4 C5` then `B4 A4 F4 G4` — the same intervals traded between two voices, one rising one falling, both collapsing back to G. Independent weaving, convergent pitch.
- `G4 ×6 | G4h` — six repeated quarters on the same note: six voices literally speaking in unison, then the held "one thread." The music *enacts* the consensus it describes.

### Stanza II — the gate and the statutes

**Rhyme pairs**
- *gate / freight / wait* — the tollhouse and the weighing. Every guest is measured by what it carries ("freight" = payload, weight, checksum). The third rhyme, *wait*, is the same vowel emptied out: after the audit, only silence remains.
- *told / hold* — report and law. The motor's verdict isn't opinion; it "had statutes to hold." A validator bound by rules — a checksum, a protocol, a filter with no false negatives.

**Musical line**
- `C4 G4` alternating seven times — the tonic–fifth oscillation *is* the motor's hum, a pendulum swinging at the gate, testing each arrival against the same two poles.
- `G5 F5 E5` then `D5 C5 B4` — a stepwise descent, the "story told" walking down the scale line by line, statutes recited.
- `r-h` — the rest is the wide hall, quiet in wait. Then `C4 E4 G4 C5 E5 G5 | C6w` — a full C-major arpeggio climbing two octaves to a whole note: the verdict revealed whole and consonant. The hall was waiting for *harmony* — the moment the scattered threads sound as one chord.

So together: **I proves that identical isolated work yields one durable truth; II is the gate that weighs each claim against the rules and, finding it lawful, lets the room go still — a system in quiescence, commit pending.**

## The continuation

III.
Six threads that agree are but one, | D4q E4q F4q A4q B4q C5q | C5h
When the loom and the labor are done; | D4q E4q F4q A4q B4q C5q | C5h
  The thread held its light | E4e F4e A4e C5q
  Through the sweep of the night, | B4e A4e F4e C5q
And the six are the sum of the one. | C5q C5q C5q C5q C5q C5q | C6w

**Why this works as a continuation:** The rhyme scheme holds (AABBA: *one/done/one*, with the inner pair *light/night* — the thread's persistence through the dark clearing of stanza I's broom). Musically it quotes stanza I exactly — same six-note hexachord for the six weavers, same trading figures — but where stanza I hung unresolved on the **dominant G**, stanza III lands every cadence on the **tonic C**. The middle lines now rise to C5 instead of falling back to G4, and the six unisons are answered by a final whole note an octave higher: the six *are* the one, at last closed, no longer in suspense. Dominant was the question; tonic is the seal.
