## Rules, scene by scene

**Labels:** **Confident** = the story states or structurally requires it. **Likely** = strongly implied but not stated. **Guess** = my inference, could be overreading.

---

### The frame (arrival)

1. **Enter contested ground with tools of record and access, not weapons.** Scene: "She carried no weapon except a slate and a ring of keys" — a writing surface and the means to open doors. **Confident** (the sentence pointedly enumerates what she does and doesn't carry).

2. **Cache essential records before the foreseeable disaster.** Scene: her mother hid the ledger "before the flood." **Guess** — it's backstory, but the story opens with it for a reason.

### The archivist's stool

3. **A seat holds only the person sitting in it — never confuse occupying a position with bearing weight.** Scene: "Miriam saw that the seat had not carried anything but him." **Confident.**

4. **Don't reach past your support.** Scene: he leans back for a higher volume and the three-legged stool splits. **Confident.**

5. **When an individual falls, the institution does not fall with him.** Scene: the shelf doesn't move, the books stay. Judge structures by what remains standing, not by who collapses. **Likely.**

6. **Recover by direct, self-supported effort, not by acquiring a better stool.** Scene: he climbs "by pressing his hands against the wall." **Likely.**

### The clerk and the blank space

7. **Erasing a record is theft of the thing itself, and the case is complete on showing the absence.** Scene: "The ink was here, and now it is not; that is theft enough." **Confident.**

8. **Destruction will be described as maintenance.** Scene: "he had only cleaned the page." Expect the laundering verb. **Confident.**

9. **The blank space is the exhibit — point at it.** Scene: the owner "pointed to the blank space where the name had been." **Confident** (and it recurs at the ending, so it's load-bearing).

### The man in the doorway

10. **Authority is not established by announcement, arrival, or posture; it is established by whom the workers actually look to.** Scene: the servants "looked past him" to the seal-holder. **Confident.**

11. **Authority is a transferable token, and a quiet touch on it outranks a loud command.** Scene: she doesn't raise her voice; she touches the seal, and they move. **Confident.**

12. **Standing in the doorway is not commanding the door. Occupancy is not control.** Scene: "no one treated his standing there as a command." **Confident.**

### The linen map

13. **When the map and the floor disagree, trust the floor; verify passages with hands and ears.** Scene: "The map still promised an opening, but the stone was cold and unbroken beneath her hands" — she turns back to where the boards "sounded hollow." **Confident** (the text literally says she "trusted the floor").

14. **Retire a refuted guide permanently, but keep it.** Scene: she folds the map small and "never used it again." **Confident** on retiring it; **guess** on why she keeps it — as a warning, or as evidence of its own wrongness.

### The dark corridor

15. **When lost: stop moving, keep a hand on something solid, and say out loud that you don't know.** Scene: "I do not know this passage" — and "it was on the strength of saying it" that help comes. Admission is a signal that makes you findable; concealing ignorance keeps you lost. **Confident** (the narrator generalizes it: "a house hears a person who admits being lost").

### The ledger room

16. **Over-deletion documents itself.** Scene: the line "struck through so hard the paper had torn." The harder the concealment, the more visible the act. **Confident.**

17. **Events are readable from physical state without reading the text.** Scene: wet pen, ash, torn paper — she "did not need to read the name to know what had happened." **Likely.**

### The confrontation at the door

18. **Against someone who can take by force, don't promise resistance — promise truthful, total exposure.** Scene: "she would let him, and then she would tell everyone in the house exactly what he had done. He stepped aside." **Confident.**

19. **You may lose the object; never lose the account.** Concede the thing, retain the testimony — the telling is worth more than possession. **Confident** (her whole method is this).

### The ending

20. **Legitimate authority arrives quietly and is confirmed by material care, not applause.** Scene: the seal is given "without ceremony"; the servants don't cheer but bring bread and a chair by the fire. **Likely.**

21. **Circulate copies to the external authority; keep the original, damage included, intact.** Scene: "The ledger was copied and sent to the magistrate, and the torn page was left exactly as it was." **Confident.**

22. **Never repair the wound in a record. The blank space must stay visible so it keeps generating the question "what was here?"** Scene: "so that anyone who opened the book would see the blank space and ask what had been there." The question is the safeguard. **Confident** — this is the story's most explicit rule.

---

## Rules running across the whole book

23. **Absence is never neutral.** Every gap in this world — the rubbed-out name, the missing name at the torn line, the blank space left deliberately — is a legible event. This is the master rule the ledger enforces. **Confident** as the unifying theme.

24. **Records survive by duplication, and originals are for confronting, not circulating.** Scene: the archivist opens the story copying names into a long book; Miriam closes it copying the ledger for the magistrate. **Likely** (the symmetry seems deliberate).

25. **The house is both sensor and court.** It "hears" admissions, its servants verify authority, and exposure "to everyone in the house" is a sufficient sanction. Operate as though everything is witnessed. **Likely** — implied twice (the corridor, the confrontation), but never stated as a rule.

26. **Authority gravitates to the credible witness.** The seal is given to Miriam the night she proves she cannot be coerced into silence and cannot be fooled by appearances — the house delegates power to whoever demonstrates the method it trusts. **Likely**; the causal link between the confrontation and the gift of the seal is my reading.

---

## What I'd flag as most uncertain

- **Rule 2** (pre-disaster caching) — one clause of backstory, could just be motive.
- **Rule 14** (why she keeps the map) — the text withholds her reason.
- **Rule 6** (self-supported recovery) — the wall-climbing could simply be physical description rather than an encoded rule.
- Whether the slate she carries ever "does" anything — she never visibly writes on it, which is odd given how deliberately it's introduced. Possibly a rule about carrying the means to record even when you never use it; I can't pin it down, so it stays a guess.

The cleanest compression of the whole story: **trust the floor, not the map; trust the seal, not the man in the doorway; treat every blank space as evidence; and when force threatens, let go of the object and keep the telling.**
