# Rules in "The Book"

## Confident (stated or glossed by the text)

1. **A support that has only held its present load under its present posture fails when the posture changes.** — The archivist leans back for a higher volume and the three-legged stool splits under him. The wall-shelf, anchored, does not move; the books stay. Miriam draws the moral herself: the seat "had not carried anything but him, and… even he had not stayed on it for long."

2. **When the device fails, switch to direct contact with the structure.** — After the fall, the archivist climbs "by pressing his hands against the wall." Same pattern in the east wall scene: hands on cold stone beat a linen diagram.

3. **Erasure is theft; a blank space is a permanent accusation.** — The clerk rubs a name out of the register with a damp cloth; the owner seizes his wrist: "The ink was here, and now it is not; that is theft enough." The clerk's intent (cleaning) is irrelevant; the absence is the act.

4. **Leave the wound unrepaired — the damage is the exhibit.** — The ledger's last line is struck through so hard the paper tore; at the end, the torn page is "left exactly as it was, so that anyone who opened the book would see the blank space and ask what had been there." Procedure extends rule 3: don't fix what accuses.

5. **Authority is demonstrated by who is obeyed, not by who is present or vocal.** — The tall man announces himself master and blocks the inner hall; the servants look past him to the woman with the seal. She never raises her voice — she touches the seal and they move. His standing in the doorway is not treated as a command.

6. **A self-announced claim needs no refutation, only noncompliance.** — Nobody argues the tall man down, strips his coat, or names him a fraud. The household simply does not route commands through him. Noncompliance is silent, collective, and sufficient.

7. **When a representation conflicts with the ground, trust the ground — and retire the instrument.** — The linen map promises an opening through the east wall; her hands find solid, unbroken stone. She pockets the linen, trusts the floor, and turns back to the boards that "still sounded hollow." She keeps the map folded and never uses it again.

8. **In disorientation: stop moving, say the true thing out loud, wait.** — The lamp fails; the dark comes all at once. Miriam puts her hand on the plaster and declares to no one, "I do not know this passage," stands still, and the boy comes with the lamp — "because a house hears a person who admits being lost." Declaring ignorance is the operational move; striding is what the dark defeats.

9. **Against a man who rules by appearance, testimony outranks resistance.** — Asked what she will do if he takes the ledger anyway: she will let him, then "tell everyone in the house exactly what he had done." He steps aside. The credible threat of exposure prices the object above its worth to him.

10. **Legitimacy is undramatic; provisioning is the tell.** — The seal is handed over "without ceremony." The servants do not cheer — they bring bread and a chair by the fire, and the house settles into its old quiet. Real transfer shows up as ordinary service, not acclaim.

11. **Externalize the evidence to a neutral third party before it can be suppressed.** — The ledger is copied and sent to the magistrate. Knowledge that lives only in the house (or only in Miriam) is still hostage to the house.

## Guesses (inference beyond what the text asserts)

12. **Fresh tools date the act.** — The ledger is found open with "a pen still wet with ink" beside the torn strike. The text hands you the wet pen; the conclusion (the erasure was recent, done by someone with access and time) is yours to draw.

13. **Search among the remains of failed destruction.** — The ledger sits in "a room where the fire had burned down to ash." Reading: an attempted burn that exhausted itself leaves survivors, findable precisely by its failure. The story never says the fire was set for the book.

14. **Your kit should be access and record, not force.** — "She carried no weapon except a slate and a ring of keys." She wins the house with keys (rooms), a slate (record), speech (exposure), and a seal she never claims credit for. The inventory is stated; the generalization is mine.

15. **Hide the record before the catastrophe, not during it.** — The ledger survived because her mother hid it "before the flood." Backstory, one clause — but the rule is legible: pre-position evidence where the destructive force won't think to look.

16. **What "had happened" is unknowable by design.** — "Miriam did not need to read the name to know what had happened." Whether the struck name was her mother's, a person un-personed, or a claim erased — the story withholds it deliberately. The rule you can trust is the *form* (a name violently removed); the content is a hole the text intends you to feel.

## The spine

If the twelve confident rules collapse into one: **fixed things beat perched things; absence testifies; and what moves people (a seal, a threat of telling, an admission of being lost) beats what merely stands in doorways.** Miriam never wins a confrontation — she arranges for the truth to be cheaper to live with than the theft.
