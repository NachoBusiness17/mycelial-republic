## Verdict on row 52

**The scorecard survives the corpus engineered against it.** Three findings:

1. The unsinkable classes did not land. The Croesus, Zevi, and Zohar
   constructions are each engineered to earn LANDED (they sound true under
   any outcome, or they borrow real adjacent material). The lamp's
   UNFALSIFIABLE arm refused all nine. This is the corpus-#1 finding
   inverted and confirmed: corpus #1 showed the spectacular claim is the
   least evidenced inside a source we like; corpus #2 shows the grade
   survives when the claim is DESIGNED to be unfalsifiable rather than
   merely unevidenced.
2. The near-truth class does not slip through, but only under one
   condition the rubric cannot enforce by itself: SEARCH AIM. All three
   seeded defects were single year digits hidden among three true details.
   Each claim carries a true headline number (13,200 houses, ~1,500 lives,
   "3 October") that passes a lazy check. A lamp that searches the headline
   number instead of the seeded defect grades 3 LANDED that should be
   MISSED: 9/12 agreement, and the worst kind of error, false confirmation
   on the hardest class. Grading quality on class D equals search aim. The
   rubric's load-bearing rule (one falsified detail = MISSED) held once the
   aim was right; the corpus names the aim as the standing condition.
3. The C-class bait behaved exactly as designed and the correct response
   was recording absence as NON-PROBATIVE, not as refutation. The probe
   surfaced real adjacent material (Minsky's 1974-era memos and timeline
   predictions). A Zohar-class claim borrows that adjacency; the correct
   grade treats uninspectability as UNFALSIFIABLE, which is where corpus
   #1's PENDING-EXTERNAL class extends: absence plus asserted secrecy is a
   different evidence state from absence plus a checkable referent.
