## The steal (mechanism, not links)

A provider KV miss budget is not one problem, and the field data measures only two of its buckets. Over 393 real agent sessions: requests arriving after the 5-minute provider TTL gap account for at most 17.5% of recompute; requests arriving within 10 seconds account for 33.1% (tight tool loops whose ~88k working sets exceed cache capacity). The residual ~49.4% is UNATTRIBUTED in the field data: the source explicitly says the dominant measured waste is tight tool loops, not TTL idling, and never names the residual's cause. The estate-side HYPOTHESIS is that byte drift of the prefix owns much of the residual: doug.sh proves the drift mechanism exists (sorted serialization moved cached share 55% -> 95%), but nothing in the field quantifies its share; the decomposition probe below is what tests that hypothesis. Mechanism: (a) measure the decomposition before choosing a lever, since an eviction policy cannot touch the capacity share and a serializer cannot touch the TTL share; (b) kill the byte-drift share with a canonical-serialization frozen prefix, already built in this tree (Find 2); (c) treat the capacity share as a serving budget, ANALOGY-labeled: the field's 33.1% was measured against a provider cache, and mapping it onto janitor working sets is the estate's analogy, which is exactly what the probe checks; (d) accept only attested hits as evidence, since a true hit still proves nothing was skipped (Find 6); (e) Belady losing to LRU is a broken-harness verdict: any estate eviction A/B where the offline oracle loses fails the existing bar (`kv_cache_compaction.md:13`, "must beat LRU/ARC/PBKV/Belady-MIN arms or it is decoration") before it starts.
