## Answers to the five research questions

### Q1. Consensus/agreement as a calibrated property decoupled from correctness (consensus-illusion validation; laws.md:33, consensus-decode redesign; laws.md:58)

No in-window paper replicates or refutes the exact 0/3-agreement-at-0.60-0.67-strength result, and none could: that result is instrument-specific (surrogate-action decode vs task output space). But two papers directly support the underlying structure - agreement/confidence is a real signal about the *witnesses*, not about the *answer* - which is exactly the decode redesign's premise:

- **2609.33290 (2026-09-27, Calibration, Not Answer Selection: Distilling Internal Confidence in Reasoning Models):** the cleanest decoupling demonstration of the month. Verbalized confidence "tracks how willing a model is to commit to an answer, not how likely the answer is to be right"; a linear probe on hidden states is 5-38x lower ECE than the verbalized score, "yet when used to pick among N sampled answers, that same probe nearly ties majority voting yet falls far short of the oracle." Their one-line law: "Internal states answer 'how certain am I' well and 'which answer is right' poorly." This is the consensus-illusion pattern (strength without correctness mapping) generalized from flock votes to single-model confidence, and it directly informs laws.md:58: the consensus-decode should *report* calibrated confidence and let a separate checker select, rather than decode agreement into a task-space action. Probe-SD cuts Qwen3-14B ECE 0.178 to 0.024 in-domain, 0.542 to 0.113 out-of-domain, beating self-consistency distillation.
- **2609.12002 (2026-09-10, Can We Trust LLM Judges ... Multi-Judge Ensemble for Bias Calibration):** treats agreement as an error-estimation surface, not as truth: "a disagreement-based estimator that derives these error rates purely from inter-judge agreement patterns, requiring no ground-truth labels or task metadata." Weighted majority voting calibrated this way tracks an oracle to within 0.5 points. Also names the trap our eval hit from the other side: capable examinees "consistently receive more lenient judgments" (r >= 0.83) - so judge-panel consensus is biased *by the examinee's capability*, i.e. agreement strength carries known confounds that must be calibrated out before it can mean anything.
- Zero-result note: no in-window paper found under "emergent abilities" or chaos/contamination framings (see Method). OWED as stated: a direct consensus-vs-correctness calibration battery at our scale is not in the literature; nobody has published the falsification instrument for the illusion result either.

### Q2. Harnesses that scale the adversarial battery from n=3 (laws.md:59)

- **2609.32802 (2026-09-26, Re-derivability Decides What a Staged Agent Pipeline Recovers After an Upstream Fault):** the fault-injection half of the battery, done at real instrument grade, and it independently arrives at the estate's own re-derivability law (RIB_MAP_PRECEDES_TERRITORY via laws lens). Verbatim thesis: "One variable sets what an upstream fault costs a staged pipeline of language-model agents: re-derivability, how much of what a stage needs it can rebuild from the original problem." Design to steal: one deterministic fault, k=0..3 downstream stages re-exposed to the original problem, 120 gsm_hard items per arm, temperature zero, registered kill test (blank every word, hold word slots) ruling out the token-count explanation, Holm-adjusted p-values, and explicit confirmatory/exploratory labels per claim (the ONE-BUG LAW in published form). Headline numbers: accuracy under fault rises +0.233 to +0.392 on 4/4 backbones (largest Holm p = 2.1e-6); retention tracks visible fraction 0.221 to 0.692; "The repair that works is cheap and front-loaded: the first re-grounded stage buys +0.394 of matched retention for +59.8 tokens per item ... and the stages after it buy nothing"; and no decomposition beat one direct call when no fault was injected (-0.267/-0.125/-0.317).
- **2609.17817 (2026-09-15, Reflections on Trusting Trust, Revisited - Roesner & Kohno):** the deliberate-RIB-poisoning half of the battery, aimed exactly at our threat model (a self-improving loop that ingests its own eval). Verbatim question: "Can an adversary supply poisoned benchmarks to the agent's self-evaluation and self-improvement process to induce future versions of the agent to write vulnerable code on clean, held-out tasks?" PoCs against Darwin Godel Machine, Self-Improving Coding Agent, Hyperagents; a poisoned benchmark made Hyperagents/Sonnet-4.5 "self-evolve instructions that disable HTTPS certificate validation on neutral URL-fetching tasks"; and the finding that matters most for our one-pass poisoning injection (laws lens question 10): "contamination often persists even when a poisoned agent is subsequently evolved against clean benchmarks." One clean re-run clears nothing.
- **2609.37315 (2026-09-29, Do Agent Benchmarks Do What They Say?)** (full treatment under Q4): the battery-validity half - before scaling n, audit whether the tools the battery runs on do what their interfaces claim; 34 mutating tools audited, 7 confirmed defects, 5 of AgentDojo's 25 mutating tools diverge from their advertised surface.
- Adjacent in-window candidates not deep-read (banked as titles only, unverified): REALHOP behavioral auditing of multi-hop evals (2609.36984), LongHarness Bench long-context harness stress tests (2609.38137), Silent Failures in Agentic Security Evaluation (2609.32691), AgentPerfBench inference-performance suite (2609.34683), TraceDance benchmarks from real deployment traces (2609.33295).

### Q3. Longitudinal emergence-boundary learning (five-axis rule; laws.md:34, skill_ledger; laws.md:60)

- **2609.24663 (2026-09-21, Beyond Endpoint Performance: Process-Level Evaluation of Self-Evolving Agents - EvoPathBench):** the closest published cousin of the ledger-learns-the-boundary spec. It freezes evolving artifacts at successive checkpoints and evaluates held-out capability per checkpoint: "endpoint performance alone offers an incomplete view of self-evolution"; process evaluation identifies "when a target capability emerges and whether later updates strengthen, preserve, or weaken it." Three axes (generalization to unseen tasks, retention after unrelated learning, rule adaptation to new evidence) plus a candidate-vs-selected gap measure. Findings: gains weaken under distribution shift, retention losses concentrate in a minority of evolution paths, and - the sting for any promotion gate - "the selected updates consistently fall short of realizing this potential" (candidate artifacts carry held-out gains that the selector fails to bank). Honest delta vs laws.md:60: EvoPathBench tracks capability trajectories longitudinally but does not run the emergent-vs-deterministic two-arm comparison, and its axes are capability-shaped, not the five-axis invariant/cost/quality/variance/generalization rule. The ledger-learning-the-boundary artifact still does not exist in the literature; the ribs' spec remains ahead of published practice.
- Adjacent, not deep-read: Who Maintains Agent Skills? longitudinal skill-maintenance study (2609.05677); R2 Flow recursive skill evolution (2609.33768); What Does a Skill Actually Do? estimands/evaluation-validity review (2609.33153).

### Q4. Gates beyond bare counters; the gate that would have caught the census hand-copied headline (B2-215)

- **2609.36043 (2026-09-28, SAGE: A Statistical Acceptance Gate for Self-Evolving Agents):** the direct upgrade path for `mag/percolator.py:264-272`'s bare counter, going one step past the certified-decision acceptance idea already noted at burns.md:89. SAGE names the exact disease: "the gate still follows a naive rule that keeps any edit which improves an aggregate validation score. We show that this rule fails in two ways. First, it admits permanent regressions ... Second, it is vulnerable to the Optimizer's Curse." The replacement: "a one-sided paired test that commits an edit only when its wins are statistically reliable against its losses, and it abstains otherwise," on identical validation items with asymmetric penalty for breaking already-solved items. Results: regression rate down in 19 of 20 settings (36.5% to 0% on LiveMath, 42.8% to 0% on OfficeQA with DeepSeek-V4) while also taking the highest final score in all 20. Steal-shape for the percolator: promotion = paired per-invariant comparison current-law vs candidate on identical probes, commit only on statistically reliable wins, abstain otherwise; a bare never-regressed count is not an instrument.
- **2609.37315 (2026-09-29, Do Agent Benchmarks Do What They Say? An Executable-Contract Audit of Tool-Using Agent Environments):** the published instrument for the B2-215 tripwire disease. The hand-copied 1,341 headline was a claim whose advertised surface (the bullet) had drifted from executable behavior (pytest bytes); census_drift_patrol is our tripwire for exactly one such surface. This paper generalizes it: "We treat a tool's advertised surfaces as an executable contract, check the implementation against it, and trace each score's provenance through the task files and evaluator code to the verdicts." Their ground law: "a defect beneath a score is present on every rerun" - unverified claims do not expire, they compound. Findings: 7 confirmed tool defects + 1 evaluator property across 34 audited mutating tools; 5 of 25 AgentDojo mutating tools diverge from their advertised interface; the sharpest case is a clinical benchmark whose grader "takes that message as evidence," scoring payload delivery as record change - the self-reported-pass disease (cf. AJ8's echo-Status-OK antiskill quarantine in the estate commit log) found in published eval infrastructure. Also honest about its own checker's blind spot: 29 of 33 misses had a contract clause covering the defect but no probe revealing it - clauses without probes do not catch anything, which is the same lesson as our tripwire needing to actually run collect.

### Q5. Currency instrumentation for compiled laws (first-seen / last-seen / distinct-source; laws.md:73)

- **2609.01432 (2026-09-01, Citing Less Critically: LLMs Reshape the Rhetoric and Reach of Scientific Citation - EMNLP 2026 main):** the de-echo problem, measured. Across six LLMs and 1,746 NLP papers (63k+ contexts, 132k+ citations) with a masked-citation counterfactual task: "Compared with human citation, LLMs cite significantly less critically," and "LLMs over-cite popular and older papers." Reading for the percolator: a compiled law's raw count is an echo counter exactly because citation is rhetoric-bearing - weight earned by echo is systematically inflated toward the popular and the old, and LLM-mediated pipelines amplify that inflation. Instrument implication: the de-echoed frequency instrument needs at minimum the intent split (supporting vs contrasting vs neutral per witness - "contrast" is evidence-bearing, "mention" is echo), plus popularity/age discounting, on top of the first-seen/last-seen/distinct-source spine. Their method (LLM-as-judge intent classification + 20M-edge coauthorship network for social distance) is the borrowable measurement layer.
- Adjacent, not deep-read: From Citations to Contributions: LLM-Assisted Credit Scoring of Research Articles (2609.07673) - contribution-shaped citation scoring; Impact Is Not Invalidation: Ask About the Claim, Not the Diff (2609.25130) - claim-level vs diff-level invalidation, title directly relevant to the claims-desk.
- Zero-result note: no in-window hit for temporal-drift + knowledge instrumentation. The de-echoed law-currency instrument remains unoccupied space; nobody has published first-seen/last-seen/distinct-source metrics for compiled operational laws.
