### 1. Fine-tuning Qwen3.5-4B to replace Gemini Flash-Lite in a RAG pipeline (2026-09-29, id 49897103; read duane.sh/a/fine-tuning-qwen-local-minutes end to end)

The single best Q1+Q4 find. Recipe: Qwen3.5-4B, LoRA rank 32 (not QLoRA), ~24k examples, one rented H100, 1.6 hours, about $7. Pipeline: SFT with loss on context tokens only (about 8x cheaper) then DPO on the student's OWN temperature-0.8 samples (on-policy pairs) then GRPO with an Opus-graded rubric (+1 correct, -3 unsupported), lr tripled after 25 stalled steps. Training contexts were written by Claude Opus 5.5 because "an earlier attempt trained on Gemini's own outputs copied its errors". Measured on held-out rubric scoring, not loss: shipped model 7.9% unsupported vs Gemini's 20-28%; usefulness 4.19/5 vs Opus's 4.36; retrieval top-1 62% to 76% fair base-vs-tuned. Cost $76 per million excerpts, 71% cheaper than Gemini 2.5 Flash-Lite, pays back after about 1.2M excerpts. Named negative conditions (Q4 gold): imitation-only stalled (3.79 to 3.85 with more data); DPO alone traded errors rather than fixing them; the 9B student cost 3x+ for no usefulness gain. Verbatim: "It makes fewer mistakes than Gemini 2.5 Flash-Lite at 71% less cost."

### 4. The dataset where fine-tuning turned actively harmful, and how we now catch it (2026-09-29, id 49889028; thread was a 1-point stub, so read the linked cacheverifier.com/blog/drift-monitoring-real-customer-support-traffic post end to end)

The Q2 instrument. A verifier fine-tuned on older slices of real comcastcares customer-support traffic "started approving more wrong answers than it caught" because the gray-zone positive rate drifted about 5x over the test window; the harm was label drift, not echo or contamination. Three detectors, any one flags: chunked two-proportion z-test (recent vs training-window positive rate), Page-Hinkley change-point, and two-sample KS on the verifier score distribution vs the fine-tune-time baseline (catches covariate shift label monitors miss). Key sentence: "decay is a property of non-stationary traffic, not of verification as a technique". Also: fine-tune needed only a few hundred to 1,000 examples and tolerated real label noise; 3 of 4 benchmark datasets showed no decay at all.
Overlap: grep "cacheverifier"=0 and "comcastcares"=0 in both. New. This is the missing instrument shape for the estate's corpus-grade vs echo-inflated question (laws.md:50 self-marks every instructions_frontier.jsonl raw_count as inflated echo): grade corpora by drift-monitoring on held-out traffic, never by loss curves.

## Answers to the five research questions (grounded)

- Q1 (recipes since 09-28 for janitor-class absorption of procedural tapes): the converging recipe across items 1/2/3/8 is: narrow decision class first (a scorer, not a chatterer), synthetic or on-policy data generated by the funded tier (never the target model's own outputs: item 1 copied Gemini's errors), SFT on masked-to-target loss, then on-policy DPO/GRPO with a priced grader, calibrated confidence + trigger regexes for the tiny end (item 2), and hash-verified resumable checkpoints on Windows/consumer GPUs (item 8). Retention measured on task metrics (rubric usefulness, exact-call accuracy, decision-set correctness), never loss curves, in every item read.
- Q2 (corpus-grade vs echo-inflated): the instrument class is drift detection on held-out verifier scores (z-test + Page-Hinkley + KS, item 4) plus independent replication of any headline claim (item 2: 91% vs 32% when a third party re-benchmarked).
