## Answers to the research questions

**Q1 (upstream of lmcache since our 2,331-row copy, corpus.md:21).** Four landings matter: (1) prefix-warm discipline at the serving layer is now a written practice (doug.sh: vLLM prefix cache matches token-block hashes from the prompt start; a key-sort fix took 78%->95% hit ratio; CPU /dev/shm restore tier for evicted blocks; append-only results do not break the prefix; per-agent text must go AFTER shared text); (2) eviction research moved from clever policies to trace-honest baselines: a 68,266-request/393-session replay failed to beat LRU-leaf with three mechanisms - the binding constraint is capacity (33.1% of recompute follows sub-10s gaps inside tight tool loops whose working sets exceed cache), not liveness, and SGLang/vLLM already implement radix-leaf LRU with non-evictable resident-children blocks; (3) cross-sibling KV sharing has a concrete mechanism (Yandex: physical cache blocks shared across concurrent model instances with per-worker logical orderings, Hogwild!-style multi-view - the "shared KV across swarm siblings" piece, though it does NOT yet do cross-session reuse between separate requests); (4) compression went architectural (DeepSeek CED/CSA2/FP4 - already estate-known; Apple LensVLM compresses context to images at 5x/10x/15x and re-expands only relevant pages). Applied to the frozen-prefix discipline (6.5%->98%, laws.md:28 -> memory/rib/RIB_SELF_MY_EFFICIENCY.md:6-9): the discipline is confirmed as THE lever; the new deltas are (a) treat misses as a capacity/shape problem before inventing policy - byte-stable sorted tool schemas and shared-prefix-first child ordering buy more than any eviction brain; (b) the sibling-sharing mechanism says a swarm could share one physical prefix across children with different logical views instead of each child re-receiving the pack.

**Q4 (payload shape for swarm children with published reliability numbers).** The published evidence all points one way: prefix-shaped payloads beat per-child full packs. doug.sh: per-agent text placed BEFORE shared text was a named cache killer; shared-first + append-only kept 95% hit ratio and cut first-token 27s->7.3s. reschreiter's 10,186-turn audit: tool swapping itself broke only 2.4-3.4% of turns when standby lists are sorted before injection; 82.5% of misses came from unbounded file reads, compaction, and provider 5-minute idle timeouts - i.e. the shape lever (deterministic ordering + bounded reads) dominates. Yandex: shared physical blocks with per-worker logical views is the serving-layer version of "one pack, many deltas". Prompt Trees proves the same shape pays at training time (70x when the cached-token ratio is ~100). No published head-to-head of {full pack vs delta vs rib pointer} was found; what IS published is the ordering/inversion discipline the estate's deliberate-ask already embodies (frozen prefix + tight ask + contract, laws.md:28).
