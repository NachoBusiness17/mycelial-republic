## The four research questions

### RQ1 - persistent cross-session memory structures vs rib/residual/registry/leaf

**MemCodex (2609.39765, full read)** is the closest published architecture to the estate's four-fold split, arrived at independently: a coarse-to-fine layer stack **skill, graph, summary, raw**, where the raw layer is "append-only and immutable" (the estate's residual/leaf: nothing is destroyed at the bottom), every derived-layer item "stores its exact source turns, so evidence is always recoverable" (the estate's registry dereference discipline), and skills are admitted "only if replaying the procedure on demonstration sessions reproduces the recorded outcome" (the estate's receipts-before-trust law). Reads descend coarse-to-fine and stop once sufficient evidence is found. Two estate-relevant measured findings: (a) the routing channel (returning source-turn POINTERS into context) beats the content channel (inlining memory text): content-only drops 60.8 to 55.4 with recall falling to 63.0; (b) depth matters monotonically (1/2/3/4 layers = 54.1/56.5/59.0/60.8) with the graph layer adding the most (+2.5). **EnSIMem (2609.27279, abs read)** contributes the write-time half: index entries of the form `[entity][entity_type][property: value]` with preserved source turns and temporal info - a keyed, typed registry row rather than a prose summary. Estate mapping: rib = MemCodex's skill layer + routing pointers; registry = EnSIMem's typed entity-property index; residual/leaf = the append-only raw layer both systems keep recoverable. Neither system keeps a hash-chained spine; the estate's chain remains a moat (same conclusion as the 09-09 night scan, unchanged).

### RQ2 - relational/entity-relation memory evaluation (laws.md:78 "Relational memory discrimination")

SubtleMemory (2606.05761) was verified and banked by the 09-30 sweep with its waterfall decomposition; not re-derived here. **New this session, TAGGRAPH (2609.38353, full read)** supplies the evaluation DISCIPLINE the estate should run BEFORE wiring any graph retrieval into knowledge_graph.v2: build all candidate retrievers over ONE shared memory representation (shared ingestion, extraction, ontology, base graph) behind a common remember()/query() interface, ablate components separately, vary the extraction model, and always carry a strong lexical baseline. Its measured warning: on LongMemEval-S the adaptive graph method LOST to plain BM25 on the same notes (0.844 vs 0.867 MRR), and "extraction quality matters more than retrieval design" (Gemma tag vocabulary 0.718 MRR vs GPT-OSS 0.428 on identical retrieval code; 651 of 658 audited misses lacked a helpful tag under at least one extractor). For the graph lens's collapsed graph this is the difference between repairing the graph and then measuring it, vs shipping graph retrieval unmeasured. **EMIR2 (2609.32584, full read)** adds the dynamic-conflict half of the eval: MemConflict-style benchmarks where facts CHANGE mid-history (dynamic AA 0.5563 best; conditional conflicts still weak at 0.4324 across systems) - the superseded-fact regime the estate's law:/case_laws blob cannot even express today.

### RQ3 - memory write policies: keep, compact, invalidate (laws.md:54 "Caches must auto-invalidate")

Three new measured results, all pointing the same direction:
- **Temporal Shape (2609.36457, full read):** consolidation WRITERS systematically destroy the grammatical cue (progressive aspect) that READERS use to judge whether a stored fact still holds. mem0's pipeline flattened 49/128 progressives and stripped "for now" from 91/128 notes; Letta 26/29; Graphiti 15/32 - and Graphiti's undated `valid_at`/unset `invalid_at` metadata actively misled readers into treating expired facts as current. Reader-side damage is causal: with only a verb-form edit, GLM-5.2 "acted without asking" on flattened notes in 91 pairs vs 19 preserved ones (corrected p=2.8e-12). The fix is structural, not a rule: store original wording, normalized fact, temporal form, and observation time as SEPARATE fields, and derive metadata from the source. Estate mapping: this is the write-side complement of the caches-auto-invalidate law - a cache entry that has lost its staleness cue cannot be invalidated by its reader no matter what the mtime fingerprint does at load time. The rib's append-only raw spine plus derived summaries is already the separated-fields shape the paper recommends; the steal is the AUDIT (run the estate's own consolidators - brief, context-pack, summary spine - against the Lapse-style progressive/simple pair probe).
- **AMU (2609.36976, abs read):** write-time admission as a distinct, controlled stage: filter "transient requests, duplicate statements, and outdated user states" at admission, then a three-way decision per admitted record - store separately / discard as duplicate / fuse as update. That three-way verb set is the dedup vocabulary the collapsed law:/case_laws repair needs (300 rows onto one id is what happens when the fuse-as-update decision is made by id collision instead of by a reconciliation pass).
- **What Eviction Destroys (2609.08279, abs read):** the restore-counterfactual audit - reinject the gold evidence and rerun the same reader, then classify each error as recoverable (retrieval failure), irreversible (evidence evicted), or residual. Finding: at 8k budgets the irreversible share hits 1.00 for ALL four eviction policies; at 80k with top-k retrieval it runs 0.60-0.73. Estate mapping: a falsifiable instrument for the residual-vs-registry boundary - it MEASURES which compaction choices destroyed information permanently, the memory-level audit the descent protocol's falsifier ("ledger grew but no rib carries the contribution forward") wants. Not banked: MemSentry's deterministic Accept/Review/Quarantine write gate is already folded (`memory/runs/mickey17_v2/FOLD_SECTIONS.md:48`) and mined into trust_gate.v2 (`memory/capability_manifest.md:947`).
