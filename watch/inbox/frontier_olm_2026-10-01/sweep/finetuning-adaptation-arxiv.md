## Answers to the four research questions

**Q1 - QLoRA/LoRA practice 2B-8B on one consumer GPU + data-quality gates.**
2609.24177 (legal distill, 2026-09-21): 4-bit NF4 + rank-32 QLoRA on a Gemma-2-9B teacher to a 2B student; a generation gate kept 9,429 of 14,514 QA pairs (65% acceptance); adapter ships at 1.6 GB GGUF Q4_K_M and runs 4-8 tokens/s offline on a Pixel 6. 2609.40236 (radiology, 2026-09-30): fine-tuning and inference fit a single 24 GB consumer GPU at 12B scale, and the training-data SOURCE dominated the adaptation METHOD choice. 2609.23916 (rolling CPT, 2026-09-20): LoRA at sufficient rank matches full continued pretraining; data quality dominates quantity (curated 6B tokens matches a 40B slurry); the learning-rate optima for knowledge acquisition vs general capability sit roughly an order of magnitude apart. The estate's own stored copy of this abstract (state/arxiv/2026-09-22/2609.23916v1.txt line 6) carries the same recipe sentence, byte-consistent with my fetch.

**Q2 - Does fine-tuning beat retrieval for domain jargon at 2B scale?**
2609.10155 (2026-09-09) is the cleanest measured ablation found in-window: DoRA adapters measurably write an individual text corpus into SLM weights (held-out-text fit, dz = 1.27), and on the knowledge test "retrieval adds nothing on top" of fine-tuning. But the companion 2609.08532 (2026-09-08) warns on scale: of several LLMs only Qwen3-1.7B was viable, and knowledge-gap prediction improved only for corpora exceeding five million tokens - the estate's 14,859 session rows are likely far below that floor. 2609.03454 (2026-09-03, already cataloged): unconditional retrieval LOWERED quality and added safety failures for a QLoRA-tuned generator; a lightweight draft-conditioned gate (utility scores + rule trigger) deciding when to retrieve beat always-retrieve.

**Q3 - Session logs to instruction pairs, provenance gated per row.**
2609.16096 (2026-09-14) gives the conversion recipe actually used in 2026: teacher agent trajectories rewritten into explicit [obs][thought][act] chains, then trajectory-quality-weighted SFT that reweights samples toward post-edit checking (the motivation: offline SFT cannot verify whether a generated action was valid). 2609.12353 (SynthSentry, 2026-09-11) is a PRE-TRAINING corpus screen for synthetic contamination of unknown provenance (lexical diversity collapse + n-gram tail truncation + cross-model perplexity variance), but its granularity is corpus-level, not per-row - per-row provenance stamping is the gap the estate would have to close itself. 2609.40236 supplies the gate's shape: synthetic data failed to beat the un-tuned base at every training size while real-distilled labels matched GPT-4o (macro-F1 0.845 vs 0.850), so provenance class (real vs synthetic) is a first-class per-row field.

**Q4 - Catastrophic forgetting under continual adaptation of small models.**
2609.39367 (2026-09-30, theory): in a solvable two-task model, low-rank updates lessen interference BUT forgetting keeps increasing with adapter rank, and LoRA's initialization slows adaptation; masking the hidden units carrying the strongest old-task representations cuts forgetting while keeping plasticity. 2609.23916 (2026-09-20, empirics): time-incremental CPT on OVERLAPPING (non-disjoint) rolling data showed no catastrophic forgetting (13-task macro-average within 0.01 of base, 5/6 models also improved pre-cutoff recall) - the disjoint-stream continual-learning lens is a poor fit for a rolling corpus loop. Also in-window: 2609.06986 (composing anchors + merged LoRA lifts 100-task retention from 1.2% to 34.9%), 2609.34453 (SPACE-LoRA activation-subspace protection).
