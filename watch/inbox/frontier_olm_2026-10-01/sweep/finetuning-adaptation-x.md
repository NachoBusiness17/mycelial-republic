## Answers to the four research questions (X-door edition)

**Q1 - QLoRA/LoRA practice for 2B-8B on a single consumer GPU.** The 2026 X consensus is stable and the estate can copy it verbatim: 4-bit quantized frozen base + low-rank adapters, 24 GB is the comfort ceiling and smaller cards work for 2B-4B. techNmak's six-variant taxonomy (09-20, 125k views) gives the selection rule the tier ladder needs: LoRA cuts trained weights, LoRA-FA additionally targets activation memory, QLoRA compresses the frozen base, VeRA shrinks the adapter itself, Delta-LoRA lets base weights drift, LoRA+ splits learning rates. LightningAI shipped a runnable studio template (09-22) that fine-tunes Muse Glimmer locally with Unsloth - vision tower, language model, or both - and saves either as LoRA adapters or a merged model. jvr0x's walkthrough (09-22) is the honest-practice datum: Qwen3-4B on a DGX Spark, standard LoRA LR 2e-4 per the search snippet, and the critique that tutorials default to FineTome-100k generic Q&A - exactly the data the estate should NOT tune its seats on. The arXiv sibling sweep (finetuning-adaptation-arxiv.md) carries the measured consumer-GPU numbers (rank-32 NF4 QLoRA 9B-teacher to 2B student, 24 GB at 12B); this X pass adds the practice culture around them.

**Q2 - Does fine-tuning beat retrieval for domain jargon at 2B scale?** The strongest measured ablation reachable through the X door is actually a paper the search engine surfaced when it ignored the site filter: UCI's arXiv 2601.07054 (2026-01-11, OUTSIDE the 30-day window, flagged). Three 7B models, 10k+ multi-hop questions on post-cutoff 2024 events: unsupervised fine-tuning (continual pretraining) gives "only limited gains", RAG "more than doubles accuracy" for temporally novel knowledge, but supervised fine-tuning with LoRA (r=16, alpha=32 on query/value projections) is "highest overall accuracy across models and datasets" (Llama2-7B-Instruct QASC 0.864 SFT vs 0.757 RAG; 2024-Events 0.884 vs 0.753). Combined with the sibling sweep's 2609.10155 ("retrieval adds nothing on top" of fine-tuning for a written corpus), the reading for the estate is: estate-law vocabulary enters weights via labeled instruction pairs (SFT), not via raw-log continued pretraining; retrieval stays the tool for facts that change. At 2B the corpus floor matters - sibling 2609.08532 says knowledge-gap gains needed corpora over five million tokens, and the estate's 14,859 session rows (per the ask) are likely under it.

**Q3 - Session logs to instruction pairs, provenance-gated per row.** The X door's best find is cv_usk's 09-09 framing of NeoHorse-1 (arXiv 2609.08183): "Every interaction your deployed AI agent handles could double as training data to make it smarter." I read the full arXiv page: routing logs record "the predicted capability demand, selected service tier, and subsequent interaction for each user turn"; those records become training examples that "preserve interleaved reasoning, tool calls, and harness context"; admission runs through structural validation, six-dimensional semantic evaluation, and subscene-level labeling; routing signals organize SFT into a three-stage curriculum. Numbers: 4B 58.94 to 64.87, 9B 65.60 to 69.04 macro-average across eleven benchmarks; the tweet adds "+6.26" over public synthetic data - a claim NOT in the held abstract, banked as tweet-attributed. This is the estate's code-agent-data organ almost exactly (room.jsonl rows = routing-harness logs), and its provenance gate is the concrete steal: structural check + multi-dimension semantic score + label, per row, before a row enters the SFT set. No secrets-scrubbing-specific pipeline surfaced in-window (search door degraded); the held techyoutbe article (2026-07-19, outside window) carries the generic "detect and redact PII before training" line. OWED: an in-window X-native PII/provenance-gate post.

**Q4 - Catastrophic forgetting under continual adaptation of small models.** The deep read of the window: JHU's "Continual Learning Mechanisms Compose for Long-Horizon Memorization" (arXiv 2609.06986, 2026-09-07; alphaXiv post 09-09). 100-query-answer tasks learned by continual SFT with no retained examples and no task IDs: naive sequential fine-tuning ends at 1.2% average retention (~98.8% forgotten, the sibling sweep's registry line stops here); best single mechanism 8.1%; composing data anchor (replay), function anchor (self-distillation), weight anchor (regularization), and merged LoRA reaches 34.9%, "a 28-fold improvement". New numbers from this pass's full abstract read: naive half-life is "one or two tasks" vs 19/32/44 tasks for the best compositions across three datasets; replay plus merged LoRA is super-additive (Real-QA 46.9 points over naive vs ~13.9 predicted additively); the weight anchor can REDUCE retention on Symbol-QA, so the composition must be measured, not assumed. Law for the estate's rolling corpus loop: every training cycle banks a replay mix drawn from prior windows, and the sibling's rank warning (2609.39367: forgetting grows with adapter rank) says keep ranks modest. The estate already lives this shape - replay + merged adapters is structurally the residual + registry discipline.
