## Findings per research question

Q1 reasoning tax (measured cost/latency of thinking tokens): When2Think (2609.19671, 09-17) NAMES the phenomenon ("efficiency tax") and measures it on AIME24: +10.0pp Pass@3 while cutting tokens 27.9%. RATIO (2609.39801, 09-30) measures the tax where the estate actually lives, QUANTIZED local models: 3-bit quantization both degrades accuracy AND lengthens CoT; token-specific penalties recover up to +9.8 acc with CoT cut up to 51.3% (AWQ-W3 on Qwen-1.5B: 30.88%->40.66%, CoT -51.34%). GreenBench (2608.28667, 08-24, flagged: 7 days outside window) gives the janitor-side numbers: M4 Pro/Ollama v0.21.2, Llama-3.2-3B 115.2 tok/s task-avg (175.9 peak), Qwen2.5-7B 59.0 tok/s. GAP, stated plainly: arXiv carries NO measured glm-5.3-prime / qwen3.8-max-prime / grok-4.7 thinking-token pricing; provider-side price evidence lives in FACET (already held: same model priced up to 8.8x apart across providers, latency up to 68x, price-vs-latency Spearman -0.61 in 21/21 cells) and in the estate's own usage.jsonl. The quantified cross-lane reasoning tax remains an estate-measurement job; RATIO's finding implies local thinking is ALSO inflated (quantization worsens it), so burn-ledger comparisons premium-vs-local must use tokens-to-correct, not tokens.

Q2 speculative decoding / drafting on small local models: SEED (2609.36590, 09-29) is the direct fit: self-speculative, NO external draft model, last 2 layers as decoder, "2.6x on Qwen3-1.7B-Base and 2.7x on Qwen3-4B-Base" (63.2 and 43.3 tok/s vs 34.2/24.0 AR on one A6000), and 28% faster than EAGLE-3, whose published 5x+ claims collapsed to ~2x on this hardware. Caveat kept: SEED fine-tunes the target (not drop-in on stock gemma:2b/GGUF). UBTree (2609.39972) reports 5.84-6.94x but at datacenter scale. H-Spec (2609.24197) removes drafter-side KV cache (batch-1 latency +5.3-12.6%).

Q3 cheap-vs-premium routing with measured deltas (cool/heat lane map candidates): three strong entries. RSI-Router (2609.34712, 09-28): subtask-level big/small routing DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash ($0.30/$1.20 per M in/out) vs Qwen3.5-9B ($0.01/$0.04): beats big-only at 48.3% cost; ALFWorld 98.44 vs 90.62 at -75.3% cost; Terminal-Bench 2.0 +16.7% perf at -18.0% cost. Notable negative result: a small model routing by direct difficulty estimate UNDERPERFORMED random routing; subtask-type routing is the signal that pays. SaveRouter (2609.37402, 09-29): routing must pay for its own supervision; 33-41% of feedback suffices; break-even volume cut 1.9-9.5x; more supervision is not always economically better. Task-Type Routing (2608.23023, flagged: 37 days old): across 21 routing methods, nearly all the achievable gain is a STATIC task-type->model table: "assigning each task type one model in advance... improves 21 of the 29" improvable questions; the static table answered 262/294 at $3.33/run vs best single model 245 at $7.69. Estate read: the lane map (gemma:2b scut -> gemma4 worker -> flash -> primes) IS a static table; the paper says that table captures most of what a learned router would, and run-to-run scoring noise (5.37% of pairs) exceeds the residual learned-router edge.

Q4 concurrency / rate-limit envelopes / prefix reuse vs the estate's rolling-throughput finding: no arXiv paper measured a hosted-provider RPS/429 decay curve comparable to AGENTS.md's width law (width 10 clean, 15-16 untested, rolling-throughput limit). Nearest neighbors: FACET's provider latency dispersion (68x, 18x throughput spread within one model across providers) says per-provider envelope measurement is mandatory before width claims transfer across lanes; "Who Pays" (held) reports GPU util pegged at 97-99% across 2-16 configured req/s (3.7-26.9 completed req/s) on an H100 vLLM, i.e., provider-side saturation looks like high utilization, not errors, until throttling; NebulaSD (2609.29364) pools draft/target workers and lifts request-round rate 50.4-72.6% under concurrency; RR-Evict (2609.32278) cuts agent-workload P99 TTFT up to 75.4% via prefix-cache eviction tuning; AgentReplay (2609.32283) warns benchmarking with sampled (not recorded) output tokens distorts serving comparisons. Prefix-reuse doctrine, externally confirmed (Fancy Eviction, held): recency/LRU is the foundation; fancy eviction adds little because agentic prefix reuse is paced by active sessions.

Q5 throughput-per-dollar on estate-class hardware (llama.cpp vs vLLM vs Ollama): no single 2026-09 benchmark paper compares all three stacks on identical consumer hardware; two adjacent results cover the space. GreenBench (Ollama/llama.cpp on M4 Pro 48GB): 3-3.8B models deliver 2.6-4.2x higher throughput than 7-9B; theta proportional to N^-0.85 (R^2=0.94); Qwen2.5-7B is the Pareto pick at 57% MMLU / 59 tok/s; 0.09-0.25 J/token system-level; code gen is 5-8x more energy-hungry than MMLU. Cascadia (2609.38697, 09-30): commodity Intel AIPC fleet (CPU+iGPU+NPU), no control plane, three-node Phi-3.5-mini NPU testbed 3.10x single-node throughput at ten concurrent requests; four-node 4.06x; optional KV-cache mobility with cold recompute on miss. Serving-stack confound warning for any estate eval of janitors (2609.26693, held): Ollama 0.30.8 gates the tools request per model by a static template flag (Phi-3, Gemma-3 rejected with HTTP 400 BEFORE inference); turn-pooled vs per-instance estimates differ by up to ~55 points; the harness checklist (log serving-level failure as first-class outcome; per-seed rates with intervals; pin stack+version) applies to every janitor benchmark the estate runs. Backend-choice effect size is not small either: 2608.04714 (Aug, flagged) attributes ~39% of benchmark-score variability to the inference framework (3 models x 5 frameworks x 6 benchmarks x 4 generation modes).
