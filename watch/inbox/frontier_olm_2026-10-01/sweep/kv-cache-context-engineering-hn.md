## Findings (strongest first)

### 1. Keeping vLLM's Prefix Cache Warm Between Agent Turns - doug.sh, 2026-09-18 (HN 49758259, 3 pts)
https://doug.sh/posts/vllm-kv-cache-agents/
Q1 bullseye: how vLLM's GPU prefix cache actually keys, and what breaks it in agent loops. Blocks are matched by a hash chain (each block plus all blocks before it); lookup walks the prompt front-to-back and stops at the first non-matching block: "a change at token 12,000 of a 120,000-token prompt throws away 108,000 tokens of finished work." Root cause in the wild: nondeterministic serialization. Their MCP tool schemas arrived with shuffled JSON keys (Go map iteration upstream), and since Qwen's chat template renders tool definitions before the system prompt, "one shuffled schema invalidated everything behind it." Fixes, measured on a 120k-token-turn agent: sort JSON keys in the template (`tojson(sort_keys=True)`), stable-first/volatile-last prompt layout, compact as rarely as possible, append-only tool results. Cached share of prompt tokens went 55% -> 95% (the sort fix alone 78% -> 95%); TTFT 26-28s -> 7.3s; worst case 514s -> 54s. Cold prefill ~700-900 tok/s vs ~3s for 2,000 appended tokens. Measure cached-token share, not hit counts. Hybrid-attention caveat: cache granularity becomes 2048-token blocks, not 16.
Estate: this is the byte-identical law of `corpus/steal/ZCODE_OPERATING_PLAYBOOK_20260925.md:10` ("Amend-cache is keyed on byte-identical ask text") restated at the serving layer, with numbers: shuffled keys = total cache loss behind the mutation point. Direct input to hermes carrier turn-shaping and any mag lane that talks to a self-hosted prefix-caching server. Turn-2 no-echo defect class: appending results is safe; mutating anything upstream of position N silently forfeits everything behind it.

### 2. LRU is harder to beat than the KV-cache papers suggest - github.com/gauravapiscean/agentic-kv-cache, 2026-09-10 (HN 49643543, 108 pts, 58 comments)
https://github.com/gauravapiscean/agentic-kv-cache
Q3: eviction policy for long agent sessions, measured not theorized. Replayed 68,266 requests from 393 real Claude Code sessions plus Mooncake traces through a block-granular prefix-cache simulator; LRU over radix leaves (the production baseline) beat LFU, TTL-300s, and a three-component learned policy (+H/+HC/+HCG: Bayesian return predictor, position-dependent recompute cost, session-granularity eviction). At 8,000 blocks: 83.48% hit rate for LRU-leaf vs 82.89/71.77/68.63 for the challengers; LFU 63.61%. The useful part is the decomposition of recompute: only 17.5% of recompute tokens follow gaps >5 minutes, while 33.1% come from gaps under 10 seconds. Quote from the author's top-level comment: "The dominant waste is tight tool loops whose 88k-token working sets exceed cache capacity, not sessions idling past a TTL." A 5-minute TTL produced byte-identical results to plain LRU-leaf in every run (capacity-bound, not TTL-bound). Harness falsifier worth stealing wholesale: Belady (the offline oracle) losing to LRU exposed a broken simulator that let policies evict the prefix they were building; their rule: "If you build one of these, make your first test 'does Belady beat LRU?'" Thread pushback worth keeping: imtringued notes prefix caching makes turns 1-50 cheap but quadratic attention still makes later turns cost more regardless; talolard (neocloud) says at GB300 scale with NVLink + KV offload to DRAM you can run wild TTLs and still hit SLA.
Estate: do not spend a lane building clever eviction for agent sessions; spend it on working-set size (the 88k-token loop) and capacity. Our janitor-first stack is capacity-poor, so the finding bites harder here, not softer.

### 3. Quantifying Context Rot - boolean.ai, 2026-09-16 (HN 49734038, 3 pts)
https://www.boolean.ai/blog/context-rot-quantified
Q4 numbers, and they are memweave-shaped. 20 terminal-bench v4 agentic coding tasks, prefill context injected at 0k / 250k / 500k tokens, GPT-5.6 Sol vs Claude Opus 5, with unrelated (Spark Q&A) vs task-related prefill. Unrelated context: GPT-5.6 Sol falls 31% -> 24% pass rate at 250k; Opus 5 flat within noise. Related context: per the post, Opus 5 "now drops from 50% pass rate to 40% pass rate, when the context is related to task." Task-RELEVANT context hurt a frontier model more than irrelevant filler did. GPT-5.6 Sol shows "momentum-induced collapse" at 250k+: it snaps back to answering the earlier Q&A instead of the task. They correct for tokenizer drift (~1 GPT token ~ 1.7 Claude tokens) by re-plotting in MB of UTF-8.
Estate: direct justification for memweave structure-feeding and context-pack budgeting: raw bytes of related context are not free quality, they are a measured tax (10 points of pass rate on a frontier model). Budget context-pack to the smallest structure that carries the bonds; carrier echoes should ride in appended, fresh sessions, not long prefills. Overlap: "context rot" as a concept is already held in 10 memory files (e.g. `memory/steal/CONTEXT_WINDOW_HOP_RIGHTSIZING_20260810.md`); this specific related-vs-unrelated measurement is new.

### 4. DeepSeek-v4.1 Flash: Pushing the Limits of KV Cache Compression - zartbot.github.io, 2026-09-17 (HN 49735410, 131 pts, 10 comments)
https://zartbot.github.io/blog/model_arch/dsv41flash_arch/en.html
Q3: what 2026-grade KV compression looks like at 1M context. FP4 (MXFP4-style) main KV via QAT, SWA local KV held at FP8 for sensitivity; 512-dim latent sharing across heads (MLA-style); encoder merges 2 adjacent positions into 1 cache entry; CSA2 cross-layer sharing (3 encoder + 1 decoder copies of global KV, Full/Reindex/Reuse modes); a Hierarchical Sparse Indexer restricting later layers to a 16,384-position candidate pool; Causal Encoder-Decoder where only the first 20 of 40 layers process most prompt tokens. Quote: "Combined with FP4 quantization, the storage growth of the global main KV and the Indexer is about 890 bytes per token." Net: ~1/4 the runtime KV and ~1/8 the persistent storage of the predecessor at the same sequence length, 552B total params, ~420 tok/s reported. Notably: NO eviction is described; the design buys headroom with compression and sharing instead. Thread signal: mmastrac ran an incremental context compactor over it at "an effective session length of 5M, with the model running around 300k-400k and it was holding on both speed and intelligence."
Estate: if the remote-specialist scarce tier moves to v4.1-Flash-class models, per-token KV cost drops ~4-8x, which changes the long-session economics of the carrier. Also a template: compress-and-share beats evict for quality preservation.

### 5. Improving Throughput by Optimising KV Cache Efficiency for Agentic Workloads - j9s.io, 2026-09-14 (HN 49701675, 3 pts)
https://j9s.io/posts/agentic_inference/
Q3, self-hosted and immediately applicable. Agentic traffic shape: median ~15,146 prefill tokens per turn, ~501 decode, tool-result gaps of 0.18s median (vs 96.24s chat) - so blocks are evicted during tool gaps and must be recomputed on return (the "rebuild tax"); under load, "prefix reuse drops precipitously as request's blocks are evicted during their gaps (tool calls) in order to admit new requests." Three levers, measured: FP8 KV quantization (`--kv-cache-dtype fp8_e4m3`) halves per-token cost 256KiB -> 128KiB and "moved the elbow closer to a concurrency of around c=40 (up from around c=20)"; CPU-RAM offload at 120GB kept prefix reuse near 100% through the sweep (40GB saturated and cycled); and the main contribution, madvise-style eviction hints from a proxy (`dontneed` for finished trajectories, `pageout` when the expected gap exceeds reload time, `willneed_soon`/`willneed_later`), driven by per-tool EWMA gap durations, lifting GPU-local hit rates 1.06-1.44x and throughput 1.04-1.27x (author flags run-to-run confounds).
Estate: the hint idea maps cleanly onto our own orchestration: we KNOW when a lane's turn is done (dontneed), when a tool call is short (keep), when a session resumes tomorrow (pageout). A proxy emitting those hints is a steal-map item for any vLLM lane.

### 6. The KV cache as an agent runtime - research.yandex.com, 2026-09-05 (HN 49578969 and 49598646)
https://research.yandex.com/blog/the-kv-cache-as-an-agent-runtime
Q1/Q3 frame shift: stop treating KV as a decoding cost center. "The KV cache is the model's active execution state." Partition the cache into reusable blocks stored once in block-local coordinates (per-block RoPE rotations), then give concurrent workers different logical views over the same physical blocks: Hogwild-style parallel instances that see each other's partial generations, separate thinker/writer streams (AsyncReasoning), and append-only I/O streams so new observations join a persistent state without restarting from the original prompt. Measured (AsyncReasoning paper): up to 80x reduction in time to first non-thinking token, up to 12x total user-perceived delay, keeping much of reasoning's accuracy.
Estate: this is the physics under the RIB/mesh design - shared state, per-seat logical views, append-only joins. If a frontier lab ships a serving API for multi-view blocks, the mesh's fan-out shape gets a native primitive. Watch item, not a build item.

### 7. Faster prompt lookup drafting in llama.cpp - jadidbourbaki.github.io, 2026-09-26 (HN 49859982, 89 pts, 12 comments)
https://jadidbourbaki.github.io/blog/prompt-lookup-llama-cpp/
Q1, llama.cpp internals. Prompt lookup decoding: "Prompt lookup decoding is technically a special case of speculative decoding that uses a really stupid draft model, an n-gram model." llama.cpp keeps three n-gram caches (context/dynamic/static) counting followers; drafting scores the vocab by follower count with a 100x boost when the static cache agrees. The work is data-structure engineering: no-copy inner maps (drafting 4.5-25.6x faster), segmented dense map, sorted-vector binary search, Lemire binary-fuse constmap for the static cache (load 3.76s -> 0.23s on a 541MB corpus), threshold precheck (overall up to ~140x on the drafting leg), memory up to 2.6x lower. Acceptance rates held identical; workload was WikiText-103 replay on an M4 Pro. Honest caveat, stated in the thread too (S0y asked, nobody answered): these are drafting-latency and cache-load numbers, NOT end-to-end tokens/sec gains - the user-facing win depends on acceptance rate against real prompts. Side drama: the author was banned from llama.cpp over an accidental fork-tag PR, so the optimization lives on a fork (Lemire contributed there).
Estate: for our local janitors, this says llama.cpp n-gram drafting is cheap to keep on (prompt-repetitive workloads are exactly ours), but do not bank a tok/s claim until someone measures one.

## Cross-cutting synthesis
1. Byte-stability is now a measured, named discipline at the serving layer, not just a workflow-cache trick: shuffled JSON keys behind the tool-schema render point nuked 95%->78% cached share (item 1); frontier APIs enforce a 1024-token minimum prefix on top (item 8). The ZCODE playbook's byte-identical law extends verbatim: stable bytes first, append-only after, sort all serialization.
2. Eviction: LRU-leaf is the baseline to beat and nobody beat it on real agent traces; the waste is capacity (88k-token working sets in tight tool loops), not idle TTLs (items 2, 5). Buy capacity (quantize, offload) before buying cleverness.
3. Quantization at 2026 lengths: FP4/FP8 KV with sharing (latent, cross-layer, sequence-merge) holds quality at ~890 B/token and 1M context, with no eviction at all (item 4); plain FP8 doubles resident concurrency (item 5).
4. Degradation: at 250k-500k prefills, task-RELATED context is a measured quality tax (50%->40% pass rate on a frontier model), worse than irrelevant filler - the strongest public number yet for feeding structures instead of prose, and for keeping carrier echoes in fresh sessions (item 3).
