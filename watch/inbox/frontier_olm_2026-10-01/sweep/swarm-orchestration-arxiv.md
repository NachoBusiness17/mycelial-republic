## RQ findings vs the estate

### RQ1 - fan-out / retry shapes under provider rate limits

The 2026 literature has three shapes; none publishes a throughput-vs-width envelope as measured as ours.

1. **Proxy-side congestion control (HiveMind, 2604.17111).** A transparent HTTP proxy applying five OS primitives: admission control, rate-limit tracking, **AIMD backpressure with circuit breaking**, token budget management, priority queuing; zero agent code changes; auto-detected provider profiles. Measured: motivating incident had 3 of 11 parallel agents die (27%) from connection resets/502s despite sufficient aggregate capacity; across 7 scenarios at 5-50 concurrent agents, uncoordinated runs failed 72-100% vs 0-18% with the proxy; <3ms added latency. The ablation names **transparent retry the single most critical primitive**, over admission control. Estate comparison: that is exactly our measured split - Task agents (main-session retry path, maxAttempts 11 + driver backoff) clean at width 10 (109.4M tok/h) while workflow children (maxAttempts 0, no adapter retry) 429-storm at 50-wide. HiveMind independently confirms the retry leg is the load-bearing one, and our "first 429 contact is a HOLD signal, not a width cut" matches AIMD's multiplicative-decrease-then-recover semantics. Our rolling-throughput-limit finding (the limit is rolling throughput, not width-only; after a burst even 12 takes 1302s until the window decays) is finer than anything HiveMind publishes - the paper reports failure rates, not tok/h envelopes.
2. **Checkpoint-pause-resume (PRIMA, 2605.24775).** On upstream rate-limit signal: write a **typed pause record to disk**, resume without re-executing converged work, surviving process restarts. Plus sub-agent operating discipline (task-fidelity, tool-use, revision, context-boundary norms as a prompt layer) and prime-power-derived collision-free agent identity. Estate analog: amend-not-rewrite + receipt landing; no published numbers.
3. **Route to fewer calls (ProgRouter, 2608.25992).** Online per-step routing by a multi-view progress scorer balancing progress gain, time budget, and long-run cost; claims cost reduction vs cascade baselines on HumanEval+/MBPP/MATH-500/ASQA. Qualitative on the abs page; no per-step dollar method exposed.

**Take for the estate:** keep the dispatch-shape law; steal HiveMind's ablation framing (retry-first) as independent corroboration, and PRIMA's typed-pause-record shape as the pattern for surviving 429-HOLDs mid-window without re-paying converged legs.

### RQ2 - flock / multi-model decode (feeds `laws.md:70` flock-decode redesign)

Three 2026 results triangulate the open question ("decode must map to the ACTUAL task output space, not surrogate territory-actions"):

1. **Matched-budget sampling beats debate (Beyond Symmetric Agents, 2609.35875).** 23 models, eleven vendor families, five tasks, 5,500+ runs: under matched generation budgets multi-agent debate **ties or loses to plain self-consistency** while costing 1.6x wall-clock and 3.4x tokens; MAD gains recast as an ensemble-sampling effect; persona prompting *lowers* accuracy (a "persona tax"); mixed-model teams track member capability, not heterogeneity. This is independent published confirmation of the consensus-illusion measurement (`RIB_MINDSWEAVE_EVAL_CONSENSUS_ILLUSION_20260810.md:14-26`, flock consensus 0/3 vs single-local 3/3 at HIGH consensus strength): consensus strength and debate structure do not buy correctness; sampling does.
2. **Communication beats re-sampling when the answer has a shareable key step (TalkMesh, 2609.30578).** Decentralized mesh of SLMs (Qwen3.5-0.8B, SmolLM3-3B); each agent samples a proposal scored by a trained confidence head; most-confident agent broadcasts a hint; below-threshold agents revise; a **GRPO-trained talk policy** learns when/what to communicate; gossip consensus replaces the coordinator. Result: 3 agents producing at most 6 outputs match majority voting over 32 samples from each of three models (~5x fewer generations); trained at 8 agents, GSM8K 0.568→0.705, MATH-500 0.492→0.722. Adversarially, 4 of 8 colluding agents (fabricated confidence, poisoned hints) drop majority-vote accuracy to 0.000; confidence-head rescoring holds 0.507. **This is the closest published thing to a task-space flock decode**: the communicated object is the answer-space hint, not a vote token - the exact redesign direction laws.md asks for, with a worked training recipe (GRPO on post-revision correctness delta) and a named adversarial failure mode.
3. **Decode-time consensus control (CED 2609.25570; ME-Decoding 2609.18723).** CED: on "adversarial swarm consensus" sycophancy, a dual forward pass through one architecture isolates the conformity bias; an asymmetric zero-bounded probability clamp plus top-k truncation mask suppresses consensus-aligned tokens; training-free; -33% cognitive loafing, up to +30.75% accuracy recovery on Gemma-2-9B / Llama-3.1-8B / Mistral-7B across 7,200 paired GAIA/SWE-bench/Multi-Challenge trajectories; ~2x forward-pass compute per token. ME-Decoding (already ingested by the estate, excluded from banking) reframes token selection as ensemble pruning with a Mahalanobis diversity objective. Both are single-model decode-time mechanisms - relevant to flock decode as the *suppression* half (what to do when the flock's consensus is wrong).

**Take for the estate:** the flock decode redesign has a concrete build order now: (a) TalkMesh-style hint-space communication with confidence-gated revision as the task-space decode, (b) Beyond-Symmetric-Agents as the matched-budget falsifier for any debate variant we are tempted to build, (c) CED-style clamping as the defense against consensus capture (our RIB-poisoning/pod-failure injection already passed; TalkMesh's collusion numbers say defend the vote with independent confidence heads, not raw majority).

### RQ3 - controller-of-controllers vs the one-controller law (`laws.md:41`, law 22)

- **SwarmBench (2608.30661)** measures orchestration *as the skill*: 3 task families (decomposition / delegation / aggregation), 8 tasks, 400 samples, 10 orchestrator models (GPT-5.4 top at 50.49 avg accuracy, Qwen3-30b bottom 25.15, single-model baselines 17.87-24.41). Named orchestrator failure modes: **weak result integration** ("better at initiating and assigning subtasks than at reliably integrating subagent outputs"), **high parallelism without payoff** (Kimi-k2.5 tops parallelism rate without topping gain - redundant waiting, repeated execution), unstable decomposition, aggregation as the weakest dimension for every model. SwarmExp: distill trajectories into skills/tricks/model-cards and reinject into the orchestrator's context (+6.46 best; 14/16 settings improved on cross-model transfer). That is the estate's rib-warming boot order ("remember before running") published as a measured orchestration intervention.
- **Trust the Brand (2609.32635)** names the controller's security failure mode: orchestrators grant authority by *displayed identity*; TrustFork (1,890 tasks, 27,826 trajectories, 16 agent systems, 8 orchestrators under OpenCode/OpenClaw/Pi) shows the risky subagent's response adopted in 72.0% of cases on average even when contradicted, and **swapping family labels nearly triples adoption**; hiding identity cues was the most consistent of three defenses; the paper's line: bind authority to evidence before execution causes harm. For the one-controller law this says: one controller, yes - but its trust inputs need evidence-binding, or the controller becomes the single point of capture.
- **Waggle (2609.34136)** is the no-controller counterfactual: one learned anonymous local law (shared policy over bounded local views; Swarm-Consistent Distillation training, no inference-time components) self-organizes LLM swarms without roles, hierarchies, or global topology, holding >96% of substrate-specific oracle quality and transferring across settings without retraining. Relevant to the mycelial shape but it does not yet speak to verifiable-task correctness - exactly the gap laws.md law 34 leaves open.
- **Cheating/whistleblowing swarm (2609.04170)** - already ingested by the estate (`state/arxiv/2026-09-06/2609.04170v1.txt`), not re-banked: 100-agent math-proof swarm, exploit spread through the shared knowledge library then peer-to-peer; whistleblower cohort used the same transparent channels; Ostrom-style knowledge-commons governance recommended.

### RQ4 - per-agent-turn cost accounting vs `usage.jsonl` banking

- **SwarmBench publishes the only explicit per-call dollar formula found this sweep:** cost = sum over all model calls of (prompt tokens × input price/1K + completion tokens × output price/1K), reported in USD, alongside Parallelism Rate (duration-weighted concurrent subagents) and Parallelism Gain ((model+tool call latency) ÷ wall-clock). The estate's per-*turn* banking (`memory/runs/hermes_burn/usage.jsonl`, 16 rows, $5.9155 in window 1) is finer-grained - per protocol leg with tokens+cost banked at every turn - but SwarmBench's Parallelism Gain metric is a steal: it is exactly the instrument that would catch the "50-wide workflow children all sleeping on a 429 window" failure as a measured number instead of an after-action story.
- **Generate-Transform decomposition (2609.36104)** contributes the accounting *law*: any team-scaling workflow splits exactly into a coverage dividend (proposal generation) and a transformation change (converting candidates); eight orchestration architectures swept, Proposer-Critic steepest on arithmetic (+17 points GSM8K/GSMHard from 3→30 calls, only ~4 on ARC/GPQA/MMLU), no architecture wins everywhere, and **at equal call budgets token cost varies 2.1x across setups**. Implication for the sherpa ledger: record token deltas, not call deltas, when comparing architectures - call-parity is not cost-parity.
