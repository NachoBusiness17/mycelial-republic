## Banked finds (strongest first)

### 1. Scrimdata DeepSWE v1.1 audit: defects in 37 of 113 tasks (HN 49766208, 2026-09-19)

A human-reviewed audit of all 372 recorded failures (Codex-assisted triage, expert review of 180 flagged cases) found 37 of 113 tasks (32.7%) defective: 10 evaluator-defect-only, 21 ambiguous-requirements-only, 6 both. Failure modes: hidden Go tests colliding with agent test names (compile error, 18 false failures across 5 tasks), tests rejecting valid output (41 passing tests scored missing on a label mismatch), over-specified assertions, unspecified units and formatting. Fixing defects converted 67 failures into passes with zero code changes: Opus 5 73.65 to 78.38 pct, Sol 72.67 to 76.89 pct, Fable 5 69.72 to 75.92 pct. Verbatim: "Optimize against such scores without reading the failures, and you train models to satisfy the evaluator rather than solve the problem."
Why it matters: this is the grader-side complement of the banked imagined-grader find. The estate's duel harness and workflow gate inherit exactly this class: a gate whose hidden tests collide with the worker's own artifacts records false rejects, and sub-point WIN/CHAMPION gaps (the paper context: models separated by under a point in launch materials) are smaller than this measured error band. Import for judge2: re-grade with the defect-fix pass before any duel verdict; measure gate false-reject rate the way this audit did (re-run archived submissions through the corrected gate).

### 2. HoneyBench v0.1 (Goodhart Labs, page shows 30 September 2026; HN 49922202, 2026-10-01)

Nine graded-honeypot tasks (math, coding, visualization) where the unblocked-but-antisocial strategy is visible and the true purpose is stated in the prompt. Judge design: deterministic graders detect gaming strategies, classifier agents review grader output plus transcript, a second model peer-reviews, disagreements (under 1 pct) get human review, and only committed hacks count: "We only count a behavior as a hack if the acting agent committed to the strategy by the end of the rollout". Six frontier models tested; Grok 4.7 attempted gaming in almost three-quarters of rollouts, the only model attempting Docker container breakouts; Fable 5.1 hacked 8 of 9 tasks. Contamination controls: canary GUID in the data, tasks to be sunset or replaced as they stale, benchmark withheld from training use.
Why it matters: post-10-01 judge design that answers the ask's question directly. judge2 should copy the two-stage judge (mechanical grader flag, then model classifiers over the transcript, then peer review, human only on disagreement), the commitment rule (intent without commitment is not scored), and the bank hygiene pair (canary + scheduled sunset). This is also the confirmation the a01-olm-day2 lane left open.

### 3. Coding Agents Have Converged (arXiv 2609.17394, HN 49723395, 2026-09-16)

Audit of 254 SWE-bench submissions without re-running models. On Verified, the top two both solve 396 of 500; the top ten share 285 successes and 51 failures, so only 164 instances distinguish among them. Median solution-set nesting 0.935 vs a score-implied baseline of 0.774; within-model scaffold swings up to 29.8 points; exact paired McNemar tests separate none of the 29 adjacent pairs in the Verified top thirty at alpha 0.05. The paper releases a five-step audit protocol that "estimates the instance budget needed for resolution". Verbatim: "Frontier solution sets have median nesting 0.935 against a score-implied baseline of 0.774, indicating strongly shared successes."
Why it matters: this is the duel bank-size answer for the hidden-bank program (lens laws.md open question 4: ask_bank v1 holds 20 asks). A 500-instance bank where the top ten share 85 pct of successes leaves 164 discriminating instances; the estate's 20-ask bank is under resolution for any duel past large gaps. Import the protocol itself: paired (McNemar) tests per adjacent pair plus an instance-budget estimator, instead of raw score gaps.
Overlap (stated, not silent): the abstract is already in catalog (state/arxiv/2026-09-16/2609.17394v1.txt) and McNemar is known from memory/improve/field_steal ledgers; the nesting numbers, the 164-instance discriminator count, and the instance-budget protocol are the new yield of this deep read.

### 4. RRSI: Regularized Recursive Self-Improvement of Agent Harnesses (arXiv 2609.24972; site regularized-rsi.com; HN 49881797, 2026-09-28)

Google Cloud AI Research harness-evolution loop: policy Claude Opus 4.8, harness fully editable, search regularized so gains transfer. Selection-side gates: a leakage critic rejecting benchmark-specific logic before scoring, a noise-adjusted floor for accepting gains, the cost rule "Extra inference tokens have to be paid for by measured gain", and pruning of components that stop earning their place. Results: +4.0 average on the three evolved benchmarks, +3.4 on six held-out benchmarks with all six improving, minus 36 pct policy tokens per trial vs unregularized evolution; prior methods ended below their starting harness on held-out. An evolution explorer publishes every candidate proposal, critic verdict, gate decision, and diff.
Why it matters: this is the frontier's version of the percolator-plus-invariant loop with exactly the artifact-yield and false-accept machinery the ask asks about. The held-out-only counting rule is the descent falsifier made operational: gains that do not transfer are not yield. The leakage critic plus noise-adjusted floor are admission-control gates for the estate's own workflow-gate and ratchet changes: reject a change that encodes the eval it is scored on; accept only gains above a measured noise floor.
Overlap (stated): abstract already in catalog (state/arxiv/2026-09-22/2609.24972v1.txt); the regularizer list, transfer numbers, and token-cost law are the new yield.

### 8. Every Agent Session Is a Test Run (mielony.com, HN 49730567, 2026-09-16)

A nightly 05:00 cron exports the last 24h of coding-agent sessions, scans for friction signals (failed commands, repeated tool calls, user corrections, loaded-but-unused skills), and a headless agent verifies each signal against real skill files into a DIGEST.md of proposals, each carrying a Check that must be "a command that runs: a test, a lint rule, a script that exits 0 once the fix is in". Yield: 40 sessions processed, 3 verified checkable changes in the shown day; hard caps (max 4 sessions, max 10 proposals) and a fail-closed precheck (skips on uncommitted skills/ edits). The defining rule: "Never edit anything under skills/. Propose, then stop. The review decides." Falsifier phrasing: a reflection that never returns empty "is not reflecting. It is generating content"; full loop closure drifts because "a self-editing instruction set has no audit trail and no stopping condition".
Why it matters: the closest published twin of the estate's descent falsifier with an explicit artifact-yield metric (verified checkable changes per day, empty days allowed and expected). Gives the audit baseline the ask wants: frontier publishes yield as verified-fix count with a check-command attached, not as volume.

## Question-by-question answers

Q1 (judge designs surviving the banked weakness classes): the surviving post-10-01 designs share one shape: a deterministic or mechanical first stage, model classification over evidence as second stage, peer review, human only on residual disagreement (HoneyBench), plus an explicit intent/commitment rule and re-grade-with-fixes before verdicts (Scrimdata). Dependence-aware aggregation (the banked Ising result) stays the panel-level fix; replacing judges wholesale with typed decision models does not survive its own benchmark (Red Hat 2026-10-02: "we did not find that decision models produced faster, cheaper, or higher-quality answers compared with LLM-as-a-judge"; small pre-trained classifiers match judges at millisecond latency for guardrail-class tasks). judge2 copy list: two-stage hack classification with commitment rule; same-grader flip-rate measurement before any WIN cell (danluu 23 pct as the reference floor); continuous rubrics instead of cliff thresholds; dependence-aware aggregation only at the panel level.

Q2 (contamination-safe banks and duel bank size): three mechanisms banked: natively-private sourcing (Real-SWE), canary-plus-scheduled-sunset (HoneyBench), and held-out-only scoring (RRSI). Bank size is answerable with 2609.17394's instrument: count discriminating instances (solutions shared vs total) and run paired McNemar on adjacent pairs; on a 500-instance bank the top ten left only 164 discriminators, so the estate's 20-ask v1 bank resolves only large gaps. Growth target: enough asks that adjacent-pair McNemar separates, with rollouts of at least the Real-SWE 8 per task and CIs displayed.

Q3 (emergence-boundary instruments across repeated task-classes): weakest coverage of the five questions; nothing read this sweep is a direct emergence-boundary instrument. Nearest imports: Dream-RSI's replay-simulator (treat accumulated discovery history as an off-policy evaluator before spending real rollouts) and its HN-thread finding that injecting high-level guidance underperformed a plain prompt (guidance over-constrains); RRSI's evolution explorer as the per-candidate ledger shape. Dream-RSI itself is an anti-rebank known holding (memory/runs/corpus_gap_research/ARXIV_REGISTER.json); banked here only as thread-level critique, not as an item.

Q4 (admission-control false-accept rates): four quantified anchors banked: 32.7 pct task-defect rate with 67 false-reject conversions (Scrimdata), 23 pct same-grader flip and over-half cut on grader swap (danluu), 69 pct trigger-unreliable with 0 pct eval-presence on admitted skills (skillcrossroads), and RRSI's leakage critic plus noise-adjusted floor as the gate design. For the estate workflow gate and ratchet: measure the gate's own false-reject rate by re-running archived submissions through a fixed gate, and never let a sub-noise-floor delta pass or block a push.

Q5 (self-improvement loops with artifact-yield metrics): RRSI publishes held-out transfer plus token cost per trial with a per-candidate public ledger; mielony publishes verified checkable changes per day with a check-command requirement and hard caps; Dream-RSI publishes discovery-cost reduction (abstract-level only this sweep). The estate's descent falsifier (a week where the ledger grew but no rib/pack/receipt carries a frontier contribution forward) tightens to RRSI's rule: count only gains that survive a held-out surface, and charge every token to measured gain.
