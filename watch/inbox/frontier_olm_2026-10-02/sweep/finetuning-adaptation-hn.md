## Banked items (strongest first)

### 1. Sidekick's continual learning loop (Shopify Engineering, story 2026-09-04, item 49561583)
THE Q3 answer from production: a deployed agent adapts to its own traffic daily without rot. Mechanism: mine anonymized production traffic for judge-scored hard negatives; a frontier panel critiques each failure, an arbiter merges critiques into a repair instruction, the conversation is replayed and re-scored; passing replays become RL trajectories. Training is two-stage: SFT on full trajectories including chain-of-thought, then GRPO with the calibrated judge as reward. The forgetting law, verbatim-class: "Training on both new and previous trajectories limits drift and catastrophic forgetting across cycles" (post cites arXiv:2410.21228), plus "The self-healing pipeline runs daily, continually adding new trajectories to the training corpus." Evals that catch decay: judge backtested against prior A/B tests, targeted degradation tests (deliberately degrade one behavior, check the matching rubric criterion fires), Cohen's kappa on annotator agreement, and the warning "It's only a proxy, so you need to establish that it reflects performance on real traffic." Outcome numbers: GraphQL agent surpasses frontier quality at ~96% serving-cost reduction (about $27M/yr to ~$1M), 6,000-token prompts gisted to ~1,500 with no measured judge quality loss. Steal target: the seat-tape organ (lens/corpus.md:33-34) gets its missing per-cycle replay-and-regrade loop; the "new + previous trajectories mixed each cycle" rule is the anti-forgetting curation law the estate's SFT corpus plan lacks.

### 2. Fine-tuning Qwen3.5-4B to replace Gemini Flash-Lite (duane.sh, story 2026-09-29, item 49897103)
THE Q1 recipe, validated by task metrics not loss: LoRA rank 32 SFT (one pass, ~24k examples, 1.6h, ~$7) then DPO then GRPO; serves on one RTX 4090 24GB worker at $76/M excerpts, 71% cheaper than the Gemini model it replaced; the 9B variant needed 48GB and Gemma 4 26B-A4B needed 80GB for weights alone (gemma-class is NOT the consumer-VRAM pick at 26B; 4B is). Grader was Opus, blind, position-rotated, 631 excerpts. Shipped GRPO step-80 checkpoint: unsupported claims 7.9%, missed references 9.5%, usefulness 4.19/5; retrieval top-1 hit 62% to 76% vs 50% for production Gemini. The curation law is the banked mem0 lesson in reverse: "a student copies its teacher's mistakes along with everything else." First attempt trained on 23,800 saved production contexts from Gemini 2.5 Flash-Lite and copied its errors (~20% unsupported claims, 41% restated lines); the fix was fresh labels from a stronger teacher on randomly sampled positions across a year of documents (pilot: usefulness 4.36 vs 2.69, unsupported 2% vs 28%). DPO lesson: "Every pair rewarded explaining more, and none rewarded stopping where the source stops" (pairs must be the model's own mistakes). This is directly the estate's session-tape-to-SFT pipeline: do not train on your own frozen seat output alone; relabel with a stronger judge and sample across the whole tape, not the recent tail.

### 4. Mini-AGI: dynamic continual learning on 8GB VRAM (story 2026-09-21, item 49783133, 277pt/81c)
Q3 evidence with honest caveats. MoE where experts are added and pruned while training on one continuous stream; trunk LR at 0.1x expert LR keeps core weights stable. Author: "the finding is that slowing down the LR or the trunk, while keeping the LR of the experts is enough to eliminate most of the forgetting" (chess-only experiment; "There is no special algorithm"). Hardware: RTX 3070 Laptop 8GB, batch-1, expert offloading to disk. Thread pushback that the estate should bank alongside: abeppu "doesn't the trunk then _eventually_ still undergo catastrophic forgetting, it just may take much longer?"; jmatthews "the lever is essentially Biderman et als 'learn less, forget less'"; synctext "No relation to related work, no description of algorithm, no ablation study, just hand-waving"; lostmsu's benchmark counter (8M dense, ctx 64, enwik9 2h = 1.15 bpb). Bankable law: low trunk LR is a cheap forgetting brake for janitor-tier continual fits, and the eval that catches overclaiming is a fixed held-out probe plus a dense-batch baseline, exactly the two checks the thread ran on it.

### 6. The dataset where fine-tuning turned actively harmful (cacheverifier.com, story 2026-09-29, item 49889028)
Q2/Q3 eval design: a verifier fine-tuned on earlier customer-support traffic began approving more wrong answers than it caught when the gray-zone positive rate drifted ~5x; harmful-beats-nothing is the failure mode when the corpus is a moment instead of a distribution. Detection they shipped: chunked two-proportion z-test vs the training baseline, Page-Hinkley change-point test (catches gradual drift a z-test averages away), and two-sample KS on the verifier's own score distribution vs a baseline captured at fine-tune time. Law, verbatim-class: "The gray-zone positive rate ... isn't a fixed property of a task" and "decay is a property of non-stationary traffic, not of verification as a technique." Steal: any seat-tape SFT for the janitors needs a frozen-at-train-time score-distribution baseline and a Page-Hinkley monitor on replay, or the corpus organ ships a model that quietly rots (this is the instrumented version of the banked spec-mortality law).
