### 1. Auditing and Repairing LLM-as-Judge Failures in a Production Text-to-SQL Pipeline (arXiv 2609.30290v1, 2026-09-09), Haowei Liu, Hsin-Tai Wu, Yi Fang
https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30290
Quote: "Pairing the weak judge with a stronger one degrades agreement, whereas three strong judges under unanimity routing reach kappa = 0.79 at 89.7% auto-coverage."
Why it matters: the estate's RQ1, RQ2, and RQ3 answered in one production audit with pre-registration and code. The deployed premium judge had never been measured against humans and read kappa 0.04 on a disagreement-enriched set (0.42 uniform-random), over-flagging 77.1% of human-FAITHFUL cases, traced to one mechanism they name GRADE-HALLUCINATION. A self-hosted Qwen3.6-27B replacement scores kappa 0.72, in the same range as Claude Opus 4.7 (kappa 0.71), at roughly 1/300 the per-call cost; the authors state the head-to-head is underpowered at n = 96 and that this hardly matters for the deployment decision. Unanimity routing measured in the wild: it WORKS when the underlying judges are strong (kappa 0.79, 89.7% auto-coverage), which reconciles with the 1wdasar anti-signal at 120B: unanimity is an anti-signal exactly when the panel is miscalibrated, and a coverage filter when it is not. Also the cheapest possible falsifier for the estate's funded judge tier: measure judge-human kappa before trusting any judge, premium or local.

### 2. Can We Trust LLM Judges: A Study of Capability-Dependent Biases and Multi-Judge Ensemble for Bias Calibration (arXiv 2609.12002v1, 2026-09-10), Gemma Zhang, Prachi Badarayani, Asmi Kumar, Sadid Hasan, Sulaiman Vesal
https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12002
Quote: "our label-free WMV tracks an oracle with perfect error-rate knowledge to within 0.5 percentage points on average, outperforming both individual judges and unweighted majority voting."
Why it matters: RQ1's weighting mechanism for a local ensemble. On 36 judge-examinee pairs (4 benchmarks x 6 models), a model's task accuracy strongly predicts its judging accuracy (Pearson r >= 0.90 on most models) and inversely predicts directional bias (r <= -0.83), yet all judges grow more lenient toward stronger examinees (r >= 0.83). Their calibrated weighted majority voting derives per-judge false-positive/false-negative rates purely from inter-judge disagreement patterns, no ground-truth labels, and tracks the oracle within 0.5pp. That is the missing piece for the estate's local-ensemble-vs-funded-judge duel design: weight the panel by estimated error rates instead of trusting unanimity or averaging.
