## Findings per research question

### RQ1. 2-4 bit weight quantization quality retention on 2-8B models on agentic/tool-use tasks

- **The window's honest answer is still: nobody on the HN surface benches 2-8B models on agentic tasks as a function of bit-width.** The instrument of record remains 27B-class (Quesma Qwen3.8 27B, held 09-28: "if you go with a 4-bit quantization Q4_K_M (17GB), you won't notice a difference on these benchmarks"; 1-bit collapses to random-chance on GPQA Diamond; Terminal-Bench 2.1 as the agentic instrument). VeriLoop's ladder (held 09-30) measures logit-retention vs a frozen BF16 root, and says so plainly: "these are quantization-retention measurements, not downstream benchmark-loss percentages", with per-quant agentic reruns explicitly not done ("The parent nine-benchmark campaign was not independently rerun for every quantization tier").
- **NEW practitioner confirmation of the agentic cliff (Magnitude founders, Launch HN thread, banked below):** their catalog deliberately stops at 4-bit weights because "going below 4-bit quickly leads to serious issues with thinking, tool calls, and overall model coherence." That is a shipped-stack product decision keyed to exactly the failure mode RQ1 asks about (tool calls), on agent-serving workloads, and it lands the cliff between 4-bit and 2-bit, consistent with Quesma.
- **2-8B-specific held-out agentic quant evidence: not found this window (OWED, repeat of the 09-30 sweep's honest gap).** Nearest coverage is still indirect.

### RQ3. Is KV quantization co-designed with weight quantization in shipped stacks, and the joint quality cost

- **YES, and this window moved it from paper to product three times over.** (1) Magnitude ships KV cache quantization (TurboQuant-inspired) as a core feature next to 4-bit weights: 8-bit keys, 4-bit values, cutting KV memory by over half, with a RULER-based retrieval benchmark behind the claim it "does not seem to negatively impact retrieval or coherence"; the founders' honest cost statement is selective precision, same shape as the held DeepSeek v4.1 Flash design (MXFP4 main KV + FP8 where "sensitive to quantization, FP8 precision is retained"; catalog holds the tech report, 8 rows). (2) Lifeboat's container image ships "the 2x concurrency, FP8 KV cache, speculative decoding and safetensors weights" as one feature bundle. (3) The estate already holds the pattern's paper record: kvmem_digest.md:34 (Q8 weights + FP8 KV), catalog 19b9109a137c (2609.04098, NVFP4 W4A4 GatedDeltaNet), and the Quesma thread's skolos runs NVFP4 weights AND cache on an RTX 5090 ("264k cache allocated for pool, 10k tok/s prompt processing, 200 tok/s generation"), reporting coding agents "regularly reach 200k context used without noticeable degradation", citing the held 2609.04098 that KV quant has "almost no impact on this model all the way to q4".
- **Joint quality cost honestly reported:** no shipped stack publishes a joint weight-x-KV bit-matrix benchmark. Magnitude's RULER check is retrieval-only; skolos's "no noticeable degradation" is anecdote; DeepSeek keeps SWA KV at FP8 precisely because sensitivity is layer/type-selective. The field's implicit law matches the estate's auto_quantize invariant: quantize by measured sensitivity, keep the sensitive spine high-precision.

### RQ4. Does quantization move free-lane token economics enough to shift the cheap-first threshold

- **The bit-width lever itself is saturated at 4-bit.** Below 4-bit the quality cost turns nonlinear exactly where the spend laws care (agentic tool loops): Magnitude floors at 4-bit for product reasons; Quesma (held) shows 2-bit still at Opus-4.7/Gemini-3.1-Pro level on Terminal-Bench but visibly degraded, 1-bit collapsed; the held 2606.00206 shows quantized models overthink (longer CoT for less accuracy), so lower bits can cost MORE tokens at equal success. Cheap-first should therefore keep its 4-bit floor for judgment-adjacent work; sub-4-bit bits buy FIT (bigger model or more context in the same VRAM: IQ1_M 41K to 65K context on 24 GB), not savings per correct answer.
- **Where the real economics moved this window (three mechanisms, all banked below):** (1) KV quantization doubles session density per GPU (Magnitude: "28% less per-agent memory", "27% less memory"; Lifeboat: "2x concurrency" bundled with FP8 KV) - directly the Jev-per-dollar lever for swarm lanes. (2) Drafting/speed multipliers: prompt lookup drafting made up to "42x faster while using up to 2.6x less memory" in llama.cpp (drafting-latency metric, not end-to-end tok/s; acceptance unchanged). (3) The threshold-shift candidate: llama.cpp now serves decision models over `/v1/systemone` (PR #29818), GGUF decision models from 144M to 27B answering in "3 ms" to "43 ms" with `"output_tokens": 0` - a scoring judge at 3-12 ms replaces generated-token judging entirely. For the estate's janitor/scut lane that is a bigger cheap-first move than any quant level: routing, verification, and agent-step checks stop being generation workloads at all.

### RQ3. Can GGUF metadata alone fill a routing-grid serving column, and where does the prediction break?

**Yes as a per-host-fitted, decode-only, single-sequence first-pass estimate; the paper's own limitations name where it breaks (all verbatim from the full text this cycle):**
1. Prefill is out: "we treat prefill as unsupported under this protocol"; prefill errors rise to 76.2/68.1/133.3 pct at 16K depth, and Eq. (3) has no existing-prefix term, so cached-prefix serving (the estate's 98-pct-cache-hit shape) is structurally outside the model.
2. Windows cards are the weak host: RTX 5080 decode MAPE 36.1 pct (test median/max 25.8/69.0), prefill 108.2, vs 13.1/14.4 decode on the two Apple hosts.
3. Fitted coefficients do not transfer: all-target transfer MAPE (14.5/15.4/20.8) is worse than target-fitted (11.0/12.9/13.8); "Separate fitting should not be confused with hardware generalization"; "Configuration-level decode ratios span 1.76-3.26x, ruling out one universal multiplier."
4. Serving shapes are absent: "All reported throughput is single-sequence", llama-bench microbenchmarks only (512-token pp + 128-token tg), no batched, concurrent, or multi-turn workloads.
5. Spill uncertainty on the RTX host: largest analytical working set exceeds reported VRAM before graph buffers, and "Windows shared-memory spill therefore cannot be excluded for the largest files."
