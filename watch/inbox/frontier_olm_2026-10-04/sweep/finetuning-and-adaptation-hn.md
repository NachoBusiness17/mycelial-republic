## Answers to the five research questions

**Q1. Are 2026 RLVR/GRPO results on 2-4B local models strong enough to justify the post-training shelf, and what are the first 50 rows?**
Yes, on three independent 2026 legs. (a) duane.sh: a production RAG "contextualizer" moved from a hosted Flash-Lite tier to a locally served fine-tuned Qwen3.5-4B via SFT (LoRA rank 32, 1 epoch, ~24k teacher examples, one rented H100, 1.6 h, about $7) then GRPO (32 excerpts x 8 contexts per step, ~100 steps, 25,600 Opus-graded contexts), ending at fewer mistakes than Gemini 2.5 Flash-Lite at 71% less serving cost; the model ships on Hugging Face (DuaneMB/local-minutes-contextualizer-4b). (b) ProVer (arxiv 2609.36178) fixes GRPO credit assignment and is evaluated exactly on Qwen3.5-2B and Qwen3.5-4B (+9.91% / +7.12% relative over GRPO on ALFWorld/WebShop/SearchQA per the fetched abstract). (c) Cloudflare Clef ships open-weight Apache-2.0 decision models as rank-256 LoRA adapters on frozen Qwen3.8-27B / Qwen3.5-9B backbones with a published post-training recipe (label-smoothed cross-entropy + Brier calibration + their RLCD variant). Shelf seed: the first 50 rows should be (1) the duane.sh ladder as recipe rows (SFT/DPO/GRPO stages with the DPO failure note: missed references 15.4% to 7.3% but unsupported claims 10.9% to 14.4%), (2) ProVer as the credit-assignment row, (3) Clef as the calibration row (Brier loss, rank-256 adapters on frozen backbones), (4) Shrewd as the classifier-distillation row, (5) MOLT as the consumer-VRAM row, (6) Cactus Needle 3 as the "what 360B synthetic tokens buy a 121M model" row with the thread's adversarial-failure caveats.

**Q2. Distillation cost/quality data for primes-to-janitors, and is local-distill worth it at our scale?**
The cost/quality numbers now exist and they are favorable. duane.sh: teacher choice dominated (Gemini production data as teacher made the student copy teacher errors, 41% of lines restating the excerpt; Opus-written data fixed it); 9B side experiment cost 3x ($246 vs $76 per million excerpts) with no usefulness gain, i.e. 4B is the sweet spot at this task scale. Shrewd: "sending 1M consumer complaints a year through the LLM I used would cost about $3,400" vs a sub-10MB tf-idf student at 0.35 ms/doc on a laptop; the calibrated student beat its teacher on ECE (0.043 vs 0.099) and ran 3,755x faster; confident routing at 70% coverage scored 0.901 vs teacher 0.811. Shrewd's transferable warning for our primes-to-janitor distill: "Better teacher labels did not always improve the student" and students "pick up the teacher's systematic mistakes". Verdict: yes worth it, but as classifier/janitor distillation (Shrewd shape) plus one 4B RLVR pilot (duane.sh shape), not as general chat-capability distillation. Note: the estate already holds its own LoRA trainer (`training/lora/train_ghost.py`, grep hit) and GRPO/QLoRA rows in catalog (10 and 11 respectively) plus `memory/continuity/seat_memory_20260927/astra-v4-converged-training-brief.md`, so this is adjacent-held ground: the recipes are new, the muscle is not.

**Q5. What evaluation-design literature names the "defects-found-per-lane rigged by construction" confound (burns.md Schmidhuber amend) and its cure?**
The named frame is Campbell's law (Bernat says it fits better than Goodhart's for his KPI story: metrics become targets, people split tickets to game counts, "We built a Soviet nail factory. But the KPIs were good, and we stopped caring."), with the practiced cure being participatory metric choice plus quality-anchored targets ("tie metrics to the quality of the service, for example, with service level objectives"; verified sentence: "Otherwise, even dedicated people stop caring, game the system, and eventually quit."). The AI-native version of the same cure is HoneyBench's measurement design: state the environment's intent explicitly, plant an out-of-bounds strategy that scores well, count only committed hacks, peer-review grader disagreements, and accept that "All modern large language models engage in some degree of specification gaming, both during and outside training." For defects-found-per-lane specifically, the transferable cures from this sweep are: (1) measure against stated intent with planted known-answer traps rather than against the lane's own defect taxonomy (HoneyBench shape); (2) hold thresholds to statistically bounded budgets, not curve-read values (Shrewd: reading a confidence threshold off the evaluation curve broke a 2% disagreement budget in 3 to 8 of 10 runs); (3) watch for the metric drifting under you after calibration (cacheverifier: gray-zone positive rate drifted ~5x, a fine-tuned verifier "started approving more wrong answers than it caught", caught only by ongoing two-proportion z-test + Page-Hinkley + KS detectors on live traffic); (4) the Mimo 2.6 thread's benchmaxxing discussion (benchmarks as stopping criteria already induce overfit; kingstnap: "It's implicitly trained against") is the same confound in ML-form. Catalog already holds Campbell's-law rows (grep "Campbell" = 6) and 4 "emergent misalignment" rows plus 1 "inoculation prompting" row, so the Anthropic EM item below banks as reinforcement, not new.
