When the task changes, the pin resets, and routing starts fresh." Plus the tiny-router economics: "The model has to be tiny, because a routing decision that costs as much as the call it's routing saves nothing." And the name: "This is called model affinity."

## The steal

Two mechanisms, one per find.

(1) MODEL AFFINITY (avichawla). Provider prompt caches are per-model namespaces: input a model has already seen costs roughly 90% less (fresh-eye discount), but the cache is specific to the model. A router that chases per-call price (easy call to a 15x cheaper model) switches models mid-task, throws away the accumulated warm cache, and re-bills the full accumulated context at cold rates on the new model; the bill stays the same as before even though most calls were routed cheap. Cost inversion: a cache-hot expensive model can cost less than a cache-cold cheap model. Production shape: route ONCE PER TASK, not once per call; the first call routes and the chosen model gets PINNED to the session; every later call of that task goes to the same model so the cache stays warm; the pin resets at task change, where switching is free. Corollary: the router itself must be tiny, because a routing decision that costs as much as the call it routes saves nothing.

(2) WIDTH ATTRIBUTION, confirmation-grade (ClaudeDevs). Vendor-official wording that Claude Code sessions spawning "excessive parallel subagents" burned "usage faster than expected", and that 5-hour and weekly rate limits were RESET for all users after the spawn fix. The drain is a rolling-window burst effect of the spawn shape, not permanent consumption. This confirms the estate's width law on the disease side and upgrades its first-contact-is-HOLD ruling (AGENTS.md:36) from operator measurement to vendor-confirmed disease class.
