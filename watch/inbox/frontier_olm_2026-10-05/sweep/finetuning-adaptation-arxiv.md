### Banked items (8, strongest first)

1. **SFT or RL for Tool-Calling Agents? A Controlled Study Across Data, Method, and Scale (2609.17848, 2026-09-15, REALM @ EMNLP 2026).** Full read: https://arxiv.org/html/2609.17848v1 . Six Qwen3 models 0.6B-32B; LoRA r=32 alpha=64 dropout 0.05 on all linear layers, AdamW lr 1e-4 (LLaMA-Factory); datasets xLAM/APIGen 56,443, ToolACE 9,715, Glaive-FC-v2 46,204, mixed at up to 20k per source. SFT+LoRA best in-distribution in 15/18 settings; GRPO wins transfer by <1 point; SFT-then-GRPO rarely best. At 4B: "LoRA performs better on all three datasets: 88.0% versus 80.4% on xLAM, 30.4% versus 22.8% on ToolACE, and 47.6% versus 44.1% on Glaive." Quote: "Additional analysis further confirms that LoRA outperforms full-parameter fine-tuning, demonstrating that LoRA better preserves pretrained agentic behavior." RQ1 core recipe; and its negative result answers RQ2 honestly: the eval battery is entirely tool-calling exact-match (held-out 1,000-example splits x3, 54 transfer settings; BFCL/API-Bank relegated to an appendix note) with NO general-capability benchmark run. The field's headline recipes still skip the Gero-4B blocker check.

2. **Keyword Harnesses Fail Open: A Cheap Diagnostic Ladder for Tool-Use Claims in Small Language Models (2610.02142, 2026-10-01).** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02142 . A 1,109M model whose 6B-token tool-SFT phase erased the `<|tool_call|>` trigger prior (first-token prob ~1e-4..1e-5) scored 0.660 on lenient keyword metrics vs 0.650 for a healthy 661.6M sibling, while emitting 0/6 valid tool calls on training rows. The ladder: lenient score, verbatim-reproduction check, first-token probe, post-repair validation, negative-prompt over-trigger test; "costs minutes of CPU time." Repair: 2,202-step diverse SFT at 5x lr (3.3 GPU-h), valid emission 0.100 -> 0.959, embedding-drift check (97.7% of the bf16 table bit-identical). Quote: "This cheap diagnostic ladder costs minutes of CPU time and should gate tool-use claims on small models." RQ2: the cheapest rung of a viable Gero-4B battery - a CPU-minutes pre-gate before any GB-class download decision.

3. **When Synthetic Data Hurts: On Catastrophic Forgetting in Skill Retrieval for LLM Agents (2609.10750, 2026-09-09, EMNLP 2026 Industry).** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10750 . Synthetic-data SFT of a production 0.6B Qwen skill-router (34,396 skills) improves in-distribution retrieval but collapses real/OOD retrieval. EWC, LwF, embedding-anchor regularization and L2-init all retain OOD while still banking +13.98% in-distribution. Quote: "We found that the synthetic-data fine-tuning improves in-distribution retrieval but it causes catastrophic forgetting." RQ2/RQ3: the eval shape is a triangle (synthetic in-distribution / real / OOD) - the minimum battery for any free-lane synthetic finetune, and the mitigation list is the recipe for keeping the janitor fleet's general competence during one.

4. **Time-Incremental Continued Pretraining of LLMs: Knowledge Updates Without Catastrophic Forgetting (2609.23916, 2026-09-20).** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.23916 . Six open-weight models (OLMo2, Llama-3.1/3.2, Gemma-3-1B; 1B-8B), FineWeb-Edu dumps strictly post-cutoff. Quote: "What does it cost? Almost nothing: the macro-average across a thirteen-task suite stays within 0.01 of the base for every model." The 13-task suite with a macro-average-vs-base pass line is the closest published template for the Gero-4B battery; also: data quality beats volume (a curated 6B-token slice matches a 40B one), acquisition-vs-retention optimal learning rates differ ~10x, and sufficiently high-rank LoRA matches full CPT. RQ2 (+RQ1 rank evidence).

5. **EDGEGEN: Improving Tool-Calling Agents Beyond Happy Paths with Synthetic Edge Case Generation (2609.24115, 2026-09-21).** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24115 . Extracts compliance rules from the agent's spec, generates database-grounded edge-case tasks designed to violate them, closes the loop on tau2-bench airline: finetuning gains "2 percent to 42 percent" mean progress while some baselines degrade; harness optimization +10%/+30% for Gemma-4-e4b. Quote: "The resulting pipeline forms a fully automated closed-loop system that requires no human annotation." RQ3: zero-annotation edge-case synthesis from specs the estate already holds (queue manifests, lane contracts); no code/data release stated, so reproducibility is from-method only. Dataset sizes/model list not in the abstract - OWED if the door needs them.

6. **On-Policy or Off-Policy Learning? A Systematic Study of Distillation Dynamics (2609.35259, 2026-09-28).** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35259 . Llama3 + Qwen2.5 families, strong-to-weak distillation, rollout policy x KL direction x learning rate varied independently: KL direction drives task performance/coverage, learning rate governs forgetting and update sparsity; forward KL is robust to rollout policy, reverse KL is not; on-policy's Countdown generalization edge does not survive RLVR. Quote: "Overall, our results challenge the view that on-policy rollouts are inherently preferable and show that their value depends critically on the objective, evaluation setting, and optimisation hyperparameters." RQ2/RQ4: for any distilled-calibrator run, the forgetting knob is lr, not data provenance - cheap to control.

## Answer sketch per research question

1. Recipes under 10k examples: the only in-window hits at <10k scale are 2609.25373 (9.5k synthetic, 270M, already banked 09-28) and EDGEGEN (sizes unstated). The controlled study shows LoRA r=32 alpha=64 beats full FT everywhere on 0.6B-32B tool-calling (2609.17848), consistent with the 09-28 sweep's rank law (2608.25677: r<=16 for knowledge injection). Under 10k examples nobody in-window published a general recipe; the 56k/46k/9.7k dataset trio of 2609.17848 is the field's working scale, and its 20k-per-source mixing result is the transfer lever.
2. Minimum viable battery (the Gero-4B unblock): three published patterns compose into one cheap battery - (a) 2609.23916's ~13-task broad suite scored as macro-average delta vs base with a within-0.01..0.05 pass line; (b) 2609.10750's triangle (synthetic in-dist / real / OOD) for whatever narrow skill the finetune adds; (c) 2610.02142's CPU-minute ladder (verbatim-reproduction, first-token/trigger probe, negative-prompt over-trigger, embedding-drift) as the pre-download gate. Supporting: forgetting is lr-governed (2609.35259) and mitigable with EWC/LwF at +13.98% in-dist retention (2609.10750). Notably the flagship tool-calling study itself ran no general-capability battery - the estate's demand here is ahead of the field, so compose (a)+(b)+(c) rather than copy any single paper.
3. Zero-cost synthetic pipelines: EDGEGEN (spec-rule-driven edge cases, no human annotation) and QVAC's weak-student-error-signal loop are the two reproducible shapes; both run with local janitors + one funded teacher, fitting free-lane sensing-only doctrine. The 09-28 sweep's Data Turnstile (2607.29250) remains the open-source tooling entry; AutoDataBench (2609.35025, pass-1 only) warns agents authoring training data score <20/100 at default budgets - keep the synthesis template-written, not agent-freestyled.
4. Licensing: no in-window arXiv paper states distillation-license terms in its abstract; the only direct statement found is 2502.06659's ToS warning (2025). QVAC's corpus license is unstated on its abs page (OWED at artifact). Honest conclusion: arXiv cannot answer RQ4; the governing documents are provider ToS and dataset/model repo licenses, and at least one published result shows distilled teachers are fingerprintable, so "internal use" is not secrecy-protected. Escalate the per-provider ToS read to an operator-gated door before any frontier-trace distillation run.
