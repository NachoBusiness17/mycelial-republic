## Research-question verdicts

RQ1 (2026 serving wins for 2B-4B janitors on consumer hardware). Three banked answers. (a) Galahad 2609.39358: byte-exact KV-state reuse inside llama.cpp/vLLM/SGLang (no runtime source modified), 98.7% of prompt tokens were previously-read text on seven datasets, and on llama.cpp with Gemma 4 31B a 97k-token recall test went from 10/100 at 9.3 s / 2,754 J to 98/100 at 3.0 s / 572 J, and 100/100 at 0.59-0.64 s with document selection added; bit-identical logits (262,144/262,144), fails closed. This is the biggest measured lever for janitors that re-read the same files every cycle, and it lands in Ollama's own backend. (b) Execution-Path Qualification 2610.03228: the negative-result governor. On real consumer stacks (Arc B570 Vulkan, RTX 5070 Ti, M4 llama.cpp/Metal, Lunar Lake 358H) with Qwen3-8B + Qwen3-0.6B drafters, speculative decoding produced no portable gain: 358H below parity even at zero draft cost (CPU draft 0.734x, NPU draft 0.352x, arithmetic cap ~0.83x), and the same M4 gave 0.842x on a mixed 24-prompt workload but 1.387x on HumanEval fixed-k=2; the sign flips by workload, so the fleet must gate speculation per workload class, never assume it. (c) BitNest 2610.02800: W4-draft-nested-in-W8-target self-speculation shares one physical weight (6.22 GiB for 7B, same as plain W8), 95.2% acceptance, 1.48-1.61x over FP16, about 1.5x on Jetson Orin NX 16 GB at 0.95 J/token: the memory-fit option when there is no VRAM for a separate drafter. EdgeAgent 2610.03394 adds the scheduling half on Apple M4 (below). Notably absent: no 2026 paper in-window claims a new paged-attention-style breakthrough; the wins have moved to memory reuse (Galahad), speculation qualification (EPQ/BitNest), and stall-filling schedulers (EdgeAgent).

RQ2 (speculative decoding for structured tool-call/agentic outputs). The field has moved exactly here. LEAP 2610.02670 is the definitive read: action-phase speculation with a 0.6B drafter LoRA-trained on the target's own committed actions raises agreement from 2-25% to 64-75% and yields 1.18-1.63x end-to-end (Qwen3-32B, Gemma-4-31B targets; OpenAGI, TaskBench, tau2-bench, BFCL; one shared H100), with a latency framework that predicts measured speedup within 0.10x on 19/21 configs. Its stated costs matter to us: up to k+1 verification requests (+16% completion tokens on tau2), gains collapse to 1.02x with +5 s tool delays, and only synchronous propose-verify loops are covered. AgSpec 2610.01108 is the zero-training alternative: retrieval drafting over session/workspace/global corpora with emission-format indexing hits 2.27-4.37x (bs1) to 4.76x (bs16) on SWE-bench Verified/TeamBench, beating EAGLE-3 in most settings, at 1.1-5.5% overhead and GB-scale host memory; its warning that session corpora may hold sensitive content maps one-to-one onto our inbox discipline. EdgeAgent confirms the same structure from the systems side: drafting difficulty alternates between "complex reasoning and predictable structured generation" (its words), so draft budgets must be dynamic. Net for the estate: structured tool-call output is MORE speculative-friendly than prose (deterministic schemas repeat), but only with drafters trained on, or retrieved from, the agent's own emission history; off-the-shelf small drafters are confirmed waste.

RQ3 (reproducible per-tier inference-budget accounting). Weakest area in the literature; honest gap. The `all:"inference budget"` query returned 10 papers, none proposing a per-tier accounting method. The one reproducible primitive is What Does a Token Cost 2610.02491: a teacher-forced 15-model panel (Qwen2.5 0.5B-72B, OLMo-2, R1-distills; 50,019 prompts) where a token's "sufficient compute" is the FLOPs of the smallest model that reproduces it given the correct prefix. A 0.5B agent reproduces 92-95% of reference tokens, the costliest 10% of tokens carry 64-80% of FLOPs, and the map cuts routing latency 7.59 to 5.12 s (projected) and draft tokens -32.6%. That is exactly the shape the inference-budget law needs (per-token tier assignment by measured sufficiency, not by vibes), and it is reproducible from public checkpoints. Hard caveats the paper itself states: its latency/energy numbers are projections from published vLLM benchmarks, not deployed end-to-end measurements; the map is oracle-supplied (built on checker-verified references); and cheap reproduction does not guarantee reliable continuation (0.5B free-running loses 9.44 pp vs 72B). Adjacent non-banked signals: Engineering Sustainable Agents 2610.03010 (agentic setups use 6.36x more energy for limited accuracy gains) and ePACT 2610.01784 (energy-commitment tracking for serving). No paper found that accounts per-tier across a tiered fleet like ours: the estate's usage.jsonl + per-receipt burn ledger is ahead of what is published; the MoA sufficiency panel is the piece worth stealing as a local instrument (it runs on our own fleet: gemma:2b, gemma4, plus remote lanes as panel members).

RQ4 (is the 109.4M tok/h width-10 envelope account-specific?). Short answer: yes, and no public literature measures anything like it, so it cannot be externally validated, only internally re-measured. Direct evidence of the gap: the `(rate limit OR rate limiting) AND (LLM OR language model)` query returned only 2 in-window papers, both irrelevant (OCS training scheduling; robot MPC). No paper in the 90-candidate pool measures account-level rolling-throughput envelopes on a commercial LLM API. The closest published layer is multi-tenant serving fairness on shared GPUs: FairInference 2609.18112 (delta-token fairness: a token taking d time units in isolation completes within d+delta multi-tenant; SGLang v0.3.0, single A100, Llama-3-8B) and its baselines VTC/DLPM/FairServe. Two things transfer. (1) Published fairness guarantees explicitly presuppose rate limiting: FairInference's own limitations say bursts beyond the rate limit "may result in non-theoretical behavior", i.e. the provider-side token bucket our 109.4M tok/h figure probes is an exogenous input the literature assumes rather than characterizes. (2) Its measured failure mode (a high-demand tenant degrading well-behaved tenants >60x TTFT on plain SGLang) is the same rolling-throughput contention physics our envelope measurement hit from the client side. Also relevant from the same pool: Jumping the Line 2610.03430 shows prediction-based LLM schedulers (the kind providers run) can be gamed via output-length manipulation (up to 1.53x completion speedup): published confirmation that provider-side schedulers are manipulable at the margins, and that our width-10 envelope is a property of THEIR scheduler state, not of the Task-agent shape alone. Verdict to bank: treat 109.4M tok/h as an account-and-epoch-specific measurement (consistent with unpublished token-bucket rate limiting), cite FairInference as the closest public anchor for the contention physics, and re-measure after any provider-side change; nothing published contradicts it, nothing published confirms it.
