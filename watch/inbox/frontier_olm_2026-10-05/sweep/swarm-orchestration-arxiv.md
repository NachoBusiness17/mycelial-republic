## Answers to the four research questions

### RQ1: retry/backoff/admission control against rolling-throughput caps

The literature does NOT have a width-vs-throughput envelope study like ours (nothing in the feeds measures an LLM-provider rolling throughput cap as a function of fan-out width; the closest artifacts study retry policy shape, not width). What exists:

- **Compound AI System Reliability (2610.02503)**: a 23-failure-mode taxonomy from 150 production incidents (97 public post-mortems from LangChain/AutoGen/CrewAI-class stacks + 53 enterprise, Jan 2024 to Dec 2025, kappa 0.84). Our exact failure classes are IN their orchestration/integration categories: "Infinite retry / loop deadlock (6.0%)", "Agent starvation under load (5.3%)", "Dead-letter task accumulation (4.0%)", "Rate limit exhaustion cascade (3.3%)". Five-pattern catalog: circuit breakers (open after 5 failures/30s, and must watch semantic quality because "Most AI failures return 200 OK with degraded content"), output quality gates, component isolation (per-component rate-limit budgets), semantic validators, typed interfaces. Numbers: cascade depth -89%, silent degradation caught 73%, blast radius -64%, MTTR 8.4min vs 28.7min with 3+ patterns. Their admitted gap: the retry-with-backoff baseline was deferred to future work, and one incident was a $2,400 weekend retry loop. This validates our B2-443-style census approach and says: policy shape (breaker thresholds, budgets, gates) is where the field standardizes, not width.
- **ContinuityBench (2607.15899, 2026-07-17, outside the 30-day window, banked anyway for RQ1 strength)**: multi-provider failover benchmark; concrete retry law: `t_wait = min(30s, 2^a + U(0,1))`, max 5 attempts, on 429/502/503/504; fixed-interval retries against a rate-limited fallback produced a self-sustaining retry storm that crashed the proxy ("thundering herd"), while exponential backoff with jitter desynchronized retries. Also: stateless failover drops all context (CPR 0.00%) vs history-forwarding (99.20%). For us: the Task-agent path's driver backoff is the right family; workflow children with maxAttempts 0 die clean instead of storming, which this paper would endorse (no synchronized retry herd at width 50).
- **InFlowOp (2610.01017)**: fault-local retry at workflow granularity: "Pause: halt at the subtask that falters, not at the workflow it belongs to"; cost-ordered repair ladder (re-assign before re-decompose), verified fixes only, rollback otherwise, and replay of cached results outside the affected closure. Up to +21.9% over single LLM at matched budgets. This is retry-without-restarting-the-fan-out: the cheap dispatch shape for flaky lanes.
- **Inherit-MAS (2610.02396)**: execution inheritance: reuse stored node results only on exact SHA-256 request-key match (node spec + ordered upstream records + model/decoding settings + context fingerprint); state-changing nodes always live. Cuts worker tokens 29.1%/34.6% (WorkBench/HotpotQA) vs identical reruns. The academic twin of our incremental-report()/amend-don't-rewrite salvage law, with measured numbers.
- **AgentRM (2603.13110, March 2026, listing-level only, NOT banked)**: OS-style middleware with MLFQ scheduling and rate-limit-aware admission control for agent fleets; exists, worth a later deep read.
- **ASAD (2610.00629)**: the admission-control answer in miniature: width as a function of demand. Signals (severity, AST depth, cyclomatic complexity, call-graph fan-in/out) classify each task SIMPLE/COMPLEX; simple = single pass, complex = up to 4 specialized agents, bounded retries (3 per agent, 5 global). "agent configuration should be a function of problem complexity rather than a fixed predefined choice" - 4-9% better fix precision with 32% fewer agent activations than static teams. Verdict for the estate map: a cheaper dispatch shape than fixed width exists and is published, but it is per-task complexity gating, not a measured width cap.

### RQ2: heterogeneous-tier dispatch vs the driving ladder

- **AgentRouter (2609.22951)** - already held in memory (quote_audit_wide_20260925), excluded from the bank but it anchors the field: 12M classifier, <5ms/step, four tiers, per-step routing, 72% cost cut at 97.3% quality; per-step RouteLLM/FrugalGPT only 31%/44% because single-turn signals miss trajectory dependencies.
- **What Does a Token Cost? (2610.02491)**: token-level measurement across a 15-model panel (0.5B to 72B): "On three core benchmarks, a 0.5B agent reproduces 92-95% of reference tokens" and "The most expensive 10% of tokens account for 64-80% of estimated FLOPs." Map-guided routing to the 0.5B tier beats a confidence cascade on latency at equal accuracy; but delegation length matters: handing a 0.5B a 64-token stretch costs ~9.4 accuracy points. This is the strongest published support for the ladder's cheap-first default, with a hard caveat: cheap lanes for long handovers degrade, matching our free-lane-as-SENSING-only doctrine (cheap output is not trusted output). Limitation: the map needs oracle reference responses, so it is a research instrument, not a live dispatcher.
- **Routing wave (Sept-Oct 2026, listing-level)**: RouteFM (2609.37362, pretrain-once router), FlexRouter (2609.38585, DPP-selected complementary model sets), SaveRouter (2609.37402, sparse supervision cuts break-even deployment volume 2-10x), FACET market-aware provider routing (2609.37902, same model differs by provider), SeLMRoute (2609.34736), CreateScore (2610.02972, uncertainty-gated 8B to 120B escalation, -65.2% tokens). None dispatch on the axes our ladder uses (funded-vs-free, sensing-vs-judgment); all route on quality/cost/latency of model instances. Gap = our ladder has no published counterpart: nobody prices judgment climbs against budget with a falsifier. That is estate-original.

### RQ4: N-lane merge without a coordinator throne, under the consensus-illusion law

- **MIRROR (2610.02349)**: quorum merge without any trusted central party: sender replicates identical canonicalized bytes over k=2t+1 independently addressed routes (t+1 full payload, t witness routes carrying only a 32-byte SHA-256 digest); receiver plurality-votes digests counted against k (not replies received) so withholding cannot shift the threshold; accepts only if C(H*) > k/2. 0.0% attack success below alpha=0.5, sharp failure at the theoretical threshold, 1x token overhead vs 35x for LLM-as-judge defense. Their binding assumption: route independence ("The route-independence assumption is the binding constraint."). For our sibling lanes: N-lane outputs CAN merge by digest plurality without a throne, but only if lanes fail independently - same-provider lanes share failure groups.
- **Silent Dissent (2610.02702)**: the consensus-illusion law, measured: Asch-style confederates flip stated answers while the J-lens shows conformed models still represent their original premise ("Stated consensus in multi-agent debate can thus overstate agreement."); hiding an agent's own prior answer swings conformity 8% to 89%; latent readout is a monitoring signal, NOT a better voting rule (it rarely beats stated votes in free debate). Direct instrument candidate for our verdict-merge honesty checks.
- Supporting (listing-level): MiniRep (2609.39297, reputation-weighted debate aggregation robust to malicious agents), Bayesian Dialectical Argumentation (2610.02005, per-agent reliability from deliberation traces under persistent adversaries), PRIMUS (2609.07910, BLS aggregate signatures + consensus tokens with safe-kill thresholds), "Multi-agent discussion gains less when dissent is withheld" (2609.38324, discussion beats majority voting only below a critical dissent-withholding rate), "Everyone Conforms, No One Believes" (2608.02758, 64-94% public conformity with private dissent).
