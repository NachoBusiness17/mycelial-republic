## Research-question verdicts

RQ-route-once (route once per task, pin the model). The publish record now has both halves. PaR 2609.32917 (banked by the swarm lane today, read by me) is the route-once shape validated: tier assignment folded into plan time lands on the cost-accuracy frontier, cuts cost 44% vs all-frontier for 2.9 points, and its PaR-Lite ablation attributes the win to whole-workflow visibility, not the classifier. Its caution transfers directly: the pilot rho (failure amplification vs naive independence) read 3.33 for cheap-tier mixing vs 1.27 all-frontier, i.e. cheap routing can compound failures on compositional workflows. SaveRouter 2609.37402 (mine) supplies the missing accounting half: routing must be judged on break-even volume (supervision cost repaid by serving savings), and sparse supervision cut break-even 1.9-9.5x while using 33-41% of feedback; appendix E.1 shows the cost-minimizing and payback-minimizing supervision levels differ (K=7 vs K=2 on MMR-Bench, K=8 vs K=2 on RouterBench), and random-K reached break-even earlier than SaveRouter on LLMRouterBench. Net: pinning survives as a design (plan-time routing is published and on-frontier), but the estate's version needs its own break-even row (router build+upkeep cost vs measured savings per question class), and the 36.3% question-class law is the natural conditioning variable. Fast Models Slow Evidence 2610.02267 (banked by open-and-local today, read by me) adds the governor: zero-shot System-1 routing failed outright (Jev answers cheap for 99.5% of requests; Laya AUROC 0.48, flips with option order), and its self-audit deflated a 23.9% claimed saving to 4.3% by charging the gate's own tokens - the exact accounting the estate's register-row discipline already demands.

RQ-width (12 to 16 Task-agent rung, rolling throughput). No public paper measures commercial-API account-level rolling-throughput envelopes (yesterday's sweep found the same hole; this window's q4 yielded only 16 unique candidates and none on client-side envelope measurement). The new usable piece is Attention Tax Handoff Tax 2610.06069 (mine): a measured model of when fan-out/delegation beats one agent thinking longer, with two crossover conditions and a real measurement - context degradation 2.1% failure at 2k tokens to ~68% at 36-45k, handoff taxes 0.077/0.026/0.015 nats at 137/183/255-token notes, crossover predicted at depth 10, decomposition winning at depths 20/50/100 within 9pp of prediction. The estate-relevant translation: parallel width wins only when the shared-failure floor of the workers sits below the single-agent's saturated thinking error, and delegation wins only when avoided attention cost exceeds the handoff tax - both measurable with estate instruments (duel logs, usage.jsonl) before buying the 12-16 rung. SLOWeave 2609.07883 (mine) adds the server-side half: adaptive prefill chunking against decode deadlines lifts goodput 39% (25ms TPOT, mixed) and 3.3x (10ms, mixed) over fixed chunks, a primitive relevant to any local llama-server batching.

RQ-thinking-economics (EMPTY_REASONING wiring, off-state re-measurement). ConfSFT 2609.31619 (mine) is the strongest action item: self-supervised confidence training (600 AIME problems, no length objective, no inference-time mechanism) cuts generated tokens 10-19% at matched accuracy on Gemma-4-E2B (-10.3%, 60.8 to 60.1), Qwen3-4B (-19.2%), Nemotron-8B (-11.1%), GPT-OSS-20B (-11.1%), preserving reasoning composition (Schoenfeld episode shares) where explicit length-penalty RL redistributes it, and beating DEER early-exit which crashed Nemotron LiveCodeBench 44.2% to 17.4%. Gemma-4-E2B is the estate's own short-worker model: a fine-tune lane for the janitor is now published-recipe territory. Calibrated Instrument 2609.18005 (mine) is the re-measurement methodology: a formally calibrated LLM-judge with a provably-null condition, resolving 4-bit vs 16-bit to indistinguishable at +/-0.3 points while 3-bit costs 0.5 prose / 1.1 math and early exit costs 0.7 prose / 2.5 math - the domain-dependence is exactly why the off-state re-measure must be per-question-class. SELF-POT 2610.01110 (mine) prices the thinking budget: selection and failure handling change realized gains from the same candidates (selection raised correct programming submissions 376 to 453 while saving 12-49% of logical API cost; judging-with-fallback 186 vs 182 for voting), and it charges every call including critique in dollars - the schema the burns ledger already wants.

RQ-switch-machinery (keep or retire in-session switching). RSI-Router 2609.34712 (banked by the evals lane today, read by me) is the pro-retention counterweight: subtask-level large-small switching within a task beat large-only at roughly half cost (48.3% pairing figure; 8.1-82.2% per-benchmark cuts, ALFWorld -75.3% cost with success 90.62 to 98.44), and beats 9 routing baselines on the Pareto frontier - but the paper never measures switch frequency or per-switch overhead separately (routing calls are bundled into totals), so the published evidence does not price a switch, it prices a policy. Sibyl 2610.05383 (mine) reframes the question: instead of a router deciding to switch, the small model LEARNS escalation as an action (call_cloud) - 0.6B agent at 95.2%/80.4% success on ALFWorld/WebShop with 0.8/3.9 cloud calls per trajectory (7.7%/2.8% of steps), beating RouteLLM 65.7%/22.8% and Hera 65.0%/26.2%, beating cloud-only Qwen3.7-Max success while cutting inference spend 95.4%/66.8%. The estate's zero in-session switches in 36,567 rows reads, against these two papers, as: our lanes never learned when switching pays; the machinery is not dead weight in the literature, but it is unexercised in our dispatch shape - the retirement decision should wait for the learned-escalation experiment, not the census alone.
