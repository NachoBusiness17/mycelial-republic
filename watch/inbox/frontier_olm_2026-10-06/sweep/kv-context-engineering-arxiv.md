## Pass 2 deep reads (7 papers fetched and read 2026-10-06)

1. **Request Order Matters: Cache-History Sensitivity in Selective KV-Cache Reuse for Rolling Agents** (2610.05833, 10-05, full HTML). Rolling agents (evict oldest document, append newest each call) break exact prefix caching, so they use CacheBlend-style selective reuse: load cached per-chunk KV, recompute a small budget of tokens to repair cross-chunk attention. Finding: with accumulated cache history, the SAME prompt yields different answers depending on request order: token top-k selection answers varied 69.0% of the time across 5 request orders vs 26.1% for document-aligned selection (5% recompute budget). Fidelity to full prefill: chronological order shows no significant gap (86.8% vs 85.4%, p=0.12), but the other four orders drop token top-k to 19.8-41.5% while document-aligned holds 54.3-91.7% (+34.5 to +52.5 pts, all p<0.0001). Ablation: contiguity is the active ingredient, not document boundaries or deviation ranking (four contiguous-span policies 89.4-92.8% vs token top-k 38.6% under fixed-random order, even though top-k captures 99.7% of measured KV deviation). Cache-reset control shows no gap (94.0% vs 92.6%, p=0.23): the disease is accumulated history, not reuse per se. TTFT: both selective policies ~5.7x median speedup over full prefill (426/415 ms vs 2,408 ms on an NVIDIA L4), Qwen3-8B, 7,735 rolling updates, 84-doc windows, ~7.2-7.7k-token prompts.
2. **Spend Bytes on Breadth (BreadthKV)** (2610.05685, 10-05, full HTML). Fixed KV byte budget split between token count and precision: ~3-6x more tokens at 2-4 bits (KIVI-style) + SnapKV-D eviction, bit-width chosen by 60-problem end-to-end calibration. BreadthKV beats eviction-only in 17 of 18 settings (pooled +17.4 pp); Qwen3-8B AIME25 at B=512: SnapKV-D 5.8% vs FullKV 65.4%, BreadthKV at B=1024 62.9% at 0.10x FullKV memory-time. Bit optimum is model- and budget-dependent: Qwen3 prefers 2 bits everywhere, R1-Qwen 3@B512/4@B1024, R1-Llama 3/2; calibration beats always-4-bit by 4.5 pp. 1.25-bit collapses (0-12% calibration accuracy); offline attention-error metrics do not predict end-to-end accuracy (an oracle evictor still loses ~13% of future attention mass at 10% retention, yet quantize-plus-evict beats oracle eviction end-to-end at every byte ratio). Derailment mechanism: eviction deletes evidence that a result was verified, model re-derives endlessly ("wait" frequency 6.4 vs 3.7 per 1000 tokens). Triton int4 decode kernel 1.70 ms vs 1.63 ms fp16 FlashAttention at 32K on H200. Explicit contrast with long-INPUT work: earlier papers found 2-bit KV sharply degraded on long inputs; here 2-bit wins because the cache is written during generation and errors compound.
3. **HEAR Protocol** (2610.06597, 10-05, full HTML). Harnesses (AutoGen, LangGraph) hold workflow intent, lifecycles, reuse expectations; engines (vLLM, SGLang) hold KV residency, queues, pressure; no shared control plane, so engines evict KV right before reuse and harnesses schedule cold requests before cache-hot ones. HEAR standardizes four message classes (intent/requirements harness->engine; state/outcomes engine->harness) plus message force (intent vs control, observation vs guarantee). Results: SCBench Qwen3-8B closed-loop: 1.61x batch completion, median TTFT 63.1s -> 28.3s (2.23x) vs FCFS's 19.2% KV reuse. Mooncake production trace: session-aware retention lifts reuse 19.5% -> 31.9% at 50% load; 1.89x P50 TTFT at 100% load. LangGraph GLM-4.7-Flash BrowseComp-Plus: 1.23x wall time, prefill tokens -76.2%, cache reuse 6.2% -> 80.3%, accuracy preserved (46.63 vs 46.15). No mapping universally optimal: wrong role-to-mode mapping costs 7.8-14.0%.
4. **Behavior-Preserving KV Cache Compression** (2610.06479, 10-05, abs page; HTML 404). Training-free eviction scored by KL divergence between the compressed-cache next-token distribution and the full-cache distribution, using pre-eviction forward statistics so no separate masked pass per candidate. Claim: preserve predictive BEHAVIOR, not proxy importance (attention mass). Largest gains over attention-based heuristics under aggressive compression; end-to-end speedup over full-cache inference in evaluated settings. No numbers in the abstract.
5. **The Optimization Landscape of Learning Compacted Context Models** (2610.05885, 10-05, full HTML). Learned KV compaction (train g_phi to map long cache to short so a frozen Qwen3-4B performs as on full cache, KL on answer tokens) has a pathological landscape: even the compression-free linear map's "global solution sits in a needle-sharp basin surrounded by a wide, flat plateau." Simplifying the writer (single-head cross-attention Fast Diamond) beat expressive writers: 92.48% MCQ vs full-context control 98.29%, HAK 83.38%, Cartridges 80.61%, H2O 74.01%, at 0.05 s/document. Higher compression: 89.49% at 8x, 75.58% at 16x, 56.26% at 64x. Recurrent/iterative compaction is the hardest case: Fast Diamond decays at 16k-32k compactions; common fixes (denser losses, self-study queries, partial weight relaxation) "do not reliably carry over to compressive or recurrent caches."
6. **Total Cost of Agency** (2609.23790, 09-20, full HTML; ALREADY BANKED 09-28, re-read only for corroboration, NOT re-banked). Memory injection = 13.6% of actionable variable cost, 27.6% at depth 6, linear (R^2=0.9974); window K 32->2 cuts injected tokens 28.7% at accuracy within seed variation; two-pass non-billable attribution (~10 ms/node); provider cache pricing cited (reads 0.1x, 5-min writes 1.25x, 1h writes 2x); injected fresh upstream outputs called poor caching candidates (first injection always a write; distinct system prompts break prefix sharing); caching explicitly NOT evaluated. My read matches the 09-28 bank line for line.
7. **Decoupling Memory from Context (GraphMemory)** (2610.02687, 10-02, full HTML). Append-only memory grows per-query context O(N); GraphMemory decouples stored memory from exposed context via a retriever: graph nodes = ACE-style knowledge bullets, edges = helpful/harmful Beta-posterior weights; LLM picks <=2 entry sections, deterministic BFS <=2 hops, <=12 nodes, <=2,500 tokens, no full-memory fallback. Under bounded retrieval, per-query context stays constant while memory grows. Results: 81-85% fewer training tokens, 78.6-92.2% fewer test tokens, 21-34% less training time (Formula/Claude: 20M vs 131M tokens, $24.00 vs $157.20); accuracy within 4.0 pts of ACE (beats it +1.5 with quantized Qwen3.8-27B on Formula, trails 2.94 on FiNER); inference ~1.8x slower per query from retrieval.

## What the sweep banks toward the five research questions

**Q1 Theo falsifier B (cache-writes-dominate-spend on our dispatch shape).** Not run here: the DIGEST probe order (frontier_olm_2026-10-05/DIGEST.md, probe 1) defines falsifier B as pure log arithmetic over `logs/provider_usage.jsonl`, and this sweep ran no estate logs. What the window adds: (a) the external record still has NO measured cache-write share for agent dispatch: the closest paper, Total Cost of Agency, explicitly did not evaluate caching (frontier_olm_20260928/sweep/kv-cache-context-engineering-arxiv.md:118), so the falsifier cannot be settled from literature, only from our receipts; (b) TCA does hand us the measurement instrument: two-pass non-billable token attribution at ~10 ms/node, and the pricing frame (reads 0.1x, 5-min writes 1.25x, 1h writes 2x) that makes write-share the dominant lever once writes cost 1.25-2x; (c) Request Order Matters implies any spend model with a STATIC hit rate misprices rolling sessions: hit/answer behavior depends on accumulated request history (69.0% vs 26.1% variation by order), so falsifier B should bin our receipts by session shape (append-only vs mid-context-edit) before computing the write share, or the number will not be stable across days.

**Q2 strict-prefix cache-resume (law stamps append, boot prefix byte-identical).** The rolling-agent paper is the direct external test of our append-only design: exact-prefix breaks when retained blocks SHIFT (their windows slide), not when content is appended at the tail. Our law-stamp append keeps the head byte-identical and grows the tail, which is exactly the chronological-order shape they measured as fidelity-safe (86.8% vs 85.4% full prefill, p=0.12). The paper's warning for us: the moment anything reorders or rewrites a retained block (a re-render, a re-flow, a template change), selective-reuse quality collapses to 19.8-41.5% fidelity for deviation-ranked repair, and CONTIGUITY of what you recompute, not smart token ranking, is what survives (89.4-92.8% for contiguous spans). Cache-hit delta expectation: HEAR's session-aware retention lifted reuse 19.5% -> 31.9% at 50% load on production traces, and its cache-hot-first ordering cut median TTFT 2.23x; our banked B4 receipt and strict-prefix law (frontier_olm_2026-10-06/lens/burns.md:56) gain an engine-side message vocabulary from HEAR (prepare/retain/release + state/observations) that our lane-runner trail events could emit if we ever want llama.cpp/ollama to see intent. Estate zknot measurement stands: stable-leading-block share 0.59% (frontier_olm_2026-10-06/lens/burns.md:88-89) is the current resume-rate ceiling any strict-prefix change would move.

**Q3 compression-gateway two-axes transfer to context-pack.** The two axes (what to keep vs what to recompute) now have separate instruments on each axis: keep-side, GraphMemory's bounded retrieval (per-query context constant as memory grows; 79-92% test-token cut at <=4 pt accuracy cost; ~1.8x slower per query) prices the keep axis as a pointer-composition problem, which is what `mag.cmd context-pack` already is; recompute-side, Request Order Matters prices the recompute axis (5% recompute budget, contiguous spans only), and HEAR prices wrong mode selection at 7.8-14.0%. Transfer verdict: context-pack composition should treat kept-context bytes and recompute-on-miss bytes as two budget lines, and HEAR's finding that no mapping is universally optimal says the pack should be role-conditioned (janitor vs judgment dispatch), not one canonical composition.

**Q4 compaction-cliff placement on zknot/warm_state.** New external anchor: learned compaction is LANDSCAPE-BRITTLE (needle-sharp basin, plateau elsewhere) and degrades specifically under ITERATIVE compaction (Fast Diamond decays at 16k-32k compactions; fixes "do not reliably carry over to compressive or recurrent caches"). Our zknot archive re-tokenizes every close at 0.59% stable share (burns.md:88-89), which is an iterative-compaction shape; the paper predicts learned/summarizing compaction will drift exactly there, corroborating the day-5 verdict that deterministic externalization (CADOC cards, banked d5) is the safer rung than model-written summaries. The Open WebUI compaction-filter pattern (banked d5, burns.md:78) is a threshold-triggered single-pass summarizer: the new paper's data says a single-pass compaction is the trainable case (92.5% at 0.05 s/doc) while chained re-compaction is where quality falls off, so the filter should compact ONCE from originals, never re-compact its own output. Placement answer for the cliff question remains OUR measurement (the 2608.22752 threshold transfer was banked day-5; this sweep adds no new threshold number).

**Q5 KV q4_0 at 128k-class on 7b.** The window's strongest external evidence lands here. BreadthKV: at a fixed byte budget the optimum bit-width is model- and context-budget-dependent (2/3/4 all win somewhere; calibration beats always-4-bit by 4.5 pp), 1.25-bit collapses while 2-bit wins end-to-end on decode-written caches, and OFFLINE ERROR METRICS DO NOT PREDICT END-TO-END ACCURACY. Behavior-Preserving KV supplies the probe shape: score a quant/evict config by KL drift of the output distribution vs full cache, not by perplexity. Together with today's quant lane's verdict ("The q4_0 KV accuracy/throughput probe therefore stays OWED locally", frontier_olm_2026-10-06/sweep/quantization-compression-arxiv.md:62-63) and the banked B4 rung (q8_0 decode 100.8 vs f16 55.9 t/s, burns.md:59), the falsifiable expectation for our owed q4_0 A/B: q4_0 KV will NOT read as a uniform win or loss at 128k; its accuracy cost is budget- and lane-dependent, and any probe scored by offline PPL can pass while task accuracy fails, so the A/B must score task-level probes end-to-end (same instrument law the quant lane set for the weight-side qwen2.5:7b A/B, same file :49-51).
