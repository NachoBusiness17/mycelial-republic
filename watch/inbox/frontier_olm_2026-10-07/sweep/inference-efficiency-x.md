## Verdicts per research question

**RQ1 cost-per-blind-point routing priors.** The strongest published prior set: LLMRouterBench (arXiv 2601.07206, 2026-01-12) - 400K+ instances, 21 datasets, 33 models; under unified eval many routers (commercial included) do not reliably beat Best Single, best methods buy roughly 31.7 pct cost cut at matched performance, and the oracle gap is driven by "persistent model-recall failures" (routing fails most when only one model can answer - exactly the estate's blind-point framing; question-side features dominate, which corroborates the estate's 36.3 pct question-class vs 3.6 pct family law at lens/laws.md:47). Fresh methods on that benchmark: RLCascadeRouter (arXiv 2608.15817, 2026-08-16) reframes cascade routing as an MDP with stop/select actions optimized directly on the performance-cost objective, no quality estimator, unseen models without retraining; SeLMRoute (2609.34736, 2026-09-28) gets 72.08 pct avg accuracy vs 69.23 for strongest-fixed (already in our catalog, age_days 2 - NOT re-banked). Production-side: Weave Router (weaveos.com blog, 2026-05-20, vendor-claimed) measures 80-85 pct cost reduction on Claude Code traffic via ONNX prompt embedding + frozen cluster centroids + cost/latency/verbosity composite under a quality-parity constraint, session-sticky routing, low single-digit ms overhead; the Oct 2 Neuron Daily article adds the Show HN claim of Terminal Bench 4.0 parity with GPT-6 Astra at 52 pct cost and 2.2x speed, results at weaveos.com/router. Vendor numbers are priors, never cell fills (INSTRUMENTS ONLY, lens/laws.md:48).

**RQ2 reasoning-channel budget steering.** Provider mechanism now explicit: Gemini 3 dropped numeric thinking_budget for thinking_level (minimal/low/medium/high per model; pro models only low/medium or low/high) per ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/thinking, which also states the EMPTY_REASONING failure in vendor words: max_output_tokens is a hard cutoff INCLUDING thought tokens, hit-mid-reasoning returns "truncated or empty output (while still billing for any thinking tokens generated)", and Google's sanctioned fix is to lower thinking_level rather than shrink the token cap - the provider-side twin of our classifier at lens/laws.md:34-36. OpenAI/Meta equivalents: reasoning_effort docs (developers.openai.com; dev.meta.ai recommends lowest acceptable level); a controlled study in GitHub issue #58369 measured 2.9x more output tokens and 4.8x more cache-read tokens at higher effort (SNIPPET-ONLY, not deep-read, OWED). Training-side alternative already held by the 10-06 sibling sweep (ConfSFT 2609.31619).

**RQ3 cheap-seat shoulders.** Weave Router is the published shape of cheap-owns-the-floor: 60-70 pct of agentic requests are "short, structurally simple completions" served by open models "at roughly one-fortieth of the cost", premium reserved by parity constraint - the vendor mirror of our shoulders law (lens/laws.md:66). Neuron Daily adds: D-Engine (14x to 42x fewer tokens than full agentic loop, tied quality 48/50) and Foxfire's split cheap-perception + planner for robot control where long reasoning hurt. Speculative decoding stays arXiv-fresh (Nucleus Speculative Decoding 2610.07822; APEX 2610.07780, titles read from the arXiv listing, not deep-read).

**RQ4 local throughput (MoE vs dense CPU/Vulkan; FP8 status).** GAP, honestly: no late-2026 page I deep-read measures 7B-class MoE vs dense on CPU/Vulkan. Snippet-level only: llama.cpp FP8 remains server/GPU-side, GGUF k-quants/i-quants/q8_0 native, direct FP8-to-GGUF conversion still issue-stage (github.com, bare URL, not resolved); an arXiv 2601 unified eval of llama.cpp quant formats exists (ID unknown from snippet). On-box numbers from the Oct 2 article: Alex Ziskand measured a 256GB M5 Ultra Mac Studio at 38.7 tok/s vs two DGX Sparks at 34.3 (hardware comparison, not MoE-vs-dense); gutsy runs a 775MB Qwen3.5-0.8B fine-tune on CPU through llama.cpp with 0.021 calibration error (a CPU-capable decision rung, sub-7B). The estate's own 72.97 tok/s Vulkan vs 6.0 CPU measurement (lens/burns.md:134-135) remains the on-box truth; the published MoE-vs-dense CPU/Vulkan cell is OWED and unmeasured publicly.

**RQ5 batch/offpeak economics.** Invoice-strength answer for the owed check (lens/burns.md:221-222): DeepSeek's official page (api-docs.deepseek.com/quick_start/pricing, fetched 2026-10-07) lists NO batch discount; the discount levers are timing and cache - off-peak = half price, peak windows only 01:00-04:00 and 06:00-10:00 UTC Mon-Fri (excluding Chinese public holidays), "All other hours are off-peak, including weekends and Chinese public holidays in full"; deepseek-flash (V4.1-Flash) cache-miss input $0.15 off-peak / $0.30 peak, output $0.60 / $1.20, cache-hit input $0.003 / $0.006 (a 50x hit-vs-miss spread that makes cache-stable prompts the real lever); V4-Pro $0.66/$1.32 miss, $1.98/$3.96 output; concurrency Flash 2500, Pro 500. Lane-runner encoding: schedule ds_workhorse batches outside the two peak windows, keep prompt prefixes stable for cache hits, weekends are fully off-peak. Structure overlap: memory/burns/MANIFEST.json already holds the window+halving shape; the price table, cache-hit spread, no-batch-discount verdict, and concurrency caps are the new bytes. Cross-provider: OpenAI Batch API (developers.openai.com) holds 50 pct off, 24h window, 50K requests / 200MB per file, 2000 batches/hour, single model per file, no reasoning-effort documentation on the page; Anthropic Message Batches and Gemini Batch also flat 50 pct (LeanLM comparison, snippet). A time-based (DeepSeek) vs batch-queue (OpenAI/Anthropic/Google) split is the publish shape.
