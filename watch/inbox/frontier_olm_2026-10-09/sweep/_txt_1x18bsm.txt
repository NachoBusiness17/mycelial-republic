=== ? | /u/fantasticsid on Quantization of Linear-Attention (Qwen & Kimi) | by /u/fantasticsid
This is interesting, but it's not useful at all at the scale most private/local inference operates at.
 Example: each 27b Qwen sequence wants around or about 200MB of recurrent state across all 48 GDN layers at F32. Quantising this to 6 bit is going to drop that to perhaps 40-50MB? At the cost of some quality?
 This is useful for people running giant concurrent batches across tens of users, where the recurrent state overhead (which is a fixed overhead per-sequence, even if the KV cache is basically empty) actually adds up.

=== ? | /u/FullOf_Bad_Ideas on Quantization of Linear-Attention (Qwen & Kimi) | by /u/FullOf_Bad_Ideas
Here's the launch command I ended up with
  SGLANG_PP_LAYER_PARTITION=8,10,8,8,8,9,8,5 PYTORCH_CUDA_ALLOC_CONF=expandable_segments:True NCCL_P2P_LEVEL=SYS python -m stepquant.sglang --model-path /path/to/Qwen-27B-W8A8 --pipeline-parallel-size 8 --state-format stepquant4 --plan artifacts/reproduction/plans/qwen-stepquant4.pt --attention-backend flashinfer --reasoning-parser qwen3 --max-running-requests 1024 --host 0.0.0.0 --port 2300 --kv-cache-dtype fp8_e5m2 --kv-watermark 0.8 --chunked-prefill-size 1024 --context-length 8192 --mem-fraction-static 0.835 
 PP layer partitioning coupled with set context length (I forgot to set it earlier) plus using flashinfer instead of Triton, plus fp8 kv cache with reduced chunked prefill. STEPQuant doesn't support PP or batch size larger than 512 but Claude coded it up for me.
 Feel free to ask questions, the workload I'm running has code here - https://github.com/adamo1139/polskie-dane-instruktazowe
