## Answers to the four research questions

**Q1 (prefix-cache economics at 50-wide fan-out vs byte-identical-ask).** The frontier converges on: the cache is a per-(model, session-prefix) asset, so economics live at routing/ordering time, not per ask. Harness Tokenomics (2609.28919) prices it: one user turn is k+1 requests over one model's prompt cache, so routing is legal only where no conversation rebuilds its cache (session start, side lanes, subagent launch), recovering 13-21% of spend; on long tool-heavy sessions the priciest model can be cheapest (crossover ~71k tokens at 18 tool steps), which is the deliberate-ask pay-the-context-tax-once doctrine with a price sheet attached. HEAR (2610.06597) says the byte-identical rule is only the harness half: engines need a protocol to hear workflow intent (KV reuse 19.2% -> 87.6% on SCBench, 1.61x batch speedup). Request Order Matters (2610.05833) is the fan-out hazard: with persistent caches and selective reuse, identical prompts answer differently depending on request order (69.0% answer variation under token top-k); 50 concurrent children sharing rolling prefixes sit exactly there. When Fancy Eviction Fails (2609.28870): on production traces recency dominates 14 policies; LRU plus selective enhancements is the verdict, vindicating the estate's LRU null-arm instrument. PCR (2610.02766) prices retention windows exactly and finds simple ordered (coherent) timeouts hit the unrestricted optimum in 118/120 cases: cheap rules, priced properly, beat fancy.

**Q2 (KV management vs L0d shedding + prefetch spilling).** ReFold (2610.07863) is the strongest direct answer to L0d: fold the rendered context, keep the underlying history, make every removal reversible, and rewrite the cached prefix only every few steps (2.5x token cut, KV halved, up to 92% of forced compactions avoided, no task-success loss). That is shedding with a restore path, i.e. the exact missing piece of a context-pack shed. TempoKV (2609.35065) is the prefetch-spilling counterpoint: record reuse hits as metadata-only claims and commit staging resources only when estimated time-to-use falls to time-to-ready (63-91% byte-time cut vs immediate staging); early signal, late commitment. Lachesis (2610.08378, census) and LM-CXD (2609.26828, census) extend the tiering story; RaReCache (2610.11358) makes the janitor/specialist ladder concrete: a 23x-smaller model prefills for the big one, recompute 30% of positions, keep 95-99% accuracy (5.0x median TTFT at saturation).

**Q3 (does LLMLingua-class compression clear LENS 2).** The systematic evidence says: not by token counts alone. Beyond Token Savings (2609.32961, already banked 2026-10-03, ~35,000 runs) shows fewer tokens need not mean faster or cheaper (cache invalidation re-bills prefixes; summarizer calls ate 28-46% of solved-run wall-clock), and policy rankings reverse across models, so any compression verdict must be an instrument-only, per-task-coverage, measured verdict, which is LENS 2 exactly. New this window: When Fancy Eviction Fails releases production traces + simulator (byte-local reproducible); MORSE (2609.27380, census) shows evidence survival under compression depends on context ordering; the Attribution-Compression Frontier (2609.14245, census) measures citation-precision collapse under compression, a receipts-law result. No banked item ships an LLMLingua-class benchmark that is byte-reproducible locally yet; that stays OWED.

**Q4 (deliberate-ask / context-tax-once analogue).** Three independent pricings landed: Harness Tokenomics derives the payback formula for mid-task switches (n* = (W_t + W_h - R_t - R_h)/(R_h - R_t) with return; Opus 5 -> Sonnet 5 pays back in 27 requests) and shows cache-read price asymmetry flips which tier is cheapest; PCR makes "how long to keep the paid-for prefix" an exact min-cut with an auditable benchmark; Total Cost of Agency (2609.23790, abstract-level) decomposes multi-agent bills and finds memory-injection tokens hit 27.6% of billed cost at depth six (uncached case, prompt caching explicitly not evaluated). The estate's frozen-RIB doctrine maps to the router's session-start decision point: choose once where the cache stays warm, never re-pack mid-flight.
