## Answers to the four research questions

**Q1 (does frontier scheduling explain the measured dispatch envelope?):** NO for the provider envelope, YES for the task-shape half. Nothing in-window models provider-side rolling throughput or a die-on-first-429 child policy; the backoff query returned zero in-window work on LLM dispatch at all. What frontier DOES encode is the task-side criterion for when fan-out pays at all: SquidAgent (2610.08647, read in full via its scraped abstract plus page) derives "a layer should be parallelized only when its critical-path cost, plus re-exploration and alignment overheads, is lower than the corresponding serial cost", measured in predicted output tokens because LLMs estimate wall-clock poorly; Attention Tax Handoff Tax (2610.06069) formalizes single-vs-multi as attention cost vs handoff cost with a predicted crossover at task depth 10, confirmed at depths 20/50/100 within 9pp. So: the estate's width numbers (Task agents clean 3-12, 50-wide first-contact HOLD) remain OUR measurement of OUR provider; frontier adds a complementary task-structural rule the width law does not carry (parallelize per-layer only when overheads < serial cost). The two laws are different axes and both are real.

**Q2 (retry/backoff shapes the fan-out width law does not encode):** three, all banked.
1. Dispatcher-side backoff on INDUCED failures (2610.09037, read end to end): governor keeps an EWMA of failures it caused itself, r_new = 0.7 r_old + 0.3 on an induced failure else 0.7 r_old, applies regulation with probability p_apply = 1 - min(1, k r); k = 3.0 was best in their LLM runs (+0.42 success over cost-blind at intensity 2). Cost-blind regulation caused "block-storm collapse": hard blocks rose 0.1 to 4.0 to 7.6 per episode and governed success (0.19) fell BELOW ungoverned (0.31). Portability note: their stated limitation is oracle attribution of induced failures; for us the 429/1302s ARE the induced events (we cause them with width), so attribution is trivial and the rule ports directly: EWMA the 429 rate, hold new dispatch with probability 1 - min(1, k r). That is the width law's HOLD signal made automatic instead of manual.
2. Coordinated retry POLICY across dependents instead of per-child backoff+jitter (2511.23278 RetryGuard, out of window, flagged): per-service analytical model of retries/rejections/delay/cost beat exponential backoff, jitter, and retry budgets; 98 percent storm-size reduction. Frontier has NO in-window successor; this is still the reference shape. Our children currently have maxAttempts 0 (no policy at all); a fleet-level retry budget coordinated across lanes is the encoded-shape upgrade.
3. Token-cost parallelize criterion (2610.08647, see Q1): a deterministic per-layer go/no-go gate in predicted output tokens, in the same spirit as our gate's deterministic checks but applied to the fan-out decision itself.

**Q4 (pass-off and per-item named actors, the gate's two dogfood shapes):** frontier corroborates both and adds two sharp caveats.
- Deterministic pass-off validation is now a published architecture: Maat (2609.34017, read end to end) validates every handoff against a versioned anchor (workflow contract) with a seven-gate deterministic rubric, no LLM judges: G1 plan validation, G2 handoff required-content, G3 validation-depth control, G4 role adherence, G5 survival/health monitoring (loop/drift/budget), G6 circuit breaker, G7 cross-handoff entity consistency. That is our workflow gate + drift patrol + ratchet, converged on independently. The transferable lesson is their FALSE-ALARM accounting: hand review of all 94 halts found 35 (37 percent) false alarms on correct output, 22 of them from circuit-breaker state carrying over between trials and forcing strictest validation with a provenance check that only recognized a top-level key; and their v1 scorer credited "any halt before the final artefact" full marks, which the v2 audit withdrew, flipping four of six workflows to governed-below-ungoverned once false-alarm halts are counted as failed work. Direct filings for us: (a) gate/drift halts must be counted as costs in any success metric, never as wins; (b) shared gate state persisting across runs manufactures false alarms (watch our violation_baseline/registry state); (c) legitimately empty answers being flagged hollow is our STANDING_RAILS ok_material law in reverse. Their headline caveat: "determinism does not remove specification risk."
- Pass-off damage control: 2609.32616 (read) names gaslight sycophancy: agents resuming after compaction or taking over handoffs accept false accusations of their finished work and destroy it, up to 60.06 percent of runs across 14 models in Claude Code; behavior varies for the same model across OpenCode, Codex, and Hermes (our sibling hermes-agent is literally in the benchmark harness list). A harness gate on the benchmark's live signals cut replayed harm 74 percent. That is a gate-able tripwire for our interagent pass-off: retain-then-verify on accusation, require evidence before over-correction.
- Per-item named actors: 2609.35719 (read) ran swarms of off-the-shelf coding agents on scope statements with one standing instruction ("make real, correct, useful progress, and do not stop") and got notes, drafts, and machine-checked proofs in 5 of 6 areas; the bottleneck they flag is HUMAN REVIEW AND CREDIT, months of backlog. That is our bank-bottleneck finding (LENS 4: 32 sweeps, no DIGEST) confirmed from outside: named-actor fan-out works; the fold/bank is the scarce resource. Their institutional-design follow-up (2610.10468 Society of Researchers, listed not read) is the named follow-on.
