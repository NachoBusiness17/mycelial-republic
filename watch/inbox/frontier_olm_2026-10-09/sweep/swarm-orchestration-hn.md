## Answers to the four research questions

**Q1 (does scheduling research explain the dispatch envelope, or is it provider-side?):** Frontier swarm work does NOT explain the envelope; the provider's limiter arithmetic does. The rate-limiter taxonomy shows "100/min" is either a fixed window (can pass 2x at seams) or a token bucket (guarantees an average, not a cap), so the estate's measured "rolling throughput cap, first-contact-429 as HOLD, post-burst 1302s decay until the window clears" is exactly client-side contact with a server-side bucket/window, not an agent-scheduling phenomenon. What frontier scheduling research adds is the client's two real levers: WHEN to send (pacing) and WHAT to do after a kill (resume vs redo). HarnessTax reinforces that harness/dispatch differences move cost (up to 5x) far more than success (+/-2 to +/-5 points), so the envelope debate is an economics debate, not a quality one.

**Q2 (retry/backoff shapes the fan-out width law does not yet encode):** Three. (a) RESUME-AT-RESET: agent-limit-retry keys recovery to the provider's own reset timestamp read from the transcript, resumes the same session by id, and never re-dispatches (their census: 449 killed, 8 resumed, 438 redone when this is absent). Our law encodes interval backoff and width caps; it does not encode "429 = scheduled wake-up at resetsAt". (b) EFFECT-DEDUPE RETRIES: pi-temporal retries model calls freely because the transcript dedupes them, and never auto-reruns unknown-outcome tool calls (claim files + outcome-unknown reporting). This is the safe-retry half that would let workflow children retry instead of dying on first 429. (c) QUEUE-FIRST PACING + STICKY ROUTES: Relay paces at the gateway within a wait budget before degrading, applies model cooldowns, and keeps per-conversation routes sticky to protect KV cache. Estate delta: B2-320 already landed jittered exponential backoff (uniform(0,min(60,2*2^n))), a 60/min token bucket with retryable-vs-permanent classification, and per-lane breakers in scripts/union_fullsend_day.py (memory/burns/LEDGER.jsonl:479), but memory/research_polished/kv_cache_compaction.md:23 still records fan-out craft as "unconsumed by code" for mag/cache_router.py. The missing two shapes are resume-at-reset and sticky-route pacing.

**Q3 (does the agent_platform mine shelf hold flock-decode precedent?):** NO at filename and index level; the shelf is the wrong shelf. The flock-decode open question is Q1 of lens/laws.md:251-256: the flock eval was wrong 3/3 at high consensus strength and "decode must map to the ACTUAL task output space, a real redesign, not a patch" (RIB_MINDSWEAVE_EVAL_CONSENSUS_ILLUSION_20260810.md:40-43), i.e. a consensus-to-verdict decode problem. **Q4 (interagent pass-off and per-item named actors):** Both gate dogfood shapes are confirmed and extended by frontier practice. Pass-off: Chief of Staff passes work via committed brief files and an MCP board with atomic status flips, treats sessions as disposable and the board as the memory, and verifies by re-running claimed commands (exit codes authoritative), never by trusting self-reports; its thread independently converges on plain Markdown tickets + one strong planner over many executors. Named actors: Swarm tags every agent prompt with a job tag and gates completion on an independent judge's "met" verdict; agent-limit-retry gives each subagent a durable id whose on-disk context survives limit kills and resumes BY ID, not re-dispatch. The extension the gate does not yet encode: named actors should be durable ACROSS kills (resume-by-id), and pass-off receipts should be re-runnable commands rather than claimed outputs.
