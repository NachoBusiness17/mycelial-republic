### 1. Toby Ord, "AI Scaling vs Human Scaling" (published 2026-10-09, today) + his
launch tweet 2108542318022459549 (10-09 12:57Z)

Tweet resolved via fxtwitter (`_fx_tobyord2.json`, 716 views); essay fetched in full at

power law in tokens, H80 proportional to T^gamma; UK AISI CTF chart (July 2026): early
models needed "10,000x as many tokens to scale their time horizons by 10x" (gamma =
0.25) while later models need "just 20x as many tokens" (gamma ~ 0.75), the improvement
landing "in less than a year" with a possible plateau at gamma = 2/3; METR-era humans
gain "about 4x as much score per doubling" of resources vs AIs. The swarm law composes:
H80 proportional to N^(lambda*gamma); worked example at lambda = 0.5, gamma = 0.5: a 10x
time-horizon gain costs either "a chain of thought 100x as long" or "10,000x as many
agents"; and the RSI gate r = lambda*gamma/beta, where gamma < 1 makes an intelligence
explosion harder. Also read: Import AI 475 (Jack Clark) covering the predecessor
"Swarm Scaling" essay, incl. the stepping-on-toes frame: 10x agents yields "10lambda x
as much" performance, only 3x to 5x.

measured envelope (width 10 clean, 109.4M tok/h) is a THROUGHPUT envelope; Ord supplies
the CAPABILITY envelope: parallel width buys wall-clock speed but sublinear capability

capability from width alone needs 10,000x agents, so the cheap win is shape (dispatch
