## Q1 — consensus-decode redesign prior art (flock verdict illusion, strength 0.60-0.67 agreeing 0/3)

**YES, adopt-don't-invent exists.** Amazon Science's ICML 2026 piece "When LLM judges agree, should we believe them?" (Balasubramanian, Podkopaev, Kasiviswanathan) does exactly the flock-to-output-space mapping: model the judge panel as a network, learn per-judge reliability AND pairwise dependence, treat true labels as latent variables, infer via alternating updates — unsupervised, no ground truth needed. Panels of 10 judges at temperature zero on three binary tasks: dependence-aware Ising aggregation beats weighted majority vote by 9-14% (relevance 0.912 vs 0.820; toxicity 0.792 vs 0.694; summarization 0.806 vs 0.737, uniform vote 0.561). Direct quote: "the important question is not only how many judges agreed but how independently they arrived at that agreement." This is the instrument for RIB_MINDSWEAVE_EVAL_CONSENSUS_ILLUSION's exact disease (flock = N cheap samples -> consensus at strength 0.60-0.67 agreeing 0/3 with ground): the flock's samples are one model's correlated draws, i.e. the degenerate case of their panel analysis — the dependence term would correctly discount it to near-zero independent evidence. Their diversity rule operationalizes law 18: "mixing model families helps only if it changes error patterns." HN thread (49699590, 49 comments) adds: qarl cites Cohen/Hamri/Geva/Globerson "LM vs LM" (cross-examination detects >70% of incorrect claims at >80% precision — the cheap second-judge corroboration move); piih gives a correlated-blind-spot example across Gemini/Claude/GPT (all say Pit Imp has 2 eyes); pessimizer warns correlation-discounting can become sculpting the opinion range you want.

## Q2 — multi-judge calibration, closing Ember second-judge debt without doubling spend

Two complementary steals. (a) The Ising aggregation above needs MORE judges but they can be cheap ones — it explicitly evaluates the panel as a whole, so a flock of cheap locals + one remote gets a calibrated confidence instead of a second premium judge seat. (b) softwaredoug "Classic ML to Cope with Dumb LLM Judges": treat many dumb judge outputs as ML features, train a decision tree on human labels (7,000 examples, local laptop LLM as feature generator) — 96.5-96.7% precision at 36-41% recall vs 75.08% for a single forced judge, and vs the precision/recall death spiral of abstention tricks. Quote: "We can take lots of dumb agents, making single, basic decisions, and combine their outputs into something smarter using traditional ML." Falsifier carried by the author himself: trees overfit, "treat it as an interesting data point in a scientific lab notebook, not a guarantee." For EMBER-1's "NEVER the only judge" law this is the cheap second family: not a second LLM family, a learned combiner over the first family's attribute-split votes — though note law 18 two-family corroboration is stricter than a stacked combiner; the combiner inherits the single-family blind spots the Ising paper warns about. Use both: combiner for cheap daily verdicts, Ising-adjusted confidence for the anything-gates.

## Q3 — Maat-style false-alarm accounting for the estate gate stack

Two new instruments. (a) vals.ai's independent Jev eval carries the cleanest error-budget protocol in the wild: set a confidence threshold on 30% of audited data against an error budget, validate realized error on the other 70% — and six of twelve systems EXCEEDED their own tuned budget out of sample. That is the 37-citation-debts-accepted-on-baseline-125 question in miniature: a gate whose accepted-debt ledger grows is a gate blowing its error budget; the vals protocol (split-tune/validate) is the falsifier that catches it. (b) arXiv 2610.03448 "Passing the Test You Trained On": 15 prompt-injection detectors + 2 judges; best BIPIA detector catches only 2% of AgentDojo injections at 1% FPR; a 72%-AgentDojo detector catches 15% on tau-bench; FPRs on tool outputs "range from none to over 90%" and unlike detection rates DO transfer between benchmarks; the best detector on both agent benchmarks "shares no data with any benchmark and was trained on agent-style inputs." Implication for citation_lint/violation_ratchet/census_drift_patrol: report gate performance at a fixed low false-positive rate on OUR OWN outputs (our diffs, our prose), not on public benchmark-shaped inputs; audit for train/test contamination (a gate validated on the same prose style it will police is passing the test it trained on). The one-denominator-owed Maat debt pairs with this: the transferable denominator is the false-alarm rate, not the catch rate.

## Q4 — cheaper instrument for the owed decision-model meter rows (n>=40)

The outside world spent October running exactly this experiment, and the answer is: bounded yes, unbounded no. Red Hat (166 pts, 76 comments): "we did not find that decision models produced faster, cheaper, or higher-quality answers compared with LLM-as-a-judge"; Qwen3.6-35B judge beat Jev on injection accuracy (89.31% vs 86.35%) at LOWER latency (312ms vs 348ms); deberta-v3 pre-trained classifier nearly matched both at 54ms. vals.ai: Jev ties frontier on bounded document verification (0.975, $0.02/1k cases, 1/498th of Astra cost, 194x lower latency at 32 judgments) but finishes LAST of twelve on LegalBench (0.730 class-balanced, answered Yes to all 33 permissible_copy items) with worst ECE 0.108. tessl: 2.7x cheaper/13.6x faster on 2,725 verifier pairs but 85.9% agreement and zero ground-truth measurement. Red Hat thread falsifier (rickintoplace): on ALCE citations Jev flagged 87.5% unsupported vs 89.3% for a single-token Qwen3.5-397B readout — "speed/confidence come from single-token decoding, not Jev itself." So: the meter stays owed for decision models as SOLE instruments, but the cheap adopt is a single-token probability readout from an existing LLM + vals-style deferral routing (Jev+1% deferral to Astra = $0.27 vs $12 per 1k; on legal-style work deferral must be 64%). The meter row cost can drop ~3x by pricing label-only vs probability (LLM probability requests cost 29-101% MORE than label-only — buy labels, not distributions, except Gemini 4 Argon at -3%).

## Q5 — conditions-suspect economics under explicit thinking-off protocol

Outside replication exists and the answer is domain-split, not uniform. Swift-Qwen3.8-27B (LoRA penalizing reasoning-marker tokens): -58.3% thinking tokens (GPQA median 6,642 -> 2,771), x1.95 speed, GPQA-Diamond 88.28% vs base 88.38% at xhigh — but AIME 2026 98.67 -> 94.00 and HMMT 99.33 -> 96.00 (math drops 3-4.7 points) while LiveCodeBench v6 IMPROVED 76.76 -> 81.55. Quote: "We built Swift by identifying reasoning-marker tokens that, in our analysis, trigger overthinking in Qwen's reasoning rollouts." So the pre-2026-10-04 conditions-suspect worry (unmeasured ON default) is validated in the wild twice over: (a) Swift shows thinking-off preserves general reasoning but taxes math — an unmeasured thinking-off default is silently redistributing accuracy across task families; (b) the Red Hat thread caught the same defect in OTHER people's benchmarks: meander_water flagged that Luna "appears run with reasoning off, which seems like an unfair comparison" in AnthusAI's decision-model numbers. The estate's explicit thinking-off protocol is ahead of public practice; the replication says log per-domain deltas, not a single accuracy number, or the meter itself becomes conditions-suspect.
