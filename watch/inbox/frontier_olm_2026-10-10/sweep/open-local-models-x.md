## Q1: Do any janitor-scale open-weight families beat the incumbents (gemma:2b scut, gemma4 short worker) on measured benchmarks?

**No displacement. Outside consensus still crowns gemma4 at the small end.** The strongest measured outside ranking is Morph's "Best Ollama Models 2026" (https://www.morphllm.com/best-ollama-models, ranking table verified 2026-09-29, pull tags verified against the live Ollama library): "gemma4 (e2b 7.2GB up to 31b 20GB) now leads the small end with vision and audio; qwen3.5 spans 0.8b to 122b". Under instruments-only law, nothing in the outside data fires a swap of the scut or short-worker rungs. The adjacent rivals at janitor scale, with numbers: `qwen3.5:0.8b` at 1.0GB download serving 256K context, and `gemma4:e2b-it-qat` at 4.3GB with quantization-aware training ("an 8GB card now runs a multimodal model instead of a text-only 7B"). September arrivals at the small-to-mid end: `nemotron-3.5-lightning` (30B MoE, 3B active, 25GB, 1M context, aimed at always-on agents) and IBM `granite4.2` (2.2GB for 3b, 5.3GB for 8b, 128K context, Apache 2.0). Two steal-grade gotchas from the same page: "models under 7B lose the most quality from 4-bit quantization" (the scut rung's quant choice is the one that punishes sloppiness), and Ollama's default context is 4096, so any estate instrument that assumes long context must set `num_ctx` explicitly. The only local coder with a hard outside number in the whole ranking is devstral:24b at 46.8% SWE-Bench Verified; BenchLM (October 2026, surfaced in search, not deep-read) claims Qwen3.6-27B at 77.2 SWE-bench Verified on one 24GB GPU.

Kisoku 1.6B (https://github.com/0arch-io/kisoku, HN 2026-10-09) is the honest falsifier-side data point: a from-scratch 1.6B trained solo on a TPU v4-32 grant on ~0.5T tokens that "matches Meta's Llama 3.2 1B (five wins each) while using about 18 times less training data" (GSM8K 15.0 vs 5.8, MMLU 34.2 vs 31.3, same lm-eval-harness 0.4.13 for both). Its own README concedes Qwen2.5-1.5B and SmolLM2-1.7B beat both on most tests. Apache 2.0, GGUF available for Ollama. Value to the estate is not the weights (they lose to the incumbents) but the method receipt: per-sample eval outputs published as a dataset, a contamination audit, and a same-harness-for-every-model discipline, which is the instruments-only pattern the model garden should copy.

## Q2: Is the HN fresh lane surfacing local-serving stacks mature enough to move work from funded-remote down to free/local?

**Yes, three landed in the last 10 days, and one plugs directly into harnesses the estate runs.** Magnitude (YC S25) (https://github.com/magnitudedev/magnitude, HN 2026-09-30, 194 points / 97 comments): open-source consumer inference engine that profiles the machine, recommends models, and compiles and tunes kernels on-device: "It compiles and tunes its kernels on your device, so open models run up to 2x faster than llama.cpp", "92% faster decode on Metal, 19% on CUDA", "27% less memory per agent, freed when agents stop". Apache 2.0, runs on Apple Silicon / NVIDIA / AMD / CPU-only, Windows supported. The load-bearing detail: one-click connections for **Pi, OpenCode, Hermes, OpenClaw, Codex, Claude Code, Oh My Pi, Cline**, anything else via OpenAI-compatible API. The consumer-local tier now ships first-class harness integration for the exact seat surface this estate runs; that is the descent ramp from funded-remote to local for short-worker-class work.

GridCore (https://blokhin.us/notes/gridcore-gpu-scheduler/, HN 2026-10-06, MIT, repo w512/GridCore): a Go scheduler that runs several local models on one GPU, one llama-server per model, with class-based priority (interactive over background over batch, "Strict class order. Interactive always goes first"), cost-based eviction instead of LRU, and the safety invariant "A process with requests in flight is never stopped." Measured on RTX 4060 Ti 16GB with 4 models resident (15.5/16GB): interactive p50 3.4s / p95 4.1s across 366 requests; honest cost accounting (chat p50 1.9s idle rising to 3.4s mixed). Its indictment of the incumbent matches our pain exactly: Ollama "loads/unloads models but serves requests FIFO" with "no idea that a chat matters more than indexing". This is the multi-janitor-one-GPU problem (gemma:2b scut co-resident with gemma4 short worker) solved outside, with numbers, at ~9,700 lines and 181 tests. Bonus outside confirmation: its memory-measurement table lists Gemma 4 E2B actual at 2,924MB against 5,678MB file-size estimate, the -8% to +94% estimate error that motivated tensor-level GGUF header counting.

Counter-evidence worth holding: "Why we removed Ollama from Return" (https://returneditor.ai/blog/not-a-setting-why-we-removed-ollama/, 2026-07-03 with a September 2026 update). A legal-document-analysis product dropped Ollama entirely because Ollama 0.12's `-cloud` model suffix lets the local daemon proxy to Ollama's datacenter: "the inability to exfiltrate document content should be a property of the code we ship, not a setting". They replaced it with bundled llama-server sidecars on loopback ephemeral ports, later compiling llama.cpp themselves with remote inference and HTTPS compiled out. For a sovereignty estate this is the steal-grade line of the sweep, and it is also a standing caution for the descent: the convenient local door (Ollama) is drifting cloudward; llama.cpp-direct is the sovereignty-preserving base layer.

Consumer-side descent signal: XDA (https://www.xda-developers.com/im-not-paying-20-for-chatgpt-claude-or-gemini-because-a-free-local-llm-does-everything-i-need/, 2026-10-06, 52 points on HN): Qwen 3.8-27B, Unsloth UD-IQ4_XS build, 13.3GB on a 4070 Ti Super 16GB, llama.cpp over localhost, "The model holds about 33.7 tokens per second across runs", replacing a paid subscription for email triage, extraction, and one-shot Pygame coding; honest caveats (frontier still better at code; readers flag overnight-run loops and electricity cost). Mainstream press now treats the paid-to-local substitution as ordinary.

## Q3: Does question-class routing dominance (36.3 vs 3.6 percent variance, laws lens law 14) replicate in outside evidence?

**Not yet, and the estate should treat law 14 as still-unreplicated outside.** The closest public instrument is OpenRouter's "Model Router Benchmarks" (https://openrouter.ai/blog/announcements/model-router-benchmarks/, 2026-10-02, Brian Thomas). It benchmarks routers (OpenRouter Auto Router, Free Models Router, Unbiased Pareto, Sakana Fugu-Max, Jev Router, NVIDIA Switchyard) against individual-model baselines with a Router Index: "quality (benchmark score) at 60%, time per task at 20%, and cost at 20%". The motivating cost spread is real and large: "GPT-6 Astra's average price paid per token is over 48x higher" than DeepSeek v4 Flash, and 21x more expensive for an average 10-49 turn Codex session. But the post contains **no variance decomposition and no per-question-class tables**; routing is motivated by task diversity ("legal research vs. coding" in their words) and session stage, not by measured class-conditional dominance. Its caveats list (cache rebuild cost on model switches, insufficient prompt signal, heuristic/session disagreement, routing-latency overhead) reads like the estate's own falsifier queue for law 14. Sriti Core (https://github.com/sriti-ai/sriti-core, Apache 2.0) is a second outside build of the estate's own tier ladder (Tier 3 local Ollama free, Tier 2 Groq/Fireworks/DeepInfra, Tier 1 frontier, cascade-on-failure, semantic cache with per-class thresholds: "conversational questions allow a similarity threshold of 0.75" while "structured data extraction requires 0.92", classifier under 10ms local) but publishes zero outcome benchmarks, so it corroborates the architecture, not the variance law. Net: the 36.3-vs-3.6 claim currently stands on estate instruments alone; the public world argues routing from cost spreads, not class dominance. Law 14's falsifier has not fired outside either; it is simply untested there.
