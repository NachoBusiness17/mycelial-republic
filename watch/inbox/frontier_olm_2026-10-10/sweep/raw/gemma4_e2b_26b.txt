### Built a fully local Mac app on Ollama (dictation, meeting notes, RAG): numbers and lessons from Gemma 4 e2b vs 26b, open source
I built a Mac app that does dictation, meeting notes and "ask my work anything" with nothing leaving the machine. We've released it free and open source (GPL-3.0) through DevPlus. Links at the bottom. Sharing the stack and numbers, because the local pieces are finally fast enough for this to feel instant.
 The stack
 Speech: NVIDIA Parakeet TDT 0.6B v3 through FluidAudio, compiled with Core ML for the Neural Engine (v2 for English only). Whisper large-v3-turbo through WhisperKit for the other languages.
 Cleanup, meeting notes, answers and extraction: Gemma 4 in Ollama, picked by RAM: e2b-it-qat on 8 GB (4.3 GB download), e4b-it-qat on 16 GB, 12b from 24 GB, 26b from 48 GB (18.6 GB). Any OpenAI-compatible server works instead.
 Retrieval: SQLite. FTS5 with BM25 plus EmbeddingGemma vectors, merged with reciprocal rank fusion (k = 60). The top 8 passages go to the model as numbered sources it has to cite.
 Graph: the model extracts people, companies, projects, topics and the relations between them as strict JSON at temperature 0.
 Numbers (M5 Max, 128 GB, models already loaded)
 Parakeet v3 transcribes a 9.8 second sentence in about 100 ms.
 Cleaning up one dictated sentence: 100 to 430 ms on gemma4:e2b-it-qat, 250 to 900 ms on gemma4:26b. The first call after loading: 0.4 s vs 8.7 s.
 Hybrid search, including embedding the query: 60 to 90 ms.
 An answer with 8 cited sources: 2 to 2.5 s on e2b, 2.5 to 7 s on 26b.
 This is the highest end Mac, so I'd love to see numbers from base M1/M2 owners.
 Lessons I learned along the way
 Small models remove fillers fine but miss longer self-corrections. "let's meet at two actually three" came out as "three" on both. But "I'll send you the checklist by Friday, actually make that Thursday afternoon" came back unchanged from e2b, while 26b wrote "by Thursday afternoon". The fix wasn't a bigger model: corrections that swap a day, time, number or month ("actually", "make that", "no wait", "I mean") are now settled with a small deterministic pass before the model sees the text, and a few examples in the prompt cover the rest. Now e2b gets it right too, and 26b stopped treating a new sentence that starts with "Actually" as a correction.
 Cleanup models love to answer a dictated question instead of transcribing it. So I built a guard that compares the output with what you said (length and drift) and falls back to rule-based cleanup rather than paste words you never said.
 Keep the system prompt byte-stable so Ollama reuses the cached prefix. Everything that varies (the app you're in, your vocabulary) goes after it.
 Don't let the model do dates. It extracts the promise ("I'll send it by Friday"); a deterministic parser turns "Friday", "EOD" or "in 2 days" into a date. The small models invented times constantly.
 Reciprocal rank fusion was simpler and better than trying to put BM25 and cosine scores on one scale.
 Small models loop. A loop guard stops generation when output starts repeating, and a salvage parser keeps the well-formed JSON objects when an extraction comes back malformed.
 Two questions for this sub, which small model (under 8 GB) have you found best at transcript cleanup that respects self-corrections? And for entity extraction into strict JSON?

### /u/hfeixas on Built a fully local Mac app on Ollama (dictation, meeting notes, RAG): numbers and lessons from Gemma 4 e2b vs 26b, open source
thanks! 
 yeah, I wanted real timings, fast means nothing without them. on the hybrid search, at personal scale you really don't need a vector db. I keep FTS5 for keywords and the embeddings as normalized blobs in a plain SQLite table, loaded into memory and compared brute force. then I take the top 40 from each side and merge them with RRF (k=60). For dates, what fixed it for me was asking the model for the deadline exactly as it was written ("Friday", "EOD", "next week") and never an actual date. a boring parser can do the rest.
 The cache trick - my dictionary only changes when you add a word, so it can live in the system prompt. Anything per request (the app you're in, text around the cursor) goes in the user turn.

### /u/hfeixas on Built a fully local Mac app on Ollama (dictation, meeting notes, RAG): numbers and lessons from Gemma 4 e2b vs 26b, open source
I went through all three:
 Digests with llama.cpp: confirmed. With thinking models, llama.cpp sends the reasoning separately from the answer, and Binders only read the answer. The reasoning used up the token budget, so the digest came back cut off or empty, and the app never told you why. The next update turns thinking off in the request, and if a digest fails it now says why right in the note. Until then, starting llama-server with --reasoning-budget 0 (or --chat-template-kwargs '{"enable_thinking":false}') should make it work as is.
 Notes jumping to the top: confirmed. Just opening a note counted as an edit, so the list re-sorted. Fixed.
 Misaligned text on resize: I couldn't reproduce this exactly. I did find that list items and checkboxes wrapping onto a second line started back under the bullet instead of lining up with the text, which gets worse as the window gets narrower. Fixed that. If what you saw was something else, a screenshot would help a lot.
 One more fixed along the way, if your llama.cpp server runs on another machine, e.g. over Tailscale, macOS was blocking plain http to it.
 All fixes will be in the next update. Thanks again for helping me make the product better for everyone 😄
