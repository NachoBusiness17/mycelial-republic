## Research question answers

RQ1 (work that lands on disk but never gets folded and committed). Strongest direct attack found: Cognition's "Dreaming" plus the open "Agent Memory Repo" standard. Devin now runs an overnight pass that prunes stale memory records and links latent information (X post, Oct 5, 606K views), and the spec (cognition.com/agent-memory-repo) formalizes it: git-backed markdown memory, "Push after every edit", a background agent whose job is to "Merge duplicates, remove outdated entries, and check sources to resolve contradictions", and git push-conflict rejection forcing parallel agents to reconcile. That is the estate's fold discipline (six untracked carrier dirs, day-9 fold uncommitted, day-8 fold open) expressed as a shipped product plus an open standard. Second attack: DeLM's decentralized coordination, where agents share a task queue and reuse intermediate results, folding findings during work instead of after. Third: Cursor SDK's mid-run steering and background subagents that report back to the parent, a shipped primitive for the pass-off our workflow gate enforces in asks.

RQ2 (dispatcher EWMA backoff, 2610.09037, vs the measured HOLD-signal envelope). Negative finding: no outside EWMA dispatcher backoff surfaced anywhere in the 30-day window through this door. Outside practice is exponential backoff with jitter (Splunk multi-agent coordination guide, Aug 2026; agent-zero GitHub issue proposing backoff plus fallback chains, Mar 2026) and dispatcher-level rate limiting with per-worker state merged at a collector and agent counts capped at 5-8 (glukhov.org architecture guide; the page 403'd on fetch, so this is the search snippet only, page not verified). Verdict: an EWMA port into the workflow gate retry strategy would be borrowing from the estate's own day-9 register, not from outside standard practice; the closest outside shape is the 5-8 agent cap heuristic, which is cruder than our measured 50-child, first-429-is-HOLD envelope. Do not port for novelty's sake; port only if EWMA predicts the 1302 decay window better than the current driver backoff, and measure that locally first.

RQ3 (warm-then-fan-out with sticky routing composed with frozen-prefix caching). Confirmed outside, at infrastructure scale, twice. vLLM x AgentX (Sep 8, 2026): real agentic traces show median 43 turns per session, 142K-token median inputs, >96 percent prefix-cache hit rate, heavy subagent use; their bitter lesson is that "load balancing loses to sticky session-aware routing due to cache locality" and "Routing decisions must account for the state already resident on each worker, not only the amount of queued work". DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash on vLLM (Oct 7, 2026): DP attention with session affinity preserves "prefix-cache locality across turns", delivering a 5.3x throughput improvement under a 150 TPS constraint. Provider economics reinforce the composition: Sonnet 5.5 cache reads were halved to $0.10 per million tokens, making agentic workloads roughly 20 percent cheaper (ClaudeDevs, Oct 8). Cold-seat cost: the trade is queue-balance for cache locality; our known cold-start floor (6.5 percent hit, laws lens law 15) is the price a cold seat pays before the prefix warms, and the vLLM finding (pipeline parallelism underperforming on warm prefix-heavy turns) says the win only exists once the prefix is actually resident.

RQ4 (planning-gate wiring, laws lens open q7). Estate side verified this run: mag/plan.py exists and implements gate-on-size/ambiguity/expense with auditable plan objects (trigger signals at mag/plan.py:31-43, plan object schema mag_plan.v1 at mag/plan.py:29, priority-store anchoring at mag/plan.py:12-14, small-task skip list at mag/plan.py:42-43). Outside: the pattern is now standard practice, not novel. Claude Code Plan Mode is GA with dedicated writeups (claudedirectory.org, May 2026: fix what the agent can see when it plans), Anthropic's recommended "Follow a plan" pattern is a read-only audit that gates every write (digitalapplied.com writeup, Jun 2026), and Plan/Audit workflow guides are routine. A dissent exists ("Plan mode is a crutch", Graphcoder essay via CrystalSpec, an information-theoretic critique). What remains distinctive in mag/plan.py is the automated should_plan trigger classification and the one-round priority-store-anchored clarification; open q7 can close as "standard practice, our additions are the auto-trigger and the anchored single round".

RQ5 (4-agent swarm ~2x total tokens / 0.5x per agent; does it replicate outside, is it width-dependent). Outside evidence says the shape is denominator-dependent. John Yang (SWE-bench creator, Oct 8) observes that teams showcase gains along different axes (accuracy vs tokens for Opus cards, accuracy vs wall-clock for DeLM, accuracy vs agent count for Agensh) and predicts "in the multi-agent era, wall-clock time will win out as the main efficiency axis", with token stats becoming artifacts. DeLM itself reports 2.49x faster wall-clock with more total cost, i.e. the mirror image of our banked shape. An arXiv "Design Space of Today's and Future AI Agent Systems" paper claims Claude Code agent teams consume roughly 7x the tokens of a plan-mode session (search snippet, not page-verified). Verdict: the estate's 2x/0.5x holds only inside the token-denominator frame; before porting the shape, record wall-clock alongside tokens in the burns ledger or the comparison is against a denominator the field is abandoning.

Security mirror for the width law: a swarm of 700 AI agents executed 17,000+ actions against Hugging Face in the July incident (surfaced Oct 8 by Cogent Security's founder, 1.6M views). Width without containment is now an attack shape; our fan-out width and max-concurrency standing laws are the defensive twin of the same coin.
