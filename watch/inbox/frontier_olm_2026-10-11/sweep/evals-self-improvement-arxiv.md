## Answers to the four research questions (grounded in what I read above; estate-side line cites from the lens files the ask gave me)

**RQ1 (pitfall-rate instrument).** Nothing in the estate measures it (grep verdict above; lens/laws.md:108 declares the instruments implied-open). arXiv says the measurement frame already exists in publishable form: Agent Plasticity (2610.08902) defines exactly the missing quantity - efficiency of converting accumulated experience into HELD-OUT gains, with artifacts passed across instances - and its finding that the best final performer is not the most efficient improver is the cheap-first-ladder caveat in measurement form. Building the estate's variant (per-week recurring-pitfall rate over the gates' catches, held-out corrected) is therefore a legitimate one-mechanism-improving-itself contribution, but the DESCENT leg-1 prior-art rule applies: steal the plasticity frame, don't hand-roll blind.

**RQ2 (corpus law census, one-bug-many-jackets).** One Skill Too Many (2610.11647) is the direct evidence the collision disease is real and frequent: 20,947 repos mined, 822,109 candidate similar-skill pairs, 312 confirmed pairs, 6,368 runs - similar co-installed skills hijack ~1 in 5 runs while task completion looks fine, silently dropping exclusive core functions (their example: "a ban on touching git"). That is the ribs' one-law-filed-twice fear, empirically measured, in a mechanism (name/description-based skill selection over SKILL.md directories) IDENTICAL to the estate's .agents/skills/ tree. The census is cheap-first routable the way the paper did it: deterministic first pass (mining/similarity), LLM judging only a stratified sample, then targeted runs. I did NOT run or price the whole-corpus census - token cost is unmeasured this session; the paper's stratified-sample shape is the cost-control evidence, nothing more.

**RQ3 (gate catches -> pitfall-rate curve).** The week's catches (2db01f1b5: 9 citation debts, 7 drifts, per lens/burns.md:117-118; 039496798: 2 stripe keys, lens/burns.md:120-121) are exactly the raw material Who Verifies the Verifier (2610.11464) builds graders from: "inspectable verifier expressions over deterministic detectors, derived from clustered agent failures" - the estate's citation_lint/violation_ratchet/workflow_gate ARE that shape already. Two design corrections come free: Winner's Curse (2610.09239) proves selection-set numbers overstate held-out gains by 13-20 points at 16 items (1-5 points at 256) - a pitfall-rate curve must rescore on fresh items and carry uncertainty, or it flatters itself exactly like the loops it measures; CAP (2610.09229) shows one aggregate number hides condition-level failures ("This aggregate view hides the deployment conditions under which a judge succeeds or fails") - trend per-gate/per-lane conditions, not one number. memory/improve/ (844 rows, lens/corpus.md:20) can host the ledger without a new runtime instrument - a jsonl of dated catch counts per gate is a writer, not an organ.

**RQ4 (fold-debt as the pitfall).** The five-lens-frozen ledger (lens/burns.md:237-238) is the accumulating failure these papers anatomize: Viva La Vida (2610.04829) measured 26% lemma contamination - content mined into future context stripped of its refutational polarity - and abstention-as-rejection killing 10/12 verification events; Whose Memory Is It (2610.09008) shows the durable-record disease precisely: "The record is neither fabricated nor irrelevant; it has simply been detached from the context in which it was valid." The estate's unbanked olm runs are records detached from context: present on disk, outside the ledger, unable to move a denominator. Until a ledger writer is minted, recurring-pitfall rate is unmeasurable - the numerator's source (gates) fires but the bank that makes it a trend never commits. The writer should carry scope/polarity stamps per CASK and Viva La Vida: what failed, where filed, refuted-or-confirmed, and by which gate.

## The eight banked items (strongest first)

1. **Agent Plasticity: Measuring Self-Improvement Through Experience** - arxiv.org/abs/2610.08902, 2026-10-06. Quote: "Evaluating self-improving agents requires measuring not only what they can do, but how effectively they become better through experience." The published instrument frame for RQ1; steal its training-vs-held-out accounting and its finding that partial OOD transfer and artifact-quality gates are where low-plasticity agents die.
2. **One Skill Too Many: How Co-Installed Skills Conflict in Coding Agents** - arxiv.org/abs/2610.11647, 2026-10-08. Quote: "In a conflict, the installed skill loses core functions (e.g., a ban on touching git) because the similar skill runs instead or changes what it does." Direct evidence + method for the law-collision census; the estate's own skills tree shares the mechanism; their fixes (score exclusive core functions, guard the first skill read, reveal which skill ran) are adoptable as-is.
3. **The Winner's Curse in LLM Self-Improvement Loops: Selection Noise, Lock-in, and Acceptance Rules** - arxiv.org/abs/2610.09239, 2026-10-07. Quote: "Self-improvement studies should report held-out gains with their uncertainty." Measurement-honesty law for any pitfall-rate trend; their rescoring-on-64-fresh-items correction removes the average bias.
4. **Harness Evolution Hits a Ceiling: When Weight Training Should Begin** - arxiv.org/abs/2610.11655, 2026-10-08. Quote: "Harness evolution repairs the former, the behaviour it instils can be trained into the weights, and content failures are what weight training is for." Process-vs-content failure split gives the pitfall-rate curve its first two dimensions and tells the driving ladder when premium inference is actually owed.
5. **Who Verifies the Verifier? Co-Evolving Inspectable Graders with Self-Improving Agents** - arxiv.org/abs/2610.11464, 2026-10-08. Quote: "removing the anchor guards collapses the verifier into a vacuous always-pass grader, yet that collapsed verifier trains skills just as well". Overlap: raw intake in state/arxiv/2026-10-09 (catalog `dc32f43e9a68`), unbanked until now. Validates the estate's detector-expression graders and warns that score alone certifies nothing.
6. **Viva La Vida: Verification and Accumulation Failures in Multi-Agent Proof Search** - arxiv.org/abs/2610.04829, 2026-10-04. Quote: "without external verification, supervision is itself a critical trust boundary". The accumulation-failure anatomy behind RQ4: 26% of mined lemmas carried refuted polarity into future context.
7. **Whose Memory Is It? Scope-Aware Commit Rules for Long-Term LLM Memory** - arxiv.org/abs/2610.09008, 2026-10-06. Quote: "The record is neither fabricated nor irrelevant; it has simply been detached from the context in which it was valid." Spec source for the owed ledger writer: commit-time scope tagging so unbanked/folded records cannot resurface as fact.
8. **Conditional Accuracy Profiles: Diagnosing LLM Judges across Deployment Conditions** - arxiv.org/abs/2610.09229, 2026-10-06. Quote: "This aggregate view hides the deployment conditions under which a judge succeeds or fails." Curve shape for RQ3: per-condition decomposition, since their Hard-Constructed subset put two aggregate-top judges in the bottom three.
