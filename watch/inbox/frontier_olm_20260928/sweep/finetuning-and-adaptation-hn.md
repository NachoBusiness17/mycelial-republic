## Answers to the five questions

**Q1 — QLoRA/LoRA tape-to-model recipes and first-gain data scale.** The one full published recipe read end to end (duane.sh, 2026-09-29): Qwen3.5-4B base, LoRA rank 32, context-tokens-only scoring, SFT on 25,206 teacher-written contexts (1.6 h, ~$7 on one rented H100), then DPO on the student's own rejected outputs (on-policy pairs), then GRPO with a code-based rubric. First-gain finding: "Training on 6,000, 12,000 and 24,000 of Opus's contexts kept lowering the loss, but judged quality barely moved (3.79 → 3.83 → 3.85)" — imitation volume plateaus almost immediately; gains came from the RL stages, not more SFT. Cross-model transfer of DPO pairs failed (3.96 → 3.68 on a 9B): "Pairs have to be the model's own mistakes." Community prior (Ask HN 49807413): "the biggest and most time intensive part is generating the training dataset. building the LoRa is the easy part"; cacheverifier: fine-tuning typically needs "a few hundred to a thousand examples depending on the task".

**Q2 — corpus cleaning gates the estate's corpus-grade judging lacks.** Three gates surfaced that the code-agent-data doctrine does not name: (a) teacher-data restatement scoring — duane.sh discarded its first 23,800-example teacher set because "41% of the lines in Gemini's contexts just restate the excerpt"; (b) output-length/strategy balancing of preference pairs after the model learned "shorter is safer"; (c) drift monitoring of the serving distribution — cacheverifier's fine-tuned verifier turned actively harmful when the gray-zone positive rate "drifted by roughly 5x over the traffic window", caught by chunked two-proportion z-test + Page-Hinkley change-point + Kolmogorov-Smirnov on the score distribution. Shrewd adds a PII-gate failure mode: its PII panel misses card numbers with spaces (0.05 vs 0.7-0.96 unbroken) because synthetic training data never contained them. The estate's judging doctrine grades tapes once at intake; it has no restatement gate, no pair-strategy balance check, and no drift detector on the graded corpus.

**Q3 — fine-tuned small model vs prompted frontier on narrow chores.** Yes, with an honest caveat. CUA-S1-forms: 706,048 trainable parameters, byte-level embedder + 2-layer encoder, trained on "10,000 synthetic episodes" in "less than 30 minutes", scores "99.7% for this model and 83.6% for the hosted Jev API (jev-latest, no fine-tuning)" on form-filling decisions, ~7-9 ms vs 260-280 ms per call. Authors' own caveat: hosted Jev "has not been fine-tuned for it, so this is an experiment in scoped specialization" — the win is scoped specialization, not general capability. duane.sh's 4B beat production Gemini contexts on retrieval top-1 (76% vs 50%) at $76/M excerpts ("71% less than Gemini 2.5 Flash-Lite"). Ask HN counterweight: "almost never worth it now... An up to date LLM with a good few-shot prompt, or an agentic LLM will likely outperform a small finetuned LLM" — wins cluster at implicit-rules classification, style mimicry, privacy, and self-host cost. This is exactly the janitor shape: narrow, high-frequency, local.

**Q4 — distillation from the funded tier inside ~$180-220 credits.** Published cost curves say yes, if labels are the scarce spend: Shrewd reports active row selection "took 1.1x to 2.3x fewer teacher calls for the same student accuracy", cached teacher prefixes cost "~10% of the input price", batching panel questions into one call made judging "about eight times cheaper", and "Sonnet with this kind of selection hit its target for a third of the cost on CFPB and half on AG News". A full RAG-replacement SFT+DPO+GRPO pipeline cost ~$7 of GPU on top of teacher generation (duane.sh). Shopify's loop economics: frontier serving "could easily cost an estimated $27M per year" vs "closer to $1M: a 96% reduction". Within the estate budget: label ~10-25k janitor-chore examples with glm-5.3-prime using caching + selection + panel batching, train locally with the MOLT/LoRA path — the labeled-data stage, not the training stage, is the budget line. Also banked: "Better teacher labels did not always improve the student" (Shrewd) — a stronger funded teacher is not automatically worth 2.2x judging cost.

**Q5 — continual adaptation from the fleet's own filed workdays (DNA cycle).** Shopify Sidekick is the closest published production loop read this pass: "The self-healing pipeline runs daily, continually adding new trajectories to the training corpus" — hard negatives scored by a calibrated judge, frontier-panel critique → arbiter-merged repair instruction → replay; passing replays become SFT+GRPO trajectories, failures fall back to human annotators "against the same rubric used to calibrate the judge". Lessons: the judge/rubric is the reward, get it wrong and "everything downstream optimizes the wrong behavior"; exhaust prompt/harness improvements before weight updates. Mini-AGI (277 pts) is the streaming-learning pole — "There is no separate fine-tuning regime and no frozen base: reading and being trained are the same event", trunk LR at 0.1x for retention — but its thread mostly debunked the framing: independent replication found "the lever is essentially Biderman et als 'learn less forget less'", and the files-vs-weights objection ("Memories in files will always outperform non-frontier model weight adjustments imo") is the estate's own residual-vs-weights debate. The cataloged arxiv 2609.30652 (recursive on-policy distillation, +35.62pp over OPSD on Qwen3-8B) and sibling-lane 2609.08919 (Experience Funnel: "experience selection, not volume, is the gate") are the research pole; Sidekick shows the daily-loop shape at production scale.
