## Answers to the four research questions

### Q1. Do 2 to 4 bit weight quantizations preserve tool-call JSON reliability on 2B to 8B models, and which formats for Ollama-class serving?

Yes with a floor: the evidence says 4 bit is safe-ish, 3 bit is the working edge, 2 bit collapses, and the failure under collapse is NOT usually a JSON parse failure.

- **ACBench (2505.19433, ICML 2025, the direct eval):** across 15 models (Gemma-2B, Qwen2.5 7B/32B, DeepSeek-R1-Distill, GPTQ and AWQ pipelines), 4-bit quantization preserves workflow generation and tool use with a 1 to 3 percent drop, but "degrades real-world application accuracy by 10%-15%". So the tool-call shape survives 4 bit; end-to-end task success does not fully survive. arxiv.org/abs/2505.19433
- **PolAgentBench (2609.32042, GGUF, the Ollama-class formats study):** Bielik-11B, Bielik-Minitron-7B (2B-11B family axis) and Llama-PLLuM-8B measured at GGUF Q8_0, Q6_K, Q5_K_M, Q4_K_M, Q3_K_M, Q2_K. "All three models fall off a cliff between 3-bit and 2-bit (11B 0.716 to 0.045, 7B 0.463 to 0.149, PLLuM 0.224 to 0.015; paired McNemar p < 0.001 in each)". Hard tier: 11B falls from 36/52 to 1/52. Crucially the 11B's 2-bit failures are "end at the first step with a well-formed final_answer, no tool call and no parse error" while asserting values "that the empty tool log never produced": well-formed JSON, confabulated content. The 7B's dominant defect is envelope collapse (tool name written into the action field); PLLuM keeps speaking the protocol (71.8 to 92.0 percent of steps parse) while failing content. Format note: GGUF is called "the de facto format for local inference"; Ollama is never named; serving was llama-cpp-python 0.3.19, temperature 0, 8192 context.
- **IFFC (2608.22472, 5 days outside window, marked):** decoupling function-calling logic into a small instruction-following model "consistently outperforms both native function calling (NFC) and prompt-based function calling (PFC) baselines" and "stays robust under aggressive quantization" for on-device deployment. This supports an estate-shaped pattern: keep tool-call discipline in a small dedicated quantized model rather than hoping a heavily quantized generalist holds protocol.
- **Format verdict for Ollama-class serving (from what I read, not from any paper saying "Ollama"):** Q4_K_M is the evidenced safe floor (ACBench 1-3 percent tool-use drop; PolAgentBench 11B 0.716 at Q3_K_M), Q3_K_M is the working edge, Q2_K is a cliff, and Q2 failures are confabulation not parse errors, which is worse for a tool harness because they look like success. Caveat carried by the authors themselves: provenance of the GGUF files differed per model, PLLuM failed a pre-specified sanity gate, and small-sample ladder effects did not survive multiplicity correction.
- **Security rider (2609.14060, EMNLP 2026):** "Across three trigger-action pairs and three codebooks (NF4, FP4, INT8), AGENTQ reaches up to 100% post-quantization attack success rate with minimal loss of benign utility": a full-precision checkpoint can pass audits yet misbehave only once quantized. Defensive takeaway for any estate that deploys community GGUFs: quantization-aware safety evaluation is the paper's stated implication.

### Q2. Does the literature distinguish quantizing weights from compressing instructions, and does it touch the estate's inflated-echo finding?

The two literatures are almost fully disjoint and neither tests the estate's finding.

- Disjointness is structural, not incidental: my "quantization"+"function calling"/"JSON"/"agentic" queries return weight-compression work; my "prompt compression"/"context compression"+"agent" queries return token-and-context work; the literal phrase "instruction compression" returns only two papers ever (TAG-INSTRUCT 2505.18557, which compresses instructions into tags to AUGMENT a dataset, the opposite direction; and Gist tokens 2402.16058, soft-prompt gist learning). No 2026 paper I found studies "quantizing instructions" as a distinct object.
- The estate's specific finding (every one of the 24 rows in memory/corpus/quantized_thought/instructions_frontier.jsonl carries count_verdict "inflated echo - provenance only", so raw_count is provenance not weight, per watch/inbox/frontier_olm_20260928/lens/laws.md:5 and :60) is UNTESTED in everything I read: no paper weighs compiled instructions by usage frequency. Nearest adjacent support (weak, and I did not deep-read these two, feed gists only): PC-SubMax argues fixed token/sentence importance scores "may overlook how content contributions change with the selected subset", i.e., static importance from history is unreliable as a weight; and the hard-prompt-compression failure audit line (2608.04569, out of window) shows frequency-style extraction drops referential support sentences. Verdict: SUPPORTED in spirit (frequency-as-importance is under attack in the compression literature), UNPROVEN as a direct claim; nothing refutes it.

### Q3. Lossy context compression with measured retention on agentic tasks, and can the estate validate offline against the 581,477-row catalog?

- **AgentKV (2609.14872):** agentic future queries "form a mixture over think, act, tool, and others phases", so recency-based eviction "systematically undervalue[s] keys that upcoming phases will need"; per-phase query buffers give +5.5 task score over R-KV and 1.80x output-token throughput vs full-KV SGLang, across 2 models, 6 domains, 3 budgets. arxiv.org/abs/2609.14872
- **LOHA/ACD (2609.31430, already in catalog as abstract 80e9781ca2dd; my bank adds the numbers):** context per call drops 43 percent (Qwen3-4B) and 57 percent (SWE-Master-4B-RL) on SWE-bench Verified with resolve-rate deltas of -2.4 and -5.7 points; but under a hard 32K budget the compressed agent scores 21.1 percent vs 11.1 percent for the same adapted agent on full text. Retention numbers exist AND the budgeted regime flips the sign: compression wins when the tax is binding, which is exactly the estate's deliberate-ask situation.
- **ActKV (2609.31395, already in catalog as b38a1bead412):** "retains an average of 98.53% of FullKV's accuracy with only 25.98% of its peak KV cache memory", 3.97x token throughput; claims to be the first KV compression framework tailored to agentic inference, prioritizing action-critical entries.
- **Harness caveat (2609.32459):** component-level attribution matters: "Structured tool use and task-specific subagents give the most stable gains, while context compression and general subagents can hurt repository-generation performance." Retention percentage alone is not a verdict; the harness position of the compressor is a first-class factor.
- **Offline validation against the catalog:** feasible but nothing I read does it for us. The catalog is a file inventory (id/path/bytes/mtime/title schema per watch/inbox/frontier_olm_20260928/lens/corpus.md:42), not task traces; every paper above validates on task benchmarks (SWE-bench Verified, PolAgentBench's 67-task suite, AppWorld). A concrete estate-shaped protocol the literature implies: freeze pack text as the prompt under test, derive extractive ground-truth needles from catalog rows (titles + byte-verified paths), score retention with an ordered-subsequence/substring evaluator like PolAgentBench's deterministic evaluate(), and report ACBench-style capability-split deltas (protocol shape vs content) rather than one blended score. Not run this ask; stated as the design the evidence supports.

### Q4. SOTA prompt compression to shrink the deliberate-ask context tax while preserving frozen RIB + output contract shape?

- The deliberate-ask shape (frozen cache-shared prefix + tight ask + output contract, instructions_frontier.jsonl:1 per laws.md:18) maps onto a 2026 consensus forming in this sweep: compress the VARIABLE delta, never the frozen contract-bearing prefix.
- **PC-SubMax (2609.32474, newest method hit):** compression as regularized monotone submodular maximization under a knapsack constraint; Regularized Greedy+Max carries a deterministic 1/2 guarantee on the objective, and the method "uses encoder representations and avoids autoregressive LLM scoring during compression", i.e., cheap enough to sit in front of a local Ollama janitor. Seven benchmarks, competitive downstream scores. This is the current SOTA shape for choosing WHAT to keep; it is query-agnostic-compatible, so the frozen prefix can stay byte-stable for cache hits.
- **LOHA layout law:** keep the agent's own turns and the last K observations in hard text, compress only old observations to soft tokens, and anchor training to the plain-text view to limit drift. Direct translation for Mag: output contract and tight ask stay literal; only aged history gets lossy.
- **Risk-controlled eviction (2609.27981, catalog-held, not deep-read):** recasts eviction as risk control with finite-sample certification and full-KV fallback, which rhymes with the estate's fail-closed law.
- Out-of-window follow-ups I did NOT deep-read (feed gists only, unbanked): Cache-Aware Prompt Compression 2607.15516 (pairs query-agnostic compression with prompt caching, cheapest across configurations), Compress the Context Keep the Commitments 2605.17304 (commitment-level verifiable compression, the closest published shape to our output-contract law), Lossless dictionary-encoding prompts 2604.13066 (repetitive-structure lossless channel, matches our highly repetitive RIB grammar).
