## RQ1: do 2 to 4 bit weight quants preserve tool-call JSON reliability for 2B-8B models, and which formats for Ollama-class serving?

Findings, strongest first, all read this session:

1. Quesma, "Benchmarking Qwen3.8 27B quantizations: 4-bit holds up, 1-bit collapses" (2026-09-08, https://quesma.com/blog/qwen38-27b-quantizations-benchmarked/). Read end to end. BF16, Q8_0, Q4_K_M, UD-Q2_K_XL, UD-IQ1_S/M, all Unsloth GGUF builds via llama.cpp (2026-08-16 build) with F16 KV cache throughout. Q4_K_M matched BF16 on GPQA Diamond, IFBench, and Terminal-Bench 2.1 (89 agentic tasks, 98k context). 2-bit: mild degradation plus ~1.25x output tokens on identically solved tasks. 1-bit: collapses to near random on GPQA. Damage described as "nonlinear: first there is no measurable change, then a small decline, and finally a collapse." Author takeaway quoted in the fetch: "quantization should be embraced, rather than feared." CRITICAL GAP: no function-calling or JSON-validity benchmark was run; Terminal-Bench 2.1 is the closest agentic proxy.
2. RecursionAI, "Courier vs Ollama: Tool-Calling, Measured" (2026-07-23, https://thinkrecursion.ai/writing/courier-vs-ollama-tool-calling). Read end to end. ## RQ2: does the literature distinguish quantizing weights from compressing instructions, and does it support the estate's echo verdict?

- YES, the split is explicit. The tool-using-agent efficiency survey (202608.0365, read end to end) states its scope "focuses on agent-level overhead induced by tool interaction and excludes generic LLM inference, compression, and serving optimizations". Model/weight compression and agent-context compression are literally different sections of the field.
- arxiv 2607.25335 (Jul 2026, abstract read) compresses INSTRUCTIONS (system-prompt-shaped text) by deterministic linguistic rules, no LM forward pass at compression time, and reports it "work(s) well at light-to-moderate compression ratios and degrade(s) sharply at more aggressive settings". That is a third axis: rule-shaped instruction compression, separate from weight quant and from token-salience context compression. The estate's compile_packs (frontier->flash->local law in compile_report.json) are exactly rule-shaped instruction compression; this paper is the closest published mirror of that mechanism found this window.
- On the echo verdict: the survey's Rethink on context compression warns training-free methods "may miss latent task-context associations and prematurely discard useful components". The estate's count_verdict "inflated echo - provenance only" on the 24 frontier rows is a PROVENANCE judgment (echo of upstream text, weight 0.8, shape imperative), not a usefulness judgment; the literature's caveat says the discard is safe for serving cost but the provenance links should survive the compile, which the pack already does by keeping sources[]. No published counter-evidence to the estate's verdict was found; no confirmation either (the estate's claim is about ITS OWN corpus, not a published benchmark).

## RQ3: lossy context compression with measured retention on agentic tasks, and offline validation against the 581k-row catalog

- AGORA (arxiv 2605.26596, May 2026, html read end to end): step-level, inference-free. Structural parser + always-keep floor (system, task, current observation, pending block, last 2 steps verbatim) + a 125M RoBERTa scorer filling a char budget of 0.25x current-trajectory length, with dropped runs replaced by "[...N step(s) elided...]" markers. Numbers: ~92 percent mean-reward retention at nominal 4x compression across ALFWorld/ScienceWorld/WebShop x 3 backbones; Floor-K2 alone gets 85 percent; LLMLingua-2 and Selective Context COLLAPSE to mean reward <= 0.05 in 17 cells despite 1.3-13.3x real compression, because token-level extractors strip "action grammar" (brackets, identifiers, verbs like search[...] and click[]) and the environment rejects the residue. The always-keep floor is explicitly "format-critical and recency-critical content"; removing the last-two-steps invariant is the costliest ablation (-0.088 mr). Limitations: n=30 per cell, labels partly backbone-specific, LLM-based competitors (HiAgent, AgentDiet, ACON) beat it on $/task in 7-8 of 9 cells.
- Elastic Horizon (Sep 2026, EMNLP 2026 per search): closed-loop controller on when to stop extending the interaction horizon, up to 25 percent per-step trajectory token reduction on AppWorld at best success rates for 7B/14B backbones. Search-snippet level only; exact arxiv ID unverified (arxiv API query for "Elastic Horizon" returned an unrelated FLUID paper). OWED: the ID.
- Estate-held adjacent abstracts already in catalog (state/arxiv/2026-09/): FlexComp "One Model for Every Ratio" (2609.11192, Sep 11), "Compression Beyond the Uncompressed" (2609.05152, Sep 7), "Correct Now, Insufficient Later" (2609.20045, Sep 18), "When Can Agents Forget Their Reasoning" (2609.29875, Sep 25). Do not re-bank; read locally if the compression hub needs them.
- Offline validation blueprint for the estate: AGORA's counterfactual supervision is replayable against the catalog. Row count verified this session below (wc -l). The method: for each catalog row that records a tool-call outcome, replay the same call with the context compressed by a candidate method (AGORA floor+scorer, LLMLingua-2, truncation) and measure outcome drift; rows where the outcome flips are the counterfactual labels. AGORA itself trained on 21,523 pairs from 1,244 trajectories; the catalog (581477 rows, verified this session) is orders of magnitude larger, so a stratified sample (AGORA's 9-cell, n=30 shape) is sufficient for a first gate. Caveat from the paper to copy: keep the always-keep floor covering the estate's frozen RIB shape (system + output contract), because that is exactly its format-critical class.

## RQ4: SOTA prompt compression to shrink the deliberate-ask context tax without losing the frozen RIB + output contract

- The deliberate-ask shape (frozen prefix cache-shared + tight ask + output contract, AGENTS.md Clippy bullet) is ALREADY the SOTA shape per this window's evidence: AGORA's always-keep floor says keep system/format-critical blocks verbatim and compress only the middle history; the survey's history-compression section (Trajectory Curation: Smurfs, AGORA, MemTool, TokenPilot, Self-GC, MemAct, ContextBudget, AdaCoM; State Extraction: AgentFold, Slipstream, ContextWeaver, ACON, Memex, PEEK) is all about the middle, not the prefix.
- Rule-based compressors (2607.25335) suggest the flash/local pack rows can be compressed further by deterministic rules with no LM pass, but only at light-to-moderate ratios before sharp degradation: do not chase aggressive ratios on law text.
- LLMLingua-2-style token salience is CONTRAINDICATED for anything containing tool-call grammar: AGORA's 17-cell collapse audit is the strongest negative result this window. The estate's context_pack should never token-prune its own tool schemas or output contracts.
