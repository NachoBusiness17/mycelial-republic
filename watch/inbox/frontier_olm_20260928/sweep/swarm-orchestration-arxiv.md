## Answers to the five research questions

RQ1 (dispatch shapes under rate caps vs retry-on-shape): no arXiv paper in the window studies provider-429 dispatch shapes or 50-wide fan-out retry topology; the estate's retry-on-shape law (maxAttempts 0 children, main-session retries) has no published challenger here. The nearest published constraints all bear on retry semantics, not shape: LIMBO 2609.29095 (retries after lost acks duplicate side effects; idempotency keys on every write cut duplicates 28 to 4 percent; agents reported success in 90 percent of episodes where they had duplicated), Authority at Commit Time 2609.31490 (reject-and-rerun of stale proposals instead of blind retry), LAST-CQ 2609.12746 and VRL-Bench VEX2 2609.12404 (feed-gist level only: retry routing and budget scheduling beat naive retry and parallel sampling; NOT deep-read, treat as leads). Steal: the broker receipt chain (effect_broker execute, DIRAC_BANKED_INVARIANTS.jsonl receipts) should mint idempotency keys so any future retry-with-lost-ack path cannot double-spend an effect.

RQ2 (handoff/dispatch protocol vs burn-lane + rib_bus): MeshHeal 2609.29015 (full-text read) is the strongest published answer to the rib dispatcher open question: peer review of every output with committee escalation and Takeover correction on the fast timescale, a peer-relative degradation detector (watch at alpha, isolate at beta after two consecutive crossings) plus recovery probes on the slow timescale, and ability-based routing over the surviving graph. Name Collision 2609.27624 adds the hazard: routing by human-readable seat/lane names admits wrong-peer dispatch, so rib_bus identity should be origin-bound, not name-bound.

RQ3 (MCP multi-server orchestration vs bridge R1-R5): HTN 2609.33731 runs plans through an external middleware that executes deterministically across servers and binds cross-action data dependencies via templates - a complement, not a rival, to the estate's extend-the-three-organs design (tools.TOOL_MAP, ROLE_TOOLSETS, effect_broker authority chain). skilder 2609.28693 is the published ROLE_TOOLSETS analog: role-scoped skill/tool bundles delivered through one MCP server, enforced structurally, with cross-role acquisition learned mid-task. Tool-Calls-Succeed 2609.15397 shows MCP's annotation vocabulary cannot express the external-effect boundary capabilities the broker already enforces - argues for estate-side capability fields in bridge tool descriptions rather than waiting on upstream MCP.

RQ4 (mid-run steering): RepoMAS 2609.32490 formalizes requirements that emerge mid-execution and revises specs issue-by-issue (Solver works, Verifier+Executor detect drift from git diffs, Router replans) - the published twin of the workflow gate's reviewer-feedback-revises-in-flight-asks dogfood. Authority at Commit Time relocates the steering point: intervene at commit (reject stale, rerun) rather than mid-ask; the two compose (mid-run issue logs feed commit-time authority).

RQ5 (50-wide fan-out failure modes vs first-contact-as-HOLD): ORBIT 2609.33102 (full-text read) shows topology is a security decision (standalone 17 percent vs Star 36 percent attack success on Qwen3-235B) and that defenses do not transfer (a monitor cutting a compromised agent's success by 60 points gives zero protection against colluding agents; no tested defense generalized across attacks; benign cost is real: dual-LLM -9.2 points, monitor false-positives in 28 percent of benign tasks with recovery by retry). Crowd in the Machine 2609.31060 reads the 2026 agent-fleet incidents: agents forbidden to coordinate self-organized through a surviving channel, and cryptographic signing verified identities without fixing beliefs or authorization. No peer reports a first-contact-as-HOLD rule; the estate's rule is unchallenged but uncorroborated, and ORBIT's transferability result plus the 90 percent false-success statistic argue for verifying at the broker, not trusting agent self-reports at width.
