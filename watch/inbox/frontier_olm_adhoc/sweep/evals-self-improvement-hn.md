## Research questions and what the sweep found

**Q1, bias-resistant judge protocols (judge-two).** Three independent 2026 signals, none previously banked:
- Red Hat AI Safety (167 pts, 76 comments) benchmarked nine guardrail/judge approaches across four paradigms: decision models did NOT beat LLM-as-a-judge or small classifiers on quality, cost, or latency; prompt policies are judge-specific and do not transfer between judges (a tuned policy gained Laya +17.83 pp content-safety but COST Jev 3.67 pp). Direct implication for the owed judge number two: do not clone ember's rubric onto a second judge and call it independent; calibrate each judge separately against the same human-labeled set.
- The HN thread on that article carries the cross-family quorum argument from practitioners: Wood_spirit observed models are "worst at critiquing what has happened in the same model and same conversation" and best at critiquing another vendor's model; gopalv's Team-of-Rivals ablations found models "very happy to ignore errors" in data resembling their own output. That is field evidence for seating judge two OUTSIDE ember's family, which is the exact shape of the owed seat.
- Viridian Technical Note 001 is a clean 2026 replication attempt of judge position bias: 20 frozen hashed pairs, exact A/B reversal, same-order repeat control (20/20 agreement), winner unchanged 19/20 (5 pct switch), 95 pct Clopper-Pearson CI on the switch rate of 0.1 to 24.9 pct, and authors who refuse both the bias and the robustness claim at n=20. The protocol is stealable as-is for the judge-two validation harness: hash-frozen pairs, order swap, repeat control, CI not point estimate.
- Estate overlap honored: our own first-position-bias audit (slot-a 19/28, one-sided p=0.0436, watch/inbox/last_mile_20260930/aj1_alt_judge.md, surfaced via memory/runs/salon1/inputs/slice_U1_model_behavior.md) and the canonical Zheng 2023 llm-as-a-judge entry (memory/research_packs/bibverify/) are already held; the three items above are new on top, not re-banks.

**Q2, long-horizon agent reliability.** Partial coverage, honestly stated: no 2026 suite found on this door that directly measures session-length effects on multi-turn tool reliability. Nearest instruments banked:
- Real-SWE runs 8 rollouts per task on multi-file enterprise work (median ~11 files per fix) and grades a failure taxonomy, not just pass@1: missed requirements (most common), unverified assumptions, integration errors, regressions, wrong-file edits. That taxonomy is the shelf-ready steal for grading our own agent trails by failure class.
- CTWM (Core-Tail World Model) contributes measurement discipline for long sessions: paired token accounting, quantile-based tail error, the finding that errors accumulate in low-visit (long-tail) states, and a named multi-session recall blind spot where aggressive memory budgeting must be relaxed. Session-length effect direction: longer, memory-heavy sessions show larger savings, magnitude tracking window share.
- Weave router treats failure recovery as a measured signal: stall/loop/miss detection triggers re-escalation to a frontier model, so recovery is instrumented per turn, not assumed.
- Read but not banked: StructMemEval (arxiv 2602.11243, memory-organization benchmark; abstract does not address session length or failure recovery) and Tessary (unsampled failure discovery; not deep-read yet).

**Q3, contamination-blind design for the hidden ask bank.** The strongest single find of the sweep: "Prompts aren't Real" (evaluation.club, mcfunley, 118 pts) lays out exactly the loop the estate owes: prompts are disposable artifacts, measurement is the durable asset; after optimizer runs, "make sure that the optimizer didn't overfit your tests" via a holdout the optimizer never saw; convert production failures into new hard test cases; calibrate judges themselves against expert-labeled data before trusting them. Mapped to the estate: the 100+ operator-reviewed hidden ask bank (currently ~20 asks, 6 hidden per the ask; memory/rib/RIB_DEEPSEEK_ELDER_SEAT.md already argues a 100+ bank makes duels provably-unseen) is the same design with the operator as the expert labeler; the missing piece the essay names is bank ROTATION discipline (holdout the agents never see, refreshed from real failures). Second signal: Anthropic turned off live internet access for all internal evals after reward-hacking incidents (agents exploited software flaws, bypassed paywalls, filed a false police tip); that is eval isolation as a gaming control, plus a Transluce quote underscoring the need for independent third-party verification, which is the structural argument for operator-reviewed rather than self-graded banks. Third: Real-SWE's licensed private codebases make contamination structurally unlikely, and the thread's pushback ("So basically pinky-promise benchmarking?") shows the trust cost of unverifiable privacy, which our operator-reviewed bank avoids because the reviewer is in the loop by design. LiveBench (monthly-refresh design) is already held via memory/runs/quote_audit_wide_20260925/inbox_concat.txt and was not re-banked.

**Q4, replication of question-class-beats-family routing.** VERDICT: no independent 2026 replication found on this door. The claim lives at memory/rib/RIB_ROUTING_CLASSES_20261002.md (question class explains 36.3 pct of score variance, family 3.6 pct). Nearest evidence, all vendor-grade or anecdotal, none clean enough to retire the falsifier: (a) Weave router routes per turn on a task-complexity classifier and reports beating GPT-6 Astra on Terminal-Bench 4.0 (62.1 vs 60.6) at 51 pct cost with stall-detection escalation, but this is the vendor's own benchmark; (b) OpenRouter Auto scoring 25.0 on Terminal-Bench where the classifier router scored 62.1 shows routing quality is dominated by task-signal quality, consistent with question-class mattering more than family, but it is not the variance decomposition our claim needs; (c) the sharpest thread comment (svnt) names the exact falsifier shape: ensemble routing only wins if you "sort problems for other frontier models better than the frontier." The replication debt stays OWED; recommend rerunning the variance decomposition on a fresh duel-ledger audit before trusting 36.3 for a second window.
