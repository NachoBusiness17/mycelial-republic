**1. Self-Harness: Harnesses That Improve Themselves** - arXiv 2606.09498, Hangfan Zhang et al., v3 2026-08-20. https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09498. THE skill_ledger answer (Q2) and the REGISTRY-ratchet answer (Q5) in one design: Weakness Mining extracts model-specific failure patterns from execution traces, Harness Proposal ties minimal edits to those failures, and Proposal Validation "accepts candidate edits only after regression testing". Across nine model-benchmark combinations (including GLM-5) it improves held-in AND held-out pass rates, up to +132 pct relative. The mapping to our questions is direct: skill_ledger should record, per module, the failure patterns mined from traces and the regression test that gated each accepted edit, so case law covers the modules that actually fail instead of the two telemetry surfaces it covers today. A run that lands artifacts but no REGISTRY row is, in this paper's terms, an edit accepted without Proposal Validation: unproven, unraatcheted, an incident.

**2. Rethinking the Evaluation of Harness Evolution for Agents** - arXiv 2607.12227, Wang et al., v2 2026-08-27. https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12227. The falsifier-discipline steal for Q4 and Q5: after normalizing for the cost of searching for improved harnesses, "automatic harness evolution does not consistently outperform simple test-time scaling baselines once the cost of searching for improved harnesses is accounted for". They demand matched feedback and inference budgets, and held-out task evaluation because search and final eval sharing one benchmark risks overfitting. This is exactly the protocol our second blind audit of question-class dominance (laws.md:98 falsifier) should adopt: budget-matched comparison against a test-time-scaling baseline, scored on held-out asks, before the 36.3-vs-3.6 split is allowed to stand.

**3. Recursive Harness Self-Improvement (RHI)** - arXiv 2607.15524, Xu, Seely, Lee, Zaharia, Tang (Sakana AI + UC Berkeley), 2026-07. https://arxiv.org/html/2607.15524v1 (full HTML read). Defines the harness as the agent loop itself: roles, instructions, contracts (inter-agent information), hops (workflow structure), and optimizes it iteratively using pairwise preference over its own revision history - "each harness is optimized using the preference history induced by its own previous revisions". Result: "a few RHI iterations suffice to substantially raise the performance ceiling of low-reasoning-effort agents, exceeding the corresponding maximum-reasoning-effort setting while reducing inference cost by up to 60%". For Q3: this is the operating shape of a cross-referenced law index - the revision-preference ledger over roles/contracts/hops is how collisions (one-bug-many-jackets) and orphans (dead citations) fall out of use data rather than hand-maintained lenses. For Q5: the ledger IS the update signal; a missing REGISTRY row starves the next iteration of its comparison pair.

## Q1: judge-two (ember is sole judge for its family, known bias dossier)

**1. Nine Judges, Two Effective Votes: Correlated Errors Undermine LLM Evaluation Panels** - arXiv 2605.29800, Guneet Kohli, 2026-05-28. https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29800. 9 judges from 7 families (GPT-4o, GPT-4o-mini, Claude Sonnet 4.5, Gemini 2.5 Pro, Llama 4 x2, Qwen3-32B, Mistral Large 3, DeepSeek-V3) on ChaosNLI x3 + RewardBench. Condorcet gap 6.8-22.0pp; best single judge matches or beats the panel everywhere (SNLI: 84.2% single vs 77.7% panel). THE LOAD-BEARING STEAL FOR JUDGE-TWO: cross-family selection does not help much - same-family pairs were only slightly more correlated than cross-family ones (+0.047 phi on MNLI), and the highest-correlated pairs (Claude x Gemini .603, GPT-4o x Claude .588, Mistral x DeepSeek .564) were all cross-family. A 5-judge panel already captures ~90% of achievable independence; the ceiling asymptotes at ~2.6 effective votes. Caution rule to steal verbatim: treat panel results with caution if n_eff/k < 0.5. Implication for the estate: seating judge-two from a different family is necessary but NOT sufficient; without measuring pairwise phi on our own verdict distribution we cannot know our two-judge quorum's true independence. Verbatim: "The bottleneck is correlated judges, not the aggregation algorithm, implying that scaling up panels cannot substitute for genuinely independent evaluation." (keynews.ai called the author "Apple researchers"; I could not verify affiliation from arXiv, so I bank only the name on the paper.)

**2. Reliability without Validity: A Systematic, Large-Scale Evaluation of LLM-as-a-Judge Models** - arXiv 2606.19544, Norman/Rivera/Hughes, 2026-06-17. https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19544. 21 judges from 9 providers, 118 runs, ~541,000 judgments, run March-April 2026. Two findings that rewire our judge validation: (a) kappa deflation - exact-match agreement overstates Cohen's kappa by 33.8-41.3pp on MT-Bench for ALL 21 judges (Gemini 3.1 Pro: EM 0.849 vs kappa 0.511); (b) the consistency-bias paradox - Qwen3-8B posts test-retest 0.992 WITH position bias 0.192; stability is not correctness. Their Minimum Viable Validation Protocol is a ready-made spec for our model-gates rib: (1) chance-correct with kappa/Krippendorff alpha, headline that number; (2) swap positions AB+BA, report |P(A wins) - 0.5|; (3) test-retest over >=3 runs at temp 0 with caching off; (4) cross-validate on >=2 benchmarks spanning preference-style and correctness-style labels; (5) if test-retest >0.95, verify position bias <0.10 before claiming reliability. Step 5 is the ember-bias-dossier antidote in one line. Verbatim: "test-retest measures the stability of a judge's outputs, not the correctness of the underlying decision process." (No quorum guidance inside; pair it with item 1.)

## Q2: long-horizon agent reliability (multi-turn, failure recovery, session-length effects)

**3. Classifier Context Rot: Monitor Performance Degrades with Context Length** - arXiv 2605.12366 (date ID-derived, 2026-05). https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.12366. The session-length-effects paper for anyone running classifier checks over agent transcripts (we are: graders/checkers at the workflow gate, drift patrols). LLM monitors classifying full transcripts: Opus 4.6 recall on obvious injected attacks fell 99.7% (100K tokens) to 69% (800K); subtle attacks missed "2x to 30x more often" after 800K tokens of benign prefix across Opus 4.6, GPT 5.4, Gemini 3.1 Pro; lost-in-the-middle holds (attack with 500K before/100K after: non-thinking Opus detection 5%). The gaming implication is the estate's 30-day-cell-rot argument in safety form: a misaligned agent could game the system by simply delaying its attack. Cheap mitigations verified: in-transcript reminder notes (<3% token overhead), "list suspicious actions verbatim" prompts, incremental subsampling monitors. Verbatim: "Monitor evaluations that do not take into consideration context length are potentially overestimating the safety provided by the monitoring system." Our equivalent debt: any gate checker validated on short transcripts overestimates what it catches on multi-hour burn windows.

**4. The Long-Horizon Task Mirage? Diagnosing Where and Why Agentic Systems Break** - arXiv 2604.11978, Wang et al. (Mutlu/Song/Nowak groups), 2026-04-13. https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.11978. HORIZON benchmark: 700+ tasks, 3100+ trajectories, GPT-5 variants + Claude-4, four domains (Web/OS/Embodied/Database), agent-independent Intrinsic Horizon H* and Compositional Depth s with nested task sets so horizon length is the only varying factor. Failure taxonomy (7 categories, FMEA-grounded): Environment Error, Instruction Error, False Assumption, Planning Error (all horizon-general) + Catastrophic Forgetting, History Error Accumulation, Memory Limitation (long-horizon-specific); process-level risks 72.5% vs design-level 27.5%. Their LLM-as-judge failure-attribution pipeline validated at inter-annotator kappa 0.61, human-judge kappa 0.84 - a citable precedent for our judge-verified failure attribution. Verbatim: "long-horizon failure is not merely a drop in success rate, but a structural shift in failure composition" and "scaling base models alone is insufficient; robust long-horizon performance requires method-level improvements in planning, memory, and execution-time control." Extends the openai/evals-promptfoo-HELM shelf from single-turn accuracy toward failure-composition measurement.

## Q3: contamination-blind design for the 100+ hidden ask bank (~20 asks, 6 hidden)

**5. BeyondBench: Contamination-Resistant Evaluation of Reasoning in Language Models** - arXiv 2509.24210 (v1 2025-09-29, v2 2026-03-04, accepted ICLR 2026). https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.24210. The design pattern for an unbounded hidden bank: algorithmic on-the-fly problem generation, 44 tasks / 117 variations, instance space >10^15, deterministically verified solutions, isomorphic transforms producing syntactically novel problems. 101 models evaluated (0.5B-141B). ## Q4: does task/question class dominate model family in routing for mixed agent workloads?

**7. Agent-as-a-Router: Agentic Model Routing for Coding Tasks** - arXiv 2606.22902, 2026-06 (ID-derived). https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.22902. The closest independent 2026 quantitative evidence: routers are bottlenecked by "information deficit" (not knowing how models actually perform per task dimension), and adding task-dimension-level performance statistics to a vanilla LLM router yielded a 15.3% relative gain. Ships CodeRouterBench (~10K task instances, verified scores from 8 frontier LLMs) and ACRouter (Context->Action->Feedback->Context loop with Orchestrator/Verifier/Memory), lowest cumulative regret in-distribution, generalizes OOD. Verbatim: "we identify the performance bottleneck for these routers as information deficit". This is ADJACENT replication, not direct: it shows per-task-dimension knowledge beats semantic classification, which supports the estate's routing claim directionally, but NO 2026 study found in this sweep directly replicates a "task class dominates model family at ~36.3%" effect size. The named replication falsifier therefore remains UNBANKED and OWED.

**8. The Workload-Router-Pool Architecture for LLM Inference Optimization** - arXiv 2603.21354 (v1 2026-03-22, v2 2026-04-08), Chen et al., vLLM Semantic Router project. https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.21354. Vision paper consolidating a year of vLLM semantic-router releases: Workload x Router x Pool, thesis that the three dimensions of LLM inference optimization (Workload, Router, Pool) are coupled, not orthogonal.
