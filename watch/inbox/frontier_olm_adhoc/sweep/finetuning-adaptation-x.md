## Banked items (strongest first; quotes verbatim from pages I fetched)

1. **Self-Improving LLM Agents at Test-Time (TT-SI)** - https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.07841 - v1 2025-10-09 (UIUC + Microsoft; ACL 2026 version exists). Closest published analog to the ten-minute-self rib: the agent detects its own uncertain cases at test time, augments its own training data from them, and fine-tunes on ONE training instance per uncertain case during inference; the abstract reports "+5.48% absolute accuracy gain on average across all benchmarks". This is a weights-from-own-pitfalls loop beating standard learning baselines. The abstract does NOT report wall-clock per adaptation nor week-over-week pitfall-rate decline (OWED: paper body).
2. **TRL Sept-Oct 2026 release run (v1.13.0 through v1.15.0)** - https://github.com/huggingface/trl/releases - v1.15.0 (2026-10-08): fused LM head gives "up to 6.9x longer sequences on the same GPU; peak memory drops 52-82% for SFT, DPO, KTO, GRPO, RLOO, and distillation"; AsyncGRPO trains OpenEnv agent harnesses from validated token captures; v1.14.0 added PEFT/LoRA with adapter-only vLLM weight sync; v1.14.2 (2026-10-06) fixed silently-wrong GRPO/RLOO training past end-of-turn ids; DistillationTrainer stable since v1.10.0 (2026-08-13). The v1.0 blog positions TRL as the low-infra single-GPU stack with QLoRA. This is where the clone budget should land FIRST for a 1-8B local lane.
3. **A Systematic Evaluation of Trajectory Data Curation for LoRA Fine-Tuning of Code Agents** - https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17205 - v1 2026-07-21, revised 2026-08-28 (Yunze Han et al.). 16 experiments on roughly 67k SWE-trajectories LoRA-tuning Qwen2.5-Coder-7B-Instruct under a two-axis trajectory-quality framework (Efficiency, Style) with cross-entropy loss as primary metric. Directly supports the JIT-law premise that a CONTROLLED corpus (what we would call pitfall-bypass trajectories) is the deciding variable in whether the fold helps.
4. **verl 2026 releases (v0.7.0 through v0.9.1)** - https://github.com/volcengine/verl/releases - v0.9.1 (Sep 20): unified V1 trainer across sync, colocate_async and separate_async, delta_sharded checkpoints up to 10.3x faster weight sync; v0.9.0 (Aug 14): uni-agent long-horizon agent RL plus Continuous Token multi-turn rollout; v0.8.0 (Jun 1): SGLang rollout LoRA support (merge and native adapter) and per-unit LoRA summon with FSDP1/2. Year not printed on the page (day and month only); the sequence and v0.9.1-as-latest are as shown. Infra burden "Very high (Ray + rollout engine)" per the TRL v1.0 blog's own comparison table. Clone SECOND; its LoRA summon and rollout path is the reference design if we ever hot-swap adapters.
5. **In-Place Test-Time Training** - https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.06169 - 2026-04-07, ICLR 2026 Oral (ByteDance Seed and co-authors). Verbatim from the abstract: it "treats the final projection matrix of the ubiquitous MLP blocks as its adaptable fast weights, enabling a "drop-in" enhancement for LLMs without costly retraining from scratch"; a 4B model on contexts up to 128k; chunk-wise updates compatible with context parallelism; code at github.com/ByteDance-Seed/In-Place-TTT. No wall-clock per-task adaptation time in the abstract (OWED).
6. **ZhihuFrontier recursive-self-improvement map** - https://x.com/ZhihuFrontier/status/2095092886069211247 - 2026-09-02. Maps RSI by what is improved: test-time RSI climbs "from the current answer, into the weights, into the agent itself"; the verdict is that most systems are "nowhere near a closed loop" and the bottleneck is verification, not generation. Exactly the JIT-law frame: prompts, tools and memory evolution versus writing experience into weights.
7. **Kimi K3 LoRA training cost point** - https://x.com/jiahanjimliu/status/2082713415869665743 - 2026-07-30 (links https://fireworks.ai/blog/K3-LoRA-Training). LoRA as a "GPU-efficient method to fine-tune frontier size models by adding an adapter layer of weights"; a sample run of 20 steps and 860k tokens costs $65. The frontier-lane price anchor for Q4: one small adaptation job on a hosted frontier model costs tens of dollars, which is what pushes a repeatable lesson-distill lane local.
8. **GeneralistAI few-shot test-time adaptation** - https://x.com/GeneralistAI/status/2090161954933674398 - 2026-08-19. Robotics rather than LLM, but it is the public latency bound the JIT law wants: adaptation to new physical tasks in "as few as 1-10 gradient steps" from "1-5 minutes of data (roughly 10-50 demos)", self-described as "test-time training in a low-data regime", largely without hyperparameter tuning.

## Answers to the four research questions (with bounds)

**Q1 (JIT, TTT, LoRA-swap under 10 minutes).** Support: TT-SI proves per-case test-time fine-tuning helps text agents (+5.48% average); In-Place TTT proves drop-in fast weights without retraining from scratch at 4B; GeneralistAI proves 1-10 gradient steps from minutes of data (robotics). Bound: nothing I found publishes an intra-session fine-tune-mid-thinking-then-swap loop for text LLM agents under 10 minutes wall-clock on local hardware; per-task and per-case yes, mid-thinking no. The under-10-minute latency figure for an LLM lane remains OWED.

**Q2 (ten-minute-self falsifiers cleared publicly?).** No. TT-SI clears a weak form of falsifier two (a self-selected struggle corpus beating standard learning baselines) and the trajectory-curation paper clears the corpus-control premise. No published loop reports week-over-week pitfall-rate decline, or a held-out-pitfall fold versus prompt-only under that metric. The ZhihuFrontier map's verdict (verification is the bottleneck; almost everything sits at human-on-the-loop stage two) says the falsifiers are still unclaimed. Our rib stays falsifiable and unborrowed.

**Q3 (clone budget for a 1-8B lane).** Land `trl` first: v1.15.0's memory work targets exactly small-GPU SFT, GRPO and distillation; QLoRA is first-class; and AsyncGRPO plus OpenEnv harness training with adapter-only vLLM weight sync is the agent-fine-tuning path closest to our lane. Land `verl` second for its LoRA summon and rollout reference design (heavy infra). `deep-rl-class` and `nn-zero-to-hero` stay behind both; nothing in this sweep argues they outrank the two frameworks. Both are already names in `corpus/CURRICULUM.md:86`; the release detail above is the new part.

**Q4 (cost of distilling a session into a small local model; does it beat prompt plus retrieval?).** Cost points found: frontier lane, $65 per 20-step / 860k-token LoRA job (Kimi K3 on Fireworks); local lane gets materially cheaper in the current TRL line (a 52-82% peak-memory drop means a 1-8B QLoRA fold fits smaller GPUs), but no public per-session dollar or minutes-per-fold number for a local 1-8B distill surfaced in this sweep (OWED). Does anything beat prompt-plus-retrieval at estate scale? No published head-to-head found. Nearest signals: TT-SI's +5.48% for weights-from-own-pitfalls versus standard learning; the lsl.zone taxonomy's qualitative claim that prompt and memory methods "do not generalize well" via memorization; Cognizant's 40x claim for a NO-fine-tuning self-improving network on the other side. Verdict: publicly unresolved; our own controlled fold remains the experiment that would settle it for the estate.
