### 1. arXiv 2610.11482 — Evaluating Local Language Model Agents for Reproducible Data Engineering: An Empirical Software Engineering Study of Mobility Workflows
- URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11482 (v1 2026-10-08, García-Carrasco et al., cs.SE/cs.AI/cs.DB/cs.LG, 26pp; read abs + full HTML)
- Quote: "A quantized 9-billion-parameter model reaches 69.3% with an approximately 6.5 GB memory footprint."
- Full-text facts (https://arxiv.org/html/2610.11482v1): single "NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4090 GPU" (24 GB) via "a local llama.cpp endpoint"; 10 configs = Qwen 3.5 dense 2B/4B/9B in UD-Q4_K_XL and BF16, plus Qwen 3.5/3.6 27B dense and 35B-A3B MoE at Q4 (BF16 does not fit 24 GB); 15 mobility-workflow tasks, deterministic checkers, 5 reps x 10 configs = 1,500 attempts; for models over 2B a closed-loop workspace condition lifts pass rates 26.7-52.0 pp; best config 85.3% artifact-level success; 9B Q4 69.3%; medians per attempt: 10.5 s (9B Q4), 12.3 s (2B/4B Q4), 31.3 s (Qwen 3.6 27B), 14.7 s (3.6 35B-A3B). No tokens/s reported — wall-clock medians only.
- Why it matters (RQ1): the only paper this sweep found that runs multi-step, tool-using local agent loops on exactly a 24 GB consumer GPU with quantized open-weight models and artifact-level deterministic scoring. Answers the RQ1 shape directly: Qwen 3.5 4B/9B at UD-Q4_K_XL sustain loops; MoE 35B-A3B at Q4 is the speed sweet spot (14.7 s/attempt) on the same card. The workspace-condition lift (+26.7-52.0 pp) is a steal: inspectable intermediate artifacts are the cheap lever, not bigger weights.
- Overlap: catalog grep for ID/title/"mobility-workflow" = 0 rows; only this session's trajectory log. Genuinely new.

### 2. arXiv 2610.02142 — Keyword Harnesses Fail Open: A Cheap Diagnostic Ladder for Tool-Use Claims in Small Language Models
- URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02142 (v1 2026-10-01, Juan S. Santillana, cs.CL, 24pp)
- Quote: "Keyword-matching benchmarks can credit small models for tool use they never perform."
- Facts: matched-architecture Spanish security SLM pair (661.6M vs 1,109M) scores near-identical on lenient metrics (B4 0.660 vs 0.650); verbatim-reproduction checks split them 6/6 vs 0/6; a first-token probe traces the 1B failure to a missing prior (~1e-4-1e-5 on the tool_call token) erased by its web-heavy phase; a 3.3 GPU-hour targeted SFT repair (2,202 steps) lifts valid emission 0.100 to 0.959 on corpus rows and 0.536 vs 0.428 on unseen prompts (p=0.004); both models over-trigger (decline rates 0.09/0.17 on negative prompts); "97.7% of the bf16 table remains bit-identical" after repair.
- Why it matters (RQ1 + janitor lane): the gemma:2b/gemma4 janitors' tool-call gating must not trust keyword-grade harnesses; the ladder (verbatim reproduction + first-token probe + negative-prompt decline rate) takes minutes of CPU and directly gates any claim that a small local model "can call tools." Extends the 2026-10-01 fold's regime-dependent-measurement law onto the emission axis.
- Overlap: 1 catalog row — the raw daily mirror `state/arxiv/2026-10-02/2610.02142v1.txt` (auto-fetch, not a steal); the frontier_olm_2026-10-02 register/digest contains no row for it (grep over catalog would show 2+ hits otherwise); sibling trajectory reads on 10-02/10-03 exist. Banked as new with that mirror noted.

### 3. arXiv 2610.07781 — Quantization Effects on Tool-Failure Recovery Vary Across Prompts and Evaluation Designs
- URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07781 (v1 2026-10-06, Yuhe Hu; accepted at NeurIPS 2026 Workshop on Small Language Models for Agentic Systems)
- Quote: "one prompt, one screened task set, and one scoring policy do not establish a stable conclusion about quantized-agent robustness"
- Facts: Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct + Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct (both in the 1-8B class), 8-bit vs 4-bit, 20 deterministic tool-use tasks x 5 prompts; the 8-vs-4-bit recovery comparison "changes direction across prompts and evaluation targets" (Llama deltas 0 to +20.2; Qwen -50.0 to +35.0); evaluation-target choice alone can reverse Llama from +28.3 (full pipeline) to -17.5 (own clean-passing tasks); strict executor parsing flips Llama +28.3 to -15.0.
- Why it matters (RQ1 x RQ4 bridge): the exact measurement trap the estate's quantizer/gate organ must avoid when grading janitor quants on agent loops — matched tasks, full-pipeline scoring, stated parsing policy, uncertainty across tasks, or the verdict is prompt-lottery noise. Corroborates the fold's "quantization damage is stratified" law with a fresh mechanism (evaluation-design variance, not just capability variance).
- Overlap: 1 catalog row — raw mirror `state/arxiv/2026-10-07/2610.07781v1.txt`; no row in the frontier_olm_2026-10-07 register/digest (170 rows checked via catalog grep); sibling trajectory reads 10-07/08/09. Banked as new.

### 4. arXiv 2610.07026 — Offline AI Modules: Voice-First Offline Architecture, Hardware Reference Stack, Quantization and Benchmarking
- URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07026 (v1 2026-10-04, Afariogun, Jenrola, Nezami; cs.AI/cs.NI/eess.SY)
- Quote: "gemma-4-E2B-it achieves 28.8t/s decode throughput and 89.2% topic classification accuracy at Q4_K_M on TierB"
- Facts: first end-to-end benchmark of a voice-first fully-offline stack; three instruction-tuned models in the 2-5B class across four quantization formats; two hardware tiers (Jetson Orin NX TierB, Raspberry Pi 5 TierA); headline: "Q4_K_M quantization represents the best size-to-quality trade-off" on both tiers; all three models fit the 16 GB TierA budget; multilingual quality held via MasakhaNEWS topic accuracy and per-language perplexity drift.
- Why it matters (RQ1 tokens/s + RQ2 quant): a measured 28.8 tok/s decode for the gemma-4 E2B class at Q4_K_M on hardware far weaker than the 24 GB card — a lower bound for the estate's gemma4 janitor lane; also independent 2026 corroboration that Q4_K_M is the size-to-quality knee, now tested on two more form factors.

### 5. arXiv 2601.14277 — Which Quantization Should I Use? A Unified Evaluation of llama.cpp Quantization on Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct
- URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.14277 (v1 2026-01-11, Uygar Kurt, cs.LG, 17pp; older than 30d — banked because RQ2/RQ4 need the GGUF ground truth and nothing newer supersedes it; 14 citations)
- Quote: "Q4_K_S is the natural balanced default when stronger reduction is needed."
- Full-text facts (https://arxiv.org/html/2601.14277v1): 13 llama.cpp GGUF variants (3-8 bit K-quant + legacy) on one model, unified protocol; Q3_K_S drops GSM8K 77.63 to 68.31 (-9.32) with PPL 8.96 vs 7.32 while Q4_K_S/Q4_K_M match baseline (77.33/77.41); Q3_K_M/L recover much of it (Avg 68.07/68.78 vs baseline 69.47); Q8_0 reproduces baseline (69.41); Q5_0 tops the Pareto frontier (69.92 Avg); throughput on a 96-core Xeon CPU: F16 tg128 2.83 tok/s, Q3_K_S 9.91, Q4_K_M 5.12; no 2-bit formats tested.
- Why it matters (RQ2 + RQ4): the per-format GGUF ladder the estate's quantizer organ lacks — and it sharpens RQ4: at 3-bit the damage is already -9.32 GSM8K on an 8B, and 2-bit was not even attempted; any disk-streaming-vs-sub-4-bit comparison must beat a steep quality floor. Note the fold's existing "Q4_K_M 89.3 vs AWQ INT4 90.0" row came from a different source; this adds the full format curve behind that knee.

### 6. arXiv 2610.07070 — Turnslide: Scalable Multi-Turn Data Synthesis by Walking a Finite-State Machine
- URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07070 (v1 2026-10-05, Fainman et al. incl. Archambeau, Salinas; SLM-Agents Workshop @ NeurIPS 2026, non-archival)
- Quote: "a fully automated, lightweight synthesis framework that models each API as a finite-state machine"
- Facts: base small models "are often not good at multi-turn tool calling" and per-API fine-tuning data rarely exists; FSM walk emits state-valid tool sequences rendered with a single LLM call; targets a distribution over turns/tool-sequence/complexity rather than maximal diversity; fine-tuned SLMs hit "70.7% full accuracy over 63.4% and 53.7%" against existing synthesis baselines with "3.6-6.6x fewer tokens."
- Why it matters (RQ1): the cheapest published lever for making the 1-8B class actually sustain tool loops — synthesize the missing multi-turn data, no mock environments, one LLM call per example. Pairs with item 2's repair finding: format competence in small models is a data problem measured in GPU-hours, not a scale problem.
- Overlap: catalog grep for "Turnslide"/2610.07070/"Finite-State Machine" = 0 rows. Genuinely new.
