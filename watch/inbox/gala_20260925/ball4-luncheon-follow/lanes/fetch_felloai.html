All We Know About Google Gemini 4: Release Date, Rumors & What to Expect

By Lenka Vojtechova  ·  Updated September 17, 2026  ·  11 min read

AIFuture of AIGeminiGoogle

Gemini 4 is real, Google has named it, and it is being trained right now. On July 21, 2026, buried in the announcement of a much smaller model, Google wrote that “we have started our most ambitious pre-training run yet, for Gemini 4, and are excited by the progress”. That is the first time Google has ever confirmed the name in its own words.

Confirmed pre-training is not a release, though, and the gap between the two is where every rumor lives. There is still no Gemini 4 model, no documentation and no API access, and Google has attached no date to any of it. This guide separates what Google has actually said from what the internet has invented. In it we answer whether Google Gemini 4 is released yet, what new features and upgrades to expect, and which AI models you should use today until it launches.

The Key Takeaways

  • Confirmed, Not Released: Google confirmed on July 21, 2026 that pre-training for Gemini 4 has begun, but there is still no documentation and no API release for it as of September 17, 2026.
  • Newest Models: Google shipped Gemini 3.8 Live and Gemini 3.8 Live Extended Thinking on September 15, 2026, and both are live dialogue models rather than a new frontier generation. Gemini 3.8 Flash, from September 2, is still the newest Flash, at an introductory $0.75 in / $3.75 out per million tokens that doubles on January 1, 2027. Gemini 3.5 Pro, promised for June, is still unreleased.
  • Bigger, Not Just Faster: On the July 22, 2026 earnings call, Sundar Pichai said the next leap needs “much larger base models” and named coding and agentic coding as the gap Google has to close.
  • Use What Works: You can replicate most anticipated Gemini 4 workflows right now using Gemini 3.8 Flash on the API, or Gemini 3.1 Pro and Gemini Omni Flash in Fello AI.

Gemini 4 is confirmed to exist and confirmed to be in training, but it is not released as a public model in Google’s Gemini API catalogs. Those are two different things, and most of the confusion online comes from collapsing them. The fastest way to verify a real launch is to check the official documentation and the dated entries in the official release notes.

As of September 17, 2026, there is no official public documentation for a model explicitly named “Google Gemini 4” in the developer catalogs or API release notes, and no gemini-4 string in either the Gemini API or Vertex AI model lists. If you search through the official model lists today, you will see the two newest entries, gemini-3.8-live and gemini-3.8-live-extended-thinking, added on September 15, with Gemini 3.8 Flash as the newest Flash, Gemini 3.7 Flash and Gemini 3.6 Flash and Gemini 3.5 Flash and Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite alongside it, the Gemini Omni series, Gemini 3.1 Pro still carrying its -preview suffix, and the Gemini 2.5 stable versions.

Official announcements vs social rumors

The internet is full of speculation. You might see a post about a Gemini 4 announcement on social media, but these are often predictions rather than facts. Google typically releases new models by updating their technical documentation and publishing a blog post on The Keyword.

It is important to understand the difference between a “model update” and a “new generation.” Google frequently pushes updates to existing models without changing the major version number, and the recent jump from Gemini 3 Pro to Gemini 3.1 Pro to Gemini 3.5 Flash is a perfect example. A new “Gemini 4” generation launch would be accompanied by official documentation updates and a dated release note entry, and would likely align with a major Google product event. Google naming the model in a blog post, as it did on July 21, is a genuine signal, but it is a signal about the roadmap and not about availability.

How to check for the release

If you want to know is Gemini 4 released for real, do not rely on trending hashtags. To confirm Gemini 4 availability, rely on official sources. Consumers should watch the dated Gemini release notes, developers should check the API model catalog and changelog for new model IDs, and enterprise users should monitor the official enterprise model lists and the Google AI plans page.

Expected features and capabilities

Note: Below is inference based on Google’s public direction in agents, creation, and computer use (e.g., Project Mariner (agent prototype)) and the current Gemini roadmap signals, not a leak.

Even though the model is not out, we can form grounded expectations about Gemini 4 features based on capabilities currently shipping. The 3.5, 3.6, 3.7 and 3.8 Flash releases raised the bar with frontier intelligence at Flash speed, and Gemini Omni introduced native video generation. The next step will likely focus on how the AI acts and reasons over longer tasks, moving from “chatbots” to true “agents.” Pichai pointed the same way on the July 22, 2026 earnings call, calling coding and agentic coding an area where Google has “acknowledged we need to improve” and saying the next frontier depends on “much larger base models”.

Advances in reasoning and context

Users are hoping for a more powerful Gemini 4 context window. Current Gemini API models, 3.8 and 3.7 and 3.6 and 3.5 and 3.1 and 2.5 alike, generally support 1,048,576 input tokens (≈1M). Worth knowing before you plan around that number, Gemini 3.6 Flash retrieves only 54.0% on GDM-MRCR v2 at the full 1M mark, so a huge window is not the same as reliable recall across it. Earlier Gemini 1.5 Pro releases supported up to a 2M token context window historically, but today’s competitive focus has shifted to reasoning and tooling. We expect major improvements in deeper “System 2” thinking where the model takes time to reason before answering, better reliability with fewer hallucinations for enterprise use, and richer Computer Use that builds on the Gemini 2.5 Computer Use: automate your browser tools and the agentic gains already in the 3.7 and 3.8 Flash releases. The September 15 Live models are the first public look at that direction: Gemini 3.8 Live Extended Thinking reasons and speaks at the same time, narrating its progress through multi-step background tasks instead of going quiet while it works.

Multimodal upgrades

We also expect Gemini 4 multimodal capabilities to push past today’s baseline. Gemini Omni Flash already accepts image, audio, video, and text input and generates editable video, and Google has since added a cybersecurity-tuned Gemini 3.5 Flash Cyber, so Gemini 4 will likely extend toward longer-form video, real-time streaming, and tighter integration with agents. Two September releases already move that way: agentic video understanding, added on September 1, lets Gemini search and inspect only the parts of a video it needs instead of ingesting every frame, and Gemini 3.8 Live reads visual input in near real time while it is still talking. Imagine showing the AI a video of a broken appliance and asking it how to fix it. A future Gemini 4 agent could watch the video, identify the part, and browse the web to find the replacement without prompting.

Pricing and developer access

Money matters. When Gemini 4 pricing is eventually announced, it will likely follow the standard industry tiers, but naming has already evolved.

Subscriptions are now described via Google AI plan tiers, and the current ladder runs Free at $0, Google AI Plus at $4.99, Google AI Pro at $19.99, and Google AI Ultra from $99.99 with a $199.99 tier on top for 20x the Pro limits. A free Flash-tier model is the consumer entry point, and Google’s plans page now reads “Access to 3.6 Flash” for the free tier, so the highest-capability variant would likely sit behind Google AI Pro and Google AI Ultra when Gemini 4 ships. Gemini 3.7 Flash already works that way: in the Gemini app it reaches you only through Spark, which needs AI Pro or Ultra, and Google excludes the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom, Switzerland and Nigeria from that rollout. Gemini 3.8 Flash has followed the same shape so far, with app access reported only for Google AI Pro and Ultra and no entry in the Gemini Apps release notes yet. While we wait for official Gemini 4 pricing confirmation, it is highly probable that Google will maintain its multi-tiered approach to keep things accessible for casual users while providing high-end capabilities for power users and businesses. The shift from the old “AI Premium” to today’s “Pro” and “Ultra” levels suggests a more specialized future for Google’s subscription ecosystem.

Conclusion

The honest summary is that Google Gemini 4 has moved from rumor to roadmap, and no further. Google named it and confirmed pre-training on July 21, 2026, alongside the Gemini 3.6 Flash launch, and that is the entire official record. Four more releases have shipped since, up to the Live models on September 15, and not one of them added a word about Gemini 4. Set your expectations by Google’s own track record here, because Gemini 3.5 Pro was promised for June 2026 and has still not shipped. The smartest move is to master the tools that already exist, starting with Gemini 3.8 Flash, the new 3.8 Live voice models and the Gemini Omni creation models.

FAQ

Is Gemini 4 officially released?

No. Google confirmed on July 21, 2026 that pre-training for Gemini 4 has started, but as of September 17, 2026 there is no public documentation and no API access for it, and no gemini-4 entry in the Gemini API or Vertex AI model lists. Google’s latest releases are Gemini 3.8 Live and Gemini 3.8 Live Extended Thinking, which shipped on September 15, 2026, and the newest Flash model is still Gemini 3.8 Flash from September 2.

When is the Gemini 4 release date?

Google has not announced a date for Gemini 4, only that its pre-training run has begun. Gemini 3.5 Pro was promised for June 2026 and is still unreleased, with Google saying only that it is “currently testing with partners”, so treat any Gemini 4 timing you read as speculation.

Will Gemini 4 be free?

Google usually offers a Flash version for free, and its plans page currently lists “Access to 3.6 Flash” for the free tier, while the most powerful version typically requires Google AI Pro at $19.99/month or Google AI Ultra from $99.99/month. The newer Gemini 3.7 and 3.8 Flash releases are both paid-only in the app, reported so far on those same two plans. To check what the current free plan includes, see whether Gemini is free.
