# ARCHETYPE WORKOVER: the 6 cutter cuts, rewritten as 3-minute thrillers

Lane: jung, division B, gala_20260925 lyric-workover. Date 2026-10-01.
Receipt = this file.

## WHY THE 6 CUTS FAILED

The first-pass packs (watch/inbox/music/cutter-disco_cuts.md,
watch/inbox/music/cutter-electro_cuts.md) sing SYSTEMS to people: law names in
the chorus ("trust bytes, not reports", "GREEN IS PERISHABLE", "IT'S RIGGED"),
receipt echoes in the verses (2,568, thirteen mirrors, basetemp, census,
exit code), doctrine liturgy where a story should be. A listener who does not
live in this estate hears a work chant, not a song.

## THE WORKOVER CONTRACT

Every cut is rewritten through the archetype lens:

- PERSONA: the helmet that never comes off.
- SHADOW: the thing in the basement / the platform that farms you.
- TRICKSTER: the smirk key.
- HERO: the refusal of the crowded road.

Every cut names its archetype AND that archetype's shadow, then rewrites the
doctrine AS SYMBOL ONLY: never a law name, never a receipt, never a system
word. Structure is the operator's riddle-prose template, songified:

1. Cold open in medias res on an OBJECT WITH STAKES.
2. A VISIBLE CLOCK.
3. A NOTE (folded, somewhere it should not be).
4. DIALOGUE as the hook.
5. SHORT DECLARATIVES throughout.
6. The CLOSER lands last.

Each cut answers the survival question: what universal human situation is
this. The listener feels the sovereignty story without knowing it is one:
mask on, mask seen through, mask chosen.

Per cut below: source identity, archetype, its shadow, the universal
situation, the symbol set, the riddle elements, and the full rewritten lyric
sheet. The STYLE STRING and DNA numbers are UNCHANGED from the source packs;
these sheets replace the LYRICS blocks only. Render target tightens to about
3:00 per cut (the lyric sheets are cut to fit); paste the source pack's style
string and this sheet's lyrics. House law honored: ASCII only, no em dashes,
no en dashes, no fabricated quotes (lyrics are new words; the "doctrine under
the symbol" note cites what each symbol carries, for the record, not the
song).

## CUT 1 of 6: "SIGN FOR IT" (was TRUST BYTES, cutter-disco pack, 107 BPM)

ARCHETYPE: PERSONA, the helmet that never comes off. The courier's
trustworthy face IS the helmet: eleven years of being the one man nobody
searches. The mask pays his rent, so it never comes off in public.

ITS SHADOW: underneath the helmet, a man who has never once looked at what he
carries. Trust as anesthesia. The dread that the bag holds his own name. The
persona's price: the signature was never his own hand.

UNIVERSAL SITUATION (survives the question): living on other people's word.
The doctor's "you're fine". The partner's "it's nothing". The boss's "it's
handled". Checking costs something; the check is where you become the author
of your own life. Yes: this is every person who has ever signed for a life
without opening the bag.

SYMBOL SET: the sealed bag, the wax seal pressed by a stranger's thumb, the
manifest, the note under the strap, the depot clock, the last ferry, the
signature in his own hand.

RIDDLE ELEMENTS: cold open object with stakes (the bag heavier than the night
before, 4 a.m.); visible clock (4 a.m., half past five, the ferry that will
not wait); the note (folded once, under the strap); dialogue hook ("You're
gold. You don't have to look."); closer lands last ("In my own hand.").

DOCTRINE UNDER THE SYMBOL (for the record, not the lyric): "trust bytes, not
reports" (AGENTS.md clippy block, echoed in source pack CUT 1) becomes the
wax seal versus the manifest; the daily re-signing law becomes the signature
in his own hand at the end.

LYRICS:

[Intro - spoken over a closed filter, one bar]
The bag came at 4 a.m. It had never been that heavy.

[Verse 1]
Eleven years on the midnight run.
Same bag. Same seal. Same depot hum.
They never search a man they trust.
Tonight the wax wore a stranger's thumb.

[Pre-Chorus]
The manifest said six. My shoulder said more.
Under the strap, a note, folded once:
Count them. Nobody ever counts them.

[Chorus - dialogue]
"You're gold," she said. "You don't have to look."
"Don't have to," I said. "Never did."
The ferry pulls out at half past five.
It can pull out without me.
Look. Look. Look.

[Verse 2]
Broke the wax. Counted twice.
Nothing stolen. Nothing sold.
Just the weight of every word
I ever carried and never told.

[Bridge - half-spoken, the filter opening]
A name is a face. A face is a door.
I was the lock nobody picked
because nobody tried.

[Final Chorus - full open]
"You're gold," she said. "You don't have to look."
"Don't have to," I said. "Never did."
The ferry pulls out at half past five.
It can pull out without me.
Look. Look. Look.

[Outro - spoken, the loop filters down to silence]
Half past five came and went.
I signed for all of it.
In my own hand.

## CUT 2 of 6: "THE LEASE" (was GREEN IS A MOMENT, cutter-disco pack, 126 BPM half-time)

ARCHETYPE: HERO, the hero after the deed. The refusal of the crowded road
here is quiet: the whole town sleeps on its plaques, certain that arrived is
a place. She is the only one who refuses that road and walks to the door at
first light.

ITS SHADOW: the hero's afterglow. The trophy as a substitute for the work.
The borrowed crown. The belief that the deed is banked forever, which is the
exact hour the deed expires.

UNIVERSAL SITUATION (survives the question): "set for life" is the story
everyone wants told about them: the degree, the wedding, the ring, the
championship, the plaque on the wall. Arrival is rented and the rent is
collected at first light. Yes: this is every person who has ever framed a
moment and mistaken the frame for the day.

SYMBOL SET: the brass key the length of her forearm (opens nothing), the
plaque that says FOR LIFE, the fine print on the back, the borrowed crown,
the clock she winds alone, the alarm set for five.

RIDDLE ELEMENTS: cold open object with stakes (the key that opens nothing);
visible clock (expires at dawn, winding the clock, the alarm for five); the
note (fine print folded once, thumb-warm); dialogue hook ("Set for life,"
they said); closer lands last ("She set the alarm for five.").

DOCTRINE UNDER THE SYMBOL (for the record, not the lyric): the perishable
state you re-earn daily (source pack CUT 2, from the 48h story's closing law)
becomes the plaque that renews nightly and expires at dawn; the frozen number
that rots becomes the gold letters nobody turns over to read.

LYRICS:

[Intro - spoken, half-time kick]
They gave her a key the length of her forearm. It opens nothing. It was never supposed to.

[Verse 1]
Key to the city, brass and long.
It hangs by the door where the coats belong.
The plaque says FOR LIFE, gold on walnut.
The back of the plaque holds one line more.

[Pre-Chorus]
The line is fine print, folded once, thumb-warm:
This grant renews nightly and expires at dawn.

[Chorus - dialogue]
"Set for life," they said, and the band played on.
She read the back and told no one.
"Set for life," they said.
Life, it turns out, is billed by the morning.

[Verse 2]
The crowd went home. The lights went down.
She stood on the steps in a borrowed crown.
Every window in town was dark and certain.
She was the only one winding the clock.

[Bridge - falsetto, the filters wide open]
A deed is a door. A door is a habit.
Habits hold the house while the owners sleep.
Turn the key. Turn the key.

[Final Chorus]
"Set for life," they said, and the band played on.
She read the back and told no one.
"Set for life," they said.
Life, it turns out, is billed by the morning.

[Outro - spoken over fading arps]
The mayor slept in.
The city woke up anyway.
She set the alarm for five.

## CUT 3 of 6: "LAST HAND" (was THE SMIRK KEY, cutter-disco pack, 112 BPM)

ARCHETYPE: TRICKSTER, the smirk key. He does not try to beat the game; he
declines its frame. The grin is the tool: it opens what force cannot, and it
costs nothing to carry.

ITS SHADOW: the trickster who starts believing the house's arithmetic. The
con that turns on its own table. Using the key on the people you came in
with. The bridge names the temptation and answers it: the grin is for the
ones still betting, never for the mirror.

UNIVERSAL SITUATION (survives the question): the crooked room everyone stays
in anyway: the job that eats the decade, the market, the dating scene, the
group chat, the family role. Everyone in the room knows the game is tilted
and keeps paying for one more hand. Yes: this is every person who has ever
won the argument and lost the night.

SYMBOL SET: the chip that will not sit flat, the felt, the note under the
felt, the two-way mirror behind the smoke, the shoe that remembers, the
clocks that all face away, the door that was never locked.

RIDDLE ELEMENTS: cold open object with stakes (the chip that would not sit
flat, first honest thing in the room); visible clock (last hand at three,
house lights at four); the note (under the felt, folded twice, old as the
stain); dialogue hook ("House always wins." / "Then the house won't mind.");
closer lands last ("That was the joke.").

DOCTRINE UNDER THE SYMBOL (for the record, not the lyric): the smirk as the
master key and the rigged-room canon (source pack CUT 3, from the operator's
own founding post) becomes the house without honest clocks; the honest
refusal (the oracle that would not invent what it did not have) becomes
racking every chip and standing up slow.

LYRICS:

[Intro - spoken over the wah loop]
The chip would not sit flat on the felt. First honest thing in the room all night.

[Verse 1]
Last hand at three. House lights at four.
The mirror behind the smoke keeps score of the door.
Up eleven, down nine, six hours of almost.
The shoe remembers every card it ever wore.

[Pre-Chorus]
Under the felt, old as the stain, a note, folded twice:
The table is level. The room is not.

[Chorus - dialogue]
"House always wins," he said.
"Then the house won't mind."
"One chair," he said.
"One chair," I said.
And I racked every chip I had.
And stood up slow. And smiled.

[Verse 2]
The dealer's visor blinked. The cage counted slow.
Every clock in the building faces away from the show.
I wasn't the first to find the seam.
I was the first to use the door.

[Bridge - spoken over the filtered breakdown]
The grin is not for the mirror.
The grin is for the ones still betting on the fix.
Somebody has to walk out smiling.
Might as well be the one who checked.

[Final Chorus - drop]
"House always wins," he said.
"Then the house won't mind."
"One chair," he said.
"One chair," I said.
And I racked every chip I had.
And stood up slow. And smiled.

[Outro - spoken, the band cuts dead]
The door was never locked.
That was the joke.

## CUT 4 of 6: "SEVEN COATS" (was ONE BUG, MANY JACKETS, cutter-electro pack, 126 BPM)

ARCHETYPE: SHADOW, the disowned thing that keeps coming back in disguises.
Seven coats on seven hooks, one size of bootprint: the problem was never
seven problems. It is singular, patient, and it lives in the house.

ITS SHADOW (the shadow's own trap): projection. Hunting it in coats, in
streets, in bigger locks, in the landlady's sightings, while the root sleeps
one flight down. Every purchase is a new costume for the hunt. The basement
is yours, the bulb is yours, and what waits there wears your chin.

UNIVERSAL SITUATION (survives the question): the same fight in seven
costumes: the partner, the boss, the phone, the bottle, the relapse with a
new face, the new city with the old argument. You can kill the knock; the
knock gets new hands. The only exit is down the stairs, once, in your own
light. Yes: this is every person who has ever renovated a door when the
trouble was in the cellar.

SYMBOL SET: seven coats on seven hooks, one size of bootprint, the notes in
the pockets, the bigger lock, the cellar bulb that was never dead, the
switch, the chin in the lamplight.

RIDDLE ELEMENTS: cold open object with stakes (seven coats, seven knocks,
one bootprint); visible clock (Monday through Sunday, seven nights); the note
(folded twice, in the seventh coat's pocket); dialogue hook ("Different man
every night." / "Same boots."); closer lands last ("It has my chin.").

DOCTRINE UNDER THE SYMBOL (for the record, not the lyric): the one-bug law
(a verdict without its conditions is a rumor; source pack CUT 1 of the
electro set, from the 48h laws) becomes seven coats against one bootprint;
fail closed and append the miss becomes the bulb that was never dead, only
never switched.

LYRICS:

[Intro - spoken over a filtered stab loop]
Seven coats on seven hooks. Seven nights, seven knocks. One size of bootprint in the hall.

[Pre-Chorus]

[Chorus]

[Verse 2]

[Bridge - half-time, spoken over the loop]
It was never at the door.
The door was the costume.
One flight down. One switch. One bulb.

[Final Chorus - full clip]
Same boots. (same boots)
New coat. (new coat)
Seven doors and the same soft tread.
Same boots. (same boots)
New name. (new name)

## CUT 5 of 6: "THE NIGHT WATCH" (was GREEN IS PERISHABLE, cutter-electro pack, 124 BPM)

ARCHETYPE: HERO, the keeper's kind: unglamorous, nightly, refused. The
crowded road here is the comfortable story the whole town sleeps on (the
gauge says full, so the light must be burning). He walks the other way, up
the tower, every night, with the oil.

ITS SHADOW: the martyr's pride of the lone keeper. The flattery of the gauge
(one night won't matter). Believing the light is you instead of the oil,
which is how keepers become monuments and monuments go dark.

UNIVERSAL SITUATION (survives the question): one day at a time. Sobriety,
the garden, the marriage, the body, the flame. The gauge (the streak, the
scale, the mirror, the number you froze) can read FULL while the lamp is
cold. Only the feeding is true. Yes: this is every person who has ever kept
something alive that a stranger would swear was fine.

SYMBOL SET: the painted needle, the glass cold enough to hold, the
water-stained log, the oil, the wick, the stove and the chore, the dawn shift
change that does not thank you.

RIDDLE ELEMENTS: cold open object with stakes (the gauge reads FULL, the
lamp is cold); visible clock (the light went out on a Tuesday, dawn relieves
me at five); the note (the old keeper's log, last page, water-stained);
dialogue hook ("Gauge says full." / "Gauges don't burn."); closer lands last
("Same time tonight.").

DOCTRINE UNDER THE SYMBOL (for the record, not the lyric): the perishable
green law and the crushed-wall pole of the source pack (CUT 2 of the electro
set) becomes the flame that died on a Tuesday and nobody heard; the
denominator law (a reading is a snapshot of one moment, never a constant)
becomes the gauge that is a story and the oil that is real.

LYRICS:

[Intro - siren sweep, spoken count-in]

[Bridge - breakdown, the dynamics cut dead]
Not a monument. A stove.
Not a crown. A chore.

Again. Again.

[Drop - the wall, zero headroom]

## CUT 6 of 6: "NO CLOCKS IN HERE" (was THE CASINO, cutter-electro pack, 122 BPM)

ARCHETYPE: SHADOW, the platform that farms you. A room with no windows and
no clocks that pays you in shivers and studies your hunger. The house is
patient because the house does not need you to win or lose. It needs you to
stay.

ITS SHADOW: the farm is half yours. The house can only study hunger that is
shown to it. The debt it flashes on the screens was never yours. Walking out
is not breaking anything; it is taking your own fire back, and the floor is
built so that you forget daylight exists.

UNIVERSAL SITUATION (survives the question): the feed. The slot is the
phone, the rage carousel, the news, the app that knows your weakest hour and
sings your name in a loop. Everyone in the room is being farmed and almost
no one reads their own watch. Yes: this is every person who has ever looked
up from a screen and found the night spent.

SYMBOL SET: the player's card, the drink on the house, the bolted-down
chairs, the comp slip with your name and your hours, the wristwatch as the
only clock in the building, the daylight past the door, the fire.

RIDDLE ELEMENTS: cold open object with stakes (the clocks taken at the door,
the one on his wrist kept); visible clock (the wrist, eleven nights, closing
time); the note (the comp slip, folded once, warm as a bill); dialogue hook
("You're up." / "I'm done."); closer lands last ("The fire was mine. I took
it home.").

DOCTRINE UNDER THE SYMBOL (for the record, not the lyric): THE CASINO canon
(slot machines dispensing little jolts of fear, guilt and fresh tribal rage;
the sunburned flip; the smirk; source pack CUT 3 of the electro set, from the
operator's own founding post) becomes the house without clocks; the reclaim
(what if some of this fire is mine) becomes take the fire.

LYRICS:

[Intro - slot chime, bass crawl, spoken]

[Verse 1]
Card in the slot. Drink on the house.
Chairs bolted down. No windows allowed.

[Pre-Chorus]

Folded once in my pocket, warm as a bill.

[Chorus - dialogue]

"I'm done."
"Nobody walks at a time like this."
"Somebody's always first."
Take the fire. Take the fire. Take the fire.

[Verse 2]
The house studies hunger. I showed it mine.
Eleven nights of almost, comped and kind.
The debt it kept flashing was never mine.
I read my own watch. It said closing time.

[Bridge - chop-stutter drop]
One wrist. One watch.
One door. One walk.
The floor is built to forget you.
The daylight is not.

[Final Chorus - the smirk]
"You're up," the dealer smiled.
"I'm done."
"Nobody walks at a time like this."
"Somebody's always first."
Take the fire. Take the fire. Take the fire.

[Outro - slot chime, tape stop, spoken]
Eleven nights to learn the house's one secret:
It cannot follow you past the light.
The fire was mine. I took it home.
