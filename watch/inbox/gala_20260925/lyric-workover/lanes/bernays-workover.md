# BERNAYS WORKOVER: engineering consent for the dancefloor

Lane: bernays, division B, gala_20260925 lyric-workover. Date 2026-10-01.
The 6 cuts from the music-ractor sing opinions. Songs must engineer WANT.
This workover rewrites every lyric sheet so the hook is a want in the

## The doctrine this lane works by

1. Repetition IS consent. A hook sung twice is a belief installed. So the hook
   does not carry the opinion; the hook carries the WANT. The opinion moves
   into the verses, disguised as objects and clocks. This is the 1928 move:
   the women in the torch campaign did not want cigarettes, they wanted to be
   seen as free; Bernays sold the cigarette as the receipt of the want. Every
   cut below sells the want and lets the song be the receipt.
2. The riddle template. "You're early. / I'm late. Drive." Line one is the
   other party's move. Line two is the answer, and the answer lands last as an
   imperative or a blade (the canon cadence: short declaratives, the closer
   lands last, CANON_FIVE_POSTS_20260911.md). Dialogue does the exposition, so
   no narrator explains anything: two voices, one handoff or refusal, and the
   room picks a side.
3. The object law. Every noun is an object with stakes: a sugar bowl, a glove
   box, a stub with a date. Ban list for sung lyrics: bytes, hashes, verdicts,
   suites, commits, census, ledger, repo, lanes, invariants, entropy,
   denominators. The riddle carries an empty slot on purpose ("it", "the
   tape", "the wall"): the listener installs their own want in the slot. That
   installation is the consent.
4. The second-chorus test. By the second chorus the listener must
   involuntarily want one specific thing, legible to a stranger who knows
   nothing about any estate. Each cut below names that want in one sentence.

Falsifier for the whole lane: play a stranger one chorus; if they cannot
restate the want in their own words in one sentence, the engineering failed,
no matter how good the line reads on paper. Second falsifier: a room that
sings the hook but cannot say, if asked, what it just consented to.

Pack law honored: ASCII only, no em dashes, no en dashes, straight quotes.
No fabricated quotes: lines that echo estate doctrine carry an echoes
annotation citing the receipt path, framed as new words, never as verbatim
unless the source itself carries the line.

## The want chain (one night out, six wants)

prove it, keep it, be it, find it, catch it, own it.
TRUST BYTES makes the room want the proof. GREEN IS A MOMENT makes it want to
keep what it has. THE SMIRK KEY makes it want to be the original. ONE BUG
MANY JACKETS makes it want the one true source. GREEN IS PERISHABLE makes it
want to get there now. THE CASINO makes it want its own fire back. A stranger
reads a night out; the estate reads its own laws with the serial numbers
filed off into objects.

## CUT 1 of 6: TRUST BYTES (107 BPM, filter-disco main line)

Pack ID: cutter-disco_cuts.md CUT 1. Style string and DNA unchanged from that
pack (107 BPM kick, low-end 0.75 to 0.77, crest about 12 dB, RMS -11 to -12,
bright top toward zcr 1930/s, length target 4:47).

Street name: PLAY THE TAPE

THE ENGINEERED WANT: to be the one holding the proof. Not the story, the
stub. The stranger's version needs no estate: everyone has stood across from
somebody whose story got shinier with each telling, and felt their own hand
come out flat: show me. By the second chorus the listener involuntarily wants
to be the one who says "play the tape" instead of taking anyone's word, and
singing it twice rehearses the demand until it feels like their own.

THE CROWD REACTION: palms out flat at chest height on the pre-chorus, the
universal show-me hand. The room splits for the chorus: the suit's half sings
the con line, the room's half answers with the imperative. On the drop the
split collapses and the answer wins: the whole floor chants PLAY THE TAPE and
nobody sings the con anymore.

THE HOOK (dialogue, refusal + imperative):
"Take my word for it." / "Play the tape."

## CUT 2 of 6: GREEN IS A MOMENT (126 half-time, filter-disco pop end)

pack (126 BPM half-time feel, low-end 0.31 to 0.54 air, zcr toward 2140/s,
RMS to -9.45 on the wide chorus, length target 5:20).

Street name: IT WAS

THE ENGINEERED WANT: the want to keep. The hook carries an empty slot: the
listener has silently named their own "it" by the first pre-chorus (the job,
the flame, the lawn, the lease, the band) and by the second chorus they
involuntarily want to go check that their it is still an "is" and not a "was".
The final chorus converts the want into a vow: be the one who shows up at
dawn and re-signs. A stranger reads milk, meters, leases, summer loves; the
estate reads its perishable-green law through the same slot.

THE CROWD REACTION: the call goes to the crowd and the crowd answers "IT
WAS." flat on the one, like a verdict at a funeral. Every chorus the answer
hurts more. On the final chorus the crowd breaks form on purpose: the answer
comes back "ASK ME AT DAWN," sung like a promise, hands up and ticking like
stopwatches.

## CUT 3 of 6: THE SMIRK KEY (112 BPM, punk-funk with teeth)

Pack ID: cutter-disco_cuts.md CUT 3. Style string and DNA unchanged from that
pack (112 BPM strut, low-end 0.70 to 0.75, zcr about 1670/s, RMS to about
-9.62, built to loop long, Suno target 5:00 to 5:30).

Street name: NICE COPY

economy: fake bags, bootleg tapes, cover bands, a guest list with two names
in the same ink. Everyone knows the small hot feeling of spotting a copy, and
the bigger, hungrier one of wanting to be the thing that cannot be copied. By
the second chorus the listener involuntarily wants to be the one who answers
"It's the original," level, without raising their voice. The smirk is the
reward, not the hook; the hook is the claim.

pre-chorus is a counting chant: the mold makes a thousand, the hand made one.
On the bridge the split collapses into a gang shout nobody planned (THE

## CUT 6 of 6: THE CASINO (122 BPM, dark electro-funk, the canon cut)

Pack ID: cutter-electro_cuts.md CUT 3. Style string and DNA unchanged from
that pack (folded 122 to 123, RMS -9.87 to -11.21, low-end traded down to

Street name: I'M KEEPING IT

THE ENGINEERED WANT: to not be farmed. The house asks for your bet every hour
on the hour: your attention, your outrage, your money, your fire. The
stranger's version is the machine that knows your name and your tells, the
free drink in the wax cup, the room with no clocks. By the second chorus the
listener involuntarily wants their own reactions back: to stand up from the
machine with the money and the anger still theirs, and to check whose
register rings when they're enraged. This is the lane's namesake cut: consent
was engineered in 1928 and sold as torches; this room consents to the
refusal instead.

THE CROWD REACTION: one voice, the house, asks PLACE YOUR BET; the whole
floor refuses in unison, I'M KEEPING IT, on the money beat. By the final
chorus the refusal lands a half-beat early: the crowd answers before the
house finishes asking, and the I flips to WE. Consent, engineered, then
aimed. The outro is the only quiet moment on the record: footsteps, a door,
a match striking on the sidewalk outside.
