## 1. What is actually for sale

The first architectural fact for an economist is identifying the commodity, and the estate's record is unusually precise about it. The product is not the game. The charter decodes to five clauses (the WoW clone is the forcing project, the GWAP is the backend, the safety posture is the brand, Godot is the training engine, self-steal first). Supported as an estate asset, digest 3. As a market fact, the claim rests on a measured absence: a same-day search found "no single startup combining a play-to-label game mechanic with a human judgment API marketplace as a packaged product." PLAUSIBLE. An absence found by search is evidence, and it is not proof; the finding is dated and search-shaped, and the honest formulation is that the two-sided verified-judgment market is UNCLAIMED as of 2026-09-29, not that it is unclaimable.

The commodity, precisely: verified judgment. Play produces judgments (an intent, a report, a witness answer); agreement between independent roles converts them into labels; deterministic replay and hashed receipts make the labels auditable after the fact. The lineage pricing (digest 4) says this commodity has exactly one immortal retail example: reCAPTCHA, which reached 100M+ captchas per day and roughly 160 books a day of digitization, was published in Science (DOI 10.1126/science.1160379), was acquired by Google in 2009, and survived every rival because the work embedded in friction people already had. Every GWAP that demanded its own visit died of novelty decay, match-early equilibrium (Jain & Parkes, TEAC 2013, DOI 10.1145/2399187.2399190, incentive structure and not player ethics), retention deficit, content exhaustion, or the paywall that converts play to job. The ESP Game labeled 36M images by 2008 and then its owner let the corpus die private (~60K images surfaced), the catastrophe the estate's data-custody-as-mechanic opening exists to prevent. Supported, digest 4.

## 3. The creator-economics inversion, priced by the dead

The platform's economics are designed as a direct inversion of the incumbent stack, and the incumbents' numbers are the design inputs. Roblox pays DevEx at $0.00385 per Robux, an effective take of roughly 25 to 30 percent, with $923M cashed out cumulatively; Epic runs a 40 percent engagement pool (75 percent for brought-back players held six months) with $1B cumulative by 2026; Steam's 25 percent paid-mods experiment died in days; Rec Room is shutting down June 1, 2026 despite 150M+ users and $1M creator quarters, the strongest single datum for the export-and-ownership law; Axie Infinity priced the closed-loop token economy (SLP down 99 percent, the $625M Ronin hack) and left the survivors' rule: pay from real external revenue at published fixed rates. Supported, digest 4.

The inversion, stated as prices rather than values: payouts ride a receipt ledger with a published rate (D1 default), meters weight rare byte-backed artifacts instead of match-early volume (D2 default, which is also the Jain & Parkes match-early fix applied as a payout schedule), worlds export in open formats (the estate's own lock-in trauma as founding constraint), and play-as-work is consented and visible (payload schema public, meters on screen). Supported, digest 3. The economist's honest note: every one of these inversions is a cost until there are creators, and the platform's measured creator count is zero. The inversion is correctly priced as brand, not as margin.

The juror market has one completed experiment and its result is legible: CS:GO Overwatch ran player juries at scale from 2013 to 2023, supermajority convictions, jurors paid in XP only, and it was silently killed in the CS2 transition. Supported, digest 4. The estate's reading (pay jurors real money at published rates) is the obvious fix, and it is also the two-sided market's supply side: the same players whose agreement payloads are the product are the jurors, and the blocker role is priced as a paid adversarial audit, "this judgment survived adversarial blocking" as a quality certificate no vendor currently offers. Supported, digest 4, as a named opening. Unproven as a business: no one has paid for such a certificate yet, anywhere, including here.

## 4. The honest meters are a real technology, not a slogan

Compilation requires verifier V1-V2 (strong permanence only there), an audit record exists or the claim is refused with 409 CompClaimForbidden, the crowding-out law (Deci 1999) bans gamified compile UIs (affordance only), and the flagship claim is locked with real numbers: U1 Factorio, delta 0.15, NC at most 0.05, SC at most 0.10, and the pilot named underpowered at n=24 rather than oversold. Supported, digest 3. An economist recognizes this instrument: it is a pre-registered trial with its own weakness printed on it. That is rarer in game platform design than any graphics feature.

The liveops law table carries the same texture: 8 master laws from studied economies, exact formulas (the GaG2 logarithmic growth capped at 12,561 years; Terraria's multiplayer health recurrence to 255 players), and 18 abuse laws each documented by a fetched incident rather than invented (settlement-at-death wallets, bounded interest, published odds, sell power never immunity, ledger credit never transfers). Supported, digest 3. The economist's translation: most platforms discover abuse laws by being robbed; this one bought them secondhand from the robbed, at the price of reading. The cost advantage of an estate of cheap local AIs is exactly this: the reading is the cheap part, and the estate's own economics prove the point in its own units, with a $0.50-per-day hard cap remembered from August and a measured 109.4M tokens per hour at width 10 on the funded lane. Supported, digest 1. Cheap reading at scale is not a metaphor here; it is the production function.
