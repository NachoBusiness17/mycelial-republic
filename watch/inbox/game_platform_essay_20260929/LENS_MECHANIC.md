## 2. The port war, in the mechanic's register

The second binary is where the work lives. mangosd, the world server, does not build, and the reason is structural rather than incidental: the bots module is fused into the game tree at 120 call sites across 40 files with zero ifdef guards (Map.cpp and Group.cpp call GetPlayerbotAI() and HasRealPlayers() unconditionally). The ledger on record rules out the cheap exit: a stub shim would fake 40 files of API surface, so "the port is the honest path." Supported, digest 2, from the bots port ledger.

The port itself is a war of attrition against two moving targets: GCC 15 and Boost 1.90 compiling a tree that upstream last reconciled around a 2022 bots snapshot (mangosbot-bots @ b685d7eb, r-2022-11-26~587) inside a CMaNGOS-wotlk core @ 49dfc1f. Twelve fixes are landed in the canonical tree as of 2026-09-29, and their taxonomy is instructive. One is a genuine latent bug found by modern tooling: Log.cpp had a 20-byte timestamp buffer where 64 was needed, and glibc fortify caught the overflow. Several are API mortality: boost::asio::io_service became io_context (removed in Boost 1.86+), io_context::work became executor_work_guard, the post member became a free function, run(error_code&) lost its overload in 1.90, address::from_string became make_address. Two are CMake aging: cotire's cmake_minimum_required(VERSION 2.8.12) had to move to 3.5 because CMake 4 removed everything older. One is Linux case-sensitivity: the includes say lowercase vmap while the directory is capital-V Vmap, harmless on Windows, fatal on Linux. And a cluster are MSVC-only-isms that GCC rejects on principle: an invalid mem-initializer syntax upstream (a colon where a comma belongs, at TravelMgr.h:280), an extra qualification inside its own class (TravelNode.h:294), and two-phase-lookup failures in the strategy Value.h templates needing this-> qualifications. Supported, digest 2, each fix itemized in the ledger.

What remains is itemized too, and this is the mechanic's favorite part: the error ledger is honest about being unfinished. LastMovementValue.h:106 binds a non-const reference to an rvalue (fix known: default-construct). TravelNode.h:155/171 fails because TravelMgr.h uses Player bodies with Player only forward-declared, depending on cotire's unity build pre-including Player.h, and the preferred fix (include Player.h at the top) is expected to drag a chain of two to five more heavy includes (Player.h pulls Unit.h pulls Object.h). LogCalculatedValue needs the same this-> treatment already landed for its sibling. And the ledger predicts, without embarrassment, that the two-phase-lookup family will repeat across the strategy headers. Supported, digest 2.

A mechanic reads this ledger and sees the real shape of "revive a dead server": it is not heroics, it is twelve small autopsies and a known family of more to come (the ledger itself predicts the two-phase-lookup fixes repeat across the strategy headers; it names no count, and none is invented here). ## 4. The walls, measured

The mechanic's job includes pricing the walls, and this build path has four.

Wall one: the upstream is old. CMaNGOS-wotlk is a 2021 snapshot with unknown security and compat debt "until B1 compiles it," and the bots module is a 2022 snapshot fused in. The compile is now happening, so the debt is being converted from unknown to itemized at the rate of roughly a dozen fixes per session-day. Supported, digest 2. The external ecosystem check softens this wall: CMaNGOS-wotlk was pushed 2026-09-19 (555 stars, ~18k commits, GPL-2 with OpenSSL exception) and is CI-green on Windows with VS 2022 and Boost 1.87.0, so the upstream is alive, just not on GCC 15. AzerothCore (8,978 stars, pushed the same day) is more alive still, and liyunfan1223/mod-playerbots (1.1k stars) is the stronger bot stack, tunable to 100-120 bots on a mini-PC. Supported, digest 4. The wall is therefore not "dead code"; it is "two trees with different heartbeats, fused by a third party, on the estate's GCC 15 toolchain." The next-lane pointer on record (try cmangos/playerbots, the CMaNGOS-maintained fork, versus pinning a matching celguar commit) is the standard mechanic's move: match the transplants.

Wall two: the license. GPL-2 governs the server code, fine for sovereign use and platform hosting (the SaaS loophole: hosted use forces no source disclosure, distribution does), constraining any closed-source pivot of server-derived code; Godot (MIT) and the estate's own Python are clean. Client data may be extracted from an owned 3.3.5 client but never redistributed, and the ledger's steal is tortoise-wow's SHA-256 DBC-manifest verification pattern, turning extraction-per-user into a product feature. Supported, digest 4.

Wall three: the lawyer's thermometer, not the mechanic's, but it prices the same build. Blizzard sued Turtle WoW in August 2025, injunction around April 2026; Stormforge is shutting May 2026; Warmane tolerated, unconfirmed. The measured enforcement profile is scale plus monetization plus Blizzard-brand competition. A private, non-monetized, federated-lab realm sits outside the measured triggers, and the platform's product (verification backend) does not redistribute client data at all. Supported as measurements, PLAUSIBLE as safety: "outside the measured triggers" is a risk statement, not an immunity claim, and the estate's own record labels it exactly that way.

Wall four: the one that has nothing to do with lawyers or compilers. The platform has zero external developers, and every developer-experience claim is a hypothesis until B4. Supported, digest 3. The mechanic knows this wall personally: a garage with a perfect engine and no driver is still a garage. The mitigations on record are concrete: the repo is already private-live with the collaborator invite named as the next operator step, and the B2 shortcut (bearlikelion/WoWdot, MIT, Godot 4.7, a client that already walks around AzerothCore 3.3.5a, pushed the day before the revival began) collapses the client work from months to a fork. Supported, digest 4. Whether the fork path actually saves the months is unproven until B2 runs; "walks around" is the finder's verb, not a test report.

## 5. The realism ledger

Set against all four walls, what the build path actually holds in hand as of this run: a compiled auth server (realmd, 1.19MB), two live database tiers, full world content loaded, twelve landed fixes in a canonical tree, an itemized remaining-error ledger with predicted fixes, a completed toolchain on two operating systems, an externalized repo behind a verified gate, a 2,272-line verification family with 64 tests and 1,312 opcodes pinned by digest, a 202/202 replay claim awaiting re-execution, and a same-day fork target for the client rung. Supported across digests 2, 3, 4.

What it does not hold: a running world server, a second player, a Godot client that speaks the protocol, a GWAP payload lane with an owner (B3 is UNCLAIMED), a wired judge organ (DORMANT, honest no-op), and a single external user. Supported, digests 2 and 3.

The mechanic's verdict on the build path: this is not a moonshot shaped like a lottery ticket; it is a moonshot shaped like a queue. Every remaining item is either itemized with a predicted fix, forkable, or named as a decision with a default (the seven decisions D1 through D7 each carry a default if the operator stays silent, from ledger-over-token to replay-plus-receipts as the day-one sellable). The honest risks are three and they are named: the two-trees-one-heartbeat port could extend past prediction (the ledger itself predicts more two-phase-lookup family); the B2 fork's "walks around" is unverified; and B4 has not happened, so the platform's usability is a belief, not a measurement. Unproven, all three, and the build's own law says so out loud, which is the most a mechanic can ask of a plan.
