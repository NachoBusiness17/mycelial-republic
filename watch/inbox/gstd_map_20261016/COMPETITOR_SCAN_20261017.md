## 1. Executive Thesis

Decentralized physical infrastructure (DePIN) and agent compute networks settle billions in synthetic value, yet all share a critical structural vulnerability: they conflate liveness, staking weight, or peer voting with semantic execution correctness. When generation costs drop to zero, dishonest or lazy nodes flood networks with superficial outputs that pass validator heuristics while skipping deep invariant checks. Our coverage-receipt thesis (enumerating checks-run over checks-checkable backed by byte-exact disk proofs) provides the only deterministic defense against validator collusion and shortcut mining.

## 2. Network Survey (October 2026)

### A. Bittensor (TAO)

- What it settles: Subnet-specific digital commodities including raw inference, code generation, fine-tuning, and specialized data extraction.
- Verification model: Yuma consensus and Dynamic TAO (dTAO). Subnet validators rank miner outputs via heuristic evaluations; emissions flow to miners matching validator consensus.
- The weakness our thesis exploits: Validator subjectivity and cartelization. Miners game validator scoring scripts with prompt templates and cosmetic style matching rather than solving underlying problems. Validation is unbacked by deterministic execution traces. Our coverage-receipt requires hard programmatic checks-run proofs, rendering subjective peer grading obsolete.

### B. Allora Network

- What it settles: Context-aware inference streams and financial/economic predictions for on-chain smart contracts.
- Verification model: Regret-minimization multi-model ensemble weighting paired with zero-knowledge machine learning (zkML) proofs of inference execution.
- The weakness our thesis exploits: zkML computational overhead and scope blindness. While zkML proves a specific matrix multiplication ran, it cannot prove whether the model's outputs satisfied external state invariants or real-world falsifiers. Furthermore, zkML is prohibitively expensive for multi-turn frontier reasoning. Our coverage receipt operates at the artifact and invariant level, offering 1000x lower verification cost with higher semantic fidelity.

### C. Hyperbolic

- What it settles: Open-access decentralized GPU compute and verifiable inference serving.
- Verification model: Proof of Sampling (spChain). Randomly samples a fraction of inference queries to duplicate across peer nodes, penalizing deviations.
- The weakness our thesis exploits: Sampling latency and non-deterministic divergence. Frontier temperature settings, speculative decoding, and floating-point variances across GPU architectures produce false-positive discrepancies. Hyperbolic verifies token output similarity, not task-level execution integrity.

### D. Io.net

- What it settles: Decentralized GPU clustering, batch training compute, and ray-based distributed clustering.
- Verification model: Proof of Compute and Proof of Time. Measures hardware telemetry, bandwidth speed, GPU temperature, and heartbeat liveness.
- The weakness our thesis exploits: Liveness does not equal correctness. Io.net confirms a GPU is online and running a container, but is entirely blind to whether the work done inside the container is valid or corrupted. It settles hardware availability, leaving output verification entirely to the buyer.

### E. Grass (Wynd Network)

- What it settles: Residential bandwidth, web scraping throughput, and AI data training pipelines.
- Verification model: Proof of Connectivity. Validates IP reputation, proxy uptime, and data scrape payload hashes against target URLs.
- The weakness our thesis exploits: Scrape volume over epistemic truth. Grass verifies that bytes were pulled from a URL, but cannot verify whether the extracted information answered the user query or hallucinated downstream claims.

### F. Morpheus & Ritual (Infernet)

- What it settles: Smart-contract-triggered AI inference and autonomous agent actions.
- Verification model: Optimistic execution with challenge windows and economic slashing, or designated oracle consensus.
- The weakness our thesis exploits: Long challenge delays and capital inefficiency. Optimistic fraud proofs require long settlement windows. Designated oracle committees reintroduce centralized trust assumptions.
