## 1. Strongest line

**L35 — "The hall did not need Tom to believe the new line. It only needed him too tired to mark the old one."**

It is the only line that states the mechanism instead of the mood. Everything before it describes
pressure; this line names what the pressure is *for*, and it splits two things the rest of the parable
keeps fused: belief and marking. The county never asks Tom to agree. The letter, the archived 1987
survey, "proprietary" methodology, the two finished stories in L23 — all of it is aimed at the cost of
marking, not at the content of Tom's mind. That is also why the ending works: charcoal is free and the
walk is short (L47) is not a moral, it is a price list. It answers the L35 claim on its own terms —
marking was cheap the whole time; it had just been made to feel expensive.

Runner-up: **L21 — "She was not lying. She was reading."** Best line in the piece as prose, and it keeps
the clerk human, which the parable needs. But it is a diagnosis of one character; L35 is the doctrine.

## 2. Line I would cut

**L33 — "There was another file. Tom never saw it. People in town spoke as if they had. Dates. Complaints. A name written small. After that, the men who used to argue at the feed store got reasonable overnight. Reasonable meant sitting down."**

Two reasons. First, it is the only paragraph that asks the reader to import a second, unshown
conspiracy — a file nobody has seen, invoked by people who only *speak as if* they have — in a parable
that is otherwise careful to keep every pressure traceable to an office, a clerk, a letter, a plat.
The dread here is asserted rather than produced, and the paragraph's own hedge ("spoke as if they had")
admits it has no evidence to show. Second, it duplicates a beat the piece already lands better: the
manipulation of the town's stories is carried cleanly by L23 (two finished stories from one survey) and
by L41 ("a story that arrives already wearing your anger was cut for you"). Cutting L33 loses "Reasonable
meant sitting down," which is a good line, but that line belongs to a story about a file, and this is a
story about a fence.

Runner-up cut: **L43** ("The clerk smiled when the cars slowed down. She smiled before they finished
parking.") — it turns the clerk back into a villain after L21 spent a line making her a function, and
the reversal costs the parable more than the smile buys.

## 3. Question the parable leaves unanswered

**What happens the day after the cars stop?**

The parable ends at attention: the neighbor, then another, then the farm bureau woman, then strangers —
all stopping, all looking at the stake, all remembering something they had stopped checking (L39).
That is the hinge of the whole piece and it is left as a hinge. The crowd explicitly does not agree on
where the line goes ("one still wanted the 1987 line... one wanted no line at all and a better road"),
and the parable never says what a disagreement like that does next. Does remembering convert into a
marked line, or does charcoal weather off and the plat stand? Two people on that road — Tom and Old Man
Dubois (L29: "I know where it was, Tom. But I got the same letter.") — already know the same thing and
got the same letter, and the parable hands the act to Tom alone. The obvious unanswered question is
therefore not "where was the fence" but **who, having both received the letter, marks it together, and
what makes that mark stick where a ribbon and three fair wins did not.**

Two smaller ones, since they carry weight the parable drops:

- **Who is "the hall"?** It appears once (L35) as the actor with the interests, then vanishes. The county
  clerk, the surveying firm and "the hall" are never joined up, so the reader cannot tell whether "the
  hall" is a building, an office, or shorthand for the letter itself.
- **What makes a prior map legal?** The 1954 and 1921 maps (L13) are the only hard evidence in the story
  and the clerk's answer — "those aren't the legal maps" (L15) — is a rule, not a reason. The parable
  shows that the chain of surveys goes back further than anyone can pull (L9) and then never asks what
  would let a pulled map outrank a current one. That is the question Tom actually needed answered.
