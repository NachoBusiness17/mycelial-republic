## (4) THE PATTERN

One weakness underlies all three chapters.

**Each chapter earns its receipts and then assumes the reading it just earned.** The accuracy chapter earns 12 classes from a named corpus, then quotes the class count without the class denominator. The logic chapter earns its laws from a lane JSON, then folds them as if the fold were the lane. The operations chapter earns eight failures with receipts, then writes "F" rules that are reformulations of the failures and calls them a doctrine delta.

The pattern: **the chapter is a citation engine running without a trust anchor above it.** Every claim is well-cited to something the chapter read. No chapter carries the instrument that certifies the layer above its own citations. Accuracy citations resolve against corpus; no instrument certifies that the corpus is the corpus (denominator untyped). Logic citations resolve against the lane JSON; no instrument certifies that the lane JSON is the lane (seal-probe said ABSENT). Operations citations resolve against each failure's receipt; no instrument certifies that the delta section is derived from the failures rather than restating them (F-rule overlap, unnamed).

This is the estate's own incident 1 shape (ok:true-equals-landed) lifted one level: ok-cited-equals-true. The chapters each bank "the citation resolved" as "the claim is a doctrine item." The class of defect the accuracy chapter called `ok:true-equals-landed` recurs one syntactic level up, in all three chapters, uncaught by the citation gate the accuracy chapter proposes.

Fleet doctrine blind spot: **citation resolution is treated as a sufficient certification of a claim's content-position, and no chapter carries a super-denominator instrument that certifies the corpus of citations itself.** The estate has a citation linter (`01__logic.md`, checks run #3) and no citation-of-citation linter.
