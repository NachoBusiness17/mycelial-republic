## APPEND 3 — Line C: believable agents / simulacra (searched 2026-09-16)

**Generative Agents: Interactive Simulacra of Human Behavior** — Park, O'Brien, Cai, Morris, Liang, Bernstein (Stanford + Google), arXiv:2304.03442, UIST 2023, **7,825+ citations**.
- What they proved: 25 LLM agents in "Smallville" with memory stream + reflection + planning produce *believable* unscripted behavior — emergent party organization, information diffusion (mayoral candidacy gossip) through the social graph.
- Steal: believability comes from *architecture* (memory + reflection + plan) not from raw model size. The "thought process" is a first-class generated artifact.
- Follow-up: Park et al. 2024 "Generative agent simulations of 1,000 real people" (Stanford HAI policy brief; interview-grounded agents) — **this is the ~85% SOTA mnemonic.ai benchmarks against.** Park PhD dissertation (Stanford purl jm164ch6237) collects the arc.

## APPEND 4 — Line D: decision prediction from personality (searched 2026-09-16)

- **Big Five ↔ behavioral decision tasks** (ResearchGate 339591782): the five traits correlate with performance on behavioral decision-making tasks — classic psychometrics.
- **Big Five in AI-agent decision-making** (arXiv:2503.15497, AgentVerse framework): **extraversion + conscientiousness** measurably steer agent decisions; neuroticism/agreeableness give "more balanced responses". → Proof that OCEAN vectors move *decision distributions*, not just prose style.
- **Risky decision-making empirical study** (Garmian Univ. journal, passer 173596): agreeableness, neuroticism, conscientiousness significantly correlated with risky choices.
- **ML predicting Big Five** (ACM 10.1145/3690407.3690501): six ML algorithms classifying traits — the trait-inference direction is commodity.
- **Executive personality via ML → decision outcomes** (Wiley JBFA 10.1111/jbfa.12406): text-derived Big Five of executives linked to firm decisions — decision prediction from personality in production finance research.
- **NLP/deep-learning Big Five from text** (U Arkansas ScholarWorks; "Philip 2018" psycholinguistics): the psycholinguistic pipeline (text → traits) that mnemonic.ai-style products sit on.
- Cross-link: mnemonic.ai's own blog "How personality influences purchase behaviors" is this literature productized.

**What Line D proved:** trait vectors → decision *correlates* is replicated; trait-conditioned *generative* decision simulation with accuracy vs human self-agreement is new (mnemonic.ai Twin-2K-500, Park et al. 2024).

## APPEND 5 — Line B: brain-structure-inspired architectures (searched/fetched 2026-09-16)

- **Brain-Score** (brain-score.org; Schrimpf et al., bioRxiv 407007; NeurIPS 2019 "Brain-Like Object Recognition with High-Performing Shallow Recurrent ANNs"): composite benchmark scoring any ANN by **neural predictivity** — how well internal representations predict recorded neural/behavioral data. Proved: "brain-like" is *measurable*, and shallow **recurrent** networks beat deep feedforward on brain-likeness. MIT Quest platform, open, community leaderboard.
- **Recurrent/shallow beats deep** — direct support for the wave-lane M2 finding (recurrence as depth) and for small witnesses being enough if loops are deep.
- **Spiking/threshold decision line (arXiv API, verified today):**
  - Zhou 2018, "Spiking Neural Dynamical Drift-Diffusion Model on Collective Decision Making with Self-Organized Criticality" — integrate-and-fire neurons, collective decisions, power-law criticality.
  - Jaffard 2025, "Spiking Neural Models for Decision-Making Tasks with Learning" — proves drift-diffusion is approximable by spiking Poisson neurons (Hawkes process, local learning rule).
  - Zoltowski 2020, "Unifying and generalizing models of neural dynamics during decision-making" — DDM + multi-dimensional accumulators + **collapsing boundaries** fit to monkey parietal spiking.
  - Proved: **decision = evidence accumulation until a threshold/boundary crossing** is a quantitatively valid, fittable mechanism — with per-trait-tunable parameters (drift rate, boundary, noise, starting bias).
- **Graph-brain line:** arXiv API queries for connectome+GNN+personality → **no direct hits** (verified gap today). Adjacent verified: SA-HGNN (2026, EEG functional-connectivity GNN, depression) and federated fMRI GNN (2023, 1,218 subjects, disorder classification). The machinery (GNN over brain-graph connectivity) exists; personality-trait regression over it is not on arXiv. (Canonical CPM/connectome-fingerprint literature — Finn et al. 2015 Nat Neurosci, Shen et al. 2017 — lives in NeuroImage and was NOT fetched today; treat as unverified context.)
- Also fetched-adjacent: arXiv survey 2511.04455 (2025) on brain-inspired learning in AI (from search results).

## VERDICT (2026-09-16)

**mnemonic.ai: REAL, LIVE, and NOT what the mission imagined.** A MarTech "marketing intelligence platform" (© 2025 site): Data Hub, AI Personas, **Digital Twin**, Digital Panel, Analytics. OCEAN/Big-Five persona-synthesis is genuinely their core line ("Uncover deep psychographic insights with OCEAN personality analysis"); decision prediction is implicit (reactions to price/creative before launch) with a serious benchmark (Twin-2K-500: 2,058 people, 191,406 held-out answers, 89% of the 73% self-agreement ceiling, benchmarking against Park et al. 2024's ~85%). **Stealable:** the ceiling-relative evaluation protocol, OCEAN-as-parameter discipline, and the "synthetic customer base as sandbox for agentic testing" pattern. **Not stealable because absent:** brain structure, graph dynamics, ocean/fluid state models — their architecture is RAG + trait scores. The "physical brain structure mapped to ocean models" is the operator's own layer; nobody has shipped it.

**5 best prior works:**
1. **Park et al. 2023, "Generative Agents: Interactive Simulacra of Human Behavior"** (arXiv:2304.03442, UIST 2023, 7,825+ cites) — believable thought = architecture, not scale.
2. **Park et al. 2024, generative-agent simulations of 1,000 real people** (Stanford; ~85% of self-agreement ceiling) — individual-accurate simulated minds; the yardstick mnemonic.ai itself cites.
3. **PsychAdapter** (Nature portfolio, s44387-026-00071-9) + **BIG5-CHAT** (OpenReview TqwTzLjzGS) — trained OCEAN-conditioning of LLM output beats prompt theater.
4. **"Big Five Personality Traits in AI Agent Decision-Making"** (arXiv:2503.15497, AgentVerse) — traits steer decisions in multi-agent systems, not just prose.
5. **Brain-Score** (brain-score.org / Schrimpf et al., bioRxiv 407007) with the **DDM-threshold decision line** (Zoltowski 2020; Jaffard 2025 spiking-DDM; Zhou 2018) — brain-likeness is scoreable, and decisions are threshold crossings with fittable parameters.

**Open niche our ractor engine would occupy:** *No published system combines: (a) witnesses as modules of a brain-graph (region-like topology, not flat role prompts), (b) OCEAN vectors as physical parameters of the graph's dynamics (traits → edge weights, drift, noise, boundary heights — neuromodulator-style), (c) thinking rendered as literal state flow over that graph (the "ocean": Laplacian/wave dynamics — already kin to lane-mates M2 recurrent depth and M3 Laplacian beliefs), and (d) decisions firing only as threshold crossings, with every crossing receipted and scored against a human self-agreement ceiling.* Stanford owns believable-but-flat agents; Line A owns trait-conditioning on single flat models; neuroscience owns threshold decisions without LLM witnesses; mnemonic.ai owns the business case and the benchmark protocol but has no dynamics. The ractor engine takes one chair from each and occupies the empty middle — **locally, on small models, with receipts**.
