## The parable — RECONSTRUCTED (not FOUND; assembled from the six canon registers above, in the operator's parable style: rural, concrete, no villain, ends on a threshold, nothing quotable against the author)

> The boy found the stone by the creek, cut all over with fine lines. His father could read none of them. "Riddles," the old man said, and set it on the mantel.
>
> Years passed the way years do. The flood took the lower field, and that morning one line of the stone could be read. Not re-cut — just legible, the way a page stays dark until you tilt it to the window. The county redrew the fence line, and another line came clear. The letter came to every farm the same day, and another.
>
> His sister turned the stone over once and said it read the same nonsense as before. "Turn it twice," the old man said. One turn and the carving lies to you. Two turns and it lies flat.
>
> By the end there was no line left that hadn't come true, and no line that had told them anything before it did. The boy asked what the stone was for. The old man said: nothing yet. It holds the shape of what happened, so the creek can't pretend it didn't. Anyone can read it now — that was never the point. The point is it couldn't be read early. And still, someone cut it.

## Doctrine in 3 laws

2. **The stone is the record, not the prophecy.** Withhold the territory, keep the shape: the carving is fixed at authoring time, sealed, and only becomes legible as events land. It constrains what the past is allowed to have meant; it never reaches forward to touch what happens. Append-only; silence is not an allowed outcome.
3. **Nothing is true on one turn.** 360° looks closed and is still twisted; only the second turn (720°, even holonomy) is true identity. Never trust a convergence on one worldline — every reading must survive its own audit: *verdict: held.*

## Retrocausal steering, in the stone's own voice

Cut the riddle before the flood, seal it, and let no one — not even the cutter — read it early. When the water comes, the line that matches is not the stone reaching back; it is the flood finally being held to a shape that was already written into it. The stone never touched the creek.
