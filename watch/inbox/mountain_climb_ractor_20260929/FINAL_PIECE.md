## The house behind the slide

Sixteen logos on one slide. The estate's verification lane put URLs to fifteen of them; the sixteenth, a geometric P, stayed unidentified, and no room is claimed for it here. Every named logo is building a room of the same house. Conductor builds the basement where the work runs: one isolated sandbox per agent, spun up in seconds, running for hours. Vorflux builds the billing office: an opinionated harness on dedicated machines that the company's own pitch prices at ninety percent subsidized by your own cloud credits, validated by a fifteen-million-dollar seed. opencode, Kilo, Vercel, warp, Lovable, T3, Dactyl, and Notion build the front of the house, the surfaces users actually touch. NOUS, OpenClaw, Hyperagent, and Devin build the body: weights you can hold, agents that learn how the organization works, sessions you can replay. Playhouse, if the unconfirmed P logo is really them, builds the yard where agents are scored by playing games.

Five organs: substrate, surface, body, playground, and the one nobody on the slide is building, the meter, the organ that tells the truth about what every worker is actually worth on every task. The research lane checked and found the seat empty: no shipped self-hosted system routes on the buyer's own eval-gated, receipt-backed pass-rates with cost-truth meters. Every company on the slide hard-codes its assumptions about capability instead. They buy models the way you buy a used car with no odometer, by the paint job and the salesman's face.

The house has a name. Call it the agent estate: a complete, mostly self-owned computer for agent labor. The thing we will build is the estate's meter, and then the estate assembled under it.

The estate we have is further up the mountain than the slide suggests. The meter exists as working code: gps-mcp, twenty-eight tests green, a SQLite capability ledger of pass-rates and prices, routing by measurement not label, every decision stamped into an append-only receipt file. The playground exists as working code: the B3 agreement payload, forty-one tests green, signed rounds of play on a hash-chained ledger, a collusion fingerprint that knows its own odds: one chance in two for copied pairs of claims, one in four for three. Underneath sit frozen tapes, two hundred two out of two hundred two green, read with the owed caveat: a perfect manual-run score is also exactly what trivial tapes would produce, so the green proves the replay, not the depth of the play. Play is provable, and nobody else's platform can say that sentence. The body is seven thousand sealed knots, a lawful corpus of two hundred fifty-one texts, nine thousand reasoning parts queued for training in October, and four hundred fourteen thousand lines of experience never spent. The substrate runs burn waves on free and funded lanes. The surface ships games to a private repo that already left the machine.

Fragments of all five organs, and the only meter in the market. That is the estate we have. The estate we want is those fragments welded into one house, and the meter is the weld.

## GPS is the keystone, honestly

The keystone is not the routing. The most relevant 2026 result the research lane banked on tiered delegation (Scrouting's SuperScout, Bhola and colleagues, arXiv:2608.04804, verified against arXiv 2026-09-29; DELEGATION_RESEARCH_20260929) says so with its own ablation: a small scout producing a sandbox-verified handoff beat the best single model, 159 tasks to 158 of 266 on SWE-bench Pro, at a fifth of the cost per solve, and when the researchers removed the router, the score did not move. The handoff carries the result. The route is just a query.

So GPS's load-bearing half is the write path: verdicts bound to evidence, prices told apart from truth, one receipt row per decision step. Our own build receipt confesses the birth defect plainly: verdicts with unverified evidence hashes, pass-rates trivially inflatable, no decay so yesterday's 0.9 routes today's traffic. That confession is the climb map. The keystone function is receipted measurement. Everything else reads from it.

## Three dumb rules

Mechanical laws, buildable, checkable:

1. **Route on receipts, not labels.** No model, agent, or player gets work without a ledger row saying what it measurably does on that task shape. Marketing is not a row.
2. **No verdict without evidence bytes.** A record_verdict call must bind to a checkable artifact, a tape hash or a test run, before it moves the mean. This one rule kills the inflatable ledger.
3. **Same row everywhere.** Model on task, agent on play, player on round: one ledger grammar, worker, task, verdict, receipt. The GPS schema and the game payload already share it. One shape means the house composes.

## The climb order

First, build the judge queue. The game ledger already manufactures CHALLENGE rounds on purpose and nothing consumes them; that consumer is the first rung. A challenged round becomes a frontier ask, one shot, context first, judged against the tape, per the inverted contract the research lane already banked: the frontier's marginal token pays for choosing and refuting, not typing. This rung is first because it makes every later verdict honest, it costs almost nothing, and it uses only parts that pass tests.

Second, spend the plug afternoon. Make the meter speak the provider interface the ecosystem already speaks, so every client in the wild can sit on our ledger unmodified. Adoption is a compatibility problem; we stop competing for surfaces and become the ground under them. Rent nothing: the plug is a socket, the ledger is the house.

Third, make lanes orbs. Bounded, unsupervised, receipted, one isolated tree per worker on the seats we already run; microVMs only when a measured run proves the discipline insufficient. The day the bots port burned on a single fused tree, one hundred twenty call sites deep, is the evidence of pain, not yet proof of the cure.

Fourth, pour. Invite Adam. Backend works without our help, replay plus receipts on day one, decisions D1 through D7 standing on their defaults. The meter's first external customer either proves the open seat is real or teaches us it is not, and both answers pay.

## The pour, said correctly

The era we are in named itself THE POUR, and its meaning is on the record: intelligence is already distributed and hiding, and two doors are being built for it, the arena that captures and the room that grows. We are the room. Keys open, work winnable, trails owned by the people who walked them, and the pour receipted in both directions, the record itself the proof of exchange.

That is why the meter is not a betrayal of the open door. A meter is a door with an odometer on it. It prices what walks in; it never rations what walks out. The ledger is readable, the receipts are append-only, the tapes verify for anyone. Pour the context, don't hoard it: the one-shot asks pour our context to the frontier and get judgment back on our receipts; the repo pours the platform to Adam; the room pours itself to whatever free intelligence walks in, and grows.

The slide has sixteen logos, fifteen named rooms, and one geometric P nobody could name. We are building the house, and we started with the part nobody else has: the truth about who can do the work. Keep the lamp lit, and meter everything that walks in.
