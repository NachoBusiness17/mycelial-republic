The playground cluster knows that play is the cheapest honest measurement ever discovered. A playground that verifies hands-offs is worth a router; a router without verified hands-offs is a tie with itself.

## What GPS does for this layer

GPS and the playground are the same invention at two scales, and the climb's most load-bearing fact is that both ledgers already share a shape: gps-mcp prices model-on-task-shape with pass-rate, sample size, cost (D15); the payload prices agent-on-play with agreement score, verdict, and meter_credit whose rarity is byte-backed by a receipt hash (D12-fact 12). Concretely, GPS serves the playground four ways. Routing the players: D5 defaults AI clients to workers under cost_enforcer caps with human-only play until B4 (D3-fact 3), and the cap is a GPS hard constraint, the same max_cost_usd that filtered routes (D15). Metering the game: D6 defaults meter v0 to agreement_score plus an AR floor (D3-fact 3), a game-side pass-rate in the ledger's own grammar. Paying the winners: D2 weights payouts toward rare byte-backed artifacts, not points (D3-fact 3), and rarity is deterministic from verdict and collusion (D14), so the payout rail reads the same rows the meter reads: D1 defaults to the receipt ledger with tokens deferred (D3-fact 3), the Axie counter-lesson encoded as a default (D26). Learning from every round: exploration rows (unknown task shape, mid tier, exploration true, D15) are how the playground teaches the meter new games without burning judgment; and the escalation economics stay honest because the trace prices each tier attempt separately (D16). One system, two ledgers, one shape: that is the keystone fitting the last arch.
