## THE LAYERS (for the record, not the song)

Surface: a man saves seeds while the town buys seedless fruit. The drought
comes. He feeds the town.

Layer 2: the seeds are your data, your weights, your receipts. The merchant
is the platform that sells convenience and owns the supply chain. The
drought is the quota wall, the rate limit, the API shutdown.

Layer 3: the estate's entire thesis in a children's rhyme. Files are truth.
Chat is heat. The record stays on your machine.

## ALLITERATION MAP

V1: Pete/planted/patents/prized/pods. The P-run is the seed shape itself.
The pre-chorus drops it for the rain's universality.
V2: merchant/mocked/mason. The M-run is the merchant's voice, then Pete
"planted what he mattered" (matter = both substance and care).
Bridge: nobody/noticed/nodes/noise. The N-run is the town waking up.
Outro: save/seed/seed/save/seed. The S-run is the loop law itself.

## WHY THIS WORKS WHERE THE LAST ONES DIDN'T

The last cuts sang ABOUT systems. This one sings ABOUT a man named Pete.
The sovereignty thesis is the moral you extract yourself. The song never
says "own your data," it says "Pete had peaches." The alliteration is
playful (children's book) but the story is Aesop (you feel the moral
without being told). And the disco funk makes the parable danceable. The
gravity lives in the words, the lightness lives in the beat.
