# BIBLIOGRAPHY — the twenty that carry the library

The fleet mines a free, legal, author- or publisher-hosted corpus. Every fetch lands
with its byte count and SHA-256 in a manifest; paywalls and dead links are recorded,
not worked around. This is the load-bearing set from the mining pass this packet was
written in — twenty works, each with why it is in the library and what it changed.
Anything not listed here is present but not yet load-bearing, and we do not pretend
otherwise. (Fetch receipts: `wave_20260916/receipts/F1_AI_CANON.md`, `F2_GAME_CANON.md`,
`F3_ELECTRONICS_CANON.md`, `F4_ESOTERIC_CANON.md`;
`physical_computing/receipts/S4_CORPUS_STEAL.md`.)

## AI and learning (the machinery shelf)

1. **Sutton & Barto, *Reinforcement Learning: An Introduction*, 2nd ed.** — author-hosted
   free PDF (incompleteideas.net). The shared vocabulary for reward, baseline, and
   exploration. What it changed: our salon temperature-ladder replicate (BUILD 3c's
   sampler test) was designed as a controlled exploration sweep because this book
   defines what that means.

2. **Jurafsky & Martin, *Speech and Language Processing*, 3rd ed draft.** — author-hosted
   free draft (Stanford). What a language model is, stated plainly, with the math shown.
   What it changed: the anti-mysticism anchor — when a lane starts attributing intent to
   a model, this is the shelf the correction comes from.

3. **Goodfellow, Bengio & Courville, *Deep Learning*.** — publisher-sanctioned free HTML
   chapters (deeplearningbook.org), complete set. What it changed: mechanism over vibe —
   claims about model behavior get checked against a real textbook before they enter the
   ledger.

4. **MacKay, *Information Theory, Inference, and Learning Algorithms*.** — author-hosted
   free PDF (inference.org.uk); the delight find of the pass, not on any target list.
   What it changed: the whole "bytes not reports" habit has a formal cousin — minimum
   description length, prefer the explanation that compresses. This book is why we
   started scoring receipts by what they let a reader recompute.

## Game design (the systems shelf)

5. **Crawford, *The Art of Computer Game Design* (1984).** — public scan (archive.org);
   the first book of the field. What it changed: design as decision-making under
   constraint, which is how our world builds read now — rooms as legality tables, not
   prose.

6. **Costikyan, "I Have No Words & I Must Design" (2002).** — author-hosted PDF
   (costik.com). Converts "gameplay" from mystique into a checklist: goals, opposition,
   uncertainty, resources, information. What it changed: the primacy of uncertainty is
   the same instinct behind blind sets and pre-registration (BUILD 3e) — a game without
   uncertainty is a screen saver; a test without it is a mirror.

7. **Koster, *A Theory of Fun* (text release) + *A Grammar of Gameplay* (2005).** —
   author-hosted. Games as pattern-learning machines. What it changed: the toy-build
   lane's core verb was cut to one learnable pattern and its test counts repetitions,
   not features.

8. **Bartle, *Designing Virtual Worlds*.** — author-hosted free PDF (mud.co.uk); the
   named bar itself. What it changed: worlds are law systems, not content piles — direct
   ancestry of our world skeleton, where the ledger of moves is the game.

9. **Cook / Lost Garden, "The Chemistry of Game Design" (2007).** — free web essay,
   content-verified. Skill atoms to molecules. What it changed: build orders get written
   as atom lists a stranger can execute, which is this packet's own format.

10. **Blow, Experimental Gameplay Workshop 2005, parts 1–4.** — GDC Vault free listings.
    Design experiments to reveal the nature of the system. What it changed: nothing not
    already in falsifier law, but it is the same law spoken by builders — kept as
    pedigree, which is how cargo culting is prevented rather than committed.

## Electronics (the measurement shelf)

11. **Mancini (TI), *Op Amps For Everyone* (SLOD006B).** — TI's own mirror; TI retired
    the original literature path and the death is recorded in the fetch receipt. The one
    book that takes a non-specialist from "what is an op amp" to a working signal chain.
    What it changed: the instrumented-cube program's front end is specified from this
    book's chapters, single-supply reality included.

12. **Jung (ADI), *Op Amp Applications Handbook* + the MT-060/061/062 in-amp trio.** —
    free from the manufacturer (19 pages for the trio). What it changed: the differential
    front end that rejects the common-mode mush a moist biological chamber produces —
    the difference between measuring a fungus and measuring the room.

13. **Kuphaldt, *Lessons In Electric Circuits*, 6 volumes.** — open content (ibiblio.org).
    What it changed: the floor. Anyone on the fleet can be brought from zero to reading
    a schematic without a paid course, which keeps the hardware program stranger-executable.

14. **Keysight, *Evaluating Oscilloscope Sample Quality* (ENOB app note).** — freely
    distributed; fetched from a public mirror when the vendor 403'd, and the detour is
    recorded. What it changed: "effective number of bits" is the electronics version of
    our central law — advertised bits vs verified bits, claims vs bytes.

15. **OpenBCI Cyton board schematics.** — published reference build (docs.openbci.com).
    What it changed: the cube program stops guessing — a proven open instrument exists,
    and ours crib from it shamelessly and say so.

16. **Adafruit ADS1115 guide.** — free (learn.adafruit.com). What it changed: the honest
    cheap path — a 16-bit I2C ADC that replaces an entire discrete front end for a few
    dollars, documented for hobbyists, which keeps the $0-total honest (the dollar is in
    parts, not information).

## Mycology (the substrate shelf)

17. **Telhan et al. 2026, *Scientific Reports* (fungal electrophysiology).** — open
    access. What it changed: the anchor paper for measuring living substrate; the cube
    program's sampling rates and electrode practice trace to it.

18. **LaRocco et al. 2025, *PLOS ONE* (shiitake memristors).** — open access. What it
    changed: substrate-as-component — the living material treated as a circuit element
    with measured behavior, not a symbol.

19. **Karst et al., "Prioritize host–mycorrhizal fungus interaction studies…"
    (wood-wide-web critique), *Nature Ecology & Evolution*.** — the anti-cult paper.
    What it changed: the popular claim that forest fungal networks wire trees into a
    communicating superorganism outruns its evidence; the critique says so in print.
    It is in the library for the same reason the retraction of Adamatzky's "Fungal
    sensing skin" is tracked in our canon register: a fleet that studies substrates
    must also study its own enthusiasm. This paper is Law 3 (outside reader) in
    biology's own handwriting.

## Physics and the far shelf

20. **The moonshot anchors: Aharonov & Vaidman (two-state vector formalism),
    Kastner (transactional interpretation), Moussa et al. (time refraction, observed,
    *Nature Physics* 2023).** — all free on arXiv. Kept as a trio deliberately: two
    interpretations and one measurement. What it changed: the corpus practices what the
    packet preaches — interpretation may organize questions; only the measured paper
    gets to carry a verdict. Moussa is in because it is the one whose claim is a
    laboratory fact.

## The voice shelf (labeled as voice, never as evidence)

Esoteric/parable public-domain canon: Chesterton (*The Man Who Was Thursday*,
*Orthodoxy*), Carroll (*Alice*, *Looking-Glass*), Kafka (*Der Prozess*, German
original), Smith (*The Game of Go*, 1908), Falkener (*Games Ancient and Oriental*,
1892). These feed prompt registers — riddle cadence, mirror logic, institutional
absurdity, rules that outlive their players. They are marked as voice canon in the
manifest and are never cited as evidence for any claim. A corpus that does not label
its shelf purposes eventually cites a novel to a physicist.
