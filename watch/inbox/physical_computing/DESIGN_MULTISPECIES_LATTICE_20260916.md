## The translation table — silicon fab → myco fab

| Silicon fab | Your myco fab | Real today? |
|---|---|---|
| Wafer | spent-oat substrate block | yes — oats are a standard spawn grain |
| Doping (N-type / P-type) | species selection + nutrient traces | yes — species differ in spiking behavior; substrate formulation steers growth density |
| Lithography (pattern) | 3D-printed lattice geometry | yes — living mycelium 3D printing exists (Blast Studio prints living myco columns; academic myco-composite printing) |
| Implantation | inoculation points | yes — inoculate where the print says |
| Anneal | colonization (the block grows itself into the lattice) | yes — that IS the process |
| Device isolation | hyphal incompatibility: where two species meet they build dense zone lines | yes — real biology, and it is the interesting part |
| Adaptation | the substrate keeps living and rewiring | the claim to test, not assume |
| Yield | take rate | the risk |

The novel unit is **the zone line**: where two incompatible species meet, the block builds
a dense, charged boundary all by itself. In silicon you etch a junction; here the block
grows one. Hypothesis for cube 2: interaction zones spike differently than bulk.

## The ladder (falsifiers carried over unchanged — DAD, §4.2, §4.5 discipline)

- **Cube 1 (C17, $31 electronics first):** single species, grown, read. Prove the ear,
  prove fading memory, pass or auto-retreat. No multispecies confounds yet — attribution
  is the enemy and cube 1 keeps it dead.
- **Cube 2 — THE LATTICE:** printed two-species block (candidate pairing: a fast grower
  against a high-spiker, chosen from Adamatzky's species tables when the S1 lane lands).
  Electrode array placed AT the zone lines. Hypothesis: boundaries are the active regions.
  Geometry enforces species separation, so attribution survives without lab equipment.
- **Cube 3 — THE DIRAC LAYER:** the printed lattice carries fluid channels too. A block
  with three stacked substrates: hydraulic logic (the C18 water layer), the hyphal network,
  and the sensor grid — the mushroom/water computer as one grown artifact. This is your
  "mushroom/Dirac water computer" taken literally: spinor-parable geometry in the lattice
  design, water in the channels, mycelium in the walls.

## Honest walls

Multi-species multiplies confounds — which is why it is cube 2, not cube 1. Sterility
versus wild is an open fight (wild blocks carry the microbiome the FEMS checklist worries
about). Timescales are days-to-weeks per iteration; the fleet's trails law (append-only,
VOID-able sensor rows) is what makes slow experiments shippable. And the honest retreat
stays armed: if the lattice never outperforms bulk mycelium, it is art and habitat, and the
pack says so in advance.
