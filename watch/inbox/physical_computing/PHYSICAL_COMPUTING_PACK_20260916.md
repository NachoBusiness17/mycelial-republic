programmability/verification, not capacity; AI changes calibration economics;
pancomputationalism is a trap; analog composability is constrained; the viable architecture

measured full-system.

The two sharpest instruments produced:

- **DAD round-trip overhead test** (Node): instrument encode → physical step →
  analog-to-digital conversion → verify, end to end. Prediction: above ~10,000 variables the
  digital-analog-digital interface consumes >60% of time and energy, collapsing raw physical
  advantage to <10× full-system. The interface is where advantages go to die.
- **Correspondence cost C** (Grok): energy/time to encode, maintain the mapping, and decode a
  usable answer, divided by the native step cost. A substrate is interesting iff native-step
  × C still beats digital. Most press releases are constant-factor + structure-specific wins
  advertised as class change.

Load-bearing uncertainties: **ESS per Joule** on multi-modal distributions for thermodynamic
samplers (correlated samples = a fast machine outputting nothing); **embedding penalties**
for Ising machines (dense benchmark = native habitat; sparse industrial graphs = exile).

Preserved disagreements (do not average): Extropic is the most visible, not the most
measured; Z1 is sparse Ising/Gibbs silicon after compilation, not free-form analog;
"CIM 1000×" is not transferable; thermodynamic computing names at least two substrates
(analog/mixed-signal p-circuits vs digital stochastic CMOS); vocabulary unresolved —
Node abandons "substrate-native" for **Dynamics-Engineered Computing**, Grok keeps
"substrate-native kernels." Both live in this pack under their own names.
