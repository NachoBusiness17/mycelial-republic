## Lane 1 — Living building materials with sensing

### 1a. Bacteria-based self-healing concrete (the lineage: TU Delft / Jonkers → Basilisk BV)
- **Demonstrated:** concrete with embedded dormant *Bacillus* spores (B. pseudofirmus, B. cohnii, B. alkalinitrilicus) + calcium lactate nutrients; water ingress → spores germinate → microbially precipitated limestone (CaCO₃) seals cracks.
- **Numbers:** 0.1 mm cracks fully healed within ~200 h; 0.2–0.3 mm cracks healed within ~30 days; up to **0.8 mm** proven at higher healing-agent dosage (lab). Sources: [Amran et al. 2022 review (PMC9106089)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9106089/), [TU Delft, bacteria-based self-healing concrete](https://research.tudelft.nl/files/116911153/document.pdf), [MPI Bremen](https://www.mpi-bremen.de/en/Self-healing-concrete.html), [patent US8460458B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US8460458B2/en).
- **Living at test time:** bacteria spores dormant-alive inside the matrix (biologically "living" but NOT metabolically active until wetted). **No sensing modality** — the organism is an actuator (mineralizer), not a sensor. No electrical readout; detection of cracking is visual/manual.
- **Scale:** commercial (Basilisk Healing Agent ER7 revive pellets, liquid; field use in NL infrastructure). The largest-scale "living building material" that exists.

### 1b. Mycelium-based insulation (thermal) — industry + academic
- **Demonstrated:** grown mycelium-bound composite panels (hemp/straw + fungal mycelium) as thermal/acoustic insulation; commercial players Mykor (MykoFoam), Grown.bio, Mogu, BIOHM, Ecovative; academic hygrothermal characterizations.
- **Numbers (representative):** thermal conductivity λ ≈ 0.04–0.07 W/m·K typical of MBC insulation panels; moisture buffering / coupled hygrothermal behavior characterized (MDPI Energies 2025, "Microstructure-Driven Hygrothermal Behavior of Mycelium-Based Composites", [mdpi.com/1996-1073/18/11/2864](https://www.mdpi.com/1996-1073/18/11/2864)).
- **Embedded monitoring found so far:** searches for "mycelium insulation panel embedded sensor humidity monitoring" surface **NO commercial or academic panel with integrated sensors** — closest is post-hoc hygrothermal characterization, i.e., the sensing is done BY instruments ON the panel, not BY the panel. Sources: [ResearchGate 2025](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/392545248_Development_of_a_Mycelium-Based_Thermal_Insulation_Material), [PMC12954402](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12954402/).
- **Living at test time:** mycelium is typically heat-killed/dried at end of growth for stable panels (inert binder); some research keeps it alive but then life shortens panel life.
- **Scale:** industrial pilots (panels, acoustic tiles); EU ENTRANZE assessments for renovation market.

### 1c. ELM — Engineered Living Materials reviews 2024–2026 + programs

## Lane 2 — Embedded electronics in bio-composites

### 2a. Mycoelectronics — bioprinted living fungal bioelectronics for artificial sensation **(NEAREST NEIGHBOR #1)**
- **Source:** *PNAS* (2026), [doi:10.1073/pnas.2530456123](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2530456123); preprint [bioRxiv 10.1101/2025.10.06.680560](https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.10.06.680560).
- **Demonstrated:** additive **bioprinting of LIVING fungal mycelium networks ONTO stretchable electronics** → living thermo-responsive sensory platform. Mechanism discovered: heat-induced **vacuole remodeling/fusion modulates ionic transport → electrical conductivity change** of the mycelial network = the sensing signal. Living mycelium **self-heals** sensing function after damage and autonomously re-establishes sensor pathways in hard-to-reach locations. Hybrid "bioelectronic reflex arc": fungal thermal sensing + electronic circuits actuates muscles (neurorobotics demo).
- **Numbers:** abstract-level only (response speed "rapid"; no N/mV numbers in abstract).
- **Living at test time:** **YES — actively living mycelium** with intrinsic cellular mechanism.
- **Architecture vs OUR design:** electronics are a **stretchable 2D substrate printed UNDER the mycelium** (planar interface); material is a thin printable film, not a structural block; sensing modality = **temperature only**; application = wearables/robot skin. No metal lattice volume-distributed inside a printed block; no construction scale.

### 2b. Biovegan Leather Sensor — mycelium + polypyrrole dry electrodes (ACS AMI 2025)
- **Source:** [doi:10.1021/acsami.5c05377](https://doi.org/10.1021/acsami.5c05377).
- **Demonstrated:** mycelium-leather (grown, then processed) functionalized with polypyrrole → flexible dry electrode patches for human ECG/EMG/EEG.
- **Numbers:** tensile strength 3 MPa, elongation 13%, **conductivity 51.10 S/m**, electrode-skin interface impedance **52.6 kΩ/cm² @10 Hz**; signal quality on par with commercial electrodes.
- **Living at test time:** NO — dead mycelium leather (processed composite). Electrode is made OF mycelium composite, not embedded in a living block. Wearable patch scale.

### 2f. ETH Zurich / Chatzi lab (SMM) — living mycelium block + electrical readout for structural monitoring (**NEAREST NEIGHBOR #2 — the one to beat**)
A three-paper arc, 2024→2026, inside ETH's ALIVE/CLiMa program:
1. **"Fungal circuitry: mycelium as a living sensor for smart structures"** — Ganzeboom, Zhao, Dertimanis, Chatzi, *Proc. SPIE* 12949 (2024), [doi:10.1117/12.3010795](https://doi.org/10.1117/12.3010795). Feasibility: living mycelium as intrinsic biosensing material for SHM in buildings; electrical **potential** measurements of mycelium (early agar-stage work).
2. **"Spatial and temporal characterization of living mycelium through electrical impedance"** — [arXiv:2505.10897](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.10897) → *Smart Materials and Structures* ([10.1088/1361-665X/ae236a](https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-665X/ae236a)); ETH news: "Living Mycelium as an Actively Probed Sensing Medium" (Jan 2026).
   - **Material:** *Pleurotus ostreatus* on Grown.bio spawn-bag substrate (+flour 30 g/kg), grown 4 days in a **30 × 40 cm mold** until uniform white skin — a grown **block/panel**, not agar.
   - **Electrodes:** **1 input pair + 16 output pairs** of **iridium-coated stainless-steel subdermal NEEDLE probes inserted into the specimen after growth**; output pairs at ~2.5 cm spacing, repeated across 12 directions (0°–330° in 30° steps); 4× Digilent ADP3450 scopes, 1 MΩ input, 14-bit, 6.25 MSa/s.
   - **Modality/numbers:** broadband 1 Hz–500 kHz sweeps; RC-network + inhomogeneous-wave-correlation model → per-unit-length **R = 98 ± 6.5 kΩ**, C₁ = 0.94 µF, C₂ = 7.6 nF, G = 8.8×10⁻³ S; attenuation ≈ **−174 dB per unit length at 50 kHz**; macro-scale isotropy (SD < mean across directions).
   - **Living at test time:** **YES — and they prove it**: microwave-dehydrated samples go quiet (low ionic mobility), **rehydrated samples recover** the response → conduction is ionic/water-dependent; humidity is critical for sensing.
   - **Scale:** panel-size coupons; ambition = buildings/infrastructure SHM.
3. **"Monitoring using living materials: mycelium-based sensing of structures"** — Haywood-Alexander, Dertimanis, Zhao, Chatzi, *Proc. SPIE* 13944 (2026), [doi:10.1117/12.3090953](https://doi.org/10.1117/12.3090953) — the line continues toward living-material SHM.
- **Architecture vs OUR design:** living mycelium + electrical readout + structural application **all present**, but electrodes are **post-growth needle probes** (temporary, invasive, arbitrary geometry), substrate is a **grown bag/mold block (not a printed block)**, and there is **no co-fabricated, designed metal lattice** inside the volume.

### 2g. Mycelium-bound composite + live electrical spike readout (Adamatzky line — the sensing physics on the actual building-material class)
- **"Electrical response of fungi to changing moisture content"** — Phillips, Gandia, Adamatzky, *Fungal Biology and Biotechnology* (2023), [10.1186/s40694-023-00155-0](https://doi.org/10.1186/s40694-023-00155-0).
- **Demonstrated:** on **mycelium-bound composites (the building-panel material class itself)**: spontaneous spike trains at moisture ≈95–65% (fresh) and ≈15–5% (partially dried); spike response to water droplets on surface; occluding the surface with an impermeable layer **increases** electrical activity; **electrode depth in the block changes the signal** (explicit electrode-position sensitivity).
- **Numbers:** moisture windows above; spike trains (Hz-scale per abstract's framing; no mV tables in abstract).
- **Living at test time:** YES (live mycelium in MBC). **Scale:** lab coupons. No printed block, no embedded lattice — electrodes inserted at varied depths; readout = spike trains, not continuous structured impedance.
- Explicitly proposes "active building components, sensory wearables" as the application — the intent, again, without the architecture.

## VERDICT — nearest prior art for OUR design (printed block + embedded metal lattice + living mycelium + electrical readout)

**Does the exact combination exist anywhere found? NO.** Across living-building-materials industry (Basilisk, bioMASON, Mykor/Grown.bio/Mogu/BIOHM insulation), federal programs (DARPA ELM complete; NSF BMAT; ETH ALIVE/CLiMa), and the academic frontier (ETH Chatzi, Adamatzky, PNAS Mycoelectronics, FUNGAR), every element exists separately, but no work co-fabricates a **volume-distributed metal electrode lattice inside a printed mycelium block that stays living**, with continuous electrical readout. Corroboration from the field's own mouths: the 2026 fungal-ELM taxonomy lists **bio-digital integration** as an open challenge, and FUNGAR's final report states full monolithic construction of the living substrate **remains to be achieved**.

**Nearest neighbors, ranked:**
1. **ETH Chatzi / SMM line (2024–2026)** — living *P. ostreatus* block + 17-pair needle-probe impedance readout for SHM (R ≈ 98 kΩ/unit-length, −174 dB/unit @50 kHz, ionic/humidity-bound conduction proven). Same physics, same ambition — but grown bag substrate, **temporary post-growth needle electrodes, no printing, no designed lattice**.
2. **FUNGAR (2019–2023)** — explicitly "fully integrated structural and computational living substrate using fungal mycelium"; delivered components (memristors, oscillators, sensors) + a large woven-and-colonised demonstrator, **not** a monolithic instrumented block.
3. **Mycoelectronics (PNAS 2026)** — printing meets living mycelium meets electronics, but planar (print ON electronics), thermal-only sensing, robot-skin scale.
4. **Gyroid printed MBC (Nat Commun 2025)** — printed scaffold + mycelium at infra-structural performance, zero electronics.

**What OUR design adds beyond nearest neighbor #1:**
- **Co-fabricated, deterministic 3D metal electrode geometry** — lattice printed INTO the block during fabrication and grown through by the mycelium, instead of hand-inserted needle arrays. That buys: (a) reproducibility across specimens (ETH's needle placement is ad hoc per specimen — their isotropy/homogeneity work is exactly the reproducibility fight); (b) permanent instrumented infrastructure (needles are lab artifacts); (c) true 3D spatial sampling of the interior, not trans-surface paths.
- **Printed block form factor** — merges the gyroid line (printed scaffold geometry controls mechanical/thermal performance AND growth) with the ETH line (living electrical readout). Neither published work merges them.
- **Living-state readout from day zero** — growth, colonization, and maturation become measurable in-situ (cf. FUNGAR's components-only outcome and the taxonomy's "gradients of aliveness" — our block can report which aliveness gradient it is on).
- **Multispecies zoning** (per `../DESIGN_MULTISPECIES_LATTICE_20260916.md`, beyond K11 scope) — no found work electrodes across species zone lines inside one living block.

**Physics cautions inherited from the evidence (input to K12/conducted-block):**
- Conduction is **ionic and humidity-bound**: ETH proved dehydrated mycelium goes electrically quiet and rehydrates back; Adamatzky mapped moisture windows (95–65% fresh, 15–5% dry). Moisture must be a co-variable or the readout confounds.
