### STEP 4 — DID ANYONE ALREADY DO "FEED NETWORKS IN / READ THEM OUT"? (YES — Adamatzky school + others)

**THE definitive answer: the Unconventional Computing Lab line (Andrew Adamatzky, UWE Bristol, with Chiolerio et al.) has already done the operator's idea, repeatedly, in living mycelium:**

1. **"Electrical frequency discrimination by fungi *Pleurotus ostreatus*"** — Przyczyna, Szacilowski, Chiolerio, Adamatzky (arXiv:2210.01775, 2022): **stimulated living mycelial networks with low-frequency sinusoidal inputs; networks discriminate frequencies in a fuzzy/threshold manner; frequency mixing characterized.** Literal signal-in → signal-out through living mycelium.
2. **"Propagation of electrical signals by fungi"** — Mayne, Roberts, Phillips, Weerasekera, Adamatzky (arXiv:2304.10675, 2023): mycelium-bound composites **transmit frequency-modulated info 100–10,000 Hz with recoverable frequency**; amplitude not reproducible; NARX-derived approximate transfer function. Memristor + capacitor + sensor properties.
3. **"Mining logical circuits in fungi"** — Roberts & Adamatzky (arXiv:2108.05336, 2021): **many-input Boolean functions implemented in living *P. ostreatus* mycelium-bound composites** — inputs as amplitude/frequency of electrical stimuli, outputs decoded from responses. Explicit design intent: *"Presently the fungal materials are passive. To make the fungal materials adaptive, i.e. sensing and computing, we should embed logical circuits into them."* — that is OUR thesis stated verbatim in 2021.
4. **"Fungal electronics"** (the field name) — Adamatzky, Ayres, Beasley, Chiolerio, Dehshibi, Gandia, Albergati, Mayne, Nikolaidou, Roberts, Tegelaar, Tsompanas, Phillips, Wösten (arXiv:2111.11231, 2021): "a family of living electronic devices made of mycelium bound composites or pure mycelium… changing impedance and generating spikes… embedded into fungal materials and wearables or used as stand-alone sensing and computing devices."
5. **"On electrical gates on fungal colony"** — Beasley, Ayres, Tegelaar, Tsompanas, Adamatzky (arXiv:2107.00133, 2021): states the long-term objective of *"prototyping electrical analog computers from living mycelium networks, **including networks hybridised with nanoparticles**"* — metal-nanoparticle-hybridized living mycelium is already on their roadmap.
6. **"On Boolean gates in fungal colony"** (arXiv 2020), **"Computational universality of fungal sandpile automata"** (2020) — theory layer.

**Adjacent instrumental prior art:**
- **Buffi et al., iScience (2025)** (PMC12483595, Neuchâtel + Los Alamos + ETH): **printed-circuit "fungal potential cards" — ENIG (gold) differential electrode pairs, 2 mm electrodes, 1 cm spacing** — living *Fusarium oxysporum* bridges electrode pairs on the PCB; Pico ADC-24 ~17 Hz; Faraday cage + humidity chamber; STFT/PSD analysis (colonized vs control: ~+1604% PSD, p<0.001; biological-origin verified via biocide perturbation). **This is a ready-made methodological blueprint for our copper/silver-lattice-in-printed-block.** Does NOT cite Stamets.
- **Ecovative patent US9253889B2** (Bayer & McIntyre, granted 2016, "Method of Growing Electrically Conductive Tissue", exp. 2034): mycelium sheets grown in metal-salt medium (CuSO4/CuCl2) so cell walls biosorb metal → resistive sheets, then KILLED/dried, wiring pattern cut in. **Patent fence covers DEAD-mycelium passive circuit sheets — not living sensing substrates, not embedded live electrodes.** Our living-lattice design stays outside it, but the copper-in-mycelium-biosorption trick itself is published/claimed — we can't patent the biosorption concept, only novel living architectures.
- Cornell biohybrid robot line (Mishra/Shepherd, 2024, media-covered): fungal electrical signals used as robot controller — checking details next.
- Known critique for balance: Blatt et al. line questioning whether fungal spiking constitutes "language" (cited by Buffi et al.) — spiking is real; "language/intelligence" interpretation is contested. Checking next.

## STEALABLES for the copper/silver-lattice-in-printed-block design

- **Electrode geometry (from Buffi):** differential pairs, ~2 mm electrode width, ~1 cm spacing is the proven sweet spot; nickel/gold (ENIG) survives the biology — **copper must be plated/masked (ENIG or immersion silver) or it corrodes and poisons the culture**; avoid agar (abiotic electrochemical artifacts) — grow on our block medium.
- **Noise discipline (from Buffi + Cornell):** Faraday cage / EM + vibration filtering, shielded cabling, humidity chamber, sampling ≥17 Hz — microvolt regime for field potentials, mV regime possible in some species/stimuli (Fukasawa).
- **Analysis (from Buffi + Adamatzky):** skip spike-counting first; go STFT/PSD in frequency domain (colony signal appears >1.5 Hz vs <1 Hz background); NARX/transfer-function modeling for input→output characterization; treat amplitude as non-reproducible, frequency as the recoverable carrier.
- **Stimulus→response charter (from Adamatzky corpus):** valid I/O regime is 100–10,000 Hz; frequency discrimination and Boolean function mapping are demonstrated — design the lattice so branches form testable gate topologies (they modeled real colonies as RC graphs; our printed lattice can be the DESIGNED RC graph).
- **Metal role split (from Ecovative + TU Delft):** metal-salt biosorption during growth (CuSO4) lowers resistivity — could dope regions of the block; carbon-black/ITO comparison showed functionalization raises SNR 2.7×/amplitude 9× → our copper/silver lattice may serve BOTH as electrodes and as conductivity-tuning filler.
- **Novelty positioning (unclaimed as of this sweep):** structural printed block where the metal lattice IS the electrode network pre-placed before/with inoculation, hyphae colonizing THROUGH the lattice — vs. prior art's pads-on-surface (Buffi, TU Delft) or electronics-under-skin (Mycoelectronics). Frame as "designed RC topology + embedded lattice interface", cite Stamets for language only.
- **De-risk framing:** expect Blatt-style skepticism; validate biological origin via biocide/wetting perturbation exactly as Buffi did; measure, don't say "language".
