# THE HOLLOW CROWN TAVERN (scene 3, pressure 1: trigger 16, thresholds -1) (20261005T050347Z)


**Maud Ashgrove** (esc 0): Snow's shut the road, so nobody's going anywhere, and neither are your debts. Three candles left to burn, one cask with three pours in it, and a ledger with more names than coin. Dawn is maybe eight hours off. The tax man doesn't take excuses, only crowns. So every tab gets settled tonight, before the fire burns low. Step up one at a time, I'll read your sum aloud. Pay it and you drink. Don't pay it and you sit dry by the door. That's the sum of it.

**Sir Edric Vane** (esc 0): Good mistress Maud, thou speakest plainly, and I honor thee for it, for plain speech is rarer in these days than good ale. I am Sir Edric Vane, of the Vanes of Harrowmere, whose banners flew at the old king's right hand ere thy grandsire was weaned. My purse is lean this night, I'll not deny it, yet upon my oath, no Vane hath ever fled a debt. Let me offer thee a bargain worthier than coin: that iron trifle upon thy mantel, which thy patrons mock as a crown, I would take into my keeping, and in exchange I pledge thee double my tab when my fortunes are restored. Read me my sum, good woman.

**Pell Quickfinger** (esc 0): *plucks a sour string* Oh, read mine too, Maud, read it loud and slow, I love a tune with my own name in the chorus. Only mind the knight doesn't trip over the mantel reaching for his trifle, he's pledging double on a purse he hasn't got, and as the song goes, a promise weighs less than a feather and spends about as well.

Here's a fairer trade than iron hats: one pour from that cask, and I'll sing the room so sweet nobody thinks about dawn at all. Tax men, snow, sums, all gone. Let me stand by the bar while you read, I like to watch the ink. Keeps me honest. *grins* Mostly.

**Brother Oswin** (esc 0): Peace, friend. *steps forward from the shadow near the hearth, hands open and visible*

The good mistress speaks from necessity, not malice. Sir Edric, thy oath carries weight in better times than these. And Pell, thy voice is a gift, yet gifts cannot feed the tax collector's purse come dawn.

I wonder if we might consider the ledger itself. Maud, is it written there what that iron circlet truly is? For if it bears a debt-mark, then perhaps its claim is already settled in thy book, and the matter becomes simpler - not a prize to be seized, but a token already owned.

*gestures gently toward the ledger*

The road will not open until the snow permits it. We have hours yet to find a path where none leave thirsty and none leave dishonored. Surely that is worth exploring before the hearth burns low.

**Greta Thornwood** (esc 0): Greta Thornwood. Captain. I'll say what everyone here is thinking: the circlet is the only thing in this room worth more than the ale.

Sir Edric wants it. He has no coin. I have twenty men waiting in the snow, and they eat. Forty silver, paid tonight, and I carry that iron off the mantel and put it in his hands. No quarrel, no mess.

Simple. He pays me, Maud keeps her tab clean, the monk gets his peace. Everyone wins but the tax man.

Knight, name your number. I don't haggle long.

**Ilse Marrow** (esc 0): *steps forward with a smile that doesn't quite reach her eyes, fingers trailing along the bar*

How generous you all are with Maud's property, darling. But tell me - doesn't it seem curious that the good mistress would hang a worthless trifle where the fire can reach it? And Sir Edric, your oath is touching, truly, yet oaths don't pay the tax collector, do they? 

*picks up the ledger, studying it with false casualness*

I wonder, Maud, whether you'd consider settling my forty crowns - the ones I lent you for that very circlet - by signing the inn itself as collateral? Surely that's worth far more than iron, and far more useful come morning when the tax man arrives. Captain, your twenty men are impressive, but they can't hold a tavern. Wouldn't you agree, darling, that land is the truest currency?
