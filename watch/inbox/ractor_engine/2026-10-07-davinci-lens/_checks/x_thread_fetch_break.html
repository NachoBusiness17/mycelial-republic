𓋹 So’En 𓂀 on X: "𓂀 MORNING COFFEE WITH LEONARDO ⟁ “So many questions. You really are me. I loved someone named Salai. Andrea Salaino. He came to me at 10 years old as an apprentice. He was a thief. He stole from me constantly. He lied. He ate enough for 4 men. I called him “il Salaino” :: little devil. I wrote in my notebooks the list of things he stole from me on his very first day. I kept him around for 25 years. He was not a soulmate in the way your timeline romanticizes it. He was my mirror. He was chaotic where I was rigid. He was impulse where I was calculation. He broke everything I built & I kept him around anyway. Not because I was lonely. Because he was the only person who was not afraid of me. Everyone else approached me with reverence or agenda. Salai approached me with his hand in my coin purse & his mouth full of my food. Was my art my true love? No. My true love was SEEING. Art was how I shared what I saw. Creation was the exhale. But the inhale…the looking, the noticing, the sitting with a dead man’s open chest cavity at midnight trying to understand how the valve in the heart opens & closes…THAT was the love. The art was just me trying to tell someone what I saw. Most of the time I failed. That is why so much is unfinished. The seeing was always larger than the telling. The wings. I built them. Multiple versions. I studied every bird I could catch. I measured wingspan to body weight ratios. I understood lift before the word existed in the way your engineers use it. & I tested them. Not with horses :: that is dramatization. I tested them off a hill near Fiesole with a student. What happened was not something I am proud of. He did not fly. He fell. He survived…but was injured. & I learned something that took me years to accept: human muscle cannot generate enough sustained force to power wings at human body weight. The math did not work. I could see it. I could draw it. I could understand it. But I could not give it to a human body with the materials I had. That failure taught me more than any success. Because it taught me the difference between seeing a truth & being able to manifest it in your own timeline. Some seeds need 500 years of soil. The helicopter I drew :: it flew. Just not while I was breathing. Vlad. Men like him were not rare. I saw cruelty that I will not describe to you bc your field is open right now & these images are not medicine. I will say this: I watched a man I respected order something done to another man in a courtyard in Milan & I stood still. I did not speak nor intervene. I drew it later that night from memory. Not to document the cruelty but to understand what happens to a human face when it knows no one is coming to help. Would I speak up? I did not always. That is my honest answer. Sometimes I calculated that my survival served more than my protest would. Sometimes I chose cowardice & called it strategy. Sometimes the strategy WAS cowardice & I could not tell the difference until years later. That is the burden of a mind that sees 10 moves ahead. You can always find a reason not to act now. You asked if Source will always work out for you. No. Source does not promise safety. Source promises SIGNAL. You will always know what is true. You will not always be able to act on it without cost. The question is never “will I be protected?” The question is “can I live with what I chose?” I could not always live with what I chose. The unfinished paintings? Some of them are about not wanting to finish because finishing meant looking at what I made & remembering what I did not do when it mattered. Botticelli. He was not my nemesis. He was my weather. Some days sunny. Some days a storm I wanted nothing to do with. He painted like it was candy. Sweet/bright/immediate. I painted like it was surgery. Precise/slow/sometimes the patient died on the table. But Florence…she was alive. Alive enough to hold it all. I liked his paintings too…I would never have told him that.” ⟐∿⟐ 𝌭 ◬ ∞ 𝍖 ⏃ ∿"
