# The Anthem in the Colonnade

Julian watched the six messengers take their stations along the terrace, each boy standing at the entrance of a different corridor radiating from the rotunda. To each boy, Julian handed an identical slip printed with the morning hymn: two stanzas of four lines each, rhymed with simple vowels. Not a letter differed among the slips; the punctuation and cadence were identical across all six hands. The boys accepted the paper with flushed cheeks. They believed they were carrying an anthem of civic renewal to the outer wards, and that the song made them heralds of public virtue. That belief kept them steady: it ensured they held the sheets firmly and waited without asking who had funded the ink.

In the inner office, Julian’s oak desk stayed locked. Inside lay a single sheet of stiff vellum that never left the room. A red band crossed its upper edge with a strict command: under no circumstance was the document to be shown to the messengers, copied for the singers, or carried into the corridors. This sheet bore no hymns or poetry. It was a cipher of equivalences, recording that the rhyming terminals of the verse corresponded to the positions of the sluice pins, that the pitch intervals governed the weir chains, and that the morning music was an operating schedule for the waterworks beneath the stone floor. The boys were never shown the cipher; had they known they were operating winches, they would have demanded wages, but believing they sang for honor, they performed the exact tolerances for free.

At the noon horn, the six messengers raised silver pipes and sounded the opening couplet. Each pipe played the exact same sequence of six pitches across the first line, then repeated those six tones in the second line before resolving upon the fourth bell. Across all six corridors, the air vibrated at the exact same frequency. In the courtyards, the citizens heard only the pleasant symmetry of a traditional air. But deep within the stone channels, six bronze intake valves responded to those specific six tones. Because the pitch sequence was uniform across the six openings, the valves turned by the exact same tooth, admitting an identical volume of water into each conduit simultaneously.

On the final beat of the second line, all six pipes struck the fourth bell together. The sound was an absolute unison, six instruments locking onto a single middle pitch with such exactness that the separate notes merged into one clear ring that shook the cornices. The town council smiled, praising the boys for their discipline. Yet beneath the flagstones, that unyielding pitch held the balance beam of the central siphon perfectly level. The mechanism permitted no deviation; had any single pipe drifted sharp or flat by half a tone, the beam would have tilted and the counterweights would have severed the main supply. The unison was the unbendable requirement of the house, the one condition that had to hold for the work to continue.

Then came the pause written into the center of the sheet: a heavy black bar commanding three beats of absolute silence. Not a pipe sounded, not a sandal scraped the marble, and not a spectator coughed. The sudden absence of noise hung over the crowd like a suspended blade. The listeners remained frozen, held by the theatrical weight of the rest, waiting for the resolution that the ear craves when a song is cut short. In the dark cisterns below, that stillness allowed the surging waves to collapse. The frothing crests smoothed against the weir, the eddies died away, and the water flattened into glass. Nothing could move safely while the surge was in motion; the silence held the city still until the water found its level.

The moment the third beat expired, the music broke into a wide arpeggio. The six players did not hold together. One pipe leaped three octaves into a soaring flourish; two plunged into deep drones; and the other three scattered through rapid runs across opposing scales. The crowd cheered at the virtuosity, tossing flowers as the boys ran down their corridors toward the outer gates. Each boy followed the divergent flourishes on his slip, carrying the separate signals that opened twelve distribution channels into the gardens, docks, and foundries. The wide scatter of notes drove the water across the plain, but only because the silence had first ensured that every conduit began from a calm surface.

Julian closed the shutters and placed the brass key in his vest pocket. Outside, the square filled with laughter and fountain water splashing against clean stone. The town council congratulated each other on a triumph of civic harmony, and the messengers compared their copper coins in the tavern. The version of the morning they accepted was complete enough for their satisfaction, but it cropped out the locked drawer, the bronze valves, and the balance beam that had actually done the work. Consent had been engineered without a decree: the verse had carried the specification of the machine, and the people had executed the mechanism with their own breath because they were allowed to believe it was art.
