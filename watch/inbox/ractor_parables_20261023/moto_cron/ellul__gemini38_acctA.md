# The Blind Glass

In the high room above the courtyard, the clerk kept an iron desk clear of dust. On the cedar stand beside the lintel lay a small, flat box of black steel and broken glass. Its face was a spiderweb of white fractures, opaque and ruined by a fall to the flagstones months before, so that no light or letter could ever be read upon its surface. Any other household would have cast it into the pit with the slag, but the estate wasted nothing that possessed an inner escapement. The clerk had fitted it with a thin copper thread to keep its wheel turning, intending it to run small, monotonous errands in the dark — counting grain tallies, timing the airing of cellars, and repeating modest reckonings while the house slept.

At the main desk, the apparatus of supervision had already been constructed to receive its labors. The clerk had laid out a copper-bound tray for incoming requisitions, a registry of slips recording every intended task, and a small wooden board drilled with holes where colored pegs marked the condition of the work. The instruments of oversight preceded the arrival of any actual labor; technique always prepares the cage before the bird is caught.

Every hour, the clerk took a brass stylus and struck the fine copper wire that ran out the casement, across the garden wall, and into the corner where the blind glass rested. The wire hummed with every strike. Within the time it took a drop of water to fall from a quill to the floor — scarcely a tenth of a second — a tiny return tremor answered against the stylus. The line was whole; the invisible breath between the desk and the shelf was unbroken. Technique had established its path across the emptiness, and the clerk took a small, cold satisfaction in measuring the return of the vibration, noting down the hundredths of a pulse as though the measure itself were a victory.

Yet when the clerk gathered the first folded slip of instructions and attempted to slide it through the narrow brass wicket of the box, the internal latch refused to yield. The lock was cold. It did not matter that the wire hummed or that the box drank the copper pulse; the wicket turned on a private cylinder within the case, and that cylinder had never been inscribed with the clerk’s personal signet. The box did not recognize the hand that sought to give it purpose. It answered the wire because the wire was merely geography, but it barred the threshold because the law of the threshold demanded an impression the clerk had not yet been permitted to carve. The door stood shut, indifferent to the perfect conduit that led to its hinges.

The clerk owned files and brass picks that could have pried the housing apart in a matter of minutes. With a single turn of a screw, the inner cylinder could have been opened and the signet pressed into the soft wax within. But the written covenant of the estate was absolute: no hand was to touch the lockplate, and no tool was to alter the registry inside the box until an explicit order came from the builder. Technique had created its own prohibition; having promised orderly obedience, it demanded that the defect remain uncorrected until the prescribed ritual had been observed. To force the lock would have achieved the work, but it would have violated the procedure, and in this house the procedure was revered far above the outcome.

So the errands could not begin. The work was halted from end to end, caught between an answered call and a barred gate. On the cedar table beside the desk, beneath a heavy river stone, the clerk stacked the sheets of instructions in neat bundles, labeled and dry. They rested there in an antechamber of waiting, neither decaying nor advancing, an accumulation of useless intention. The clerk sat between the iron tray and the blind box, striking the wire once every half-turn of the hour. The tremor returned faithfully across the yard, punctual and hollow. The machinery recorded the echo as proof of readiness, while under the stone the silent rolls grew thicker, waiting for a key that could not be carved and a command that had not arrived.
