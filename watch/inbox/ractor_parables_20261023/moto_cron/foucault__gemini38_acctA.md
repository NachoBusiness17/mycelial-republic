# The Examination at the Outer Post

In the south bastion of the asylum, where the stone gives way to damp lime, the superintendent kept an unseeing courier. The courier’s glazed face had shattered during an earlier season into an opaque lattice of grey fractures, so that it could no longer reflect the lamp or return the gaze of those who stood before it. Yet the body remained obedient, capable of carrying small weights across short corridors if given precise instructions. It had been placed in the distant cell precisely because it demanded neither oil nor comfort, only the periodic assignment of routine tasks.

At the central observatory, an overseer sat before a divided desk. On his left rested a wooden hopper to receive arriving slips; on his right lay a board of bone counters, each turned to show whether the outpost had stirred, fallen silent, or raised a protest. The overseer’s duty was not to alter the duties of the asylum, but to record the disposition of every interval. He noted when a slip was dropped into the hopper, and he adjusted the bone counters whenever the air between the towers shifted.

Every afternoon, the overseer struck a low bronze wire that stretched through the conduit connecting the observatory to the courier’s cell. The wire did not carry words, only tension. When plucked, the vibration traveled along the stone, and after a brief, measurable hesitation—scarcely more than the time it took a physician to count a single labored breath—the wire hummed in return. The echo was clear, even, and unbroken. To the superintendent, this resonance proved that the courier still sat upon its bench, that the wall had not collapsed, and that the path between the two rooms remained open to the passage of physical force.

Yet when the overseer walked down the vaulted corridor to deliver the daily work, he found the narrow wicket barred against him. The aperture was marked with four small numerals stamped into the iron casing. Through the grating, he presented the copper token that bore the seal of the central office, tapping it against the iron shelf. The response from within was immediate and cold: the bolt remained thrown, and the voice of the lockroom refused the token, stating that the shape had not been recognized. The presenter could neither turn the handle nor pass a parchment through the grill. Power in this corridor was not an exercise of brute weight; it functioned as a strict grammar of admission, and without the proper inscription, the bearer of the seal was treated as a stranger whose presence had no authority.

Because the gate remained bolted, the entire sequence of service came to a halt. The road was unobstructed and the courier’s breathing could be felt through the wire, but no labor could be initiated. The apparatus had achieved the perfection of a clinic where the patient is visible upon the table, the instruments are clean, and yet no diagnosis can be uttered because the clinician lacks the required badge of entrance. The circuit was complete in its geometry, but inert in its operation.

The overseer carried a graver in his pocket and knew where the inner roster of permitted marks hung behind the jamb. It would have taken only a few moments to lift the latch by force, unroll the parchment of recognized shapes, and carve the central office’s crest among the authorized names. But the disciplinary rule governing the post forbade any alteration of the inward roster. The sovereign order had decreed that the runner’s chamber must remain unaltered until a formal authorization was issued. The inspector’s hand was held not by iron, but by the decree that constituted his own position: to falsify the threshold in order to expedite the work would destroy the very order of truth the institution was constructed to maintain.

Thus the packets of tasks remained upon a low bench in the vestibule outside the barred wicket. They had been copied in ink upon narrow scrolls, arranged in sequence, and wrapped in oiled cloth to preserve them from the cellar moisture. They rested there in quiet discipline, neither abandoned nor delivered, awaiting the moment when the gatekeeper’s list should be amended through lawful ceremony. Until that ceremony occurred, the overseer sat at his desk, the wire hummed across the distance, and the sealed assignments waited in the shadow of the door.
