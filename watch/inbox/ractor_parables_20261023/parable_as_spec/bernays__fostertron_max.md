# What the Children Sang

Maren wrote the craft of the water-year plainly first, on a single page: the iron ring set in the bridge-stone, and what to do when the water touched it — the oars carried home, the boats lashed high above the second stair, the low dock left to the river, nobody waiting for anybody. She sent the page up the valley with the mail-boat. At the narrows the clerk read everything that looked like instruction and locked it in his chest; what he waved through were saints' cards, love letters, and songs. That autumn, at the fair, she found her own page folded around a cheese.

She had stopped wasting postage. She knew by now what people kept and what they burned: they kept what they could sing. An order needs a courier; a tune walks on its own legs, and no guard suspects what children carry. So she drowned the page in a skipping-round and taught it on the wharf to the children, for the price of a honey-cake each. There was a ring that learned to swim; there were oars that went home on shoulders; there were boats tied high where the swallows nested; there was an old dock given to the river like a worn-out coat; and in the last verse, a green boat that would come up from the magistrate's landing at ring-water and carry off anyone who waited.

The green boat had never existed. Maren had set that verse in the round herself, the way a jeweler sets a false stone among true ones.

By harvest the song had walked up both valleys without her name on it. Nobody was told what it was, and nobody asked, because it arrived as a toy.

The water rose to the ring on a Tuesday in March, in the ordinary way. Afterwards a drover described a town two valleys over that Maren had never written to: at ring-water they took their oars out and carried them home, lashed their hulls high above the second stair, and stood on the high road with their arms folded while the low dock went downstream. She laid his account beside her page, act for act, and found every step present and in order. The hiding had cost nothing.

Two towns got it wrong, and differently. The first waited at the landing for the green boat until the water was at their knees, then wrote her an angry letter signed by the whole council, saying a boat had been promised. It had — by her, falsely, in a verse they had sung for two springs without once walking to the water to see whether any magistrate's landing had ever been built. To that town she sent a short reply with no apology in it.

The second town had read with its eyes open. Their schoolmistress lashed the boats where the swallows nested, and in her valley the swallows nested in a willow, and the willow walked out into the flood with three hulls under its arm. The verse itself was mud. Maren took it back to her bench and recut it — the iron rings above the second stair — and to that town she sent the new verse first, with a note of thanks.

After that she asked every drover, pilgrim, and peddler to sing her the round as their valley sang it. The verses drifted like river-stones: words worn round, lines swapped in order, the swallows replaced by iron. But the first verse — the ring that learned to swim, the oars that went home — came back word for word from every valley, from towns that had never traded with one another and did not know the others existed. On that verse she laid each new year's round, the way a carpenter builds on the one beam he has ever tested.

She never took the green boat out of the song. Every spring, in whichever valley sang it loudest, she could hear where the water would find people waiting.

The plain page stayed in her drawer, and it was truer, and it said so in more words: why the ring sat at its height and no higher, measured from the great flood year; why the low dock's piles could not be trusted; what to do when the water climbed past the second stair, which the song, being only a song, knows nothing about. Twice a year a clerk from somewhere wrote to ask why. To them alone she sent the page. Everyone else took the version they could carry, and on the Tuesday in March it was the version they could carry that stood on the high road, arms folded, dry.
