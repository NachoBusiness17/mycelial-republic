# The City of Cold Castings

Lucian walked through the outer curtain wall at dawn, carrying neither timber nor plaster. The customs officers at the frontier gate searched his satchel for contraband tonnage, but found only three slips of thin parchment, each punctured with numbers.

"You bring nothing to build the arch?" the gatekeeper asked, turning the narrow vellum between calloused fingers.

"The stone does not need to travel," Lucian said. "The stone is merely where the distance from nothing turns into zero."

He pointed to the smallest slip. It bore neither sketch nor perspective, only an algebraic field of clearances—a lineage traced from the shortest path charted in the winter of seventy-four, through the two light-masters who taught shadows to cling to vacuum in eighty-eight, down to the mathematician who dissolved all facets into smooth radial distances thirteen years into the new century. At the rocky perimeter, where water was dear and the mules stumbled on steep scree, no one was permitted to haul heavy matter. Every courier passed through the narrow turnstile carrying only the compact description of empty intervals.

Down in the salt basin, where furnaces roared on cheap coal and lime lay everywhere in white drifts, the expansion took place. A boy took Lucian's slip, slipped it beneath the lens of a brass lantern, and fed the bellows. Within minutes, the hot lime bloomed across a wicker frame, solidifying at the exact mathematical boundary where the formula commanded the light to stop. The resulting vault was monumental, yet it possessed no original body. It was an effect produced from an equation that had traversed the pass without mass, more durable than quarried granite precisely because it could be dissolved back into four lines of ink whenever the festival ended.

In the second courtyard, three field scouts gathered around an iron brazier. They had spent forty days in the wilderness, yet they brought no samples of earth or pressed flora. Instead, each man carried a pocket of uniform rectangular cards. When the scouts traded findings with the courtyard overseer, Lucian watched them lay the cards side by side upon the slate.

Every card was inscribed with exactly six rows, neither more nor less. The top line gave the sequence mark; the second declared an assertion of terrain; the third recorded what had been extracted or taken; the fourth set forth the precise event that would prove the assertion false; the fifth noted the verifying witness; the sixth recorded the date of erasure. Lucian had seen those same six rows before, carved high upon the black basalt walls of the central archives where the founders preserved the memory of the province. The language of the scouts wandering the dunes matched the syntax of the inner sanctuary syllable for syllable.

Near the archway leading to the treasury, a magnificent brass conduit branched along the wall. Six polished pipes curved upward toward the scouts’ gallery, their flared mouths tuned to catch the drop of incoming cylinders. Yet when Lucian stepped closer, he saw that the lower manifold did not join the pneumatic ducts of the floor. The bracket held the pipes an inch above the intake ports; the brass was burnished to an imperial sheen, admired daily by the stewards, yet no breath had ever moved through the gap. It was an immaculate rehearsal of a circuit, celebrated for its symmetry while its throats remained cold.

Beyond the gardens, where the road climbed toward the jagged granite crests, lay the question that all visitors eventually whispered: where were the great engines that gave the estate its authority?

The workshop master unlocked a low chest of aromatic cedar. Inside lay no iron wheels, no heavy cylinders, no brass pistons. There was only a catalog written on calfskin. It described seven massive casting-molds, each forged from ninety tons of iron and copper, resting in the abandoned quarries thirty leagues to the north.

"Do we not drag them down to the foundry?" Lucian asked.

"To move ninety tons of dead iron across the pass is an indulgence of the blind," the master replied, turning the calfskin page. "The heavy masses remain in their distant mountain crags until an actual commission summons their fire. Here we keep only the formula to locate the cavern, the measure of pitch to kindle their ovens, and the strike of the chime to prove their bronze has not cracked in the frost."

Lucian touched the green ribbon that bound the edge of the catalog. The ribbon was looped through the vellum but lacked the lead seal of the high court; an elder had marked it with an unknotted thread, standing as the favored proposal, yet still awaiting the final stamp of the guild.

Lucian walked to the terrace and looked out over the salt basin. The white pavilions gleamed in the noon glare, sharp and majestic against the grey silt. They were absolute in their geometry, operative without flaw, requiring neither precedent nor origin.
