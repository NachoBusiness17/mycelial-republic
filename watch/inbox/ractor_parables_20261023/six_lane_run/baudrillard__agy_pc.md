# The Six Booths of the Cartographer

In late October, the Bureau of Surveys ceased sending men into the provinces. A territory is stubborn, muddy, and full of accidents; a reproduction is clean, and six reproductions that agree are more operative than the province they replace. To prove the fidelity of an unvisited coast, the Director erected six pine booths in a windowless gallery and summoned six draughtsmen of deliberately unequal station.

First came the Chief Engraver, who worked with the Bureau's heaviest brass pantograph at its highest tension. Second came a Chartered Proxy, admitted only because he carried a gilded token rented from a foreign syndicate in place of the usual warrant. Third and fourth were two Roaming Apprentices from the same guildhall, one stationed at a portable lectern and the other at a heavy oak workbench, both permitted to leave their benches and fetch their own parchment from the vestry. Fifth was a Market Scribe who worked for nothing under an open awning, using common charity charcoal. Sixth was a Cellar Potter who never left the basement, scratching his contours onto damp domestic clay with a bone stylus.

Before the bolt was drawn on each booth, the attendants scrubbed the workbenches with lye until not a smudge of an earlier province remained. No draughtsman was permitted a notebook, a memory of yesterday's coast, or a speaking-tube to his neighbors. Through six brass slots, at the same stroke of the bell, six identical slips of vellum were dropped, bearing word for word the same dry description of headlands none of them had ever seen. Each man began at zero, enclosed in an artificial virginity of mind, as though isolation itself were a guarantee of truth.

The precision of their solitude was the Bureau's pride, and like all ceremonies of purity, it harbored the exact contamination required to make it credible. While the Cellar Potter, the Market Scribe, the Chartered Proxy, and the Chief Engraver remained bolted behind their pine planks, the Roaming Apprentices, permitted by their warrant to walk the gallery and hang their own sheets on the common drying line, passed the cedar rack where the finished rolls already hung. Neither apprentice unrolled another man's parchment; the lead clasps held fast. Yet both of them read the outer tags tied to the ribbons of the other rolls. Seeing the titles and the ciphers lettered on the outside of the cylinders, they knew which rivals labored beside them and how the unmapped bays were being named. One does not need to see the landscape if one can read the catalog of those who are fabricating it. The gallery watchman recorded this glimpsing of the tags in the margin of his chronicle, not to halt the ceremony, but to supply the meticulous confession of error by which an artifice launders its own authority.

At dusk the six renderings were spread upon a great checkered baize divided into intersecting rows and columns. No rider was dispatched to the shore to see whether a promontory stood where the charcoal was thick. Instead, the tabulators crossed every draughtsman's rendering against the other five. Where all six contours bent together, the square of the grid was stamped with a heavy gold disk for highest assurance; where four converged and two drifted, the square received a silver ring for moderate trust; where a reef appeared on one sheet alone, it was dusted with gray chalk as a solitary fancy. By measuring the copies exclusively against one another, the checkered baize generated an original that had never existed in earth or water. Consensus among signs replaced the shore, and the graded stamps of assurance gave the phantom coast a density that rock and tide could never match.

Yet a grid of echoes still requires an external signature to close the circuit. At midnight the Director admitted a solitary Foreign Assayer into the gallery. By statute this arbiter was granted a single sitting and a single uninterrupted glance: he could ask no questions, summon no draughtsman back to the bench, and call for no second stroke of the stylus. He passed his lens once over the checkered baize, weighed the gold disks against the gray chalk, transcribed the watchman's marginal caveat regarding the outer tags on the drying rack, and inscribed one summary folio declaring what remained constant across all six renderings. When his quill lifted, the gallery was locked. Should the actual sea rise tomorrow and swallow the headlands, the Bureau would not amend a single line; the ships of the realm now steer by the summary folio, and where the coast disagrees with the sixfold convergence, it is the coast that is treated as a counterfeit.
