# The Six Tubs at High Noon

You carry a lit tallow lantern through the olive grove at high noon, friend, squinting as if six pairs of eyes will save you from trusting your own two. In the fifth sits a barefoot whistling mendicant from the open crossroads who asks not a single copper coin for his labor. You barred the wicker dividers between them and forbade any man to pass a rib-bone scraper, a cup of vinegar, or a whispered measure to his neighbor. You stood on the cistern rim rubbing your chin, proud of how cleanly you had walled them apart, pretending that six men in six boxes make nature speak more plainly than one honest hand in the dirt.

Yet a dog under the fig tree sees what the master of the yard dresses up as purity. Four of your potters stayed still inside their wicker cages and waited for you to fetch their boards, so their eyes never strayed beyond their own thumbs. Still, their eyes were open in the daylight: each roving brother read the chalked tags tied to the necks of the other five sacks already resting on the cedar beam. You did not let him question the potters or linger for a second cup of wine.
