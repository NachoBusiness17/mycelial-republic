# The Trough and the Brass Tongue

Councillor Valentin came to the Abbey of Saint-Sernin when the fullers lifted the sluice-boards, carrying a worn Diophantus whose narrow borders held his notes on fourth powers and infinite descent. In the cloister stood a limestone trough fed from both the eastern and western cisterns. The water raced fiercely from both ends, yet at three notches along the rim tall braids of foam stood still as alabaster pillars, while between them lay hollows where an oat-husk never drifted. A young mason, hoping to spare half the water while keeping the triple crown in place, dropped the western board so only the eastern stream ran through. Instantly the three crowns flattened into a common brook that swept the husk away. Valentin raised the western board again; as soon as the contrary stream drove back against the first, the three pillars rose once more at the exact notches while the water beneath them rushed without pause.

The abbot's nephew, who had wagered five pistoles that he could pare the middle pillar to half its height, stepped forward with a wooden peel. Leaving both boards open, he thrust the blade slantwise into the central crest to trim it by a thumb's breadth. The pillar did not shrink into a smaller pillar. A shudder ran toward both cisterns, the hollows broke from their stations, and all three braids came undone at once in a churning wash that drenched the flags. Even after he withdrew the blade, the broken currents beat wildly until the bailiff drained the trough dry and loosed the twin streams afresh.

From the cloister Valentin entered the stamping-room, where two brothers struck leaden bulls for the province. The heavy upper chase held twelve cast lines of bronze—the ancient salutation and bounds of the bailiwick—wedged fast with iron keys so no letter could budge. At the foot of the block sat a tiny socket no wider than a thumbnail, into which the younger brother dropped a fresh parish numeral before each pull of the screw. Valentin watched two hundred impressions struck in order. For one hundred and ninety-nine of them, the elder brother never touched a wrench or reheated the upper block; the locked opening lines struck clean from their stored heat while only the final numeral changed. Only on the two-hundredth sheet, drawn for a foreign diocese, did they loosen the keys.

Beyond the press lay the walled garth where the cellarer bred winter pears against the frost. Every autumn he crossed pollen from wild trees and sowed a thousand chance pips, so no two saplings shared the same bark or leaf. Yet bolted across the wall hung an iron gauge pierced with three fixed rings, unaltered since the founder's day. As the novices dug each season's variable shoots, they passed stem and fruit through the rings, burning every sapling that missed the measure and grafting only the survivors to cross again. The seedlings varied and perished in every bed, yet the harvested fruit kept the founder's exact proportion year after year.

At noon Valentin climbed to the gallery where the seneschal heard market pleas. Drovers, fullers, and widows entered by the south stair, argued their causes across the oak bar, paid their fines, and left by the north stair, never the same company for two quarter-hours together. Overhead hung three brass plummets marking where the statutes on grain-measure, boundary-stones, and tallow-weights were read aloud. Whenever a buyer and seller contended hardest, their clamor gathered squarely beneath one of the three plummets, while at the benches midway between them the gallery stayed quiet. The litigants and their quarrels passed ceaselessly from stair to stair, while the three plummets hung unmoved above the sharpest collisions.

At the end of the gallery stood the narrow stone arch to the great hall of audit. Built into its jamb was a small brass escapement no bigger than a pocket-dial, whose spinning wheel gave off a low, steady hum. Every porter arriving from the yard had to rest his sack for an instant upon a projecting brass tongue before the inner bar would lift; when a sack held wet gravel instead of clean wheat, the hum dropped a note and a spring-catch tipped the load into the chute before the warden touched his keys. Outside the arch, a merchant with six pack-men tried to open his sacks and distribute the tallies across six trestles in the forecourt. The wardens overturned the trestles and forced the men back into single file. Only after a sack had crossed the humming tongue alone did the double doors open into the wide vaulted hall behind it, where thirty clerks waited at thirty tables to divide the cleared burden at once. Inside that broad hall, Valentin opened his Diophantus to the cramped border where he had once claimed a general proof for every power, and pared the ink back to the fourth power alone, whose descent had already passed the brass tongue.
