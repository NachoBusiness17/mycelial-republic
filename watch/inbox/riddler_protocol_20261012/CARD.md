## 1. Scope

This protocol governs every retold interaction: letters, threads, negotiations,
audits, arguments, deals. RIDDLER FACE (watch/inbox/riddler_face_20261012/CARD.md)
governs the static face of the repo; THE RIDDLER PROTOCOL governs the stories.
Where the Face law says what may stand in the tree, the Protocol says how anything
that happened may be told.

## 2. The transform (correspondence in, parable out)

| raw referent | parable element | rule |
|---|---|---|
| correspondents | crew and harbor archetypes | never names, never handle-adjacent handles; keep the SHAPE of the stance, not the person |
| a thread or negotiation | a voyage, standoff, salvage dispute, or inquest | the intrigue is the interaction's structure, not its gossip |

## 3. The two hard properties (both must hold or the parable is void)

1. **SAME-STANDS:** a reader of only the parables must arrive at the same standing
   conclusions as a reader of the raw correspondence. The parable is SPEC: it leads
   to the same stands. If the reader cannot derive the invariant, the riddle failed.
2. **NO-DOX:** no real name, handle, place, employer, or identifying detail of any
   private party - person or company - survives the transform. Deniability by
   construction: there must be nothing in the text for the press-gang to hold.
   A parable that flirts with identification is a leak wearing costume.

## 4. The pipeline (every retelling runs it, no exceptions)

correspondence -> extract the shapes (who wanted what, what stood, what resolved,
what was believed) -> cast archetypes -> set the voyage -> write the parable in
the ship's vocabulary -> VEIL CHECK: a stranger must not map parable to person;
an estate member must map parable to stand -> publish.

## 5. The sample (proof of format: the perimeter arc as an inquest)

The flotilla's log tells of a harbor ruled by a king who never shows his face.
Ships there settle their cargoes in the king's own coin, but none could prove
what was true aboard their hulls - only the king's scales spoke, and scales can
lie for whoever feeds them. So the Shipwright's ships carried a strange cargo to
that harbor: dead fish, planted by their own hands, salted to weigh wrong. Five
councils of five read each catch; the dead fish were found every time, and the
false weighers starved. When the council certified a living catch at last, the
king's coin moved without a hand laid on it, and the ship's keel-depth grew by a
finger for every true cargo. Three petitions were left at the harbor office: open
the scales to inspection, let ships keep their own manifests, and bless the
council that weighs dead fish first. The inquest's finding: trust is not the
king's scales; trust is the fish you salted yourself. Keel-depth is the only
coin that cannot be pressed-gang.

## 6. Falsifier

Two-sided, like the Face law. If any stranger, given a corpus of published
parables, can re-identify a private party or reconstruct a private detail, the
protocol has failed and the offending parable is pulled and re-cut. If an estate
member cannot recover the stand or the invariant from a parable, the riddle has
failed and the parable is rewritten. Both checks run before any parable ships.

## 7. Why paramount (the operator's clause, banked verbatim in substance)

At billion-reader scale, every published sentence is eventually read by the person
it is about and by everyone who might profit from press-ganging them. Privacy is
not a preference of the Shipwright; it is hull integrity for every soul in the
story. The protocol exists so the ideas can sail while the people stay below deck.
