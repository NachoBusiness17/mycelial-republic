### Extraction 1: The ledger always overcounts, and the overcount has a taxonomy

Every chronicle in 003 conducts a physical-vs-ledger count and the ledger loses by a wide margin every time, with different numbers per response because each model re-instantiates the count: 200 promised vs 40 claimable (l.14, 24-28); 306 wood vs 7,400 book (l.58); 717 physical vs 1,042 ledger (l.415); 112 vs 147 chairs and 83 vs 129 promises (l.314-320); 47 vs 9 (l.561). The count is not the point; the taxonomy of phantom entries is (l.322-336): promises fulfilled twice in the ledger and once in the town (27), chairs occupied without a corresponding promise (9), promises depending on other promises that no longer existed (16), entries copied through every season without a living witness (7), entries "invented by reconciliation" (4), entries forgotten and rediscovered in margins (3), and one entry with no chair, no witness, no author, no date, and no proof anyone spoke it - which the town marked "Unknown." and left alone (l.336-346). The strangers' failure modes for explaining the gap are named and reusable as audit-red-flag phrases: capacities rather than chairs (l.108), a parallel Shadow Ledger reclassifying contradictions as indicators (l.413), a standing gold-leaf reserve for future descent (l.421), clocks adjusted so promises are "never technically late, only pending the next revision" (l.576), and the end state: "a chair filled with perfect handwriting was functionally identical to a chair filled with a person, provided no one actually tried to sit in it" (l.576).

Estate counterpart: the staged-never-ticketed disease and the phantom 191.8M-token headline (commit 5d2263f10 message, env-provided) are exactly this class - occupancy claimed in the record with no sitter. Steal: for any done-claim, name the sitter (the exit code, byte check, ticket row, or artifact that bore the weight); keep the seven-category phantom taxonomy as audit categories for receipt sweeps.
