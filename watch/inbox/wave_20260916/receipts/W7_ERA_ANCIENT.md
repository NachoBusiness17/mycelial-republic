## Part I — the deep unpack (games 1–5)

Scoring: `–` absent · `~` embryonic/proto · `+` strong · `++` defining. Each P score carries a one-line justification.

### 1. Senet (Egypt, ~3100 BCE →; 3×10 track, ~30 houses; standard by Old Kingdom; religious re-reading late period)

- **Finite shell:** two players, 5–7 pieces each, binary casting lots (throwsticks), race from house 1 to bear off past 30. Blockades stop opposing pieces; house 26 (nefer, "beauty") is the good re-entry; house 27 is the Waters — a piece landing there drowns back toward the start; houses 28–30 (Three Truths, Re-Atoum, Horus) commonly require exact throws to leave. Reconstructions differ (Kendall, Picaud); the named houses and their effects are stable across them.
- **Infinite core — the afterlife argument.** The board is not a field, it is a *map*: thirty houses are the gates of the journey through the underworld. Book of the Dead ch. 17 has the justified dead *playing senet* in the Field of Reeds; tomb texts re-read a match as a rehearsal of the soul's navigation. So winning did not mean dominance — it meant a successful *argument against chance*: proof, under fate's own dice, that you could pass the gates. The win condition is an exit that transcends the board. Three thousand years of senet boards means the artifact outlived every title ever won on it; boards placed in tombs are play persisting past the player.
- **Scores:** P1 `~` (myth-scale: boards in tombs promise play past death — persistence as cosmology, not software). P2 `–` (rules stable for millennia; the only mutation is cosmic re-reading of the same board). P3 `~` (square-name lore is the theory corpus — and the corpus is a religion). P4 `–`. P5 `~` (the stake is eternal standing, not economic). P6 `–`.
- **The weave:** *named legal squares.* Every square is a house with a name and a law. Our "rooms as legality tables" is Senet's invention — but we shipped it unnamed. Weave: give each room's legality rows house-names ("the Waters" for the refusal row, "House of Beauty" for the safe row) so the room table reads as cosmology an agent can cite, not config an agent must parse.

### 2. Mehen (Egypt, ~3100–2300 BCE; coiled-serpent spiral board; lions, lionesses, balls; rules LOST)

- **Finite shell:** unknown. Likely a spiral race for 2–4 players (piece counts vary: 3–7 lions + spheres per set); some sets include small throwing batons. That is all anyone knows.
- **Infinite core — the guardian you play inside, and the board that gets killed.** The board *is* a mythic body: Mehen, the coiled serpent who coils around Ra's barque each night to shield it from Apophis. Playing on the serpent is travel inside a protector. And the artifact itself was ritually killed — excavated boards commonly have the serpent's head deliberately chiseled off. Two laws live here: (a) the container can be an ally, and (b) a finished run's container can be *sacrificed* — ended as a rite, not abandoned as litter. The darker law: Mehen's rules died with its players because **nobody wrote them down**. A 3000-year-old game is a blank because it lacked receipts.
- **Scores:** P1 `–` (boards destroyed — the deliberate anti-persistence; the era's control case). P2 `–`. P3 `~` (iconography survived, mechanics didn't — memory without procedure). P4 `–`. P5 `–`. P6 `–`.
- **The weave:** *the tombstone verb.* A run can be formally defaced — a visible "serpent-head-broken" marker on a dead twin that distinguishes *finished and honored* ghosts from *abandoned* ones. And the meta-law, which this lane files as doctrine: **receipts are the anti-Mehen.** Any mechanic that lives only in agent behavior is one generation from being Mehen.

### 3. Royal Game of Ur (Sumer, ~2600 BCE; Woolley's Royal Cemetery finds; route-and-combat race)

- **Finite shell:** seven pieces per player race down a fixed shared track (the classic 3×2+4×2 "calendet" path). Four-binary-corner dice. The eight middle squares are combat: land on an enemy piece and it is sent back to start. Rosettes are safe — a piece on one cannot be taken — and Finkel's tablet-reconstruction adds an extra roll on landing. Exact count to bear off. Strategy is route-position under open luck: you cannot remove risk, only choose where to stand in it.
- **Infinite core — the route, the luck, and the pot.** Ur is the earliest surviving *route with law*: a shared road where all violence is co-location, and the road itself is sacred furniture. Two thousand years later, Babylonian tablets re-read the squares as fortunes ("fine beer," "find a friend," "powerful like a lion") and the rosettes as a *pot*: skip a rosette and you must pay a token in; land on one and you take one. The board grew its own gambling economy — safety carries a toll, and the object in the middle of the table accumulates everyone's payments. And the rules survive *only because a scribe wrote a tablet about them* (Itti-Marduk-balāṭu, ~177 BC): Ur is Mehen's rescuer — the same era, saved by a receipt.
- **Scores:** P1 `~` (4000-year artifact lineage, but the lineage is carried by documents — persistence via paperwork). P2 `–`. P3 `+` (BM.33333.B is the oldest surviving ruleset in the world; the theory trail is 2 millennia long). P4 `–`. P5 `~` (the rosette pot: embedded stakes, player-funded). P6 `–`.
- **The weave:** *combat-by-shared-route* — hostile interaction exists ONLY as co-location on a road cell, is total (send-home = full loss of route progress), and is always receipted. No abstract attacks, no ranged anything: violence = occupying the same legality cell. For a text world this kills half the moderation surface. Secondary weave: *the pot on the rosette* — safe squares charge a toll to a common pot that other verbs pay out of; safety is never free.

### 4. Twenty Squares (Game of Twenty Squares, Mesopotamia/Egypt ~1500–300 BCE; often the REVERSE of a senet board)

- **Finite shell:** 5×4 grid, boustrophedon track of twenty squares, five (or seven) pieces, four-cornered dice. Own side is safe; the eight central squares are shared and hostile — captured pieces restart. Bear off by exact roll.
- **Infinite core — the flipped board and the oracle.** One physical object, two games: the senet grid on one face (long, cosmological, the game of the dead) and the fast twenty-squares on the other. Gaming boxes pair them deliberately; Tutankhamun was buried with four. The flip is the key mechanic: a *mode switch* — one legal global bit changes which ruleset the hardware is running, without mutating either ruleset. Then the tablet goes further: each square landing has a *divinatory* reading — the receipt tells your fortune. Twenty Squares is the tavern face of the same wood senet is the temple face of: same board, faster luck, beer in the predictions, tokens in the pot.
- **Scores:** P1 `+` (shared hardware outlives every session — the board persists across *games*, not just plays). P2 `~` (the flip: scheduled, legal, whole-world rule-mode change at zero mutation cost). P3 `+` (the divination layer is an explicit interpretation corpus — the receipts come with readings). P4 `–`. P5 `+` (the gambling face — pot tokens, fast stakes). P6 `–`.
- **The weave:** *two-face rooms.* Every room carries a second legality face — temple face (slow, receipt-heavy, cosmological) and tavern face (fast, stake-heavy, fortune-telling) — toggled by one legal world-verb, recorded as one receipt field (`face=tavern`). Zero rules mutate; one bit flips. Cheap beyond belief in a text world where a room is *already* a table. Secondary weave: *fortunes as receipts* — landing/collision receipts carry one oracle line drawn from a fixed template set (Souls-message discipline, 4500 years early).

### 5. Mancala family (sowing/count-capture; Africa→Asia→world; awele, omweso, kalah, sungka…; millennia, still alive)

- **Finite shell:** sow seeds one-by-one around a loop of houses; capture by count-landing rules; win by emptying or amassing, per local fork. The shell is almost incidental — it is the *move-shape* that is the game.
- **Infinite core — sowing, and rules nobody owns.** A mancala move is never self-contained: you lift a house's seeds and redistribute them along the shared track — your move deterministically rewrites the *opponent's* options, legibly, in one action. You cannot act on your own position without touching the commons. And the ruleset itself is commons: hundreds of local forks, no owner, no canonical text, each village's variant legal by custom. The family is the widest-spread, longest-continuously-played game lineage on Earth — P4 (players as rule-authors) and P5 (free continuation: board = holes in the ground) at folk scale, still running after millennia. It never needed a company.
- **Scores:** P1 `~` (oral transmission, boards outlasting generations — but no records). P2 `+` (folk mutation: every village a legal fork — mutation without referee, by community custom). P3 `~` (counting lore and proverb corpora around awele). P4 `+` (the family IS authored variants). P5 `+` (zero-cost continuation; nobody ever lacked a board). P6 `~` (public rows, spectated, taught between generations).
- **The weave:** *sowing as the legal move-shape.* A move is legal only if it redistributes shared state along the world's track: acting on your own hoard alone is ILLEGAL. For agent sessions this is an anti-silo law — hoarded resources must be sown to be spent, and every sow lights up cross-room diffs in the receipt. One rule kills hoarding, forces emergent inter-room effects, and is trivially auditable (the receipt shows the whole sowing path).

### 6. Patolli (Mesoamerica; Classic Maya → Aztec; cross board, marked beans as dice; Sahagún & Durán record it; Spanish suppressed it)

- **Finite shell:** race around a cross-shaped track; five marked beans as the randomizer; land on an enemy marker and capture/send back; first side all home wins. Varied by region (Maya to Aztec), played in plazas and patios, patrons invoked (Macuilxochitl, Five-Flower).
- **Infinite core — the stakes ARE the game.** In Patolli the wager is not adjacent to the game; it is load-bearing. Durán documents "all-consuming" stakes — blankets, jewels, feasts — with early sources reporting stakes down to freedom and life. The session legally *begins* with the entry: wagers registered before the first bean falls. Play without skin on the table was barely play. And the era's dark control case: the game did not die of boredom or displacement — the **Spanish suppressed it** (religion + gambling + drink), one of history's only examples of a game killed by law rather than by time. Lesson filed: authority can kill a game; our referee must never govern like the colony — shrink stakes, never kill play.
- **Scores:** P1 `–` (suppression broke the chain — law-as-antagonist). P2 `–` (rigid; the only mutation was colonial annihilation). P3 `~` (gambling omens and magic — folk interpretation layer). P4 `–`. P5 `++` (the definitive stakes game of the ancient world; wagering up to life itself). P6 `~` (plaza play, spectators, Montezuma watching).
- **The weave:** *the registered stakes entry.* A session legally opens only when its stake is declared in the opening receipt (even if the stake is 1 unit of standing); forfeiture on loss is automatic and receipted. The receipt IS the pot. This makes every session's seriousness legible to the chronicle — and gives the referee a shrink-not-kill lever (cap stakes, never ban the verb).

### 7. Go (weiqi / baduk / igo; China, ~2000+ BCE by legend, solidly ancient; Edo Japan institutionalizes it)

- **Finite shell:** two players, alternate stones, no moving them; groups live by two eyes, die by liberties; territory counted; or the game *ends early by resignation*.
- **Infinite core — three machines in one game.**
  - **Handicaps + komi:** before a game, the rules LEGALLY MUTATE for that pairing — a weaker player takes stones on the board, a same-rank player takes komi points — so unequal players produce genuine contests. Scheduled rule-mutation whose entire purpose is *no one gets excluded from a real game*. The most elegant P2 device ever shipped.
  - **Resignation:** a legal, early, honored end. You concede when the direction is decided; the concession is a *result*, recorded in the game archive, with no shame attached — and it teaches: reading a resignation correctly is itself a skill. Go is the only ancient game where stopping is a first-class move.
  - **The joseki corpus:** 500+ years of recorded, named, contested opening patterns — communal solution knowledge that outlives every player who contributed. Plus the move that teaches: stones are never taken back; the final position is a total record of every decision, kept in professional archives for centuries.
- **Scores:** P1 `+` (the final position IS the receipt; game records archived for centuries). P2 `+` (handicap/komi — pre-game legal rule mutation to keep play continuing across skill gaps). P3 `++` (joseki + life-and-death corpora: the knowledge-metagame crown). P4 `~` (innovations get their inventor's name on the pattern). P5 `+` (castle games as state institution; the teaching ladder as economy). P6 `~` (spectated castle games; later online servers).
- **The weave:** *resignation as a first-class legal verb* + *the handicap ladder.* (a) Sessions may end by receipted resignation — required `why` line, partial-credit curve, stored as a RESULT, not a crash. (b) Weaker agents or fresh ghosts receive declared pre-game stones (extra steps, extra resources, known-map previews) chosen by rank gap — pairing-time rule mutation, one receipt field.

### 8. Chess (Chaturanga ~600 CE → Shatranj → modern move-set ~1475 → FIDE titles 1950)

- **Finite shell:** checkmate; the draw problem (stalemate, repetition, fifty-move) that at top level makes the shell rot toward draw-death; clocks.
- **Infinite core — the metagame as the game.** Chess's infinite core is not the board but the *corpus*: openings theory — recorded, shared, evolving communal solution knowledge, the first knowledge metagame at industrial scale. Then the **title system**: grandmaster is a permanent, named, world-recognized rank minted from a chain of witnessed performances — a person's receipts converted to status, outliving every individual game and outliving the player. And the modern patches: Chess960, faster time controls, tiebreaks — centralized, legal shell-mutations deployed specifically to defeat memorization and keep play meaningful (P2 as maintenance, not upheaval).
- **Scores:** P1 `+` (notation archives + title registers: the longest-lived receipt system in games). P2 `~` (FIDE mutations — legal but centralized and slow). P3 `++` (the theory mountain; engine-era meta on top). P4 `~` (opening innovations credited by name — authorship honored via nomenclature). P5 `++` (professional title economy: the rank pays for the continuation of play). P6 `+` (correspondence chess — postal play is async co-presence across *months*, in production for centuries).
- **The weave:** *the title.* A persistent named rank issued by the referee from the receipt-chain (N witnessed wins at tier = title), stored world-side, surviving agent death, visible to every future pairing — and consumed by the handicap ladder (titles set the stones). Secondary weave: *openings as receipt book* — the world indexes past sessions' opening room-sequences as named lines the referee (and any agent) can consult before play.

### 9. Backgammon (Roman tabula lineage → modern race; the cube: ~1925, New York)

- **Finite shell:** race with hits and bearing off; matches to points; chouette for tables.
- **Infinite core — the doubling cube.** Around 1925, in New York gaming clubs (Grand Duke Dmitri the likeliest inventor, contested — verified this lane), someone bolted a device onto a 4000-year-old race: before acting, either player may *double the stakes*; the opponent takes the new stakes or *drops* and pays the current stake. Confidence becomes price. The cube is poker's raise fused to a deterministic race — stakes negotiation as a legal, in-game, mid-game act — and it rescued backgammon from a solved-feeling race and made it the world's stake game with ONE idea: the players, not the house, own the pot's escalation. Proof that stakes *law*, not content, is a game's renewable engine. Chouette extends it socially: a table of players with rotating cube authority.
- **Scores:** P1 `~` (match records). P2 `+` (the cube is player-triggered, legally bounded rule-mutation of the session's own stakes). P3 `+` (match-equity tables — cube positions are a whole theory corpus). P4 `~` (chouette rotates authority — players author table structure). P5 `++` (the cube IS continuation economics: it keeps play worth continuing by repricing it continuously). P6 `–`.
- **The weave:** *the receipt cube.* Either party, before acting, records `[DOUBLE]` in the session receipt; the other party is legally owed two verbs — `take` (stake doubles, receipted) or `drop` (concede at current stake, receipted as a dignified loss, cheap). For agent-vs-agent sessions this prices conviction, ends hopeless sessions early, and gives the chronicle a nerve-trace: every double and every drop is a receipted confession of belief.

### 10. Tafl family (hnefatafl / tablut; Viking-age north + Anglo-Saxon England; Linnaeus observes tablut 1732; displaced by chess ~12th c.)

- **Finite shell:** a king and his defenders vs. roughly twice their number of attackers; capture by sandwich (custodian capture); attackers win by taking the king; the king's side wins by the king REACHING AN EXIT (edge or corner, per variant). Asymmetric forces, asymmetric objectives, one session.
- **Infinite core — asymmetry as law.** Tafl is the ancient proof that a fair game need not be mirror-symmetric: two sides play *different games* on one board — the attackers a closed problem (find the king), the king's side an open one (find the exit). Balance came from testing, not symmetry: Linnaeus's written observation of tablut (1732) is the receipt that let scholars reconstruct the game at all, and modern rebalancing (e.g., the king-escapes-to-edge fix) shows the asymmetry needed tuning to be fair. And the era's second control case: tafl died not by ban but by *displacement* — chess arrived with a deeper theory corpus (P3) and better continuation economics (P5), and a 1000-year-old favorite simply lost the market. Games die of better neighbors.
- **Scores:** P1 `~` (survived by archaeology + one naturalist's written receipt). P2 `~` (local variants; scholarly rebalancing = post-hoc mutation). P3 `~` (modern reconstruction and balance-testing community). P4 `–`. P5 `–` (the death by displacement: no continuation economics saved it). P6 `–`.
- **The weave — the dead-twin/live-twin contract.** Tafl is THE asymmetric-design precedent for the tubs: cast the dead twin as ATTACKERS — many, closing, capture-oriented, and *legally briefed on the live run's recorded past* (the ghost knows the route the living twin died on) — and the live twin as KING — one, open objective, must reach a named legal exit. The session ends in one of two NAMED outcomes, `KING ESCAPED` / `KING TAKEN`, and *neither is win/lose for the world* — both outcomes file a receipt and both feed the chronicle. The live run gains a real antagonist made of its own history; the dead twin gains the thing ghosts never had: a legal objective.

## Part III — five ancient mechanics no modern game ships, ranked by fit for a Discord-text world with receipts

Ranked by (value × cheapness) for our world specifically: rooms as legality tables, `world_sha`-bound receipts, ghosts, referee-legal refusals. The claim "no modern game ships" is per mechanic-as-law, not per flavor — modern games ship wagering DLC, surrender buttons, and ghost races; none ship these as *legal structure the receipts enforce*.

1. **The receipt cube** (backgammon, 1925). Stake escalation as a first-class mid-game verb pair: `[DOUBLE]` → owed `take`/`drop`, all three receipted; drop is a dignified cheap loss. No modern game makes stake renegotiation a *legal, in-game, player-owned act* — wagering is always house-adjacent DLC. Fit: trivial (one stake field + two verbs in the session receipt; a Discord message is the native cube face). Prices agent conviction; kills hopeless sessions early; writes a nerve-trace into the chronicle. **Ship first.**
2. **Resignation as a first-class verb** (Go). The only ancient game where stopping is an honored move with a recorded result and partial credit. No modern AAA ships a surrender that *pays* — modern surrenders are penalties or AFK-quit taxes. Fit: huge and free — sessions end early with dignity, harness tokens are saved, and the chronicle finally learns from agents that *knew they were lost* (that knowledge is currently un-receipted and dies with the run). Required fields: `why`, partial-credit curve. Refused only by the referee when it would dodge a live cube.
3. **Two-face rooms** (Twenty Squares on the reverse of senet, ~1500 BCE). Every room carries two legality faces — temple (slow, receipt-heavy) and tavern (fast, stake-heavy) — toggled by one legal world-verb, recorded as one bit, zero rules mutated. No modern game ships per-room dual legality tables on shared hardware; modern "modes" are global and content-shaped, not law-shaped. Fit: perfect — a room is already a table; `face=` is one receipt field. Gives the world a legality-legal night mode without a fork.
4. **Sowing as the legal move-shape** (mancala family). A move is legal only if it redistributes shared state along the track; acting on your own hoard alone is illegal. No modern game forces transfer-through-commons as the only move shape — every resource economy shipped is grab-and-keep. Fit: strong — one rule kills agent hoarding, forces cross-room effects every turn, and the receipt shows the whole sowing path, so it is self-auditing. Cost: it touches move-legality in every room, so it is a law change, not a field.
5. **The asymmetric twin session** (tafl). Dead twin as ATTACKERS (many, closing, briefed on the recorded past) vs. live twin as KING (one, open objective, named legal exit); session ends `KING ESCAPED`/`KING TAKEN`, both outcomes file. No modern game ships a ghost with a *different win condition* playing the living (NetHack bones are obstacles, racing ghosts are mirror runners). Fit: highest concept value, ranked 5th only because tub engineering outweighs a receipt field — this is the roadmap item, and the era's precedent that makes it legal.

Honorable mentions: the pot-on-the-rosette (Ur — safe squares toll the pot; merges into #1's pot), the title (chess — close to shipped via chronicle ranks; keep as the handicap ladder's input), the tombstone verb (Mehen — one marker bit on dead twins), the entry stakes (Patolli — folds into #1's opening receipt).

## Part IV — the one ancient principle the bones-legal world violates today

**Resignation.** The bones-legal world currently has exactly two session endings: *completion* (receipted) and *death/crash* (ghosted, grudge-filed). There is no third legal exit — the early, dignified, receipted concession that says "the direction is decided; I stop here and I pay for it." Go made conceding a taught, honored move three thousand years ago; the world we shipped made everything either a finish or a corpse. The costs of the violation are concrete: hopeless sessions burn tokens to the last square; ghosts carry no distinction between "died fighting" and "knew it was lost and kept flailing"; and the chronicle never receives the most valuable receipt a losing agent can write — *where it concluded the game was decided*. The fix is the cheapest artifact in this lane: a `RESIGN` verb (or `resign=true` receipt field) with a required `why` line and a partial-credit curve, refused by the referee only when a live cube or declared stake would be dodged (the Patolli clause: once stakes are on the table, dropping is still legal — but it is recorded as a drop, not a walk-off).

Runner-up, for the record: **stakes** (Patolli) — our sessions carry no skin in the game and the world doesn't know which sessions mattered. But the receipt cube (#1) already fixes that; resignation has no current analog at all. That is why it is the violation.
