## Part 0 — the two rubrics in play

- **Finite shell / infinite core** (Carse, per CG2): every game ships a finite shell; the infinite game is the set of practices that keep play going after the shell ends.
- **P1–P6 score** (CG2's six infinite-game primitives, verbatim): P1 PERSISTENCE (world state survives absence) · P2 MUTATION AUTHORITY (legal channel by which rules change) · P3 KNOWLEDGE METAGAME (your real character survives every run-death) · P4 PLAYER AUTHORSHIP (players write content, not just moves) · P5 CONTINUATION ECONOMICS (structure that pays for play to continue) · P6 ASYNC CO-PRESENCE (strangers cooperate/compete across time in one world). Scored 0–3: 0 absent · 1 embryonic · 2 strong · 3 perfected/defining. (CG2's own vocabulary: embryonic / full / perfected.)

## Part I — the lineage, seven deep

### 1. Rogue (1980 — Toy, Wichman, Arnold; the origin)

1. **Shell / core.** Shell: one descent, 26+ procedural levels, fetch the Amulet of Yendor, climb back out — permadeath, turn-based, fog-of-war rooms. Core: identification (every scroll/potion starts `?` — the run forces rediscovery by experiment) and the seed. The core was never the dungeon; it was *not knowing*.
2. **Death as content generator.** Minimal, and that minimalness is the founding datum. Death gives you the ASCII tombstone (RIP, cause of death, gold) and a scorefile entry. The real content generation was indirect: because identification resets every run, every death rehearses discovery. No bones — that is NetHack's 1982/87 upgrade (Hack 1982 introduced bones; Rogue has only the grave marker).
3. **Meta-progression law.** NOTHING persists but the high-score file. Why that fits the thesis: Rogue's thesis is *everyone dies; learning is the only legitimate currency*. Any persistent power would falsify the identification game. The meta is entirely off-ledger — in the player's head, then in the nascent wiki culture.
4. **P-score:** P1 1 · P2 0 · P3 2 · P4 0 · P5 1 · P6 1 → **5**. (P6 1: the scorefile and USENET share culture are proto-async co-presence — your initials outlive you in the lab's ledger.)

### 2. NetHack (1987 — the bones era)

1. **Shell / core.** Shell: one ascension (Dungeons of Doom → Gehennom → Planes → Astral). Core: the interaction matrix — polymorph, dipping, altars, kicking, pets, everything touches everything — plus conducts (atheist, foodless, pacifist) as player-authored rule mutations inside a fixed rule text.
2. **Death as content generator.** **Bones files, the lineage's great invention:** your dead character — corpse, gear, ghost, sometimes pet — is serialized to the bones pool and loads into *other* games (historically other users' games on shared Unix systems, uid-gated). Run A's death becomes run B's encounter: a ghost with your gear guarding your loot. Add grave engraving and the "recently outlived by" list. Death became level design written by the dead.
3. **Meta-progression law.** Nothing persists mechanically — no unlocks, no stats, ever. Everything persists as *knowledge*: the wiki, the ascension set (win as every role/race/alignment), conducts. Why: NetHack's thesis is *the world is a deep puzzle of interactions; mastery is comprehension* — so the only legal persistence is comprehension, and bones files extend even your deaths into shared knowledge.
4. **P-score:** P1 2 · P2 2 · P3 **3** · P4 2 · P5 1 · P6 2 → **12**. (P3 3: the wiki-metagame is the knowledge metagame's high-water mark. P6 2: bones files = async co-presence with strangers since 1987.)

### 3. Dwarf Fortress (2006 — Bay 12, the Adams brothers)

1. **Shell / core.** Shell: the fortress lives and falls; the adventurer dies. Core: the generated world itself — centuries of history, Legends ledger, a simulation where nothing is scripted (cats dying of beer poisoning from licked-over spilled barrels). The shell is one fort; the core is a *world that keeps making stories*.
2. **Death as content generator.** **"Losing is fun" — the design law.** The fallen fortress stays on the map as a ruin your next adventurer can visit; ghosts haunt the fort in-fortress; Legends records the death of every generated historical figure; engraved walls memorialize them. DF internalized bones files: *the game generates its own ruins*. A death is not the end of content — it is theraw material of the next century of the same world.
3. **Meta-progression law.** None mechanically — donationware, no unlocks. Persistence = the world save + the Legends ledger + succession-fort stories (Boatmurdered). Why: the thesis is *the simulation is the content* — any reward loop outside the sim would falsify it. Loss is fun precisely because the loss is content.
4. **P-score:** P1 **3** · P2 2 · P3 2 · P4 **3** · P5 **3** · P6 2 → **15**. (P1 3: a world with running history that exists while you're absent. P4 3: players author history itself. P5 3: the donation model — the Adamses' livelihood IS continuation. P6 2: succession games are strangers passing one fort hand to hand.)

### 4. Slay the Spire (2019 — Mega Crit)

1. **Shell / core.** Shell: one climb, three acts (+ Act 4), the deck dies or the Heart does. Core: draft combinatorics — archetype discovery across a card pool that mutates between runs.
2. **Death as content generator.** The lineage's *weakest* death-content — deliberately clean: no corpse in the world, no bones. What death generates: card unlocks (a dead run pays new cards into the pool), map knowledge, and on dailies a leaderboard line. Death pays the *mutation budget*, not a haunting.
3. **Meta-progression law.** Unlocks = new cards/characters (Ironclad → Silent → Defect → Watcher), Ascension 0–20 per character, daily seeds. The law: **the meta never adds power — it only widens the decision space** (new cards dilute the pool as much as empower you; Ascension makes runs *harder*). Why it fits the thesis: the deck IS the character; run integrity is sacred; so the meta keeps the shell fresh without corrupting the run — Ascension is Go handicap stones institutionalized, run forward.
4. **P-score:** P1 1 · P2 2 · P3 **3** · P4 2 · P5 1 · P6 2 → **11**. (P3 3: archetype knowledge, pathing, pick-relativity — the knowledge metagame is most of the game. P6 2: seed dailies = strangers compete across time.)

### 5. Hades (2020 — Supergiant)

1. **Shell / core.** Shell: one escape attempt (Tartarus → Asphodel → Elysium → Styx). Core: the *story that advances on failure* — the relationships, the House, the reactions to your specific deaths.
2. **Death as content generator.** The industry's strongest: **death is a cutscene engine.** Every death triggers dialogue trees — NPCs remember how and to whom you died; the House deepens; the codex grows. Failure is literally the content pipeline: no death, no story. This is the underworld-narrative move the mission names.
3. **Meta-progression law.** Nearly everything persists: Darkness → Mirror of Night (real power), gems → House renovations, keepsakes, relationships, heat. The famous nuance, stated honestly: Hades *does* unlock power — but the design insight stands: **the narrative is the real ladder; power is only the ticket to see more story.** Why it fits the thesis: the game's theme is a family you cannot escape, so a loop you cannot escape where every death deepens bonds *is* the theme made mechanical.
4. **P-score:** P1 2 · P2 2 · P3 2 · P4 1 · P5 2 · P6 1 → **10**. (P2 2: Pact of Punishment/Heat = explicit sanctioned self-rule-mutation, Ascension in-fiction. P5 2: two years of Early Access as a continuation economy in dev form.)

### 6. Exanima (2015 EA — Bare Mettle Entertainment; Sui Generis' prelude)

1. **Shell / core.** Shell: Arena divisions and the five-level Underworld. Core: the physics — active-ragdoll combat where mastery is *motor learning in your real body* (mouse-directed momentum, footwork, recovery). An effectively infinite skill ceiling that lives off-avatar.
2. **Death as content generator.** Arena permadeath (guild/fighter death on the higher-stakes settings — community-notorious): your dead fighter becomes the run's story and the vault's loss. The deeper generator is Rogue's `?` scroll made physical: **items carry no stat labels — you learn which weapon is good by swinging it** (weight, reach, momentum). Every death teaches your hands; identification is embodied.
3. **Meta-progression law.** Arena: a persistent guild — fighters (levels, skills), accumulated gear, gold, with permadeath risking the hoard. Underworld: progression is within-playthrough only. Why it fits the thesis: *the body is the interface* — physics honesty means the only progression that can't lie is skill and equipment; levels are deliberately thin.
4. **P-score:** P1 1 · P2 0 · P3 2 · P4 1 · P5 1 · P6 0 → **5**. (P3 2: the knowledge metagame incarnate — muscle memory. P6 0: strictly singleplayer, no strangers in the world at all.)

### 7. Souls-like (Demon's Souls 2009 → Dark Souls 2011 → Elden Ring 2022)

1. **Shell / core.** Shell: kill the lords, credits, NG+ cycles. Core: the async community layer — bloodstains, templated messages, phantoms, invasions — plus lore-archaeology and build culture.
2. **Death as content generator.** **Bloodstains:** your death renders in strangers' worlds as a glowing stain that replays your last seconds as a ghost — death as level design for others, the bones file live-streamed. Plus the soul-run (drop your souls where you fell; one recovery chance; die again and they're gone — Wizardry corpse runs perfected), plus DeS world tendency (deaths shift whole worlds toward black) and hollow/ember state.
3. **Meta-progression law.** The key inversion vs roguelikes: **death is a setback, not a reset** — level and souls-persist, carried souls don't. No unlocks across cycles; what persists is bonfire map-knowledge, NG+ scaling, and the shared message vocabulary. Why it fits the thesis: *death is the teacher; the world is indifferent* — unlocks would cheapen mastery, so the meta is shared suffering: bloodstains are "you will die here," written by the dead. Making the character survive while the wallet drops turns every step into a risk-price decision.
4. **P-score:** P1 2 · P2 2 · P3 **3** · P4 **3** · P5 1 · P6 **3** → **14**. (P6 3: strangers co-present across time with zero lobbies. P4 3: templated messages — infinite player content inside a legal vocabulary, their great authorship invention.)

### 8. frontier_souls (the operator's western — from the estate's own bytes)

1. **Shell / core.** Shell: one run — canyon wake → walk outward (radial rings 1–6, 420px each) → the rim → kill Death (hp900 in bytes) or decompose. Core: the two clocks (radial distance = level; steps = event scheduler, FTL-style "may not appear at all"), plus the cross-run cores: `fs_lore` (THE DESERT REMEMBERS — snakes read your roll, cowboys strafe your range, packs grow on close brawls) and the chronicle/grudges. The core is the desert learning you.
2. **Death as content generator — current state.** Partial. Death feeds: the chronicle ("X holds a grudge", "death feeds"), best steps/ring, the `fs_unlocks` run counter, and the lore-adaptation (your death patterns mutate enemy doctrine *between runs* — announced by banner, persisted in localStorage). Death also heals the boss: Death's hits drink 10s of your decomposition timer, heal him 40, and **spill 8 essence orbs you can take back live in-fight** — the souls corpse-run turned inside out, recovery happening mid-duel. What it does NOT generate: **your dead run itself never returns.** No bones. (Detail below.)
3. **Meta-progression law.** Permadeath (constitution #1) — wake next morning with a scrap of soul (~12 essence), keep unlocks only. Adam's ruling still open (instinct: no; StS evidence: yes). The spike's implicit law matches StS: unlocks widen the decision space (characters, mods), never raw power — the correct lineage answer is "StS's law, Hades' death-fuel: what persists is the desert's memory of you, not your muscle."
4. **P-score:** P1 1 · P2 2 · P3 2 · P4 1 · P5 1 · P6 0 → **7**. (P2 2 is the sleeper: THE DESERT REMEMBERS is a *legal, announced rule-mutation channel* — the world changes its own doctrine between runs because of how you played. P6 0 is the estate-sized gap: no strangers, no relay, no shared seed.)

## Part II — the three specific mappings (rewind · whiskey · chronicle/grudges)

**rewind = NetHack's Amulet of Life Saving, re-expressed in time rather than space.**
Bytes: on death, if `rewBuf` holds ≥4 snapshots and the run hasn't used it: "press T — the desert gives back five seconds (once)". The Amulet of Life Saving (1987) is a single-use death-cancellation — consume it, survive. The one-chance-per-run law is also the souls corpse-run's law (one recovery attempt) — but inverted: the souls corpse-run is a *spatial* takeback (walk back to your bloodstain); the rewind is a *temporal* takeback (time walks back to you — Prince of Persia: Sands of Time 2003 / Braid 2008 grammar, outside this lineage but same family). So: **rewind = Amulet of Life Saving × corpse-run, compressed from walk-back to take-back.** Its sibling in the same file: the Preacher's Last Word sign (`player.lastWord` — cheat death once, revive at 15%) — the same 1987 ancestor wearing a different coat.

**whiskey = the roguelike haste potion, with the time-dilation arrow flipped.**
Bytes: 7% drop on kill; "the whole world drinks slow for a while"; world dt scales, the player doesn't (lines 553, 793, 1590). In-lineage ancestor: NetHack's potion of speed/haste — the consumable that buys action-economy advantage. The twist: haste speeds YOU up; whiskey slows the WORLD — same lever (relative action economy), opposite end. True birthplace of the dilation: SUPERHOT/Max Payne bullet-time — and our own mud.html already ships it as superhot orbs (world dt ×0.6 while still), so whiskey is a **mud-steal wearing a potion slot** (the whole round was canonically "steal our mud stuff," 2026-09-15).

**chronicle/grudges = Dwarf Fortress annals + grudge system, with DeS world tendency underneath.**
Bytes: `chronicle()` writes the run's log from real events ("the log writes itself" — the comment literally says DF-steal via the mud); survivors of your hits (≥2 hits or 25%) get NAMED, hunt faster, and the chronicle records "X holds a grudge" — DF's worldgen grudges (historical figures remember conflicts) made intra-run. Under both: `fs_lore` — persisted, banner-announced world-state shifts from your deeds (snakes/cowboys/packs adapt) — that is **DeS world tendency** (deeds shift the world) crossed with the AI-director. So: chronicle = DF Legends/annals; grudges = DF grudges; fs_lore = world tendency. Bones? *Adjacent but not it*: fs_lore remembers your *behavior*, not your *corpse*. The chronicle is the ledger; bones are the haunting. That distinction is exactly what's missing — next section.

## Part III — the one missing ancestor mechanic

**BONES FILES.** NetHack 1987: the dead run returns as an entity — corpse, gear, name, ghost — in a later run, ideally another player's.

frontier_souls already has every prerequisite and is one constant away:
- The chronicle already names and remembers; `fs_lore` already persists across runs in localStorage; the **stranger's grave event already exists** (storyline chain 1: grave → journal → hermit). The weave: **make the grave bones-fed.** A dead run serializes a `fs_bones.v1` record (name/char/ring/steps/cause of death/gun mod/chronicle tail — W2's law applies directly: canonical JSON, sha256-chained, no timestamps, append-only ledger, resume-from-ledger), and later runs load it: the grave you find in run 3 is YOUR run-1 corpse — named, with your mod on the body, the chronicle reading "allzaban fell here, day 3, killed by a rattlesnake." Grudge-scale escalation: the bones ghost rises as a named revenant hunting with the lore it died learning.
- This is also HG1's sovereign niche #1 (AI-run bones files — dead runs as AI haunting later runs), pointed at the mud; frontier_souls is the cheapest place to land it first because its grave event, naming, chronicle, and `fs_lore` persistence are all shipped bytes.

## Part IV — verdict: is the 3-phase death an invention or a rediscovery?

**Verdict: REDISCOVERY — a recombinant one.** Every component has a prior; the assembly is original.

- The 3-phase structure itself (HP thresholds 66%/33%, escalating patterns, adds, new mobility): the arcade boss grammar — pattern changes at HP thresholds — carried into roguelikes by **FTL's Rebel Flagship (2012): three HP-threshold phases, new adds and mechanics each phase.** FTL is the pitch's own named ancestor ("FTL meets the wild west"), so this is a rediscovery *inside the game's declared lineage*.
- Death as a characterized recurring antagonist hosting the loop: **Hades** (death is the story's host; you fight through death to argue with your family) plus the Nemesis-pursuer archetype (RE3 1999: the stalker that hunts you across the map).
- The economy inside the fight — Death's hits drink your decomposition timer, heal him, and spill 8 recoverable essence orbs: the **souls soul-run turned live** (recovery happens mid-duel instead of after death), fused with Hades' death-timer stress.

**Closest single prior: FTL's Rebel Flagship** (structure — 3 HP-threshold phases with escalating adds, from the pitch's own ancestor), with **Hades** as the closest prior for the framing (death itself as the antagonist you race toward). What is genuinely novel is not the phases but the *mutual hunt*: the player walks outward to catch Death while Death walks inward to reap you — a two-way pursuit over a walk-to-level radial world with a decomposition clock — and the in-fight timer-drinking/essence-spill economy. That assembly, to my knowledge of the record, has no shipped prior. Original recomposition, not a new primitive.
