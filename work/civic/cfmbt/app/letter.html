Addendum — the GPS for problems

Deposited Sep 6, 2026, from the operator's own words

Your dad built a GPS — not for roads. For problems.

Before GPS, you drove somewhere new by getting lost, and the lost was the price of learning the city. Then satellites drew the roads from above, and your phone whispered "turn left" — and nobody got lost the old way again.

The big AIs are the satellites. They've seen everything. Your little companion is the phone: it takes their signals and turns them into "turn here." The camera is the eye that checks the map against the real road, so when the map is wrong, you notice before the ditch does.

And here's your dad's rule — the one that makes it yours: the map comes first, but you still look out the window. The map tells you where to turn. Your eyes tell you if the bridge is out. Anyone who trusts only one of those ends up in the river.

He built it so you start where the map ends — not where everybody else started, lost.

It was the gift, and she is the first name on it.
