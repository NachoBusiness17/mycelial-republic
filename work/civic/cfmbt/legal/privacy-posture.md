# Privacy Posture — what this page publishes, what it refuses, and why

**CFMBT v5 · legal/privacy-posture.md · 2026-09-22**

## The design line

Collect what is needed to detect influence, publish what is needed to deter it,
and never publish what identifies a private investor who is not a covered person.

## The boundaries (scored we-have-a-record · basis Evidence)

1. **Privacy boundary — natural-person holder lists.** The Court of Justice of
   the European Union, joined cases C-37/20 and C-601/20 (22 November 2022),
   struck down general-public access to beneficial-ownership registers as a
   disproportionate interference with privacy. This page treats that as the
   outer fence: no open natural-person bondholder registry, ever. Legitimate-
   interest access (press, research, oversight with a stated purpose) remains
   the lawful channel where such data lawfully exists.

2. **Settlement structure — DTC book-entry.** Municipal securities sit in
   book-entry form at Cede & Co., nominee of The Depository Trust Company.
   The county cannot see beneficial owners; that is a feature of a market
   built for settlement speed. This page accepts the structure and works
   around it with decision-speed disclosure, fund-level sunlight, and the
   threshold rule. It never pierces DTC files.

3. **Federal-filing boundary — the Tower Amendment.** Exchange Act §15B(d),
   15 U.S.C. §78o-4(d), bars the SEC and MSRB from requiring issuers to file
   or disclose. It does not bar a county from adopting an ethics rule for its
   own officials and vendors. Pillar C is a local ethics rule on that hook —
   not a new federal regime.

4. **Broker KYC / BSA-AML.** Know-your-customer files belong to institutions
   and their regulators. They are not a county website feed, and nothing here
   requests them.

5. **Public-records law.** The conflict file, fund-level positions, and the
   budget line are public records and are published. Personal financial detail
   beyond a covered-person threshold filing (which collects band, not size)
   stays exempt.

6. **Over-collection control.** Pillar C collects exactly: role, name-or-seat,
   Yes/No, amount band, date. No SSN. No account numbers. No exact position
   size. Non-response is published as "no response received" with the date.

## What the market transparency regime already does — and does not do

- FDTA 2022 (Title LVIII, NDAA FY2023): muni filings become machine-readable.
  No owner identity is added.
- SEC Rule 15c2-12 (2019 amendments): private bank loans are placed on EMMA.
  No owner names.
- GFOA guidance speaks to beneficial owners of *defaulted* securities only.
- NFMA (2015) maps transaction-participant conflicts: advisory, not a county rule.
- Beneficial-ownership transparency work (Tax Justice Network REBOT 2023;
  Open Ownership Principles 2023) targets company shells, not municipal
  bondholders.
- Tower Amendment note: it bars a federal mandate; it does not bar a county
  from its own ethics rule.

## Enforcement

Existing ethics-commission penalties. If a covered person fails to disclose
and is later shown by the record to hold the bonds, that is an ethics
violation — not a missing owner registry.
